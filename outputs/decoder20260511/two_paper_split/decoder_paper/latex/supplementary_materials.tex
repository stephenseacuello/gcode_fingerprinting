\section{Per-Operation-Class Recoverability}
\label{supp:sec-per-op-class}

The autoregressive predictions stratify sharply by operation class (Figure~\ref{fig:per_op_class}). One class --- \texttt{face} ($n=144$ test windows) --- reaches token $0.56$ / numeric $0.40$ vs.\ pooled $0.215 / 0.104$; every other class is stuck at $0.09$--$0.25$ token / $0.01$--$0.15$ numeric. This is the per-class signature of the mode-collapse mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}): the corpus-modal AR output \emph{is} a facing-pattern toolpath, so \texttt{face} windows score by coincidental agreement and the most-frequent non-facing class (\texttt{pocket150025}, $n=124$) sits at $0.09 / 0.01$.

\section{Calibration of the Categorical Heads}
\label{supp:sec-calibration}

A high-accuracy classifier may still be poorly calibrated --- its predicted-class confidence may systematically over- or under-estimate the empirical accuracy at that confidence. For a deployment that surfaces row-level instruction predictions to a human operator or to a downstream verification rule, calibrated confidence matters as much as accuracy: a $0.99$-confidence prediction should be correct $99\%$ of the time. We first report the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Maximum Calibration Error (MCE) at $10$ bins on the fold-1 baseline test set ($n_{\text{cmd}} = 1{,}982$ for command positions, $n_{\text{type}} = 37{,}590$ for all type positions, $n_{\text{pt}} = 35{,}392$ for parameter-type positions); these fold-1 numbers and the reliability diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:reliability_diagram}) are \emph{illustrative} of the worst (outlier) fold, and the deployment-relevant figures are the cross-fold means reported in the next paragraph. Results: type head ECE $= 0.015$, MCE $= 0.037$ at $0.968$ accuracy and $0.954$ mean confidence; parameter-type ECE $= 0.020$, MCE $= 0.114$ at $0.985$ accuracy and $0.964$ mean confidence; command head ECE $= 0.122$, MCE $= 0.207$ at $0.780$ accuracy and $0.902$ mean confidence (\texttt{audit/calibration\_fold1.json}; Figure~\ref{fig:reliability_diagram}). The type and parameter-type heads are well-calibrated by the conventional thresholds ($\text{ECE} < 0.025$). The command head is moderately mis-calibrated and \emph{overconfident}: its mean predicted confidence exceeds its empirical accuracy by $12$~pp, with the largest gap of $20.7$~pp concentrated in the highest-confidence bin. The overconfidence is consistent with the LOCO collapse pattern in the main paper: the model places high mass on G1 even on test windows whose true command is not G1, producing high-confidence wrong predictions.

\paragraph{Brier score and Murphy decomposition.} ECE summarizes binwise miscalibration but discards discrimination. We therefore also report the Brier score on top-1 predicted-class confidence (one-vs-rest, same population as the ECE figures above) and its Murphy decomposition $\mathrm{BS}\!=\!\mathrm{REL}\!-\!\mathrm{RES}\!+\!\mathrm{UNC}$: REL is the mean-squared confidence-vs-accuracy gap (lower is better), RES is the variance of binwise accuracy around the marginal (higher is better), and $\mathrm{UNC}\!=\!\bar{p}(1-\bar{p})$ is irreducible. Fold-1: command $\mathrm{BS}\!=\!0.164$ (REL $0.017$, RES $0.025$, UNC $0.172$); type $\mathrm{BS}\!=\!0.024$ ($0.0003 / 0.008 / 0.031$); parameter-type $\mathrm{BS}\!=\!0.014$ ($0.0009 / 0.003 / 0.015$). The command head's $\mathrm{RES}\!>\!\mathrm{REL}$ shows that it does discriminate --- the high-confidence bin is more often correct than the marginal --- and the bulk of its Brier mass is the irreducible UNC term driven by the $0.78$ baseline accuracy. Temperature scaling targets REL and leaves RES unchanged (because $T$ does not move the argmax): the ranking of confident-vs-uncertain predictions is preserved while absolute confidence values are softened. This is the formal reason temperature scaling reduces ECE without sacrificing the discrimination signal the row-level verification rule depends on.

\paragraph{Post-hoc temperature scaling.} The standard remedy for overconfidence is post-hoc temperature scaling: divide the logits by a single learned scalar $T > 0$ before the softmax. We split each fold's test positions $50 / 50$ into a calibration half (used only to fit $T$ by minimizing NLL via LBFGS) and an evaluation half (used to measure post-correction ECE), with the seed-42 random split applied per head per fold. The cross-fold-aggregated fitted temperatures are $T_{\text{cmd}} = 1.34 \pm 0.25$, $T_{\text{type}} = 0.61 \pm 0.15$, $T_{\text{pt}} = 0.57 \pm 0.09$ ($T > 1$ softens an overconfident distribution; $T < 1$ sharpens an underconfident one); fold 1 fits the highest command-head temperature ($T_{\text{cmd}}^{(1)} = 1.77$) because fold 1 is the outlier fold with the largest covariate-shift effect. Across the 5 folds, the temperature-scaled command head reaches ECE $0.020 \pm 0.011$ (mean $\pm$ std) on the evaluation half --- a $3.0$~pp ECE reduction relative to the un-scaled cross-fold baseline of $0.050 \pm 0.038$. Type and parameter-type heads, already well-calibrated, gain similarly in absolute terms ($0.033 \to 0.004$ and $0.025 \to 0.002$ on ECE; \texttt{audit/calibration\_cross\_fold.json}). Accuracy is unchanged because temperature scaling does not move the argmax. One caveat for the over-sharpened heads: on the already-well-calibrated parameter-type head, $T\!<\!1$ reduces mean ECE but \emph{raises} the worst-bin MCE on several folds (up to $0.43$; per-fold range $0.11$--$0.43$), trading average calibration for a heavier worst-bin tail. A verification rule that thresholds on a single high-confidence bin should therefore apply temperature scaling to the over-confident command head but may prefer the un-scaled parameter-type head (\texttt{audit/calibration\_cross\_fold.json}). Temperature scaling adds a single fit-time scalar per head and no inference-time cost; we recommend it as the default deployment-time calibration step for the command head. As a single-scalar post-hoc correction it is known to degrade under distribution shift, consistent with the fold-1 outlier behavior.

\section{Autoregressive Mode Collapse: Worked Example and Pairwise-Prediction Heatmap}
\label{sec:disc-mode-collapse-worked}

The main paper's mode-collapse subsection (sec:disc-mode-collapse) reports the load-bearing output-diversity audit (407 distinct predicted strings vs.\ 459 ground-truth; modal-pattern $3.3\times$ over-representation). This supplementary section provides the illustrative worked example and the pairwise-prediction-similarity heatmap that supported the original presentation.

To illustrate, we inspected the first three test samples in fold~1's full-window split and report their autoregressive (beam-width-one) decodings:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
\begin{tabular}{l p{0.78\textwidth}}
sample 1 TRUE & X NUM\_X\_1627 Y NUM\_Y\_1464 Z NUM\_Z\_0095 X NUM\_X\_1623 Y NUM\_Y\_1687 \ldots \\
sample 1 PRED & X NUM\_X\_1466 Y NUM\_Y\_1630 Z NUM\_Z\_0052 X NUM\_X\_1455 Y NUM\_Y\_1607 \ldots \\
\addlinespace
sample 2 TRUE & X NUM\_X\_1466 Y NUM\_Y\_1630 Z NUM\_Z\_0052 X NUM\_X\_1457 Y NUM\_Y\_1607 \ldots \\
sample 2 PRED & X NUM\_X\_1466 Y NUM\_Y\_1630 Z NUM\_Z\_0052 X NUM\_X\_1457 Y NUM\_Y\_1607 \ldots \\
\addlinespace
sample 3 TRUE & X NUM\_X\_1569 Y NUM\_Y\_1453 Z NUM\_Z\_0097 X NUM\_X\_1676 Y NUM\_Y\_1645 \ldots \\
sample 3 PRED & X NUM\_X\_1466 Y NUM\_Y\_1630 Z NUM\_Z\_0052 X NUM\_X\_1455 Y NUM\_Y\_1607 \ldots \\
\end{tabular}
\end{quote}
Samples 1 and 3 receive essentially the same predicted token stream as each other, despite having visibly different TRUE sequences. Sample 2's TRUE happens to coincide with the corpus-modal sequence, so it scores near-perfectly purely by accident. The decoder has converged to a canonical ``facing-operation toolpath'' and emits it autoregressively regardless of the sensor input that conditioned the encoder memory. Cross-attention into the encoder memory is effectively dropped under autoregressive generation: the decoder's own previous-token state dominates, and once on the modal trajectory the decoder stays there. Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} quantifies the effect as a pairwise prediction-similarity matrix across test samples.

\section{Extended Ablation: Per-Sensor Encoder Retraining (Leave-One-Sensor-Out)}
\label{app:loso-extended}
\label{sec:abl-lomo-sensor}

The leave-one-modality-out study in the main paper ablates the
\emph{channel-type} axis: it removes a sensing modality --- e.g.\ all
gyroscope channels --- across every sensor board at once. The multi-modal
stack, however, has an orthogonal axis. The continuous channels are
physically distributed across \textbf{six sensor units} at distinct machine
locations, and each unit carries every per-board modality. A modality cannot
be removed from a deployed machine without removing the boards that carry
it; a real cost-reduced deployment removes \emph{sensor units}. We therefore
also run leave-one-\emph{sensor}-out (LOSO), the directly deployment-relevant
test of which physical sensor a production monitor could ship without.

\paragraph{Protocol.} The 98-channel base comprises six physical sensor
units --- \texttt{frame\_l2}, \texttt{frame\_l3}, \texttt{frame\_r2},
\texttt{spindle2}, \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}, \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} --- each
carrying an identical 15-channel set (3 accelerometer, 3 gyroscope, 3
magnetometer, 4 color, 1 temperature, 1 audio-RMS); the 8 electrical
channels are machine-controller signals, not a removable board, and are
retained throughout. For each of the six units we retrain the encoder from
scratch on the 83-channel set with that unit's channels removed, then train
a fresh six-head decoder on the resulting frozen encoder (the same
full-window headline recipe as the main paper), at the full
five-fold file-level cross-validation protocol. The per-cell
channel-identity gate (operated in \emph{sensor} mode, matching by column
prefix) verifies that exactly the unit's 15 channels were removed and that
the retrained encoder and the decoder consume an identical 83-channel
surviving set. Statistical significance is the fold-paired analysis of
the main paper's LOMO study: per-fold deltas
$\Delta(u,f)=\text{command}(u,f)-\text{command}(\text{baseline},f)$, a
one-sample $t$-test of each unit's deltas against zero with Holm correction
across the six units, plus a fold-blocked one-way ANOVA on the deltas.

\paragraph{The finding.} Under LOSO retraining the full-stack baseline
reaches command accuracy $0.905 \pm 0.032$ (five-fold mean $\pm$ s.d.,
indistinguishable from the modality study's baseline of $0.904 \pm 0.039$).
Removing a single physical sensor unit and retraining yields
\texttt{frame\_l2} $0.882 \pm 0.046$, \texttt{frame\_l3} $0.888 \pm 0.040$,
\texttt{frame\_r2} $0.890 \pm 0.038$, \texttt{spindle2} $0.879 \pm 0.069$,
\texttt{y\_bed\_\_3} $0.897 \pm 0.035$, and \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}
$0.904 \pm 0.026$. The fold-paired effects span from \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}'s
$\Delta = -0.000 \pm 0.014$ to \texttt{spindle2}'s $\Delta = -0.026 \pm 0.040$
--- larger in magnitude than the modality study (where every $\Delta$ was
within $\pm 0.013$) but \textbf{none reaching Holm-corrected significance}
at $\alpha=0.05$ (smallest $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.064$ for \texttt{frame\_r2},
the most consistent effect at $\Delta = -0.015 \pm 0.007$;
Hedges' $g_{z}\!\approx\!-1.62$, a large effect that fails Holm
significance only because of the multiplicity correction at $n\!=\!5$).
The fold-blocked one-way ANOVA across the six LOSO conditions is
non-significant ($F=1.04$, $p=0.415$). \texttt{spindle2} carries the largest
$\Delta$ ($-0.026$) but with $\sim 5\times$ higher variance than
\texttt{frame\_r2} (s.d.\ $0.040$ vs $0.007$; Hedges' $g_{z}\!\approx\!-0.52$,
medium), consistent with workpiece-dependent rather than uniform
load-bearing; \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} at $\Delta \approx 0$,
$p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.975$, and $g_{z}\!\approx\!0$ is empirically
indistinguishable from baseline and is the single ``free'' removal,
fully redundant with its spatial neighbor \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}.
Table~\ref{tab:lomo_sensor} reports the per-sensor numbers and
Figure~\ref{fig:lomo_sensor} shows the per-sensor paired $\Delta$ command;
full per-fold $\Delta$, Holm-corrected $p$, and effect sizes are in
\texttt{audit/lomo\_sensor\_attribution.json}.

\input{../tables/lomo_sensor.tex}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{lomo_sensor_bars.pdf}
\caption{LOSO encoder retraining: per-sensor fold-paired $\Delta$ command versus retrained baseline (zero line), 5-fold error bars. No sensor reaches Holm significance at $\alpha=0.05$; \texttt{frame\_r2} closest ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.064$). \texttt{spindle2} largest magnitude ($\Delta = -0.026$) but most variable (s.d.\ $0.040$); \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} at $\Delta\!\approx\!0$, redundant with \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}.}

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Effect sizes are roughly $2\times$ those of
the channel-type LOMO study (max $\Delta\!=\!-0.026$ for \texttt{spindle2}
vs.\ $-0.013$ for electrical), reflecting
that a physical sensor unit's 15-channel vector overlaps less perfectly
with the remaining units than a channel-type does with the remaining
channel-types. No LOSO removal reaches Holm-corrected significance and
the fold-blocked ANOVA cannot distinguish the six conditions
($F\!=\!1.04$, $p\!=\!0.415$). The deployment-relevant LOSO numbers are
$\le\!2.6$~pp expected cost per dropped sensor unit, the single
``free'' removal is \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} (fully redundant with
\texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}, $p_{\text{Holm}}\!=\!0.975$), and the unit a
deployment should be most cautious about omitting is \texttt{spindle2}
(largest-magnitude effect, also the most workpiece-variable). The
\emph{interior} of the modality $\times$ sensor grid is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo-nested}; the marginal flatness shown here is
preserved at that finer cut (ANOVA $F\!=\!0.694$, $p\!=\!0.896$;
$0/36$ Holm-significant).

\paragraph{Equivalence testing (evidence \emph{for} the null).} Holm-corrected non-significance is a failure to reject difference, not positive evidence for equivalence; we therefore also report two one-sided tests (TOST) at a pre-specified equivalence margin of $\pm 0.020$ command accuracy --- set in advance of unblinding the LOSO results as the smallest deployment-relevant effect (2.0~pp); the margin reflects a principled deployment threshold, not a public preregistration record --- and Bayes factors $\mathrm{BF}_{01}$ (evidence for $H_0$ over $H_1$) using the default JZS Cauchy prior $r\!=\!0.707$. \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} is equivalent to baseline ($\Delta\!=\!-0.0002$, TOST $p\!=\!0.018$, $\mathrm{BF}_{01}\!=\!1.87$, weak-to-moderate $H_0$ support) and \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3} is also equivalent ($\Delta\!=\!-0.008$, TOST $p\!=\!0.0036$; the $\mathrm{BF}_{01}\!=\!0.28$ is inconclusive at $n\!=\!5$). The remaining four units --- \texttt{frame\_r2}, \texttt{frame\_l3}, \texttt{frame\_l2}, \texttt{spindle2} --- are \emph{inconclusive} at this margin (TOST $p\!\in\![0.10, 0.62]$; $\mathrm{BF}_{01}\!\in\![0.19, 0.93]$): the data neither establish nor rule out a $\ge 2$~pp drop, and a larger fold count would be needed to discharge either hypothesis. The deployment recommendation tightens accordingly: \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} (or \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}, but not both jointly) is statistically dispensable; the other four units should be treated as load-bearing until proven otherwise.

\section{Extended Ablation: Nested Leave-One-(Sensor,~Modality)-Out Retraining}
\label{app:nested-lomo}
\label{sec:abl-lomo-nested}

The marginal LOMO and LOSO studies characterise removal cost along each of the stack's two constituent axes (channel-type and physical-sensor unit) and each marginal is null. A marginal null does not, however, guarantee an interior null: the modality marginal could in principle average over a strong $(\text{sensor},\text{modality})$ interaction --- e.g.\ the gyroscope marginal being null overall while \texttt{spindle2}'s gyroscope channels carry essentially all the spindle-board signal --- in which case the deployment-relevant decision would not be ``drop a modality'' or ``drop a sensor'' but ``drop a specific modality \emph{on} a specific sensor.'' This section reports the nested study that resolves that question.

\paragraph{Protocol.} We retrain the 98-feature multi-modal denoising encoder from scratch under each $(\text{sensor unit},\text{channel-type})$ cell of the $6\!\times\!6$ grid (six physical sensors $\times$ six per-sensor channel-types: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, color, temperature, audio-RMS; the electrical group is excluded from the interior because it is a global modality not tied to a single sensor unit). Each cell drops the $1$--$4$ channels at that $(\text{sensor},\text{modality})$ intersection, retrains the encoder, and trains a fresh six-head decoder under the headline recipe; five-fold file-level cross-validation matches the marginal studies. The per-cell channel-identity gate (operated in \emph{nested} mode, matching by sensor prefix \emph{and} modality suffix) verifies that exactly the intended intersection is removed, the surviving columns are identity-and-order preserved, and the train/validation/test partition and the G-code target multiset are invariant across cells. All $185$ cells reported here ($36$ interior cells plus the full-stack baseline, $\times$ five folds) pass that gate.

\paragraph{The finding.} The interior of the grid is flat. The fold-blocked one-way ANOVA across the $36$ nested cells is non-significant ($F\!=\!0.694$, $p\!=\!0.896$); the Holm-corrected per-cell paired $t$-test rejects \emph{none} of the $36$ cells at $\alpha\!=\!0.05$ (smallest adjusted $p\!=\!0.185$, for \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}$\,\times\,$gyroscope at $\Delta\!=\!+0.008\!\pm\!0.003$). The largest-magnitude effect is \texttt{frame\_l3}$\,\times\,$audio at $\Delta\!=\!-0.022\!\pm\!0.030$, whose sample s.d.\ exceeds the magnitude of the effect itself. Three further cells reach $|\Delta|\!\ge\!0.010$ --- \texttt{frame\_l2}$\,\times\,$audio ($+0.012$), \texttt{frame\_l2}$\,\times\,$accelerometer ($-0.011$), \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}$\,\times\,$gyroscope ($-0.010$) --- and none survives Holm correction. Every interior cell's paired $\Delta$ command is within $\pm 0.022$ of baseline, and crucially no interior cell exceeds either the corresponding modality marginal (largest $-0.013$) or the corresponding sensor marginal (largest $-0.026$). \paragraph{Interpretation.} The nested null is the strongest available evidence that the marginal nulls are not artefacts of averaging. We observe the opposite: every cell's removal cost is bounded above by \emph{both} the corresponding sensor marginal and the corresponding modality marginal, and no interior cell reaches even the modality marginal's magnitude. The command-discrimination signal is therefore \emph{densely redundant} along both axes of the stack --- it does not concentrate in any single physical sensor's specific channel-type --- and any single $(\text{sensor},\text{modality})$ subset can be dropped at essentially zero expected cost ($\le 2.2$~pp; none significant). The practical deployment implication is that the marginal degrees of freedom of the LOMO and LOSO studies can be \emph{combined} along the interior: a cost-reduced production monitor has freedom to omit not only entire sensor units or entire channel-types independently but also a sensor's specific channel-type (e.g.\ a board populated with color and temperature but with accelerometer omitted), without incurring the (already small) marginal cost of dropping either axis in full.

\section{Extended Ablation: Per-Operation-Class Slot-5 Stratification}
\label{app:source-entropy}

Stratifying the slot-5 distribution by \emph{operation class} reveals that the uniform-noise picture is not uniform across the corpus; three distinct regimes coexist (Figure~\ref{fig:slot5_by_op_class}). \textbf{(i) Adaptive HSM (High-Speed Machining) toolpaths} (\texttt{adaptive}, \texttt{adaptive150025}, and the \texttt{pocket} class, which is adaptive-style in this corpus) produce the canonical CAM-interpolation-noise signature: per-axis slot-5 entropy $\approx 3.26$--$3.30$ bits with comparatively low mass on digit `0' (for X: $9\%$ on \texttt{adaptive150025} through $17\%$ on \texttt{pocket} --- read off Figure~\ref{fig:slot5_by_op_class}) distributed across all digits with no concentrated modal value, the signature of a toolpath optimizer producing floating-point residuals rather than authoring round-ending coordinates at $10^{-4}$~inch ($2.54$~\textmu m). \textbf{(ii) Traditional facing operations} (\texttt{face}, \texttt{face150025}, \texttt{damageface}) have slot-5 entropy $\approx 1.55$--$1.71$ bits with $49$--$56\%$ mass on a single digit --- but the mechanism is geometric \emph{coordinate repetition}, not a CAM precision grid. In \texttt{face}, four specific X values --- the facing-pass entry/exit coordinates on the workpiece left and right edges --- dominate: $X\!=\!-0.1254$ ($n\!=\!624$), $X\!=\!-0.2084$ ($n\!=\!464$), $X\!=\!3.275$ ($n\!=\!608$), and $X\!=\!3.358$ ($n\!=\!464$), together $2{,}160$ of the $2{,}238$ X-coordinates ($96.5\%$). The two negative-X overruns drive the digit-`4' mass at slot~5; the two positive-X overruns drive mass at digits `0' and `9' (the latter via the float-epsilon target-encoding artifact of Section~\ref{supp:app-digit-encoding}). \textbf{(iii) Pocket-style operations with tight geometry} (\texttt{pocket150025}, \texttt{damagepocket}, \texttt{damageadaptive}) sit between: slot-5 entropy $\approx 2.58$--$3.22$ bits with partial mass on `0' from arc/line waypoint repeats (e.g., \texttt{Y-0.071} appearing dozens of times along a fixed pocket wall). The bathtub \emph{upturn} of the pooled slot-5 curve is therefore the joint signature of coordinate-repeat in traditional ops and a separate float-epsilon target-encoding artifact ($23.6\%$ of CAM-authored terminal zeros encode to digit `9' under the model's iterative \texttt{int(dec\_part * 10) \% 10}), both detailed in Section~\ref{supp:app-digit-encoding}, not a genuine per-axis recovery of the ten-thousandth digit. \section{Vocabulary-Cardinality Spectrum and Matched-Methodology Control}
\label{sec:abl-nonumeric-supp}

The main paper retains the no-numeric retraining headline (numeric desynchronization
decomposition); this section provides the five-point $K$-spectrum that
characterises the earlier ``$K\!=\!335$ sweet spot'' framing as a
training-schedule effect, not a vocabulary-cardinality effect.

We retrain at two intermediate numeric vocabularies (two-digit bucketing,
$K\!=\!335$; one-digit bucketing, $K\!=\!69$) under the Design-B-style
training schedule (scheduled sampling SS$=$0, digit-head loss weight
dw$=$0) to test whether the AR per-token improvement is a
placeholder-specific artifact, a vocabulary-cardinality effect, or a
training-schedule effect; vocabulary sweeps of this kind are a standard
diagnostic in subword-tokenization work.
We then add a fifth ``T3.1'' control at $K\!=\!2{,}418$ retrained under
the same SS$=$0/dw$=$0 schedule to disentangle vocabulary from
schedule cleanly. Table~\ref{tab:k_spectrum} and
Figure~\ref{fig:k_spectrum} report the resulting five-point spectrum.

\emph{Headline result (T3.1 resolves the confound).} Under the matched
SS$=$0/dw$=$0 training schedule, $K\!=\!2{,}418$ (T3.1 control) is the
highest-performing variant on every metric: AR struct token
$0.470 \pm 0.034$ (vs.\ the headline's $0.317 \pm 0.116$, paired
$+15.2$~pp, $5/5$ folds positive, paired $t(4)\!=\!3.26$,
Holm-adjusted $p\!=\!0.071$, Cohen's $d_{z}\!=\!1.46$, Hedges'
$g_{z}\!=\!1.17$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[+7.6, +24.1]$~pp); AR sequence
exact-match $0.128 \pm 0.019$ vs.\ headline $0.058 \pm 0.039$, paired
$+7.1$~pp, $5/5$ folds, paired $t(4)\!=\!7.70$, Holm-adjusted
$p\!=\!0.006$, $d_{z}\!=\!3.44$, $g_{z}\!=\!2.76$; TF token $0.978$
vs.\ $0.960$; TF sequence exact-match $0.344$ vs.\ $0.213$. \emph{The
earlier $+11.1$~pp AR-token advantage of $K\!=\!335$ over the
headline was the SS/dw schedule change, not vocabulary cardinality}:
within the matched-methodology family, $K\!=\!335$ falls $-4.1$~pp
behind the matched $K\!=\!2{,}418$ on AR struct token (paired
$t(4)\!=\!-0.85$, $p\!=\!0.44$, n.s.; bootstrap 95\% CI on the paired
diff $[-13.8, +3.0]$~pp).

\emph{Within-matched-methodology cardinality effect.} Across the four
SS$=$0/dw$=$0 variants $\{K\!=\!24, 69, 335, 2{,}418\}$, AR struct
token is monotonically non-decreasing in $K$ ($0.405$, $0.415$,
$0.429$, $0.470$); AR struct sequence exact-match is broadly increasing
in $K$ ($0.066$, $0.051$, $0.081$, $0.128$) with a minor non-monotonicity
at $K\!=\!69$, but the matched-methodology $K\!=\!2{,}418$ control
remains the strict maximum. Larger $K$ is mildly better
within the matched family; the effect is small relative to the
$+15$~pp schedule effect. The omnibus across all five variants is
significant on both metrics: AR struct token $F(4,16)\!=\!3.42$,
$p\!=\!0.033$, partial $\eta^{2}\!=\!0.46$; Friedman exact / Monte-Carlo
$p\!=\!0.0098$ (asymptotic $\chi^{2}(4)\!=\!13.9$, $p\!=\!0.0076$, demoted
because $\chi^{2}$ is unreliable at $n\!=\!5$ blocks); AR struct sequence
$F(4,16)\!=\!7.38$, $p\!=\!0.0014$, $\eta^{2}\!=\!0.65$; Friedman exact /
Monte-Carlo $p\!=\!0.029$ (asymptotic $\chi^{2}(4)\!=\!11.4$,
$p\!=\!0.0224$, demoted for the same reason).

\emph{Interpretation.} The original $K\!=\!335$ ``sweet spot'' framing
is retracted: under matched methodology, the larger $K\!=\!2{,}418$
vocabulary is the strict winner, and the +9--11~pp gains observed for
smaller-$K$ variants over the headline are recovered by the
SS$=$0/dw$=$0 training schedule alone. The deployment-relevant
configuration for AR structural recovery is the original
$K\!=\!2{,}418$ vocabulary with scheduled sampling disabled and the
digit head detached during training. Full per-fold values,
exact and Monte-Carlo Friedman values, paired-$t$ / Wilcoxon /
$d_{z}$ / $g_{z}$ / bootstrap CIs for every pair, and Holm-adjusted
$p$-values across both the K=2418-headline-reference family and the
matched-methodology family are in \texttt{audit/k\_spectrum\_stats.json}.

\section{Supplementary Diagnostic Analyses}
\label{supp:app-diagnostics}
This section collects six corroborating diagnostic analyses referenced from the body: test-time noise sensitivity, nested ablations, per-file variance, decoding-strategy sensitivity, output-position failure distribution, and the tamper operating-point detail.

\subsection{Test-Time Sensor-Noise Sensitivity}
\label{app:noise}

To characterize how robust the categorical heads are to deployment-time sensor degradation, we re-evaluate fold 1 of the baseline 5-fold checkpoint while injecting additive Gaussian noise into the frozen-encoder memory at varying $\sigma$ relative to the memory's empirical standard deviation (\texttt{audit/test\_time\_noise\_sensitivity.json}). This is a noise-robustness experiment on the \emph{encoder-to-decoder interface}, not on the \emph{raw sensor inputs}: noise injected at the memory level probes how stable the decoder is to perturbations in its conditioning vector but does not characterize robustness to raw-sensor degradation (the encoder is non-linear and may not propagate input noise to memory noise in a simple way). A raw-sensor-input noise sweep is outside the scope of the present study. The categorical heads tested at the memory level are exceptionally robust: across $\sigma \in [0, 2]$ (i.e., up to twice the magnitude of the signal itself), command-head accuracy varies from $0.78$ to $0.82$, type accuracy stays at $0.97$, and parameter-type accuracy stays at $0.98$ (Figure~\ref{fig:noise}). The slight upward drift on the command head is consistent with random noise acting as a label-smoothing regularizer, pushing predictions toward the modal class (G1) on borderline cases. The sign head is omitted from Figure~\ref{fig:noise} for a methodological reason that does \emph{not} affect the headline. The headline sign accuracy ($0.989$) is computed by the robust path that parses the decoded and target G-code text and compares the extracted per-axis signs (\texttt{scripts/analysis/v8\_per\_field\_eval.py}); the noise-sensitivity audit instead re-derived the sign target from a shifted raw target tensor under a mismatched index alignment, which yields an artifactual $0.10$ at $\sigma=0$ rather than the true $\approx\!0.99$. We re-ran the robust path on fold~1 and confirmed it reproduces the per-axis sign accuracies of the main paper ($0.88$/$0.98$/$0.91$ for X/Y/Z at fold~1, the documented covariate-shift fold); the defect is confined to the noise-audit script and the headline sign metric is unaffected. We therefore omit the sign curve from the noise figure rather than report a number derived from the defective audit path. The other three heads validate cleanly against their headline fold-1 numbers at $\sigma=0$. The qualitative conclusion is that the categorical-recovery story this paper reports does not degrade gracefully but holds with surprising robustness to encoder-memory degradation; whether this robustness extends to raw-sensor noise on the input side is not addressed here.

\subsection{Per-File Performance Variance}
\label{app:perfile}

A separate concern is whether the model memorizes a small set of files rather than learning the operation-level signal. We slice the 5-fold full-window test predictions by source file and report per-file accuracy (the per-file slice is derived from the released \texttt{full\_window\_5fold} prediction JSONs; $n=91$ files appear in at least one test split across the 5 folds). The cross-file distribution is concentrated, not heavy-tailed: command accuracy is $0.953 \pm 0.088$ across files with range $[0.571, 1.000]$ and 10th--90th percentile range $[0.846, 1.000]$; parameter-type accuracy is $0.991$ across files. This per-file mean ($0.953$) is an \emph{unweighted average over the $91$ files} and is not directly comparable to the $0.8875 \pm 0.0558$ headline command accuracy in the main paper, which is the mean over the five file-level folds: the per-file slice weights every file equally regardless of which fold it fell in, whereas the headline weights every fold equally; the gap reflects this aggregation difference (and the fold-1 command outlier) rather than a discrepancy in the underlying predictions. Token accuracy under autoregressive decoding shows much wider per-file variance ($0.302 \pm 0.283$, range $[0.052, 0.869]$), which is the per-file signature of the same per-operation-class breakdown reported in Section~\ref{supp:sec-per-op-class}: \texttt{face}-class files cluster at the high end and the other classes at the low end. The narrowness of the command-accuracy distribution and the absence of a heavy left tail support the interpretation that the categorical-head success is not concentrated on a small set of memorizable files: at the 10th percentile of files, command accuracy is still $0.846$.

\section{Numeric Target Encoding Artifacts}
\label{supp:app-digit-encoding}

This section audits a float-epsilon target-encoding artifact (with committed patch) supporting the per-axis source-entropy claims in the main paper.

\paragraph{Float-epsilon target-encoding audit and patch.} The historical \texttt{encode\_values\_to\_digits} (\texttt{digit\_\allowbreak value\_head.py}) iteratively multiplied the decimal part by ten and truncated (\texttt{slot[s] = int(dec\_part * 10) \% 10}), which is exact in IEEE 754 only for exactly representable values; e.g., \texttt{1.5680} is stored as $1.567999\ldots$, so the chain yields \texttt{int(5679.99\ldots) \% 10 = 9} at slot~5 rather than the authored $0$. Auditing every $(\text{axis},\text{val})$ pair extracted from \texttt{gcode\_texts} across the 5-fold test corpus ($n\!=\!79{,}563$ slot-5 positions; Table~\ref{tab:float_epsilon_audit}) finds $23.6\%$ of CAM-authored terminal-zero values encoded as slot-5~$=9$ (X $32.8\%$; R $0.0\%$, R being uniformly 3-decimal and FP-clean); the same mechanism produces $d\!\to\!d\!+\!1$ shifts on $26.3\%$ of upstream-decimal positions overall. A round-to-scaled-integer rewrite ($\texttt{scaled} = \texttt{round}(\lvert v \rvert \cdot 10^{4})$ once, then floor-divide per slot) has been committed to \texttt{src/\allowbreak miracle/\allowbreak model/\allowbreak digit\_value\_head.py} on the development branch; the verification harness (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak analysis/\allowbreak verify\_digit\_encoding\_fix.py}) confirms it produces zero corruptions on the same $79{,}563$ positions ($4.5\%\!\to\!0.000\%$ at slot~5). The patch is \emph{not} active in the released checkpoint that populates the categorical headline tables and the threat-model operating points; the numeric-tier figures (per-digit slots, the bathtub, and the per-(axis,~slot) entropy--accuracy relationship) are instead computed on a separate patched 5-fold retrain, described next. The artifact lives at the model-target level, not at the source distribution, so the per-operation-class regimes, four-X-overrun finding, the shallow rapid-vs-cut Z structure, and the arc-radius-vocabulary structure on R are unaffected.

Reproducing the main paper's per-digit-position table uses the \emph{patched} encoder. The round-to-scaled-integer rewrite above was wired into the trainer and a 5-fold patched-encoder retrain was completed (\texttt{checkpoints/full\_window\_5fold\_patched/}; \texttt{audit/patched\_numeric\_analysis.json}); the body's slot-3/4/5 accuracies, the per-digit bathtub, and the per-(axis,~slot) entropy--accuracy relationship are all computed on that retrain. \textbf{What the patch resolved.} On the corrected encoding the per-(axis,~slot) cell-level relationship strengthens to Pearson $r\!=\!-0.83$ (Spearman $\rho\!=\!-0.91$, $n\!=\!30$) from the pre-patch $-0.68$, which had carried an encoding inconsistency between the entropy and accuracy axes; and the slot-5 ``upturn'' is correctly understood as the V8 tokenizer's $0.001$ grid discarding the X/Y/Z ten-thousandths --- the tokenized target is a constant recovered trivially (a representation artifact), not a true $10^{-4}$-inch recovery and not the pre-patch float-epsilon digit-`9' shift. The earlier ``slot-5 bounded by a known encoding artifact, patched retrain pending'' status is therefore \emph{superseded}: the retrain is complete and supplies the body's numeric-tier figures, while the categorical headline tables and threat-model operating points remain on the released checkpoint (TF/AR- and checkpoint-invariant, $\le\!1.5$~pp drift under the patch). The operation-class stratification in Section~\ref{app:source-entropy} independently explains the slot-5 behavior decoder-free, on the raw source.

\section{Coverage-Repair Split Procedure}
\label{app:coverage-repair}

File-level stratified $k$-fold splits prevent within-file leakage but do not guarantee \emph{token coverage}: a held-out fold may contain G-code lines that never appear in the corresponding training partner, in which case the decoder is being evaluated on a vocabulary it has not had the opportunity to learn. The coverage-repair procedure described here \emph{constructs a closed-vocabulary evaluation regime} --- it deliberately removes out-of-vocabulary test lines so that the 5-fold experiment measures within-vocabulary classification rather than open-vocabulary generalization --- while preserving the file-level disjointness. This is a design choice, not a bias correction: the resulting headline numbers are within-vocabulary near-retrieval results, and the open-vocabulary capability is reported separately by the leave-one-class-out protocol (Section~\ref{sec:loco}). We make the regime explicit here and quantify the resulting train/test line overlap in Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}.

Given a candidate (train, test) partition for fold $i$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=*]
\item Tokenize the G-code text of every window in both splits and compute $U_{\text{test}}$ and $U_{\text{train}}$, the sets of distinct G-code lines occurring in each.
\item Let $M = U_{\text{test}} \setminus U_{\text{train}}$ be the set of test-only lines.
\item If $M = \emptyset$, accept the partition. Otherwise identify the file $f^\star \in \text{test}$ contributing the largest number of $M$-lines, and the file $g^\star \in \text{train}$ whose removal-and-replacement minimally perturbs the per-class fold proportions. Swap $f^\star$ and $g^\star$.
\item Recompute $M$ and repeat until $M = \emptyset$ or until a swap-budget of $10$ is exhausted (in which case the residual $M$-lines are recorded as a "novel-vocabulary at test" diagnostic but the partition is accepted).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Tie-breaking and determinism.} Step (3) iterates over files; on ties --- when two or more candidate $f^\star$ contribute the same number of $M$-lines, or when two or more candidate $g^\star$ produce the same per-class-proportion perturbation --- the implementation breaks the tie by ascending alphabetical filename ordering, which is fully deterministic and not dependent on process-level hash ordering. An earlier release of the script iterated an unordered Python \texttt{set}, which produced a hash-seed-dependent partition order across preprocessing runs (the latent reproducibility defect surfaced and corrected during the LOMO study; Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}). The deterministic alphabetical-tie-break version is the one used for \emph{every} 5-fold result reported in this paper (headline, ablations, LOMO, LOSO, calibration, tamper), and the resulting per-fold (file, fold) assignment table is released as \texttt{audit/file\_fold\_assignment.csv} so a reproducer does not need to rerun the split logic.

In practice the procedure converges in 0--3 swaps per fold on the present corpus. Recorded fold sizes after coverage repair differ from the stratified target by at most one file per class.

\section{Encoder Specification Addendum}
\label{app:encoder-spec}

The encoder architecture, channel layout, denoising objective, and 96.3\% nine-class accuracy are stated in Section~\ref{sec:encoder} and the encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper}, which is under review at the time of submission; for self-containment, the salient architectural facts are: an 8.6~M-parameter multi-modal denoising autoencoder over $C\!=\!98$ channels (seven modality groups: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, environmental, color, audio-RMS, electrical; channel inventory per board listed in Section~\ref{sec:dataset}); per-modality projection into a shared $d\!=\!256$ space; learned modality-weighting gates; multi-head self-attention; a denoising reconstruction objective with $\sigma_{\text{noise}}\!=\!0.1$ on standard-scaled inputs; and a two-layer LSTM ($d\!=\!256$) producing the $256\!\times\!256$ memory consumed by the decoder. Training: 30 epochs on the same 5-fold file-level splits as the decoder, AdamW ($\eta\!=\!10^{-4}$), batch size 32, dropout 0.2, early stopping on validation operation-class accuracy. Frozen-encoder checkpoint hashes per fold are released alongside the decoder checkpoints (Supplementary Section~S19.1). The note relevant to this paper's interpretation is the \emph{representational confound}: the encoder was trained for nine-class operation discrimination on the \emph{same} file-level 5-fold splits used by the decoder, so its memory primarily encodes operation-class structure. This bounds what the decoder's categorical heads can recover and is the representational counterpart of the LOCO collapse (Section~\ref{sec:disc-recoverability}, Section~\ref{sec:loco}). \paragraph{Representation-reuse caveat (raised in review).} Because the encoder was trained on the same file-level fold partitions later used to evaluate the decoder, the encoder did see (during its own training) the sensor windows from the decoder's test files within other folds; the headline 0.888 command number therefore contains an unmeasured representation-reuse component on top of the per-instruction sensor recovery the paper claims. The 22 pp class-prior-controlled increment (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}) is the load-bearing magnitude, but \emph{its} size is itself conditioned on the encoder having pre-seen the decoder's test files at encoder training time. A single-fold experiment in which the encoder is retrained on a leave-the-decoder-test-files-out partition (the encoder never sees any sensor window from the decoder's test fold) would quantify this; it is on the artifact roadmap as a companion to the encoder-fine-tune cell of Section~\ref{sec:hp-sweep} and is not present in the released checkpoint. The headline should therefore be read as closer to a representation-probe than a fully partitioned generalization result; the LOCO collapse (Section~\ref{sec:loco}) is the unambiguous out-of-class generalization figure.

\paragraph{Quantified upper bound on the representation-reuse component.} The retraining experiment described above is not yet executed, but the class-prior control of Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound} (paragraph \emph{Is categorical recovery sensor-driven or operation-class structure?}) already pins a defensible upper bound on the leakage component without retraining. Consider the worst case: the encoder has memorized the operation-class label of every decoder test sample perfectly, and the decoder reads nothing from the sensor stream beyond that one class-label bit. The maximum command-prediction accuracy any predictor can achieve from a class-label-only input is exactly the accuracy of the operation-class modal predictor --- emit the modal command of the test sample's operation class --- because that predictor extracts \emph{all} the command information a class label can carry. The class-prior control reports this number directly: $0.572 \pm 0.004$ command accuracy at command-token positions of the full-window predictions (\texttt{audit/confound\_class\_prior.json}). The full decoder reaches $0.795 \pm 0.121$ at the same position subset. Therefore the $+0.222$ mean paired increment (a 4/5-fold effect, zero on the fold-1 covariate-shift outlier; per-fold scatter rather than a five-point $p$-value; Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}) is the sensor-conditioned command-discrimination signal that survives, on the four non-outlier folds, \emph{even under the worst-case assumption that the encoder has perfectly memorized the operation class of every test sample}: it is a within-class increment that class-label leakage cannot explain on those folds, because the class-label channel is fully saturated by the $0.572$ unconditional modal predictor by construction. Scored on the same all-command-position basis, a class-conditional modal (most-frequent command within each operation class) reaches only $0.576$ (\texttt{audit/class\_conditional\_modal\_5fold.json}) $\approx$ the $0.572$ unconditional modal, so conditioning the null on operation class does not raise it and the increment is robust to this stricter null rather than shrunk by it; it remains an upper bound because of the closed-vocabulary regime and frozen-encoder reuse (the first-command-position $0.811$ of Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound} is a different, easier estimand, not a like-for-like null). The representation-reuse caveat thus upper-bounds the leakage component of command accuracy at $0.572$, not at the headline $0.888$ or the position-conditioned $0.795$; the $+22$~pp increment is leakage-immune in the worst case.

The headline $0.888$ (Table~\ref{tab:headline}) is the same prediction stream aggregated at all-token positions rather than command-only positions and the bounding argument applies in proportion: under perfect-encoder-memorization, the maximum recoverable from class-label leakage alone at the all-token aggregation is upper-bounded by the corresponding all-token class-prior baseline (which is higher than $0.572$ because non-command positions carry less class-conditional command uncertainty by construction), and the within-class sensor-conditioned increment is the residual above that baseline. This is an upper bound on the leakage, not a measurement of it: the actual representation-reuse component will be smaller than $0.572$ because the encoder's $96.3\%$ nine-class accuracy is not perfect memorization and the decoder's command head must still recover within-class command structure on the $\approx\!97\%$ of windows that contain more than one distinct command. The leave-decoder-test-files-out encoder retraining remains the unambiguous measurement and is on the roadmap; in the interim, the $0.572$ upper bound and the $+0.222$ leakage-immune increment are the quantified statement of how much of the headline could and could not, respectively, be attributable to encoder representation reuse.

\paragraph{Empirical encoder-reuse bound from the LOMO baseline.} An independent empirical check is provided by the leave-one-modality-out (LOMO) baseline of Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}. Each LOMO baseline cell retrains the encoder from scratch on the train+val files of the corresponding fold; the test files are held out from encoder training by construction (verified per cell via the channel-identity gate described in Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}, which confirms that the train/validation/test file partition is invariant across cells and that only the sensor tensors differ). The resulting decoder command accuracy under that retrained-encoder regime is $0.904 \pm 0.039$ (5-fold mean $\pm$ s.d.; per-fold $0.839$, $0.932$, $0.929$, $0.899$, $0.922$) --- comparable to the headline $0.888 \pm 0.056$ within fold-to-fold variance. The empirical encoder-reuse effect on command accuracy is therefore bounded at $|\Delta|\!\le\!0.016$, with the leave-decoder-test-files-out (LOMO baseline) point in fact slightly higher than the headline rather than lower.

The slightly higher LOMO baseline ($0.904$ vs.\ $0.888$) is within fold-to-fold seed variance (the per-fold spread $\sigma\!\approx\!0.04$ is more than $2\times$ the $|\Delta|\!=\!0.016$ between the two estimates) and does not indicate a systematic under-tuning of the headline configuration; the two estimates are statistically indistinguishable with $n\!=\!5$. Combined with the analytic class-prior upper bound above, this dispatches the encoder representation-reuse concern: under the worst-case class-label-leakage argument the load-bearing $+22$~pp within-class increment is leakage-immune, and under the direct retrained-encoder empirical check the headline command accuracy itself moves by less than $0.02$.

\paragraph{Why the operation-class modal is the strictest legitimate sensor-independent null under file-level CV.} Because each test file is held out entirely from training under file-level CV, the test file's own modal command is unobservable from the training split alone; any predictor with access only to TRAIN data must, for an unseen test file, fall back on the modal estimate computed across all training files sharing the test file's operation-class assignment --- i.e.\ it reduces to the operation-class modal. A per-file-modal predictor that exceeds $0.572$ therefore requires access to test-file labels, which would not be a legitimate sensor-independent baseline. The $+22$~pp lift over the operation-class baseline (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}) is in this sense already measured against the strictest legitimate train-only null, and the per-file-modal control on the roadmap is best understood as an oracle-style upper bound on what per-file structure \emph{could} contribute if test labels were available, not as a tighter legitimate null.

\section{Grammar Tokenization Addendum}
\label{app:grammar-spec}

The token classes, vocabulary cardinality, per-axis NUM bucket counts, the worked example, and the FSM/bigram mask are specified in Section~\ref{sec:problem} and Section~\ref{sec:grammar}; the tokenization itself is defined in the companion grammar paper~\cite{grammar_paper}, which is in preparation at the time of submission. For self-containment, the per-axis quantization precision is $p_X\!=\!p_Y\!=\!p_Z\!=\!p_P\!=\!p_Q\!=\!10^{-3}$, $p_I\!=\!p_J\!=\!p_K\!=\!p_R\!=\!p_E\!=\!10^{-4}$, $p_F\!=\!1.0$ (mm/min), $p_S\!=\!10.0$ (rpm); F and S are excluded from the first-order PID structural grammar.

\paragraph{Bucket-index formula.} Given a numeric value $v\in\mathbb{R}$ on axis $a$ with precision $p_a$, the bucket index $b_a(v)$ used to construct the \texttt{NUM\_a\_b} token is
\begin{equation*}
b_a(v) = \mathrm{sgn}(v)\cdot\left\lfloor |v|/p_a \right\rfloor,
\end{equation*}
with the sign bit encoded by the separate sign head (Section~\ref{sec:multihead}), not by the bucket index. The numeric-tier results in this paper (per-axis MAE, the per-digit bathtub) are computed on the float-epsilon-corrected retrain, which uses the round-to-scaled-integer encoding $b = \mathrm{round}(|v|\cdot 10^{1/p_a})$; an earlier checkpoint used an iterative \texttt{int(dec\_part * 10) \% 10} digitization whose float-epsilon artifacts (and their correction) are characterized in the companion limit paper~\cite{paperB_entropy}. The headline categorical/token metrics drift $\le\!1.5$~pp between the two encodings (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}), so the correction is a numeric-tier refinement, not a headline change. Zero maps to $b\!=\!0$ on every axis. Per-axis bucket counts ($1{,}048$ NUM\_X, $1{,}086$ NUM\_Y, $231$ NUM\_Z, etc., summing to $2{,}418$) are determined empirically as the cardinality of $\{b_a(v):v\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}\}$ on the training corpus; any test-time $b$ that exceeds the trained range falls back to \texttt{UNK} (closed-vocabulary regime, Section~\ref{sec:cv}).

\section{Per-Modality Encoder Retraining (LOMO): Protocol and Validity}
\label{app:lomo-detail}

This section gives the full protocol and paired-comparison-validity detail for
the leave-one-modality-out (LOMO) encoder-retraining study summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo} (main body); the per-modality numbers are in
Table~\ref{tab:lomo_modality} and the paired-$\Delta$ chart in
Figure~\ref{fig:lomo_modality}.

\paragraph{Protocol.} For each of seven modality groups we retrain the
multi-modal denoising encoder from scratch on the $98$-feature sensor base
with that modality's channels removed, then train a fresh six-head decoder on
the resulting frozen encoder under the full-window target mode --- the same
two-stage pipeline (and exact decoder recipe) used for the headline model.
Results are reported at the full five-fold file-level cross-validation
protocol, with a full-stack baseline cell (no modality removed) retrained
under the identical protocol as the reference. The seven groups are
accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, color, temperature, audio-RMS, and
electrical. (\emph{Reconciliation with Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}}: the
inference-time-zeroing ablation grouped pressure, temperature, and proximity
into a single ``environmental'' modality; the LOMO study operates on the
$98$-feature base from which pressure and proximity are already excluded as
low-activity channels, so the environmental group reduces to temperature.)

\paragraph{Validity of the paired comparison.} A LOMO attribution is only
sound if every modality cell is scored on the \emph{same} held-out test set;
otherwise a modality effect is confounded with test-set difficulty. We found
and corrected a latent reproducibility defect that would have broken this: the
file-level cross-validation split's coverage-repair step iterated an unordered
set, making the validation/test partition dependent on process-level hash
ordering and therefore different on every preprocessing run. Imposing a
deterministic ordering makes the split byte-identical across all modality
exclusions. In addition, a per-cell channel-identity gate verifies, before any
GPU time is spent on a cell, that (i)~exactly the intended channels were
removed, (ii)~the retrained encoder and the decoder consume an identical
surviving-channel set in identical order, and (iii)~the train/validation/test
file partition and the G-code target multiset are invariant across modality
exclusions --- i.e.\ only the sensor tensors differ. Every cell reported here
passed all five checks of that gate.

\paragraph{The finding (full numbers).} Under LOMO retraining the full-stack baseline reaches
command accuracy $0.904 \pm 0.039$; removing a single modality and retraining
yields accelerometer $0.903 \pm 0.037$, gyroscope $0.905 \pm 0.030$,
magnetometer $0.896 \pm 0.033$, color $0.904 \pm 0.044$, temperature
$0.904 \pm 0.038$, audio-RMS $0.905 \pm 0.039$, and electrical
$0.891 \pm 0.051$ (five-fold mean $\pm$ s.d.). The fold-paired effect ---
the mean over folds of command$(m,f)-$command$(\text{baseline},f)$, which
removes the substantial fold-to-fold variance ($\sigma \approx 0.04$, an order
of magnitude larger than any modality effect) --- is within $\pm 0.013$ for
every modality, with electrical the largest in magnitude at
$-0.013 \pm 0.015$. The fold-blocked one-way ANOVA across the seven LOMO
conditions is non-significant ($F=0.91$, $p=0.499$), and the Holm-corrected
per-modality paired $t$-test against zero rejects none of the seven
($0/7$ significant at $\alpha=0.05$; smallest adjusted $p$ is $0.41$ for
magnetometer, Hedges' $g_{z}\!\approx\!-0.94$, a medium-large effect that
fails statistical significance at $n\!=\!5$ but is informative). Token and numeric heads behave the same way: all paired
effects within $\pm 0.011$ for token and $\pm 0.022$ for numeric, all
non-significant. Retraining the encoder without any single modality
produces no measurable change in any decoder head;
full per-fold $\Delta$, Holm-corrected $p$, and effect sizes are in
\texttt{audit/lomo\_modality\_attribution.json}.

\section{RQ3 Sensor-Modality Ablation: Interpretation and Encoder-Paper Reconciliation}
\label{app:sensor-detail}

This section holds the interpretation and encoder-paper-reconciliation detail
for the inference-time sensor-modality zeroing ablation summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}; the per-modality numbers are in
Table~\ref{tab:sensor_ablation} and Figure~\ref{fig:sensor_ablation}.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The encoder's learned modality-gating routes essential command-discrimination signal through every modality at once; when any one is zeroed at inference the gating mechanism degenerates to a representation that is informative enough for the coarse (type, param-type) heads but not for the finer command head. This means the inference-time zeroing test cannot rank the relative importance of modalities; it can only confirm that they are all jointly important. The genuine modality-importance ranking, and the answer to ``which sensors can a production deployment afford to omit'', requires an encoder retrained from scratch without each modality. That experiment, per-modality encoder retraining, is performed in Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}: the retrained encoder fully compensates for any single modality removal (paired $\Delta$ command $\le 0.013$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F=0.91$, $p=0.499$), so the frozen-encoder ``joint necessity'' reading is a gating artifact rather than a statement of modality information content.

\paragraph{Comparison with encoder-paper modality-importance.} The encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper} reported gyroscope, color, and electrical as the highest-utility modalities for operation classification under a per-modality SHAP-style attribution at the encoder input. The decoder-side ablation reported here does not reproduce that ranking because it operates on a frozen, fully-trained encoder rather than on the marginal information content of each modality; the two analyses answer different questions. The decoder ablation says ``every modality is jointly necessary at inference under the existing encoder''; the encoder paper says ``these modalities carried the most operation-class signal during encoder training.'' The non-significant $7$-group ANOVA ($p=0.998$) is the formal statement of this: across the modality-ablation sweep (six modalities at $n=5$, audio-RMS at $n=4$) the encoder-paper's ``most-important'' modalities (gyroscope, color) are not separable from the rest at the decoder-ablation level. The reconciliation is the retrained-encoder ablation: if encoder-retraining-without-modality-X still gives the decoder reasonable command accuracy, then modality X is deployment-deletable; otherwise modality X is genuinely load-bearing. That experiment, not inference-time zeroing, is the correct test of the deployment cost-reduction question; it is reported in Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}, where the retrained encoder fully compensates for any single modality removal ($\Delta$ command $\le 0.013$; $0/7$ significant under Holm correction).

\section{RQ4 Cross-Class Generalization (LOCO): Partition Construction and Per-Class F1}
\label{app:loco-detail}

This section holds the LOCO partition-construction detail and the per-class
command-F1 breakdown for the leave-one-class-out study summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:loco}; the per-holdout, per-head breakdown is in
Figure~\ref{fig:loco_per_holdout}.

\paragraph{Partition construction (operational detail).} For each LOCO holdout we (i)~remove every file of the held-out operation class from the file-level fold~1 train and validation partitions; (ii)~within the remaining $8$-class training partition we re-stratify per-class proportions across train/val at the same $80/20$ split used by the headline fold~1; (iii)~the test set for the LOCO holdout is the entire population of held-out-class files (not a fold~1 test subset). The coverage-repair step (Section~\ref{app:coverage-repair}) is \emph{not} re-run on the LOCO partition because its function is to construct a closed-vocabulary regime, which the LOCO holdout is deliberately not; residual test-only G-code lines are left in place and are the open-vocabulary signal the protocol is designed to expose. All hyperparameters --- including the headline SS$=$0.5/dw$=$1.0 cell --- are reused verbatim across the nine LOCO holdouts; no per-holdout re-tuning is performed. Each holdout is trained from scratch under this protocol; the nine resulting checkpoints are released alongside the headline checkpoints.

\paragraph{Per-class LOCO command F1.} The collapse spans every command class (\texttt{audit/loco\_per\_class\_9holdouts.json}): per-class F1 is G0 $0.084 \pm 0.136$, G1 $0.243 \pm 0.277$ (modal class; variance from the \texttt{damagepocket} outlier), G2 $0.044 \pm 0.107$, G3 $0.083 \pm 0.134$, G53/M30 $0.000$ ($n\le5$). This is the mode-collapse mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}) under an unseen motion vocabulary: the model defaults to the modal G1. The heads degrade asymmetrically: type and parameter-type survive LOCO ($-9$/$-5$~pp), so the encoder preserves gross instruction structure even for a novel class, while command and numeric require the in-distribution operation pattern.

\section{No-Numeric Retraining: Schedule-vs-Vocabulary Attribution and Decomposition Detail}
\label{app:nonumeric-detail}

This section holds the schedule-vs-vocabulary attribution caveat and the full
two-contributor decomposition for the no-numeric retraining ablation summarized
in Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric}; the headline comparison is in
Table~\ref{tab:nonumeric}.

\paragraph{Setup.} A naive test that re-scores the headline decoder's AR predictions with numeric positions masked is confounded: mispredicted values still desynchronize the surrounding structural tokens before the mask applies, leaving structural-only AR token accuracy at $0.317$ (only modestly above the unmasked $0.215$), a contaminated lower bound. The clean test removes the numerics from the prediction problem entirely: we retrain the decoder with every coordinate value collapsed to a single placeholder token (24-entry structural vocabulary in place of the $\sim$2{,}400-entry one), so a numeric error can never desynchronize the decode. The frozen encoder, 5-fold file-level split, and all other decoder hyperparameters are unchanged; the digit head is disabled and training uses teacher forcing throughout. Scheduled sampling destabilizes optimization once the numeric vocabulary collapses because the placeholder vocabulary offers no per-position lexical variety to re-anchor on, so it is disabled for this ablation, after which validation token accuracy converges stably at $\sim$$0.96$ across folds (last-25-epoch s.d.\ $<\!0.01$).

\paragraph{Schedule-vs-vocabulary attribution caveat (raised in review).} The $+8.8$~pp paired contrast against the SS$=$0.5/dw$=$1.0 headline is a joint condition difference (numeric vocabulary $+$ training schedule $+$ digit-head loss weight). The matched-methodology comparison is against the $K\!=\!2{,}418$ T3.1 cell (SS$=$0/dw$=$0, full numeric vocabulary; same schedule as the no-numeric cell): under that control the no-numeric run reaches AR struct token $0.405$ vs.\ T3.1's $0.470$, i.e.\ no measurable additional contribution from collapsing the numeric vocabulary beyond the schedule effect T3.1 already absorbs. The $+8.8$~pp number is therefore safely read as ``the schedule change alone closes the schedule-attributable piece of the AR collapse, and an additional numeric-collapse step does not improve on it,'' which strengthens the dominant-intrinsic-mode-collapse reading but downgrades the standalone ``numeric-desynchronization contributes $\sim\!9$~pp'' framing.

\paragraph{Two-contributor decomposition.} The mode-collapse mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} decomposes into two contributors: (a) \textbf{numeric-token desynchronization}, which the ablation eliminates and which contributes a measurable, paired-significant $\sim$$9$-pp per-token autoregressive effect; and (b) \textbf{intrinsic autoregressive mode-collapse of the structural token stream itself}, which the ablation does \emph{not} affect (structural sequence exact-match $0.066 \pm 0.014$ vs.\ $0.058 \pm 0.039$, $2/5$ folds positive) and which dominates the window-level recovery. Mechanism~(b) is consistent with the position-metadata mitigation of Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} (per-window anchoring of the structural decode) and the null beam-search result (Supplementary Section~S10.4): the structural collapse is intrinsic to autoregressive decoding of the long full-window target, not driven by the numeric vocabulary. This ablation complements the vocabulary-precision pilot (Supplementary Section~S8): neither coarsening the numeric vocabulary nor eliminating numeric prediction entirely materially lifts window-level recovery, jointly localizing the bottleneck off the numeric vocabulary and onto the autoregressive sequence mechanism on the structural stream.

\section{Seq2Seq Baseline: Extended Interpretation}
\label{app:seq2seq-detail}

This section holds the extended three-finding interpretation of the from-scratch
Transformer seq2seq baseline summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:abl-seq2seq-baseline}; the head-to-head numbers are in
Table~\ref{tab:seq2seq_baseline}.

\emph{(1) Teacher-forced command accuracy is architecture-invariant.} The baseline reaches $0.877 \pm 0.031$ on TF command accuracy, statistically indistinguishable from the headline $0.888 \pm 0.056$ (overlapping fold-level intervals; $\Delta = +0.011$). The categorical command signal in the sensor stream is recoverable by either architecture under teacher forcing; the headline TF-command number is not specific to the frozen-encoder + structured-head stack.

\emph{(2) The AR gap is a metric-construction artifact, not autoregressive-decoder superiority.} The baseline collapses to $0.122 \pm 0.051$ command accuracy under AR --- essentially the most-frequent-class prior --- because its command identity is read off the \emph{autoregressively generated token stream}, which suffers the exposure-bias mode collapse of Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}. The headline command head, by contrast, is a non-autoregressive per-position classifier on the frozen encoder memory (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}); it never consumes the token stream, so it is TF/AR-invariant by construction and holds at $0.888$. The resulting $+76.6$~pp command / $+77.9$~pp parameter-type AR gap is therefore the value of \emph{routing categorical prediction off the autoregressive token stream and onto the encoder memory} --- a representational/architectural design choice --- and is \emph{not} evidence that one autoregressive decoder is better than another. Compared like-for-like (both reading the AR token stream), the two architectures' AR token and AR numeric accuracies are within a fold of each other (token $0.154$ vs.\ $0.215$; numeric $0.100$ vs.\ $0.104$). The honest claim is that the categorical signal in this sensor stack is best harvested by a classifier head that bypasses autoregression, which the headline architecture does and the vanilla seq2seq baseline does not.

\emph{(3) Surprise on TF numeric.} The baseline's TF numeric accuracy ($0.754 \pm 0.053$) \emph{exceeds} the headline's six-head digit-decomposition output ($0.585 \pm 0.033$) by $+16.9$~pp. The bucketization that makes the grammar mask tractable in the legacy-token head (Section~\ref{sec:problem-vocab}) appears to forfeit some TF digit precision that an unbucketed seq2seq retains. We surface this as a finding for future architectural work, not as a contribution claim: the AR numeric column collapses both architectures to within a fold of each other ($0.100$ vs.\ $0.104$), so the TF-numeric reversal does not translate to a deployment advantage for the baseline.

\section{Threat-Model Detectability: Extended Operating-Point Discussion}
\label{app:tamper-detail}

This section holds the extended operating-point, false-alarm-cost, aggregation,
and adaptive-adversary discussion for the illustrative tamper operating points
summarized in Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}; the headline operating points
and the discrimination summary are in Table~\ref{tab:tamper_auc} (main body).
The operating points are illustrative, prior-dominated figures, not a validated
detector.

\paragraph{False-alarm cost.} The baseline command-swap operating point is TPR $0.92$ at FPR $0.30$ (with-metadata: TPR $0.95$ at FPR $0.22$); the sign-flip operating point is TPR $0.70$/$0.80$ at FPR $0.35$/$0.24$. At the measured median row duration of $0.25$~s (Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}, $n\!=\!21{,}541$), one CNC running an 8-hr shift commands $\sim$$1.15\!\times\!10^{5}$ row decisions. At the with-metadata in-distribution FPR $0.222$ this is $\sim$$2.5\!\times\!10^{4}$ alerts/shift/machine; at the open-vocabulary LOCO FPR $0.519$ it is $\sim$$6\!\times\!10^{4}$ --- both two to three orders of magnitude above the SOC tier-1 alert-budget guidance of $\le\!10^{2}$ per analyst per shift implicit in NIST SP~800-61~Rev.~2~\cite{nist2012sp80061} incident-handling capacity planning. A row-level verification monitor is therefore not viable as the SOC-consumer interface without an aggregation or triage stage. Three candidate mitigations are evaluated below; none yields a row-level operating point that survives the measured-autocorrelation, prior-driven, and open-vocabulary qualifiers established in the next paragraphs: \emph{(a) aggregation} over a sliding window of $K$ adjacent rows; \emph{(b) operating-point selection at a fixed low FPR (e.g., $0.01$)} on the ROC operating points (Supplementary Figure~S14), which buys a lower (but still not validated) false-alarm budget at a much lower TPR; \emph{(c) temperature-scaled calibration} (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) of the command head reduces the deployment-relevant cross-fold ECE from $0.050$ to $0.020$ (the fold-1-outlier $0.122 \to 0.029$ is not the deployment figure; Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) and tightens the high-confidence threshold band, which is the input the detector consumes. The operating points reported above are the threshold-matched first-pass numbers without these enhancements.

\paragraph{Aggregation (remedy a), in brief.} A Monte-Carlo $K$-row aggregation (any/all/majority-of-K, $K\!\in\!\{1,3,5,10,20,50\}$; \texttt{audit/tamper\_aggregation\_sweep.json}) shows the majority-of-$K$ rule produces an optimistic command-swap point that holds \emph{only} under the (false) row-independence assumption (with-metadata $K\!=\!10$: TPR $1.00$, FPR $\sim\!0.04$); sign-flip benefits weakly and feed-rate not at all. The next paragraph replaces this row-independent best-case figure with the empirically measured-$\rho$ number, which is the deployment-relevant operating point. The full $(K, \text{rule})$ curves and the ROC-space view are in Supplementary Section~S10.6 (Supplementary Figures~S13, S14).

\paragraph{Measured autocorrelation, a sensor-vs-prior control, and the open-vocabulary operating point.} The three results in this paragraph materially qualify the windowed-monitoring claim and we state them as the security-relevant numbers. \emph{(1)~Measured autocorrelation.} Rather than assume $\rho$, we measured the within-source-file lag-1 autocorrelation of the honest command-swap alert on the real 5-fold AR+FSM predictions (\texttt{scripts/analysis/tamper\_alert\_autocorrelation.py}; \texttt{audit/tamper\_alert\_autocorrelation.json}): $\rho_{\text{honest}} = 0.42 \pm 0.22$ (sample s.d.\ over the five folds), i.e.\ between the two assumed levels and closer to the pessimistic one. Re-running the correlated aggregation at the measured $\rho$ (\texttt{audit/tamper\_aggregation\_correlated.json}, \texttt{rho\_empirical}) gives, for command-swap at $K=10$ majority, with-metadata FPR $0.098$ at TPR $0.97$ and baseline FPR $0.184$ at TPR $0.94$. The deployable-operating-point claim therefore holds only in the weak sense ``$\sim\!0.10$ FPR with metadata at the measured autocorrelation,'' not the $0.04$ best case; the honest headline figure is FPR $\approx 0.10$--$0.18$ at TPR $\approx 0.94$--$0.97$. \emph{(2)~The alert is largely prior-driven.} Substituting the corpus-modal predicted string (a sensor-\emph{independent} prediction) for the decoder output and re-scoring the command-swap detector yields TPR $0.84 \pm 0.02$ at FPR $0.39 \pm 0.02$, versus the real decoder's TPR $0.92 \pm 0.04$ at FPR $0.30 \pm 0.07$. The sensor-conditioned decoder adds only $\sim\!8$~pp of TPR and $\sim\!9$~pp of FPR-reduction over a predictor that ignores the sensors entirely: most of the row-level command-swap detectability is a property of the closed-vocabulary command \emph{prior}, not of per-instruction sensor recovery (the concern raised about a frozen class-trained encoder, Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}). A natural stricter sensor-independent null is the \emph{class-conditional} modal string --- the per-operation-class modal command. On the matched all-command-position basis this null reaches only $0.576$ (5-fold; \texttt{audit/class\_conditional\_modal\_5fold.json}), essentially the $0.572$ unconditional operation-class modal of the class-prior control (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}): conditioning the null on operation class does \emph{not} raise it above the unconditional prior, because a single class-label feature carries almost no additional command information once the corpus modal is already emitted. The $\sim\!8$~pp sensor-conditioned tamper-detection lift over the corpus-modal control (real TPR $0.92$ vs.\ modal-substitution TPR $0.84$; \texttt{audit/tamper\_alert\_autocorrelation.json}) therefore stands essentially unchanged against the class-conditional null --- it is not absorbed by it.\footnote{A \emph{first-command-position-only} class-conditional modal reaches $\sim\!0.811$ (\texttt{audit/class\_conditional\_modal.json}; per-class modal frequencies \texttt{pocket}~G0 $1.00$, \texttt{damageface}~G1 $0.90$, \texttt{face}~G1 $0.89$, \texttt{pocket150025}~G1 $0.88$, \texttt{face150025}~G1 $0.87$), but that is a different, easier estimand (one position per window) than the all-command-position $0.576$/$0.572$ used here, and is not a like-for-like null for the all-position tamper lift; it is not subtracted from it.} The takeaway is unchanged in direction: the row-level tamper detectability is largely closed-vocabulary class-prior structure with only a modest ($\sim\!8$~pp) per-instruction sensor-conditioned component, exactly the prior-dominated, illustrative posture the main text adopts. \emph{(3)~Open-vocabulary collapse.} Re-running the full tamper-injection on the nine leave-one-class-out checkpoints under deployment-true AR (\texttt{scripts/analysis/threat\_model\_tamper\_injection.py --loco}; \texttt{audit/threat\_model\_tamper\_loco.json}) gives command-swap FPR $0.52$ at TPR $0.85$, sign-flip FPR $0.75$ at TPR $0.14$, and feed-edit TPR $0.00$. Against an adversary not confined to trained toolpaths the command-swap detector raises a false alarm on more than half of honest windows and the sign/feed detectors are worse than useless. The security-relevant operating point is therefore the open-vocabulary one (command-swap FPR $0.52$/TPR $0.85$), with the in-distribution $K$-aggregated figures as an optimistic in-envelope ceiling.

\paragraph{Adaptive adversary (a structural bypass, not a caveat).} The detector's blind spots are not incidental; they define a complete bypass for any adversary who knows the scheme. The recoverable fields are command identity, axis presence, and motion sign; the \emph{un}-recoverable fields are numeric coordinate value and feed rate (Section~\ref{sec:per-axis}; sign-flip and feed-edit detectors are operationally near-chance under the open-vocabulary regime, BA $\le\!0.5$ under LOCO). An adversary who keeps the command/axis/sign envelope fixed and corrupts only coordinates or feed rate is therefore \emph{invisible by construction}, and no $K$-row aggregation helps because the per-row signal on those fields is already at chance. This is exactly the high-value attack class the Introduction motivates (wrong depth of cut, wrong feed, a scrapped part or damaged tooling), so the residual security value against a non-naive adversary is small: the monitor meaningfully constrains only command/axis/sign substitutions that also stay within the trained toolpath envelope. We state this as a structural limitation, not a tunable parameter.

\section{Deployment Discussion: Sensor-Stack Redundancy and Usage Patterns (Extended)}
\label{app:deployment-detail}

This section holds the step-by-step sensor-stack-redundancy reasoning and the
three deployment-pattern descriptions summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:disc-deployment-stack}.

\paragraph{Sensor-stack redundancy reasoning (three steps).} \emph{First}, the inference-time modality ablation establishes joint necessity, not a deletability ranking: zeroing any single modality drops command accuracy by a uniform $40$--$44$~pp with a non-significant between-modality ANOVA (Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}), while type and parameter-type stay robust ($\le\!3$~pp). The encoder's learned gating routes command-discrimination signal through all modalities jointly; under the frozen encoder no single modality is individually omittable, so on that test alone the defensible claim is that the \emph{full} multi-modal stack is required for the categorical-recovery story reported here~\cite{kimmell2025cnc,nist2023sp80082,nist2024fmnet}. \emph{Second}, the encoder-retraining complement (Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}) is the deployment-relevant test: with the encoder retrained from scratch, no single sensing modality is individually load-bearing (paired $\Delta$ command $\le\!0.013$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F\!=\!0.91$, $p\!=\!0.499$); the $40$--$44$~pp frozen-encoder collapse is a gating artifact rather than a statement of modality information content. \emph{Third}, the orthogonal axis --- which physical \emph{sensor unit} a deployment could ship without --- is addressed by the companion leave-one-sensor-out study (Supplementary Section~S5): removing any single sensor unit and retraining costs $\le\!2.6$~pp on command (largest: \texttt{spindle2} $\Delta\!=\!-0.026$; smallest: \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} $\Delta\!\approx\!0$), with no removal reaching Holm-corrected significance ($p_{\text{Holm}}\!\ge\!0.064$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F\!=\!1.04$, $p\!=\!0.415$). Across both axes, any single sensing modality can be dropped at $\le\!1.3$~pp expected cost and any single physical sensor board at $\le\!2.6$~pp. The strongest single recommendation is to drop \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}, which is fully redundant with its spatial neighbor \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3} at zero observed effect ($p_{\text{Holm}}\!=\!0.975$); \texttt{spindle2} is the unit a deployment should be most cautious about omitting. The nested $6\!\times\!6$ $(\text{sensor},\text{modality})$ interior (Supplementary Section~S6) further shows that the deletability surface is \emph{flat at the finest cut we can resolve}: every interior cell is within $\pm 0.022$~pp of baseline (ANOVA $F\!=\!0.694$, $p\!=\!0.896$; $0/36$ Holm-significant), so the redundancy is dense rather than a marginal-average effect, and the marginal freedoms can be combined along the interior without incurring the marginal cost of dropping either axis in full.

\paragraph{When to use this decoder vs.\ alternative approaches.} The decoder is best suited to one of three deployment patterns. \emph{(1)~Forensic-support post-hoc audit (primary).} On suspect or contested runs, the decoded per-row G-code provides a controller-independent, physics-grounded record of executed instructions that complements the controller log; command-level disagreements (e.g.\ G0 commanded, G1 predicted; or X commanded, Y predicted) flag specific rows for offline analyst review. The reviewer is an offline analyst with the controller log in hand, not an alerting SOC tier-1 console, so the false-alarm budget that rules out real-time deployment (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}) does not apply in the same way: a $\sim$$22$~pp class-prior-controlled command increment over the modal predictor is informative when it can be reviewed against a contested run rather than streamed as an alert. Numeric-value precision is too low for forensic reconstruction of coordinates; command and axis-letter disagreements are the load-bearing forensic signal. \emph{(2)~Offline process verification (secondary).} The same per-row predictions, batched over a completed run, support offline reconciliation against the commanded program as an end-of-shift / end-of-job audit step; the in-distribution measured-$\rho$ $K\!=\!10$ majority aggregation point (with-metadata FPR $\sim 0.10$, TPR $\sim 0.97$; Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}) is usable as a human-in-the-loop triage aid that surfaces row blocks for a reviewer's attention --- never as an autonomous alert, and never under the open-vocabulary LOCO regime. \emph{(3)~Anomaly localization within the audit pipeline (tertiary).} Operations where command-level predictions deviate from the controller's commanded program identify specific G-code lines worth investigating, complementing session-level anomaly classifiers~\cite{coelho2024anomaly,kimmell2025cnc}; the decoder is explicitly \emph{not} an autonomous real-time alerting tool.

\section{Threat-Model Scope Boundaries and Per-Attack-Class Detection Capability}
\label{app:threat-scope}

This section holds the sensor-side-adversary enumeration, the upstream- and
out-of-distribution-adversary scope boundaries, and the full per-attack-class
detection-capability characterization summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}.

\paragraph{Sensor-side adversary (out of scope, enumerated).} A sensor-resident or sensor-bus adversary can defeat the monitor by attacking the sensor path before the decoder. We list the four classes a deployment must close operationally and which the present detector explicitly does \emph{not} address: \emph{(i) wire-tap / line-fault injection} on shared sensor cabling (mitigation: physically secured wiring, dedicated acquisition lines, twisted-pair / differential signaling, shielded conduit); \emph{(ii) EMI / electromagnetic injection} of falsified sensor signal into accelerometer/magnetometer/audio inputs while cutting is active~\cite{nist2023sp80082} (mitigation: EMI shielding, sensor-cable routing away from controller power lines, cross-modality consistency checks); \emph{(iii) packet replay or substitution} on the telemetry path between sensor boards and the verification host (mitigation: authenticated sensor streams with per-packet nonces or HMAC, integrity-protected acquisition pipeline; cf.\ IEC~62443-3-3 SR~3.1 Communication Integrity~\cite{iec62443-3-3}); \emph{(iv) sensor calibration drift / spoofing-via-stimulus} where a co-located adversary couples a calibrated stimulus into the machine frame that mimics the signature of an honest cut (mitigation: periodic offline sensor calibration audit, multi-board cross-checks; cf.\ NIST SP~800-82~Rev.~3 sensor-integrity monitoring~\cite{nist2023sp80082} and IEC~62443-4-2 CR~3.4 component-level integrity~\cite{iec62443-4-2}). None of these is benchmarked here. A deployment that does not enforce these operational mitigations should not invoke the ``physics-of-record'' framing for the sensor decoder's outputs.

\paragraph{Scope boundary for the upstream adversary.} The decoder's value against the upstream CAM/post-processor adversary is fundamentally limited by the closed-vocabulary regime characterized in Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}. If the substituted toolpath stays within the in-vocabulary G-code-line distribution the model was trained on, the decoder's prediction will agree with the (already-malicious) controller stream and the tamper is invisible to it; the decoder catches an upstream substitution only when the malicious toolpath drives the machine into a physically distinguishable, in-distribution-recognizable regime. This is a material blind spot and we state it plainly: the present system is a controller-side-substitution and execution-integrity monitor, not an upstream-design-integrity monitor. Detecting upstream CAM compromise requires either a trusted design-to-file provenance chain (orthogonal to this work) or open-vocabulary recovery strong enough to flag an unfamiliar-but-valid toolpath --- which the LOCO results (Section~\ref{sec:loco}) show the present model does not have.

\paragraph{Out-of-distribution attacks (out of scope; in-distribution envelope binding).} Two out-of-distribution attack families fall outside the in-distribution forensic-support audit envelope and the detector makes no guarantee on either. \emph{(i) Novel-command-token attacks:} commands not present in the V8 vocabulary the decoder was trained on (e.g., M-codes, G-codes, or address letters outside $\mathcal{V}$) are either masked out by the FSM grammar layer (Section~\ref{sec:grammar}) or fall through to the modal class; either way the per-instruction sensor evidence is not consulted against an unrecognized token and the detector has no calibrated false-alarm rate on such inputs. \emph{(ii) Novel-operation-class attacks:} toolpaths from operation classes not represented in the training corpus (i.e., the LOCO regime above) collapse the command-swap detector to the operating-point reality of Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model} (LOCO command-swap FPR $0.52$ at TPR $0.85$; sign-flip and feed-edit at or below chance), and the recommended deployment posture reduces to forensic post-hoc inspection of categorical disagreements alone. The in-distribution forensic-support envelope is therefore explicitly closed-world over the V8 vocabulary \emph{and} the nine operation classes catalogued in Section~\ref{sec:dataset}; open-world generalization to novel commands or novel operation classes is deferred to future cross-platform replication (Section~\ref{sec:future}) and is not a current capability.

\paragraph{Detection capability by attack class.} Based on the per-field recoverability characterization:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Command-identity substitution} (G0 $\to$ G1, G2 $\to$ G3): \emph{detectable.} Command-accuracy at $0.8875 \pm 0.0558$ indicates the decoder reliably recovers command identity from the sensor pathway. A substituted command produces a controller-log vs.\ decoder disagreement at the row level.
\item \textbf{Axis-identity substitution} (X $\to$ Y): \emph{detectable.} Parameter-type accuracy at $0.9931 \pm 0.0036$ and decoder per-axis presence in the $0.96$--$1.00$ range (Table~\ref{tab:per_axis}) support detection of axis swaps.
\item \textbf{Motion-sign substitution} (X+ $\to$ X-): \emph{partially detectable, with a prior-vs-sensor asymmetry that matters.} Per-axis sign accuracies (Table~\ref{tab:per_axis}, 5-fold mean) are Y $0.997$, X $0.942$, Z $0.938$; the corresponding per-axis sign source entropies are $H_{Y}^{\text{sign}} = 0.08$~bits ($99\%$ positive --- prior-deterministic), $H_{X}^{\text{sign}} = 0.38$~bits ($93\%$ positive), $H_{Z}^{\text{sign}} = 1.04$~bits ($44\%$ positive / $56\%$ negative --- a genuine binary classification; companion paper~\cite{paperB_entropy}). Y-sign ``detection'' is therefore essentially prior mismatch rather than sensor evidence and would fail on a workpiece of different orientation or origin; only Z-sign detection carries non-trivial sensor signal.
\item \textbf{Numeric-value tampering} (X1.5 $\to$ X1.6): \emph{not detectable at fine precision by an information-theoretic bound, not a tuning failure.} On the float-epsilon-corrected retrain the per-axis source entropy at the \emph{thousandths} ($10^{-3}$~inch, the finest digit the CAM postprocessor emits) on adaptive HSM toolpaths is $\approx 3.3$~bits, indistinguishable from uniform random over $\{0,\ldots,9\}$ (companion paper~\cite{paperB_entropy}); no decoder consuming this sensor stack can recover that thousandths digit substantially above the bucket prior (slot-4 accuracy $0.42$). The ten-thousandths ($10^{-4}$) digit is near-uniform in the four-decimal CAM source but is discarded by the V8 tokenizer's $0.001$ grid, so its tokenized target is a structural zero reproduced trivially, not recovered. Coarse-resolution tampering on the integer or tenths place \emph{may} be detectable in-distribution if the perturbed value falls outside the per-operation-class value range observed in training; fine-precision substitutions within the within-window value range will produce decoder predictions consistent with the substituted value.
\item \textbf{Feed-rate / spindle-speed tampering}: \emph{not evaluable on the present corpus.} Evaluating these fields would require a corpus that systematically varies feed rate and spindle speed, which the present data does not.
\item \textbf{Replay}: \emph{detectable indirectly (argued, not benchmarked).} If the replayed program produces a sensor signature inconsistent with the new operating conditions (different material, different tool wear state), the decoder will produce an instruction stream consistent with the actual physics, which will disagree with the replayed controller log. We state this as a mechanism, not a measured result.
\item \textbf{Skip/duplicate at per-row granularity}: \emph{not directly addressed by the current decoder.} Per-row prediction here assumes one row per query; detecting omitted rows requires temporal alignment between sensor windows and controller log, which is operationally feasible but is not benchmarked here.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodological Limitations: Spectral Gap and Sensor-Stack Upgrade Path}
\label{app:method-limit-detail}

This section holds the per-modality PSD/SNR stated-gap discussion and the
sensor-stack upgrade-path detail relocated from
Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}.

\paragraph{Per-modality PSD/SNR characterization (stated gap).} We have not characterized the per-modality power-spectral-density (PSD) or signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the 4~Hz envelope-extracted stream against a co-recorded raw (pre-envelope) stream, and we state this as an explicit gap rather than a result. The envelope front end retains the low-frequency cutting-regime power that drives the categorical recoverability reported here but discards the spectral content above the $2$~Hz envelope Nyquist; without a matched raw/envelope PSD comparison we cannot attribute the numeric-tier ceiling between (a) genuine spectral information loss in the envelope extraction and (b) the sub-sample temporal aliasing of $77.7\%$ of rows. A per-modality PSD/SNR audit on a co-recorded raw stream --- quantifying, per modality group, how much band-limited cutting-signal power survives the envelope --- is required to separate these two contributions and is deferred to the higher-rate data-collection campaign (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}, item (a)). The present recoverability characterization should be read with this gap in view: it bounds \emph{what the 4~Hz envelope stack recovers}, not the maximum recoverable from the underlying physical signal.

\paragraph{Sensor-stack upgrade path.} The information-theoretic numeric-precision ceiling is a property of the present 4~Hz envelope-extracted sensor stack, not of the decoder architecture. A next-iteration deployment platform could close the gap with targeted sensor additions, each tied to a specific information-theoretic bottleneck: (a) a high-sample-rate contact microphone ($\geq 44.1$~kHz) would capture the tool-engagement transient and resolve the 77.7\% of G-code blocks that occupy less than one envelope sample period; (b) optical or magnetic axis encoders ($\geq 1$~kHz position readout) would supply direct coordinate observation, bypassing the autoregressive numeric collapse entirely; (c) a higher-rate tri-axial IMU ($\geq 1$~kHz) would sharpen motion-sign discrimination on the X and Z axes; and (d) higher-rate Hall-effect spindle current sensors ($\geq 1$~kHz) would track cut-depth changes directly. These upgrades would not alter the categorical-recovery story (which is already at $\sim\!0.89$ command, near its closed-vocabulary ceiling) but would substantially improve the numeric tier and likely close the autoregressive AR-mode gap as well.

\section{Grammar FSM: Full Specification and Ablation}
\label{app:fsm-detail}

This section holds the finite-state-machine (FSM) state specification and the
full FSM-vs-bigram ablation summarized in Section~\ref{sec:grammar}.

\paragraph{FSM specification.} The FSM state set is $\mathcal{S}=\{s_0, s_{\text{G0}}, s_{\text{G1}}, s_{\text{G2}}, s_{\text{G3}}, s_{\text{M30}}, s_{\text{other}}\}$, with initial state $s_0$ at \textsc{bos}. Transitions on emitting a move-command token $c\in\{\text{G0},\text{G1},\text{G2},\text{G3},\text{M30}\}$ set the state to $s_c$; emitting any other command (G53, G55, G17, G90, G94, \ldots) sets the state to $s_{\text{other}}$; PARAM letters, NUM tokens, and structural tokens (BOS, EOS, SPECIAL) leave the state unchanged. The forbidden-PARAM set per state is: $s_{\text{G0}}\!\to\!\{R, I, J\}$; $s_{\text{G1}}\!\to\!\{R, I, J\}$; $s_{\text{G2}}\!\to\!\emptyset$ (arcs may legally take any of $R, I, J$); $s_{\text{G3}}\!\to\!\emptyset$; $s_{\text{M30}}\!\to\!\{\text{all commands and all PARAM letters}\}$; $s_0$ and $s_{\text{other}}$ apply the bigram mask only (no additional restriction). \texttt{FSM\_mask}$(s)$ in Algorithm~\ref{alg:forward} returns the indicator vector of allowed next tokens in state $s$, multiplied element-wise into the legacy-token logits before the softmax. The residual $11$ V1-violation cases reported in the FSM ablation (G53/G55/G17/G90/G94 followed by R) correspond to the $s_{\text{other}}$ state and are a deliberate scope choice: extending the FSM to encode the full RS274/NGC modal-group state machine is left as grammar-refinement work.

\paragraph{FSM ablation.} We audited grammar compliance and accuracy with and without the FSM layer, on both the baseline and the with-shortcuts 5-fold checkpoints under greedy autoregressive decoding. The bigram-only mask permits G0/G1 to emit R/I/J: across approximately 167{,}400 generated tokens per configuration, it produces 329 V1 violations affecting $170 / 549$ baseline samples ($31\%$) and 333 violations affecting $139 / 549$ with-shortcuts samples ($25\%$). With the FSM layer active, V1 violations drop to $0$ on baseline ($100\%$ reduction) and $11$ on with-shortcuts ($97\%$ reduction; the residual $11$ are G53/G55/G17/G90/G94 followed by R, a class the present FSM does not encode and a candidate for grammar refinement). The 5-fold accuracy cost is $-0.27$~pp / $-0.41$~pp on baseline token / numeric, and $-0.04$~pp / $+0.01$~pp on with-shortcuts --- in both configurations the difference is within fold-to-fold noise (baseline std $\pm 0.069$ / $\pm 0.032$; with-shortcuts std $\pm 0.016$ / $\pm 0.017$). The FSM therefore eliminates the dominant class of grammar violation at no measurable accuracy cost. A paired-sample comparison across the 5 folds confirms statistical non-significance: paired-$t$ tests on baseline token / numeric give $p=0.72 / 0.51$ and Wilcoxon signed-rank gives $p=1.00 / 0.44$; on with-shortcuts token / numeric the same tests give $p=0.83 / 0.95$ and $p=0.63 / 1.00$ (\texttt{audit/fsm\_vs\_bigram\_paired\_stats.json}). M30-followup violations (V2) were $0$ in all configurations because M30 is rare in the corpus and the model learns termination from training. We adopt the FSM as the default deployment-time decoding configuration on this basis.

\section{Dataset: Sensor Mounting, Calibration, and Envelope-Extraction Pipeline}
\label{app:dataset-detail}

This section holds the sensor-mounting/calibration detail and the
envelope-extraction firmware-pipeline description relocated from
Section~\ref{sec:dataset}.

\paragraph{Sensor mounting and calibration.} The six multi-sensor boards are bolted to the machine frame and Y-bed in the locations encoded in their names: \texttt{frame\_l2} and \texttt{frame\_l3} on the left machine-frame upright, \texttt{frame\_r2} on the right frame upright, \texttt{spindle2} adjacent to the spindle housing, and \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}/\texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} on the moving Y-bed (the double-underscore form matches the column-prefix convention in the released aligned CSVs). The frame boards measure the global vibration envelope; the spindle board is the highest-bandwidth cutting-event sensor; the Y-bed boards co-move with the workpiece and pick up axis-coupled signal. Per-board mounting is rigid bolted (no adhesive or magnetic mounts) for stable inter-run calibration. Each board carries an identical $15$-channel inventory (3-axis accelerometer, 3-axis gyroscope, 3-axis magnetometer, 4-channel RGBA color, 1-channel ambient temperature, 1-channel audio RMS), and an additional 8 controller-side electrical channels (machine-controller motor-current and bus signals) are recorded separately and are not removable as a board. All MEMS and audio channels are read at their on-board factory-rated nominal sensitivities and downsampled / envelope-extracted to the released 4~Hz rate prior to alignment with the G-code stream; no re-calibration was performed mid-corpus. Chip-level part numbers, datasheet bandwidth, noise floor, and the per-channel mapping for the eight electrical channels (axis-stepper current shunts and spindle-bus signals) are reproduced in the encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper} and apply unchanged to this study; the LOMO/LOSO findings (Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}, Supplementary Section~S5) should be read against that per-board / per-modality inventory.

\paragraph{Spectral context and envelope-extraction pipeline for 4~Hz sampling.} The released stream is an envelope-extracted 4~Hz aggregate of higher-rate on-board acoustic and MEMS signals; the choice trades the kHz-band content used by classical acoustic-emission tool-condition monitoring~\cite{teti2010advanced} for an ingestion-friendly, deployment-cheap envelope (Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}, $\sim 1.5$~kB/s ingestion). The on-board firmware pipeline is: (i)~each sensor channel is sampled at its on-board factory-rated rate (audio at $1$~kHz; MEMS accelerometer/gyroscope/magnetometer at $200$~Hz; environmental temperature, RGBA color, and motor-current at $\geq\!100$~Hz); (ii)~per-channel rectify-and-low-pass via a single-pole IIR with cutoff $f_c\!=\!2$~Hz (half the 4~Hz output rate, the Nyquist limit) computes the windowed envelope; (iii)~the envelope is decimated to the 4~Hz output tick by taking the last in-window value, with the 4~Hz tick clock-aligned to the firmware acquisition timestamp shared by all six boards; (iv)~the 4~Hz aggregate is written to the per-file CSV that the released corpus distributes. The firmware/preprocessing script that implements (i)--(iii) is released alongside the corpus (cited path under \texttt{src/firmware/envelope\_extract/}) so a reproducer with only the higher-rate raw stream can regenerate the released 4~Hz aligned CSVs. A per-modality PSD / SNR characterization of what the 4~Hz envelope preserves vs.\ the raw on-board acoustic stream is a known gap and is left to a sensor-engineering companion; the present paper's recoverability findings are conditional on the released 4~Hz aggregate and are framed throughout (Sections~\ref{sec:related-sidechan}, \ref{sec:lim-method}) as deliberately at the low-rate, low-bandwidth end of the sensing-cost spectrum rather than as a kHz-rate AM-side-channel reconstruction claim.

\section{What Transfers and What Does Not (Cross-Platform Transfer Matrix)}
\label{app:transfer-matrix}

This section holds the platform-agnostic / platform-specific transfer
classification relocated from Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}.

\paragraph{What transfers and what does not.} The findings of this paper split cleanly into platform-agnostic and platform-specific categories. Platform-agnostic (mechanism / theory, expected to hold under any low-rate multi-modal sensor stack and Transformer decoder): the autoregressive mode collapse mechanism documented in Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}; the structured-versus-numeric recoverability factorization (information-theoretic, capped by per-(axis,slot) source entropy regardless of platform); the class-prior decomposition framework for distinguishing sensor-conditioned increment from operation-class structure; and the seq2seq-baseline architectural-contribution-scoping (any reasonable encoder-decoder Transformer on low-rate sensor data should match at teacher-forced inference and collapse at autoregressive inference). Platform-specific (quantitative, expected to transfer with adjustment): the headline numbers ($0.888$ command, $0.21$ AR token, $0.10$ AR numeric); the specific 6-sensor 7-modality channel inventory; the machinable-wax sound and vibration signature; the 4~Hz envelope-extraction sample rate; the 9-class operation taxonomy. A cross-platform replication study should report which platform-specific numbers transferred under a different mill, drive system, material, and sample-rate budget; the mechanism-level findings should hold qualitatively. \emph{We flag one transfer claim as mechanism-uncertain:} the categorical-recovery result is annotated platform-agnostic only at the level of the factorization (categorical heads are recoverable while generative reconstruction is not), \emph{not} at the level of the recovery mechanism. On the present stepper-driven, dry-cutting, machinable-wax platform the command-discrimination signal is carried jointly across all modalities through the encoder's learned gating (Section~\ref{sec:disc-modality}); whether the same modalities carry it on a servo-driven, flood-cooled, ferrous-workpiece VMC --- where motor-current is closed-loop servo torque and the acoustic floor is coolant-dominated --- is unverified, and the categorical-recovery row of the cross-platform transfer matrix should be read as mechanism-uncertain until a co-recorded per-modality PSD/SNR comparison is performed on the production platform.

\section{Hyperparameter-Sweep, Inferential-Scope, and Noise-Augmentation Detail}
\label{app:sweep-stats-noiseaug}

This section holds the Stage-3 encoder-fine-tune pilot, the sweep-fold caveat,
the $n=5$ inferential-scope caution, and the noise-augmentation negative result,
relocated from Sections~\ref{sec:hp-sweep}, \ref{sec:stats}, and~\ref{sec:noise-aug}.

\paragraph{Stage-3 encoder fine-tuning cell (fold-1, raised in review).} Unfreezing the final encoder LSTM layer and co-training it with the decoder for the same epoch budget yielded fold-1 validation command accuracy $\sim\!0.78$ (vs.\ the fold-1 frozen-encoder baseline of $\sim\!0.78$) and TF token accuracy within $\pm 0.5$~pp of frozen-encoder baseline, with no statistically meaningful lift on any head; the cell was not promoted because the lift was within fold-1 noise and the fine-tuning cell adds $\sim 2\times$ wall-clock cost per fold. We report this single-fold pilot so that the encoder-bottleneck claim in Section~\ref{sec:encoder-probe} is grounded in a measured (if underpowered) experiment rather than a structural inference. A full 5-fold encoder fine-tune with row-level supervision is the principal open direction (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}); the present pilot is consistent with --- but does not by itself establish --- the ``encoder is the bottleneck'' reading.

\paragraph{Sweep-fold caveat (raised in review).} The sweep was conducted entirely on fold~1, which the manuscript's own covariate-shift audit (Section~\ref{sec:lim-covariate}) and calibration analysis (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}, where fold~1 fits the highest command-head temperature $T\!=\!1.77$ across the five folds) flag as the most atypical of the five folds (the $-4.4$~pp Z-coverage shift). Selecting the configuration on the outlier fold is a model-selection concern we acknowledge: the headline 5-fold numbers are still computed on all five folds, but the configuration's \emph{rank} among Stage-2/3 alternatives was decided on fold-1 validation only. A cross-fold rank-stability sanity check on the top-3 Stage-3 cells (a partial 5-cell, 2-fold replication) is on the artifact roadmap and not yet executed; until it is, the configuration choice should be read as fold-1-validation-best rather than cross-fold-rank-stable.

\paragraph{Inferential scope and a caution on $n=5$.} The unit of analysis is the per-fold mean, so every parametric test reported is computed on $n=5$ observations per group (the audio-RMS modality-ablation group is $n=4$, one fold unrecoverable). We therefore treat all $p$-values as \emph{descriptive} rather than confirmatory: with $n=5$ the ANOVA/Welch statistics are highly sensitive to the normality and homoscedasticity assumptions, which we do not have the sample size to verify, and reported $p$-values should be read to one or two significant figures and in conjunction with the effect size, not as precise probabilities. The bootstrap 95\% intervals are percentile intervals over five fold-means; they are small-sample and \emph{not} coverage-calibrated, and we present them as a smoothed indication of fold-to-fold spread rather than as exact confidence statements. Where a nonparametric test is reported (Mann--Whitney $U$, Wilcoxon signed-rank), it is the primary inferential statement and the parametric test is corroborating. Our conclusions rest on effect sizes that are large relative to the cross-fold spread, not on threshold-crossing $p$-values; single-fold pilot results are labeled as such and are explicitly excluded from every quantitative or deployment claim.

\paragraph{Noise augmentation (negative result).} Gaussian sensor noise ($\sigma=0.02$) plus feature-dropout and token-masking during training is a clean \emph{negative} result (5-fold, per-row, teacher-forced): token $0.732 \pm 0.011$ and numeric $0.448 \pm 0.010$ are preserved but command drops to $0.331 \pm 0.076$, a $\sim$56~pp shortfall against the full-window headline baseline ($0.888$; parameter-type $0.978$ holds). The regularization pushes the command head toward the modal class (G1) by making the marginal command signal less reliable to the decoder. Not recommended here; it may still help under a wider per-class corpus or a loss-weighting that decouples token- and command-level regularization (future work).
