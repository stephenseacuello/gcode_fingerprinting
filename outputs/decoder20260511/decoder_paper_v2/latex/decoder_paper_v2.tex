\Title{Structured Multi-Head Decoding for Per-Field Computer Numerical Control G-Code Recoverability from Multi-Modal Sensor Embeddings: A Cross-Validated Characterization Study}

\newcommand{\orcidauthorA}{0009-0009-0292-9706}

\Author{Stephen S. Eacuello $^{1,}$*\orcidA{}, Romesh S. Prasad $^{1}$ and Manbir S. Sodhi $^{1}$}

\AuthorNames{Stephen S. Eacuello, Romesh S. Prasad and Manbir S. Sodhi}

\address{%
$^{1}$ \quad Department of Industrial Systems Engineering, University of Rhode Island, Kingston, RI 02881, USA}

\corres{Correspondence: seacuello@uri.edu}

\abstract{
\emph{Scope:} this paper characterizes which fields of a G-code line are sensor-recoverable from a multi-modal CNC monitoring stack, under a closed-vocabulary in-distribution evaluation regime ($97.6\%$/$99.0\%$ verbatim test-line overlap; Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}); the open-vocabulary complement is a leave-one-class-out (LOCO) collapse to command accuracy $0.213 \pm 0.191$ (Section~\ref{sec:loco}). The model (\modelname{}) is a multi-head Transformer decoder on a frozen multi-modal encoder, with per-row targets and grammar-constrained inference. We do not claim a working end-to-end recovery system. \emph{Headline:} recoverability factorizes along a categorical-vs-numeric axis. Categorical structure --- command, axis presence, motion direction --- is recovered from sensors alone at $0.888 \pm 0.056$, $0.993 \pm 0.004$, $0.989 \pm 0.006$, identically under teacher-forced (TF) and autoregressive (AR) decoding. A class-prior control attributes $\sim\!0.57$ of the $0.79$ command-position accuracy to the operation-class prior the frozen encoder already encodes; the $\sim\!22$~pp within-class sensor-driven increment, not the $0.888$ headline, is the robust core. Numeric precision is bounded by two coupled mechanisms: exposure bias collapses TF token / numeric ($0.781 / 0.585$) under AR ($0.215 / 0.104$; AR sequence exact-match $0.026$); and an information-theoretic floor identified by per-(axis, slot) source-entropy analysis (Pearson $r\!=\!-0.96$ over the six digit slots) shows the $10^{-4}$-inch digit reaches the uniform-noise ceiling on adaptive high-speed-machining (HSM) toolpaths. A no-numeric retraining ablation decomposes the AR collapse into a $\sim\!9$-pp numeric-desynchronization piece and a dominant intrinsic structural mode-collapse ($5/5$ folds, $p\!<\!0.02$); a five-point vocabulary-cardinality spectrum with a methodology-matched $K\!=\!2{,}418$ control (T3.1, SS$=$0/dw$=$0) identifies the matched control as the highest-performing variant (AR struct token $0.470$; AR struct sequence $+7.1$~pp paired vs.\ headline, Holm-adjusted $p\!=\!0.006$). The deployment-recommended configuration is therefore the existing $K\!=\!2{,}418$ vocabulary trained under SS$=$0/dw$=$0. \emph{Tamper-detection scope:} the row-level command-swap detector is largely operation-class-prior-driven ($\approx\!8$~pp sensor-conditioned over a sensor-independent modal predictor) and collapses open-vocabulary (LOCO FPR $0.52$, TPR $0.85$); sign-flip and feed-rate substitutions are at or below chance. The favorable in-distribution operating point ($K\!=\!10$ majority-vote at measured $\rho\!=\!0.42$, TPR $\sim\!0.95$ at FPR $0.10$--$0.18$) is an in-envelope ceiling, not a deployment guarantee. Numeric/feed-rate substitutions are undetectable on the present 4-Hz stack ($77.7\%$ of rows occupy $\le\!1$ sample period), so an adversary corrupting only those fields is invisible by construction. The contribution is a characterization of what physics-of-record sensing can and cannot recover.
}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A modern Computer Numerical Control (CNC) milling machine is a tightly choreographed system: motors drive a cutting tool along multi-axis toolpaths specified by a G-code program, with tolerances measured in micrometers. Every cut leaves a multi-modal sensor signature --- vibration through the machine frame, acoustic emission, motor-current draw, ambient temperature and illumination --- that is in principle informative about the instruction stream that produced it. Whether that signature is informative enough to \emph{recover} the instruction stream line by line, without access to the controller, is the question this paper takes up. The applications are direct: continuous process verification, post-hoc forensic audit of contested runs, and detection of malicious or accidental G-code substitution in increasingly networked manufacturing environments~\cite{nist2023sp80082,nist2024fmnet,sturm2014cyber,yampolskiy2018security,zhong2017intelligent,kimmell2025cnc}, where the cost of an undetected deviation in feed rate, depth of cut, or motion command can be a scrapped part, damaged tooling, or worse~\cite{teti2010advanced,serin2020tool}.

Multi-modal sensor monitoring of CNC machines is a mature subfield, but its targets have been broad. Tool-wear estimation~\cite{teti2010advanced,serin2020tool,zhu2009wavelet,mohanraj2020tool,wang2024multisensor} and operation-type classification~\cite{stathatos2024cnn,coelho2024anomaly,wang2018deep,zhao2019machine} answer session-level questions: \emph{is the tool worn?}, \emph{what kind of operation is running?} Their target labels are coarse --- a single value per recording, or a class per file. The finer question --- can the specific G-code instruction executing at this moment be identified from sensors alone? --- has been largely absent from the CNC literature, despite its direct implications for tamper detection, intellectual-property protection~\cite{alfaruque2016acoustic,song2016my,chhetri2016kcad,jamarani2024screg,chattopadhyay2025video}, and root-cause analysis on contested runs.

\paragraph{Why CNC milling is harder than the additive-manufacturing analogue.} The closest body of work is side-channel reconstruction of toolpaths in additive manufacturing~\cite{alfaruque2016acoustic,song2016my,chhetri2016kcad}, where Fused Deposition Modeling deposits material one axis at a time in a controlled environment, sensors can be placed freely without risk of damage, and the sensor-to-G-code mapping is comparatively low-noise. CNC milling is a substantially harder signal environment. Simultaneous multi-axis interpolation, nonlinear force-engagement relationships, structural vibration through the machine frame, and high-energy chip ejection corrupt sensor signals and physically constrain where sensors can be mounted~\cite{teti2010advanced,wang2018deep}. The mapping is also many-to-one: many G-code instructions produce broadly similar sensor signatures when their cutting parameters coincide, and the same instruction can be cut at different speeds, depths, or materials.

\paragraph{Building on prior work in our group.} The sensor encoder used in this study is a prior contribution that achieved 96.3\% accuracy on nine-class CNC operation classification using a multi-modal denoising autoencoder over six distributed sensor boards~\cite{encoder_paper}. That work established the encoder's representational competence at \emph{operation-class} granularity. A companion contribution defined a hybrid grammar-based tokenization of G-code suitable for sequence models~\cite{grammar_paper}. The present paper takes the encoder as a fixed feature extractor and the grammar as a vocabulary, then asks what additional information the encoder's per-window latent carries about the row-level instructions that drove the cut.

\paragraph{Three challenges specific to this task.} Direct G-code generation from sensor signals raises issues that off-the-shelf sequence-to-sequence models~\cite{sutskever2014sequence,bahdanau2014neural,vaswani2017attention,graves2013speech} do not address: (i)~a 64-second window straddles tens to hundreds of G-code rows, so a naive window-level target destroys row-level information; (ii)~the numeric vocabulary contains thousands of bucket tokens, most absent from any one training fold; and (iii)~positional metadata (window index, file identifier) is an obvious shortcut that inflates accuracy without proving sensor-driven recovery~\cite{meyes2019ablation,jia2016deep}. The contributions below address each.

\textbf{Research questions.} This study is guided by four research questions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Per-field recoverability.} Which components of a G-code line --- command identity, axis presence, motion direction, numeric value --- are recoverable from sensor data alone, and with what fidelity?
\item \textbf{The metadata-versus-sensor split.} Does exposing positional metadata (window index, file identifier) to the decoder improve recovery, and which fields benefit if so?
\item \textbf{Sensor-modality contribution.} Which of seven sensor groups (accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, environmental, color, audio-RMS, electrical) carry the strongest instruction-level signal?
\item \textbf{Cross-class generalization.} Does the decoder generalize to machining operation classes it never saw during training (leave-one-class-out)?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\arabic*)}, leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Per-row supervised target formulation.} Each sensor window is mapped to the single G-code line that fires within it, yielding approximately $\sim$19{,}500 training samples per fold versus $\sim$300 for a window-level alternative, while eliminating within-window label ambiguity.

\item \textbf{\modelname{} architecture.} A Transformer decoder with six structured prediction heads (token, type, motion-command, parameter-type, sign, digit) operating on the frozen latent of a prior multi-modal denoising encoder~\cite{encoder_paper}. Inference is grammar-constrained at the token head so that structurally invalid transitions are masked at runtime, following recent work on grammar-aware decoding~\cite{picard,outlines,xgrammar}.

\item \textbf{Per-axis recoverability characterization.} An evaluation that decomposes G-code into per-axis fields and reports separately for each field whether (a) the axis is detected, (b) the motion direction is correct, and (c) the numeric value is recovered. The corpus supports this analysis on the X, Y, Z, and R fields; F is reported only at thin support, and S, I, J have effectively zero positive support and are not evaluable (Table~\ref{tab:data_coverage}). This separation reveals which fields are sensor-recoverable and which are not.

\item \textbf{Ablation matrix.} Cross-validated ablations of (a) positional-metadata access, (b) per-row vs. full-window target formulation, (c) sequence-classifier prior, (d) vocabulary precision, (e) each of seven sensor modalities, and (f) a no-numeric retraining of the decoder (Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric}) that decomposes the autoregressive mode-collapse into a measurable numeric-desynchronization contribution and a dominant intrinsic structural-stream collapse. Methodology follows ablation-study practice in industrial neural-network research~\cite{meyes2019ablation,jia2016deep}.

\item \textbf{Cross-class generalization study.} A leave-one-class-out study across nine machining operation classes that stress-tests whether the decoder memorizes per-class shortcuts or generalizes to unseen toolpath strategies.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work in CNC monitoring, sequence-to-sequence modeling for manufacturing, grammar-constrained decoding, and side-channel recovery of manufacturing instructions. Section~\ref{sec:problem} formalizes the task, vocabulary, and evaluation metrics. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the model architecture and training procedure. Section~\ref{sec:setup} details the dataset, preprocessing, and experimental protocol. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} presents the headline RQ1 result and a set of supporting analyses (per-axis recoverability, per-digit decomposition, per-operation-class breakdown, decoding-strategy sensitivity, output-position failure distribution, failure-mode analysis, autoregressive mode collapse, and a no-numeric retraining ablation that decomposes that collapse), then addresses RQ2--RQ4 in dedicated subsections. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} interprets the results and discusses limitations and deployment implications. Section~\ref{sec:future} outlines a designed-experiment data collection that targets the unrecovered G-code fields identified by this study.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We review four bodies of work that bear on the present study: sensor-based CNC monitoring and modality fusion (Section~\ref{sec:related-cnc}); sequence-to-sequence and grammar-constrained decoding (Section~\ref{sec:related-seq2seq}); side-channel reconstruction of manufacturing instructions (Section~\ref{sec:related-sidechan}); and manufacturing cybersecurity context (Section~\ref{sec:related-security}).

\subsection{CNC Process Monitoring and Multi-Modal Sensor Fusion}
\label{sec:related-cnc}

Sensor-based monitoring of machining processes is a mature subfield. Early work identified force, vibration, acoustic emission, temperature, and motor current as complementary modalities for tool-condition assessment~\cite{teti2010advanced}. Hand-crafted features extracted via Fourier and wavelet transforms became standard practice~\cite{zhu2009wavelet,mohanraj2020tool}. Deep learning enabled end-to-end feature learning from raw signals, reducing reliance on domain-specific feature engineering~\cite{wang2018deep,zhao2019machine}. Convolutional networks applied to vibration spectrograms and raw multi-channel signals achieved strong results on bearing fault detection~\cite{janssens2016convolutional}, surface-roughness classification~\cite{bhandari2021comparative,stathatos2024cnn}, and CNC anomaly detection~\cite{coelho2024anomaly}.

Multi-modal fusion strategies span early, late, and intermediate approaches~\cite{lahat2015multimodal,ramachandram2017deep}. Cross-modal attention has shown particular promise when modalities are complementary rather than redundant~\cite{cao2023crossattention,wang2024multisensor}. A comprehensive review by Tsanousa \etal~\cite{tsanousa2022multisensor} confirms that the field remains dominated by tool-condition and operation-classification tasks. A separate, model-based line --- digital-twin and digital-shadow process verification~\cite{bergs2021digital} --- compares observed machine state against a simulated reference of the \emph{intended} program; it is complementary to, and orthogonal in trust assumptions from, the present work, which reconstructs the executed instruction stream from sensors alone without a reference model or a trusted controller. Motor-current and servo-drive signatures are an established adjacent modality for load and toolpath inference within this tradition~\cite{teti2010advanced,wang2018deep}, and form one of the seven modality groups fused by the encoder used here. Two gaps persist relative to instruction-level recovery. First, target labels in prior work are session-level (tool worn / not worn) or coarse class labels (turning vs.\ milling); per-row instruction targets are largely absent. Second, ablation studies that progressively remove sensor groups to quantify their marginal contribution~\cite{meyes2019ablation,jia2016deep} are themselves uncommon in CNC monitoring, leaving practitioners without quantitative guidance on which sensors carry instruction-level signal as distinct from operation-class signal.

\subsection{Sequence-to-Sequence Modeling and Grammar-Constrained Decoding}
\label{sec:related-seq2seq}

Sequence-to-sequence neural models were established for natural-language translation~\cite{sutskever2014sequence,bahdanau2014neural,vaswani2017attention} and have since been applied across speech~\cite{graves2013speech}, time series~\cite{wen2023transformers}, and robotics~\cite{robotics_s2s}. Long Short-Term Memory networks~\cite{hochreiter1997long} remain widely used for sequential industrial data where streaming inference and causal ordering matter~\cite{malhotra2015lstm}. Denoising autoencoders~\cite{vincent2010stacked} learn representations robust to input corruption --- a property particularly valuable when industrial sensors are subject to mechanical, electromagnetic, and thermal noise.

When the output sequence follows a known grammar, constrained decoding can prune structurally invalid tokens at inference time. Picard~\cite{picard}, Outlines~\cite{outlines}, and XGrammar~\cite{xgrammar} have demonstrated efficient runtime grammar masks for SQL and JSON generation. G-code is amenable to the same treatment: instructions consist of an optional command token followed by address-value pairs subject to first-order ordering constraints~\cite{grammar_paper,gcode_foundational}. Recent work on large-language-model code generation for additive manufacturing~\cite{gllm,image2gcode,chatcnc} addresses a related but distinct task --- generating G-code from natural-language descriptions or images --- and does not consider sensor-conditioned recovery.

\subsection{Side-Channel Reconstruction of Manufacturing Instructions}
\label{sec:related-sidechan}

The closest analog to this work is side-channel reconstruction of toolpaths in additive manufacturing. Al~Faruque \etal~\cite{alfaruque2016acoustic} recovered Fused Deposition Modeling print geometry from acoustic emissions, achieving high agreement with the source G-code. Song \etal~\cite{song2016my} showed that a smartphone microphone alone could recover sufficient information to reproduce printed objects. Chhetri \etal~\cite{chhetri2016kcad} extended the approach to a detection-oriented setting, identifying kinetic anomalies indicative of attacks. More recent work uses optical~\cite{chattopadhyay2025video} and combined acoustic-magnetic~\cite{jamarani2024screg} side-channels to reconstruct full part geometries from 3D printers. Sturm \etal~\cite{sturm2014cyber} and Yampolskiy \etal~\cite{yampolskiy2018security} survey cyber-physical attack taxonomies in additive manufacturing. We note one important methodological distinction: the AM side-channel literature typically uses \emph{raw acoustic emission} signals sampled at kHz--MHz rates, whereas the present work uses an \emph{audio-RMS envelope} scalar feature sampled at $4$~Hz. The audio-RMS feature retains the energy magnitude of the cutting sound but discards the spectral content that drives most prior AM-side-channel methods; the resulting per-instruction recovery is therefore not directly comparable to the AM literature and is more conservative in its sensor demands.

CNC milling poses a substantially harder sensor environment than additive manufacturing for the reasons stated in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: simultaneous multi-axis motion, nonlinear force-engagement, and structural vibration. As a consequence, the additive-manufacturing recovery methods do not transfer directly. Kimmell \etal~\cite{kimmell2025cnc} demonstrated sensor-based detection of control-injection attacks in CNC machining using energy-anomaly thresholds, but their task is binary detection rather than instruction-level reconstruction. To the best of our knowledge, no prior work reports per-row G-code recovery from CNC sensor data with structured-output evaluation.

\subsection{Manufacturing Cybersecurity and Process Verification}
\label{sec:related-security}

The cybersecurity case for instruction-level sensor verification is direct. The machining-specific instruction-stream-integrity gap is articulated in the FMNet 2024 research-needs report~\cite{nist2024fmnet}; NIST SP 800-82 Rev.~3~\cite{nist2023sp80082} provides the general operational-technology framing, specifically its continuous-monitoring guidance under the Detect function and its supply-chain-risk guidance, against which an out-of-band physics-of-record monitor --- one whose guarantee holds only insofar as the sensor path itself is trustworthy (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}) --- is a complementary control. Adversarial G-code substitution in PLC and CNC controllers is an established attack surface~\cite{zonouz2014plc,sturm2014cyber,yampolskiy2018security,kimmell2025cnc,goh2017dataset}. Reconstructing the actually-executed instruction stream from external sensors --- independent of the controller --- provides an integrity-checking baseline that cannot be subverted by controller-resident malware. The present work characterizes which components of that instruction stream are realistically reconstructable on present-day sensor hardware, and which require additional sensor coverage or richer training data to recover.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Task Definition}
\label{sec:task}

Let $W \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$ denote a sensor window of $T$ samples at $C$ channels, recorded over a fixed window length aligned to a CNC milling operation. Let $g$ denote the G-code line that was executing within $W$. The task is to learn a mapping
\begin{equation*}
  f_\theta: W \mapsto \hat{g}, \qquad \hat{g} = (\hat{t}_1, \hat{t}_2, \dots, \hat{t}_L),
\end{equation*}
where $\hat{g}$ is a sequence of $L$ G-code tokens drawn from a vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$. The training objective minimizes a multi-head cross-entropy loss with the auxiliary structured heads described in Section~\ref{sec:decoder}, plus an end-of-sequence token. At inference, the decoder generates tokens autoregressively under a grammar mask that zeros structurally invalid transitions in the legacy-token head.

\subsection{Vocabulary and Token Types}
\label{sec:problem-vocab}

We tokenize G-code with a hybrid scheme that disentangles structural primitives from numeric values~\cite{grammar_paper,gcode_foundational}. The vocabulary contains four token classes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{SPECIAL tokens} --- \texttt{PAD}, \texttt{BOS}, \texttt{EOS}, \texttt{UNK}, \texttt{MASK}.
\item \textbf{COMMAND tokens} --- literal motion or machine commands (\texttt{G0}, \texttt{G1}, \texttt{G2}, \texttt{G3}, \texttt{M3}, \texttt{M5}, \texttt{M30}, \ldots).
\item \textbf{PARAM tokens} --- address letters that introduce an axis or rate parameter (\texttt{X}, \texttt{Y}, \texttt{Z}, \texttt{I}, \texttt{J}, \texttt{K}, \texttt{R}, \texttt{F}, \texttt{S}, \texttt{P}).
\item \textbf{NUMERIC tokens} --- bucketed numeric values, one bucket per (axis, magnitude) pair. The full vocabulary contains 1{,}048 \texttt{NUM\_X} buckets, 1{,}086 \texttt{NUM\_Y} buckets, and 231 \texttt{NUM\_Z} buckets, with smaller bucket sets for the remaining axes. Total vocabulary size: 2{,}418.
\end{itemize}

A typical G-code line in the corpus, e.g. \texttt{G1 X1.4919 Y1.4848 F22.}, becomes a token sequence \texttt{BOS G1 X NUM\_X\_1492 Y NUM\_Y\_1485 F NUM\_F\_22 EOS}. The bucketization discretizes numeric values to a fixed precision; the per-position digit head described in Section~\ref{sec:decoder} provides an auxiliary regression-style signal to mitigate the resulting discretization loss.

\subsection{Per-Row Target Formulation}
\label{sec:per-row}

A naive supervised target assigns one G-code label per sensor window. In the present corpus, a 256-sample window at 4~Hz spans 64 seconds and contains an average of 60 distinct G-code rows (median 29, max 237). A window-level target therefore aggregates the information across these rows, destroying the row-level distinctions that distinguish, for instance, an \texttt{X} positive move from an \texttt{X} negative move within the same window. We instead emit one $(W, g_r)$ training pair per G-code row $r$ that fires within window $W$, where $g_r$ is the token sequence for that single row. This per-row formulation:

\begin{itemize}
\item Removes within-window label ambiguity. Each $(W, g_r)$ pair has exactly one correct target.
\item Increases the training-sample count by an order of magnitude ($\sim$19{,}500 per fold versus $\sim$300 for the window-level alternative).
\item Decouples the supervised target from the windowing schedule, preventing the model from inferring labels via positional shortcuts on \texttt{(window\_index, source\_file)}.
\end{itemize}

A complementary \emph{full-window} formulation, evaluated as an ablation (Section~\ref{sec:abl-target-mode}), retains one sample per window but uses a multi-line target consisting of all G-code rows that fired within the window, concatenated in temporal order. This formulation has fewer training samples per fold but a richer per-sample supervisory signal, and tests whether row-level autoregressive context (the previous row's tokens) closes any per-row recovery gaps.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

We report seven complementary metrics:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Token accuracy} --- proportion of non-padding token positions in the generated sequence whose argmax matches the target. The headline metric for sequence-quality.
\item \textbf{Sequence accuracy} --- proportion of test samples for which \emph{every} non-padding token matches the target. A strict exact-match metric.
\item \textbf{Type accuracy} --- per-token classification accuracy on the 4-way SPECIAL/COMMAND/PARAM/NUMERIC type head. Diagnoses whether the model is in the right structural mode at each step.
\item \textbf{Command accuracy} --- per-token classification accuracy on the 6-way motion-command head, evaluated only at positions where the target carries a COMMAND token.
\item \textbf{Parameter-type accuracy} --- per-token classification accuracy on the 10-way axis-letter head, evaluated at PARAM positions.
\item \textbf{Sign accuracy} --- per-position classification accuracy on the 3-way (positive, negative, absent) motion-sign head, evaluated at the relevant axis positions.
\item \textbf{Numeric (digit) accuracy} --- per-digit-position correctness pooled across all six digit positions in NUM tokens. The companion regression measure for numeric values is mean absolute error (MAE) of the reconstructed coordinate.
\end{enumerate}

Macro precision, recall, and F1 are computed for each classification head, using scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn} on the per-token confusion matrix.

\paragraph{Teacher-forced vs.\ autoregressive evaluation.} For any autoregressive sequence model, two evaluation regimes give materially different numbers and reflect different deployment scenarios.

\emph{Teacher-forced} (TF) evaluation supplies the ground-truth previous token at every position during the forward pass and measures the model's per-position classification accuracy under that ideal input. It is the standard objective during training and the standard sweep-time metric. \emph{Autoregressive} (AR) evaluation requires the model to consume its own previous predictions at each step, with no access to ground truth, and measures end-to-end generation quality under the actual deployment condition where no controller log is available to anchor the sequence.

The categorical heads in our decoder (type, command, parameter-type, sign) are independent classifiers on the encoder memory and therefore report identical accuracies under TF and AR. The autoregressive-token heads (legacy-token, digit-value, and the derived sequence and numeric metrics) report substantially lower accuracies under AR than under TF, as exposure-bias errors compound along the generated sequence.

Throughout this paper we explicitly distinguish the two regimes. The headline result table reports both regimes for the autoregressive heads, since the TF numbers correspond to the standard sweep-time evaluation used during hyperparameter selection while the AR numbers correspond to the realistic deployment scenario in which the decoder must recover G-code without a controller-log reference. For the categorical heads, a single number suffices because the regime distinction does not apply.

\subsection{Threat Model}
\label{sec:problem-threat-model}

We state the threat model up front so that every recoverability result that follows can be read against it; Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model} treats the operating-point and adaptive-adversary detail.

\paragraph{Trusted elements.} The multi-modal sensor stack and its acquisition pipeline are trusted: sensors are physically secured, the telemetry path is integrity-protected, the recorded sensor stream faithfully represents the physical machine state, and the decoder model and verification host are not co-located with the controller. This is realistic for a process-monitoring deployment where sensors and analysis live outside the CNC controller's trust boundary, but it is a non-trivial operational assumption (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}, ``Sensor-side adversary''): a controller-resident adversary with bus access to shared sensor infrastructure is out of scope, as are sensor-side wire-tap, EMI injection, packet replay, and calibration-spoofing attacks.

\paragraph{Untrusted elements.} The CNC controller, its firmware, and any G-code dispatch path between controller and machine are untrusted. The adversary may have read/write access to the controller's program memory, may inject substituted G-code, may modify cutting parameters mid-execution, or may replay an earlier benign program while a different program is intended.

\paragraph{Attack classes considered.} (i)~\emph{G-code substitution} (e.g., \texttt{G1 X1.5} becomes \texttt{G1 X2.5}; substituted instructions may differ in command identity, axis, sign, or numeric value); (ii)~\emph{parameter tampering} where the program is structurally preserved but cutting parameters are altered (feed rate, spindle speed, depth of cut); (iii)~\emph{replay} of a previously-executed valid program while a different program was commanded; (iv)~\emph{skip / duplicate} of executed lines while the controller log reports nominal execution; (v)~\emph{upstream CAM / post-processor compromise} where the toolpath is corrupted before the operator-signed G-code file is produced. Per-attack-class detectability is reported in Table~\ref{tab:tamper_auc} (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}).

\paragraph{Adversary model: orthogonal defenses.} The sensor decoder is intended to run in parallel with --- not in place of --- the cheaper orthogonal defenses (file-hash / cryptographic signature on the G-code file; TCB attestation on the controller). Each defense covers attack classes the others cannot. The sensor decoder's residual value is the attack subset that survives a signed-G-code audit and beats controller attestation that does not extend to runtime memory: runtime substitution, selective skip / duplicate, and wrong-workpiece / wrong-fixturing. Within that subset, the decoder is further bounded by the closed-vocabulary in-distribution regime under which it is trained (Section~\ref{sec:cv}) and by the per-field recoverability characterized below. The full three-layer defense-in-depth comparison is Table~\ref{tab:lit_comparison} and Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}.

\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System Overview}
\label{sec:system-overview}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{system_overview.pdf}
\caption{System overview. A 64-second sensor window ($256$ samples $\times$ $98$ channels across seven modality groups) is mapped by the frozen \encname{} encoder (8.6\,M parameters) to a $256 \times 256$ memory tensor. An 8-layer Transformer decoder ($d_{\text{model}}{=}384$, 12 heads) consumes this memory via cross-attention and generates tokens autoregressively. The system has three stages (Figure~\ref{fig:system-overview}). (i)~A multi-modal sensor encoder maps a (256-sample, 98-channel) sensor window into a (256-step, 256-dim) memory sequence. (ii)~A Transformer decoder consumes this memory via cross-attention and generates a token sequence representing the G-code line that was executing within the window. (iii)~A grammar mask zeros structurally invalid transitions in the decoder's softmax distribution at inference time. The encoder is frozen for all experiments in this paper; its training is described in a separate contribution~\cite{encoder_paper} and summarized here only insofar as it influences the decoder.

\subsection{Sensor Encoder (Frozen)}
\label{sec:encoder}

The encoder is a multi-modal denoising autoencoder coupled to a temporal LSTM (\encname{}). It accepts seven modality groups --- accelerometer (3-axis, 6 boards = 18 channels), gyroscope (18), magnetometer (18), environmental temperature (6), ambient color (24), audio RMS (6), and motor current (8) --- for a total of $C=98$ channels. A per-modality projection layer maps each modality into a shared $d=256$ space; learned modality-weighting gates~\cite{lahat2015multimodal,ramachandram2017deep} produce a fused representation; a multi-head self-attention stack~\cite{vaswani2017attention} captures cross-channel interactions; a denoising reconstruction objective~\cite{vincent2010stacked} encourages noise-robust representations; and a final two-layer LSTM~\cite{hochreiter1997long,malhotra2015lstm} produces the temporal memory consumed by the decoder. The full encoder has 8.6~M parameters and was trained for 30 epochs on the same file-level 5-fold splits used here, reaching 96.3\% test-set accuracy on the same nine-class operation-type task~\cite{encoder_paper}. The encoder is frozen for all decoder experiments and serves as a fixed feature extractor. Figure~\ref{fig:sensor_gcode_overlay} shows the raw input the encoder receives for one test window, with the seven modality groups aligned in time against the G-code instructions that drove the cut.

\subsection{Decoder Architecture}
\label{sec:decoder}

\modelname{} is an 8-layer Transformer decoder with $d_{\text{model}}=384$ and $h=12$ attention heads per layer. Each decoder layer consists of (a)~masked multi-head self-attention over the partial output sequence, (b)~multi-head cross-attention over the encoder memory, (c)~a position-wise feed-forward network with GELU activation, and (d)~residual connections with pre-layer normalization~\cite{ba2016layernorm}. Dropout~\cite{srivastava2014dropout} of $0.1$ is applied after each sub-layer. The decoder has $31.28$~M trainable parameters at the headline configuration (the Transformer stack is $18.9$~M; the remainder is the grammar-mask + sensor-prior machinery and the six output heads, embedding, and positional encodings --- component breakdown in Appendix~\ref{app:repl-inference}); with the frozen $8.6$~M encoder the total system size is $\approx\!39.9$~M parameters.

\subsubsection{Multi-Head Structured Output}
\label{sec:multihead}

Six prediction heads share the decoder's final hidden state at each output position. Each head is a linear projection followed by a softmax (or a six-position softmax in the case of the digit head). The heads are trained jointly with a weighted cross-entropy loss:
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{decoder}} =
    \lambda_{\text{tok}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{tok}} +
    \lambda_{\text{type}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{type}} +
    \lambda_{\text{cmd}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cmd}} +
    \lambda_{\text{param}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{param}} +
    \lambda_{\text{digit}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{digit}} +
    \lambda_{\text{sign}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{sign}}.
\end{equation*}
Default weights are $\lambda_{\text{tok}}=3.0$ and all auxiliary $\lambda=1.0$. The auxiliary heads serve two purposes: they regularize the shared representation toward token-type-aware features, and they expose structural prediction errors that would otherwise be hidden inside aggregate token accuracy.

\paragraph{Legacy token head.} A vocabulary-sized softmax (2{,}418 classes) operating on the decoder's final hidden state. This head is the primary inference output: at each timestep, the next token is drawn from this distribution. A grammar mask (Section~\ref{sec:grammar}) zeros structurally invalid transitions before the softmax.

\paragraph{Type head.} A 4-way classifier over \{SPECIAL, COMMAND, PARAM, NUMERIC\} on each output position. Trained against the type of the target token at that position. This is the coarsest classification head and the easiest task; its accuracy is therefore an upper bound on the structural sanity of the decoded sequence.

\paragraph{Motion-command head.} A 6-way classifier over \{G0, G1, G2, G3, G53, M30\}, evaluated against the target's command token at COMMAND positions. Distinguishes rapid moves (G0), linear cuts (G1), clockwise/counter-clockwise arcs (G2/G3), the work-coordinate-cancel command (G53), and program-end (M30). G53 and M30 have very small support ($n\!\approx\!6$ per fold, $\le\!30$ pooled; Table~\ref{tab:per_class_command}); the per-class F1 for these classes is correspondingly underpowered (see Section~\ref{sec:lim-power}).

\paragraph{Parameter-type head.} A 10-way classifier over \{X, Y, Z, I, J, K, R, F, S, P\}, evaluated against the target's address letter at PARAM positions. On the present corpus only X, Y, Z, R, and F have positive support in the test splits (see Table~\ref{tab:data_coverage} for per-field frequencies); the remaining trained labels (I, J, K, S, P) do not appear in the test data and therefore do not have per-class entries in Table~\ref{tab:per_class_param_type}.

\paragraph{Sign head.} A 3-way classifier over \{POSITIVE, NEGATIVE, ABSENT\} that predicts the sign of the most recent axis-value. This head provides direct supervision on motion direction independently of the discretized numeric value.

\paragraph{Per-position digit head.} A six-position 11-way classifier (digits 0--9 plus PAD) that reconstructs the numeric value of each NUM token digit-by-digit. The six positions cover the precision range observed in the corpus (typically $0\!-\!2\!.X\!X\!X\!X$ for an X or Y coordinate). Decomposing numeric reconstruction by digit position makes the manuscript's per-position recoverability analysis (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}) possible and provides a regression-style auxiliary signal that complements the discretized NUM-token head.

\subsubsection{Grammar-Constrained Decoding}
\label{sec:grammar}

G-code follows a regular grammar: a line starts with an optional command (G/M) token; each address letter is followed by exactly one numeric value; certain commands (e.g.\ G2, G3) require specific subsequent fields. We encode the local part of this grammar as a static $|V| \times |V|$ binary transition mask and apply it to the legacy-token logits at each inference step, zeroing out structurally invalid transitions: BOS must be followed by a command or PARAM letter; a PARAM letter must be followed by its matching NUM token; a NUM token must be followed by another PARAM letter, a command, or EOS. The mask reduces, on average, the valid next-token set from $|V|=$ 2{,}418 to 31.8 tokens, a reduction that improves both calibration and exact-match accuracy. Implementation follows the runtime grammar-mask pattern established in Picard~\cite{picard}, Outlines~\cite{outlines}, and XGrammar~\cite{xgrammar}.

\paragraph{Scope and limitations of the local mask.} The bigram mask above does not encode command-state-dependent rules of the form ``the active command is G0, therefore the next address letter must not be R'' (R is an arc-radius parameter valid only with G2/G3). Such rules require a finite-state grammar (FSM) over a multi-token history. A separate inference-time FSM layer encodes the following higher-order rules on top of the bigram mask: G0 and G1 (linear moves) forbid R, I, and J; M30 (program end) forbids any subsequent command or PARAM letter. The FSM tracks the most recently emitted move-command as state and zeros forbidden transitions in the legacy-token logits before the softmax. The FSM is applied at autoregressive inference only; teacher-forced training is unaffected because the supervisory signal already encodes correct grammatical structure through the targets.

\paragraph{FSM ablation.} We audited grammar compliance and accuracy with and without the FSM layer, on both the baseline and the with-shortcuts 5-fold checkpoints under greedy autoregressive decoding. The bigram-only mask permits G0/G1 to emit R/I/J: across approximately 167{,}400 generated tokens per configuration, it produces 329 V1 violations affecting $170 / 549$ baseline samples ($31\%$) and 333 violations affecting $139 / 549$ with-shortcuts samples ($25\%$). With the FSM layer active, V1 violations drop to $0$ on baseline ($100\%$ reduction) and $11$ on with-shortcuts ($97\%$ reduction; the residual $11$ are G53/G55/G17/G90/G94 followed by R, a class the present FSM does not encode and a candidate for grammar refinement). The 5-fold accuracy cost is $-0.27$~pp / $-0.41$~pp on baseline token / numeric, and $-0.04$~pp / $+0.01$~pp on with-shortcuts --- in both configurations the difference is within fold-to-fold noise (baseline std $\pm 0.069$ / $\pm 0.032$; with-shortcuts std $\pm 0.016$ / $\pm 0.017$). The FSM therefore eliminates the dominant class of grammar violation at no measurable accuracy cost. A paired-sample comparison across the 5 folds confirms statistical non-significance: paired-$t$ tests on baseline token / numeric give $p=0.72 / 0.51$ and Wilcoxon signed-rank gives $p=1.00 / 0.44$; on with-shortcuts token / numeric the same tests give $p=0.83 / 0.95$ and $p=0.63 / 1.00$ (\texttt{audit/fsm\_vs\_bigram\_paired\_stats.json}). M30-followup violations (V2) were $0$ in all configurations because M30 is rare in the corpus and the model learns termination from training. We adopt the FSM as the default deployment-time decoding configuration on this basis.

\paragraph{Forward-pass pseudocode.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:forward} summarizes the deployment-time grammar-masked autoregressive forward pass that produces the token sequence reported throughout this paper. The bigram mask and the finite-state-machine layer are both applied to the legacy-token logits before the softmax; the structured heads (type, command, parameter-type, sign, digit) read the same hidden state but do not consume the autoregressive token stream.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Grammar-masked autoregressive forward pass for \modelname{}.}
\label{alg:forward}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Sensor window $W \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times 98}$; frozen encoder $E_\theta$; decoder $D_\phi$ with heads $\{h_{\text{tok}}, h_{\text{type}}, h_{\text{cmd}}, h_{\text{pt}}, h_{\text{sgn}}, h_{\text{dig}}\}$; bigram mask $M_b \in \{0,1\}^{|V|\times|V|}$; FSM transition function $\delta(\cdot,\cdot)$; max length $L_{\max}$.
\State $\mathbf{m} \gets E_\theta(W)$ \Comment{$256 \times 256$ memory; encoder frozen}
\State $\hat{g} \gets [\textsc{bos}]$; $s \gets s_0$ \Comment{state of the FSM}
\For{$t = 1$ \textbf{to} $L_{\max}$}
  \State $\mathbf{h}_t \gets D_\phi(\hat{g}_{1:t-1}, \mathbf{m})$ \Comment{cross-attend over $\mathbf{m}$}
  \State $\boldsymbol{\ell}_t \gets h_{\text{tok}}(\mathbf{h}_t)$ \Comment{legacy-token logits, $\in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$}
  \State $\boldsymbol{\ell}_t \gets \boldsymbol{\ell}_t \odot M_b[\hat{g}_{t-1}, :]$ \Comment{bigram mask}
  \State $\boldsymbol{\ell}_t \gets \boldsymbol{\ell}_t \odot \mathrm{FSM\_mask}(s)$ \Comment{FSM mask on top of bigram}
  \State $y_t \gets \arg\max_v \, \mathrm{softmax}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_t)_v$
  \State $\hat{g} \gets [\hat{g}, y_t]$; $s \gets \delta(s, y_t)$
  \If{$y_t = \textsc{eos}$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
\EndFor
\State $\{\hat{y}_{\text{type}}, \hat{y}_{\text{cmd}}, \hat{y}_{\text{pt}}, \hat{y}_{\text{sgn}}, \hat{y}_{\text{dig}}\} \gets \{h_{\text{type}}, h_{\text{cmd}}, h_{\text{pt}}, h_{\text{sgn}}, h_{\text{dig}}\}(\mathbf{H})$ \Comment{auxiliary heads on hidden states $\mathbf{H}=[\mathbf{h}_1,\dots,\mathbf{h}_t]$; do not consume $\hat{g}$}
\State \textbf{return} $\hat{g}$, $\{\hat{y}_{\text{type}}, \hat{y}_{\text{cmd}}, \hat{y}_{\text{pt}}, \hat{y}_{\text{sgn}}, \hat{y}_{\text{dig}}\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Scheduled Sampling}
\label{sec:scheduled-sampling}

The decoder is trained with teacher forcing as default. Optionally a scheduled-sampling regime~\cite{scheduled_sampling} is applied: with probability $p$ that linearly increases from $0$ to a tuned maximum $p_{\text{max}}$ over training, each input position is replaced with the model's own previous prediction rather than the ground-truth target. This reduces exposure bias between training and inference. The maximum $p_{\text{max}}$ was selected by the Stage-2 hyperparameter sweep (Section~\ref{sec:hp-sweep}) on per-row fold-1 validation token accuracy; the headline value is $p_{\text{max}}\!=\!0.5$, which lifted per-row command accuracy by $+17$~pp over $p_{\text{max}}\!=\!0$ at the cost of optimization stability at smaller numeric vocabularies (Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric} body, where the Design-B-style runs use $p_{\text{max}}\!=\!0$ for this reason). Values tested: $p_{\text{max}} \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$; $0.5$ was the validation-best across the swept range.

\subsection{Training Configuration}
\label{sec:training}

The decoder is optimized with AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} ($\eta=$ $5 \times 10^{-5}$, weight decay 0.05) for up to 300 epochs with early stopping on validation token accuracy (patience 75). The first 10 epochs use a linear warm-up. Batch size is 64, mini-batches are shuffled per epoch, and gradient clipping is applied at norm 1.0. Best-checkpoint-by-validation-token-accuracy is retained.

The decoder is trained \emph{without} positional metadata in its baseline configuration: \texttt{window\_index}, \texttt{total\_windows}, and \texttt{source\_file} identifiers are not exposed to the decoder. Section~\ref{sec:abl-positional} ablates this choice. Figure~\ref{fig:learning_curves} shows the 5-fold training and validation loss / token-accuracy trajectories.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Sweep}
\label{sec:hp-sweep}

The training configuration was selected through a three-stage hyperparameter sweep on fold 1.
\textbf{Stage 1} (8 cells) explored learning rate, batch size, model dimension, and loss-weighting on a coarse grid.
\textbf{Stage 2} (12 cells) refined around the Stage-1 winner by varying training techniques: curriculum learning, scheduled sampling, label smoothing~\cite{szegedy2016inception}, dropout, layer depth, and focal loss~\cite{focal_loss}.
\textbf{Stage 3} (14 cells) tested architectural variants: encoder fine-tuning, pointer-network output, regression-head numeric output, cross-window attention, multi-seed variance, and alternative encoder configurations.
The composite winner across all 34 cells (selected on a weighted-geometric-mean score) was used as the configuration for the 5-fold experiments reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

The corpus consists of 120 aligned CSV files recorded from a Bantam Tools desktop CNC milling machine~\cite{encoder_paper}. Six distributed multi-sensor boards (\texttt{frame\_l2}, \texttt{frame\_l3}, \texttt{frame\_r2}, \texttt{spindle2}, \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}, \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}) sample synchronously at 4~Hz; the double-underscore form matches the column-prefix convention in the released aligned CSVs. Each file carries:
\begin{itemize}
\item Accelerometer (3 axes $\times$ 6 boards = 18 channels);
\item Gyroscope (18 channels);
\item Magnetometer (18 channels);
\item Ambient color sensing (RGBA $\times$ 6 boards = 24 channels);
\item Environmental temperature (6 channels);
\item Audio root-mean-square (6 channels);
\item Motor-current draw (8 channels);
\end{itemize}
plus an aligned per-sample G-code line. We exclude proximity and pressure channels following the consistency audit reported in the encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper}, retaining $C=98$ sensor channels.

The dataset spans nine machining operation classes: \texttt{adaptive}, \texttt{adaptive150025}, \texttt{face}, \texttt{face150025}, \texttt{pocket}, \texttt{pocket150025}, \texttt{damageadaptive}, \texttt{damageface}, \texttt{damagepocket}. The \texttt{150025} suffix denotes a variant feed/spindle parameter; \texttt{damage} prefixes denote operations executed on a tool that subsequently produced surface defects. These nine classes cover three motion strategies (face, pocket, adaptive) crossed with parameter and condition variants.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Windowing}
\label{sec:preprocessing}

Sliding sensor windows of $T=256$ samples (64 seconds at 4~Hz) at stride 64 yield approximately $\sim$1{,}500 windows per fold. Each window is centered on a region of continuous cutting and aligned to its overlapping G-code rows. The per-row target formulation (Section~\ref{sec:per-row}) emits one $(W, g_r)$ pair per G-code row $r$ that fires within window $W$, yielding $\sim$19{,}500 training pairs, $\sim$7{,}000 validation pairs, and $\sim$7{,}500 test pairs per fold.

\paragraph{Sampling-rate context.} The 4~Hz sensor sampling rate is load-bearing for what is and is not recoverable. A row-execution-duration audit over the 120-file aligned corpus ($21{,}541$ row-execution instances; \texttt{audit/gcode\_row\_durations.json}) shows the median G-code row executes in a single $0.25$~s sample and $77.7\%$ of row-execution instances occupy $\le\!1$ sample period --- temporally unresolvable as distinct transients at $4$~Hz. For context, conventional acoustic-emission and vibration tool-condition monitoring samples at $\geq\!10$~kHz~\cite{teti2010advanced}; the present stack is deliberately at the low-rate, low-bandwidth end of the sensing-cost spectrum, and every recoverability result below should be read in that light (full physical-aliasing discussion: Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}).

\subsection{Five-Fold File-Level Stratified Cross-Validation}
\label{sec:cv}

We use file-level stratified five-fold cross-validation to prevent within-file leakage. Each operation class is divided across folds in proportion to its frequency, so each fold contains representative coverage of all nine classes. Within a fold, train and validation splits come from the same fold partition; the test split is the held-out partition.

\paragraph{Closed-vocabulary evaluation regime (load-bearing).} We then run a G-code-line \emph{coverage repair} step (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage-repair}): any G-code line that appears in the test split but not the training partner triggers a file swap between the partners, until every test-line also appears in training. This step does not remove a confound --- it \emph{constructs} a deliberate closed-vocabulary evaluation regime in which every test line has been seen verbatim during training of the same fold ($97.6\%$ of distinct test lines and $99.0\%$ of test line-instances; Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}). The 5-fold headline numbers must therefore be read as a within-vocabulary classification / near-retrieval result, \emph{not} as open-vocabulary instruction recovery. The matched open-vocabulary number is the leave-one-class-out (LOCO) command accuracy of $0.213 \pm 0.191$ (Section~\ref{sec:loco}); the two should be read side by side throughout this paper.

\paragraph{Within-fold non-independence.} Preprocessing uses overlapping sensor windows (window $256$, stride $64$, i.e., $75\%$ overlap between adjacent windows from the same file), so adjacent windows within a single file are not statistically independent. File-level disjointness across folds is preserved, but the effective number of independent observations within a fold is smaller than the raw window count. The covariate-shift audit (Section~\ref{sec:lim-covariate}) and bootstrap confidence intervals on the 5-fold means use the per-fold mean as the unit of analysis, which sidesteps the within-fold non-independence.

\subsection{Baselines and Ablation Designs}
\label{sec:ablations-design}

We compare \modelname{} against the following ablations and baselines, each trained under identical preprocessing, splits, and hyperparameter configuration:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No-shortcuts baseline} (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}). The headline configuration with positional metadata not exposed to the decoder.
\item \textbf{With-shortcuts ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:abl-positional}). The same model with \texttt{window\_index}, \texttt{total\_windows}, and \texttt{source\_file} hash exposed as additional decoder inputs. Tests whether positional metadata inflates accuracy beyond what sensor signal alone can recover.
\item \textbf{Full-window target ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:abl-target-mode}). The same encoder but with multi-line per-window targets instead of per-row targets. Tests whether autoregressive row-to-row context within a window resolves the per-row ambiguity discussed in Section~\ref{sec:per-row}.
\item \textbf{Sensor-modality ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}). For each of the seven sensor groups, we zero the corresponding channels at the encoder input and evaluate the resulting decoder. Pooled across five folds for the gyroscope and color modalities; fold-1 only for the others.
\item \textbf{Sequence-classifier (pattern-aware) ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:abl-pattern}). A sequence-classifier head trained jointly with the legacy-token head predicts which whole G-code line the model is reconstructing and biases the token distribution toward that line's tokens.
\item \textbf{Vocabulary-precision ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:abl-vocab}). A coarser 2-digit numeric bucketing reduces vocabulary size by approximately $5\times$.
\item \textbf{Noise-augmentation ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:noise-aug}). Gaussian noise injection on sensor channels, feature dropout, and token masking during training~\cite{srivastava2014dropout,jia2016deep,meyes2019ablation}.
\item \textbf{Leave-one-class-out generalization} (Section~\ref{sec:loco}). For each of the nine operation classes, train without that class in the training partition and evaluate on its held-out test samples.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:stats}

For each five-fold ablation, we report mean and standard deviation across folds, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) against the no-shortcuts baseline, and Cohen's $d$ effect size. ANOVA is computed with SciPy~\cite{virtanen2020scipy}; bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals use 10,000 resamples. Significance levels are reported as $\ast\,p<0.05$, $\ast\ast\,p<0.01$, $\ast\ast\ast\,p<0.001$.

\paragraph{Inferential scope and a caution on $n=5$.} The unit of analysis is the per-fold mean, so every parametric test in this paper is computed on $n=5$ observations per group. We therefore treat all $p$-values as \emph{descriptive} rather than confirmatory: with $n=5$ the ANOVA/Welch statistics are highly sensitive to the normality and homoscedasticity assumptions, which we do not have the sample size to verify, and reported $p$-values should be read to one or two significant figures and in conjunction with the effect size, not as precise probabilities. The bootstrap 95\% intervals are percentile intervals over five fold-means; they are small-sample and \emph{not} coverage-calibrated, and we present them as a smoothed indication of fold-to-fold spread rather than as exact confidence statements. Where a nonparametric test is reported (Mann--Whitney $U$, Wilcoxon signed-rank), it is the primary inferential statement and the parametric test is corroborating. Conclusions in this paper rest on effect sizes that are large relative to the cross-fold spread, not on threshold-crossing $p$-values; single-fold pilot results are labeled as such and are explicitly excluded from every quantitative or deployment claim.

\subsection{Non-Neural Baseline Comparison}
\label{sec:results-baseline}

Two lightweight classifiers on the \emph{same} mean-pooled encoder embedding the decoder consumes --- a scikit-learn \texttt{HistGradientBoosting} (HGB) tree-booster and the two-layer MLP probe of Section~\ref{sec:encoder-probe} --- anchor the headline and isolate the contribution of the decoder's autoregressive multi-head architecture from the encoder embedding itself. Both hit the always-most-common-class data-coverage ceiling on the binary fields ($\approx\!0.88$ \emph{has-X}, $0.99$ \emph{has-F}) --- the correct reference points for the decoder's per-field numbers --- and both fall well short on multi-class command identity (HGB macro-F1 $0.23$, MLP $\approx\!0.24$), consistent with the per-row modal-row ceiling (Section~\ref{sec:encoder-probe}). HGB does reach macro-F1 $0.86$ on the one genuinely within-window-diverse field (\emph{has-Z}), confirming the pooled embedding carries some per-row Z signal. The full per-task baseline table and analysis are in Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}.

\subsection{Numeric Digit Head Decomposition}
\label{sec:numeric-decomposition}

The digit head achieves $0.5850 \pm 0.0331$ mean accuracy under teacher-forced evaluation, dropping to $0.1038 \pm 0.0322$ under autoregressive evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:headline}); the per-position decomposition below is computed at the TF condition. The pooled 5-fold per-slot accuracy is bathtub-shaped (Table~\ref{tab:per_digit_position}, Figure~\ref{fig:per_digit_position_curve}): slot~0 ($10^{1}$, tens) reaches $1.0000$; slot~1 ($10^{0}$, units) reaches $0.7516 \pm 0.0338$; slots~2--4 (tenths--thousandths) bottom out at $0.62$--$0.70$; slot~5 ($10^{-4}$, ten-thousandths) recovers to $0.8636 \pm 0.0357$. The per-axis breakdown of this curve (Figure~\ref{fig:per_axis_bathtub_overlay}) shows that the pooled bathtub is averaging structurally different per-axis profiles: X and Y track the classical $\cup$-shape, but Z reaches $0.98$ at slot~2 and drops to $0.37$ only at slot~4 (the cutting-depth-step quantization explained below), and R is uniformly $\ge 0.88$ across all six slots (small discrete arc-radius vocabulary).

This profile is strongly anticorrelated with the \emph{source entropy} of each slot. We computed Shannon entropy of the empirical digit-value distribution at every slot of the model's fixed six-slot grid ($[10^{1}, 10^{0}, 10^{-1}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-4}]$, replicating \texttt{digit\_value\_head.py} exactly) across the same pooled population the accuracy denominator uses (\texttt{audit/digit\_entropy.json}). Per-slot accuracy and per-slot source entropy are strongly anticorrelated (Pearson $r = -0.96$, $p = 0.0023$; Spearman $r = -0.83$, $p = 0.04$; Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_accuracy_overlay}): wherever the digit distribution is highly non-uniform the model recovers it accurately, and wherever the distribution approaches the uniform-noise ceiling ($H_{\max} = \log_2 10 \approx 3.32$ bits) it cannot. The same inverse relationship holds at the per-(axis, slot) cell level (Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_accuracy_scatter}, Pearson $r = -0.78$ over 30 cells; the per-cell coefficient is weaker than the pooled value because per-axis cells span a wider entropy range, particularly R's tightly-packed low-entropy cells and F's printf-padded zeros, both of which fall on the high-accuracy/low-entropy regime line individually but compress the bivariate spread).

The per-axis decomposition (Figure~\ref{fig:digit_value_histograms}) grounds this in the CNC physics of the corpus. \textbf{High-order digits} are physically constrained: the tens digit (slot~0) is uniformly $0$ across X, Y, and Z (every coordinate $|\text{val}| < 10$ in the present small-part corpus), and the units digit (slot~1) carries most of its mass on $\{0, 1\}$ for X/Y ($75$--$77\%$) and is identically $0$ for Z (every $|\text{Z}|<1$). These cells have entropies $0.00$--$1.72$ bits, well below the uniform ceiling, and they are precisely the cells the model recovers near-perfectly. \textbf{Low-order digits} approach the ceiling: across X, Y, Z the $10^{-2}$, $10^{-3}$, and $10^{-4}$ slots have per-axis entropies in the range $3.26$--$3.31$ bits --- empirically indistinguishable from uniform random over $\{0,\ldots,9\}$. This is the empirical signature of CAM toolpath-generation noise: the fourth decimal of a coordinate ($10^{-4}$ inch) is interpolated by the CAM postprocessor at a resolution below any physical determinism the sensors carry at $4$~Hz, so it is information-theoretically near-unrecoverable from the sensor stream regardless of decoder design. The verdict holds across all three primary axes (\texttt{cnc\_verdict.lowest\_decimal} in \texttt{audit/digit\_entropy.json}). The $10^{-1}$ slot is a useful counter-example: for Z it has entropy $0.70$ bits (mass on $0$/$1$) because Z values cluster on small magnitudes (cutting-depth steps), and the model accordingly recovers Z's tenths slot with high accuracy --- a per-axis demonstration that the source-entropy account correctly predicts where recovery is easy and where it is not.

Stratifying the slot-5 distribution by \emph{operation class} reveals that the uniform-noise picture is not uniform across the corpus; three distinct regimes coexist (Figure~\ref{fig:slot5_by_op_class}). \textbf{(i) Adaptive HSM (High-Speed Machining) toolpaths} (\texttt{adaptive}, \texttt{adaptive150025}, and the \texttt{pocket} class, which is adaptive-style in this corpus) produce the canonical CAM-interpolation-noise signature: per-axis slot-5 entropy $\approx 3.26$--$3.30$ bits with comparatively low mass on digit `0' (for X: $9\%$ on \texttt{adaptive150025} through $17\%$ on \texttt{pocket} --- read off Figure~\ref{fig:slot5_by_op_class}) distributed across all digits with no concentrated modal value, the signature of a toolpath optimizer producing floating-point residuals rather than authoring round-ending coordinates at $10^{-4}$~inch. \textbf{(ii) Traditional facing operations} (\texttt{face}, \texttt{face150025}, \texttt{damageface}) have slot-5 entropy $\approx 1.55$--$1.71$ bits with $49$--$56\%$ mass on a single digit --- but the mechanism is geometric \emph{coordinate repetition}, not a CAM precision grid. In \texttt{face}, four specific X values --- the facing-pass entry/exit coordinates on the workpiece left and right edges --- dominate: $X\!=\!-0.1254$ ($n\!=\!624$), $X\!=\!-0.2084$ ($n\!=\!464$), $X\!=\!3.275$ ($n\!=\!608$), and $X\!=\!3.358$ ($n\!=\!464$), together $2{,}160$ of the $2{,}238$ X-coordinates ($96.5\%$). The two negative-X overruns drive the digit-`4' mass at slot~5; the two positive-X overruns drive mass at digits `0' and `9' (the latter via the float-epsilon target-encoding artifact of Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding}). \textbf{(iii) Pocket-style operations with tight geometry} (\texttt{pocket150025}, \texttt{damagepocket}, \texttt{damageadaptive}) sit between: slot-5 entropy $\approx 2.58$--$3.22$ bits with partial mass on `0' from arc/line waypoint repeats (e.g., \texttt{Y-0.071} appearing dozens of times along a fixed pocket wall). The bathtub \emph{upturn} of the pooled slot-5 curve is therefore the joint signature of coordinate-repeat in traditional ops and a separate float-epsilon target-encoding artifact ($23.6\%$ of CAM-authored terminal zeros encode to digit `9' under the model's iterative \texttt{int(dec\_part * 10) \% 10}), both detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding}, not a genuine per-axis recovery of the ten-thousandth digit. Representative G-code rows for each regime are shown in Table~\ref{tab:gcode_examples}, and a position-level prediction-versus-target plate (one supervised position per operation-class family, with predicted and target digits rendered as colored tiles per slot) is given in Figure~\ref{fig:gcode_line_example}.

\paragraph{What \emph{is} reliably recoverable.} The negative bound on slot~5 is half the story; the per-axis source-entropy map (Figure~\ref{fig:digit_value_histograms}, Figure~\ref{fig:quantization_heatmap}) identifies the cells where recovery \emph{is} structurally available, and they are non-trivial. In-distribution coarse-resolution recovery is reliable across all primary axes: the tens digit of X, Y, and Z is uniformly $0$ in this corpus and the model recovers it at $100\%$ accuracy; the units digit reaches $\ge 75\%$ on X and Y (mass on $\{0, 1\}$) and is identically $0$ on Z; Z's tenths digit recovers at high accuracy from the cutting-depth-step quantization ($86\%$ mass on `0', $H = 0.70$~bits, Figure~\ref{fig:digit_value_histograms}). R is recoverable across \emph{all} six slots ($H = 0.00$--$2.39$~bits, $34$--$100\%$ modal mass; Figure~\ref{fig:quantization_heatmap}) because the corpus uses a small discrete set of repeated arc radii rather than continuous values. The defensible characterization of the digit head is therefore a \emph{coarse-resolution coordinate recoverer} that picks up the physically- or geometrically-constrained digit positions (whole-inch magnitudes for X/Y/Z, cutting-depth steps for Z, arc-radius vocabulary for R) and is bounded at the uniform-noise ceiling at the fine-resolution positions where the CAM postprocessor's interpolation residuals dominate. Figure~\ref{fig:bits_recoverable_per_axis} summarizes this as a per-axis ``bit budget'': of the total source entropy in each axis's coordinates, the fraction approximately recovered by the decoder.

\subsection{Calibration of the Categorical Heads}
\label{sec:calibration}

A high-accuracy classifier may still be poorly calibrated --- its predicted-class confidence may systematically over- or under-estimate the empirical accuracy at that confidence. For a deployment that surfaces row-level instruction predictions to a human operator or to a downstream verification rule, calibrated confidence matters as much as accuracy: a $0.99$-confidence prediction should be correct $99\%$ of the time. We first report the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Maximum Calibration Error (MCE) at $10$ bins on the fold-1 baseline test set ($n_{\text{cmd}} = 1{,}982$ for command positions, $n_{\text{type}} = 37{,}590$ for all type positions, $n_{\text{pt}} = 35{,}392$ for parameter-type positions); these fold-1 numbers and the reliability diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:reliability_diagram}) are \emph{illustrative} of the worst (outlier) fold, and the deployment-relevant figures are the cross-fold means reported in the next paragraph. Results: type head ECE $= 0.015$, MCE $= 0.037$ at $0.968$ accuracy and $0.954$ mean confidence; parameter-type ECE $= 0.020$, MCE $= 0.114$ at $0.985$ accuracy and $0.964$ mean confidence; command head ECE $= 0.122$, MCE $= 0.207$ at $0.780$ accuracy and $0.902$ mean confidence (\texttt{audit/calibration\_fold1.json}; Figure~\ref{fig:reliability_diagram}). The type and parameter-type heads are well-calibrated by the conventional thresholds ($\text{ECE} < 0.025$). The command head is moderately mis-calibrated and \emph{overconfident}: its mean predicted confidence exceeds its empirical accuracy by $12$~pp, with the largest gap of $20.7$~pp concentrated in the highest-confidence bin. The overconfidence is consistent with the LOCO collapse pattern in Section~\ref{sec:loco}: the model places high mass on G1 even on test windows whose true command is not G1, producing high-confidence wrong predictions.

\paragraph{Post-hoc temperature scaling.} The standard remedy for overconfidence is post-hoc temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration,desai2020calibration}: divide the logits by a single learned scalar $T > 0$ before the softmax. We split each fold's test positions $50 / 50$ into a calibration half (used only to fit $T$ by minimizing NLL via LBFGS) and an evaluation half (used to measure post-correction ECE), with the seed-42 random split applied per head per fold. The cross-fold-aggregated fitted temperatures are $T_{\text{cmd}} = 1.34 \pm 0.25$, $T_{\text{type}} = 0.61 \pm 0.15$, $T_{\text{pt}} = 0.57 \pm 0.09$ ($T > 1$ softens an overconfident distribution; $T < 1$ sharpens an underconfident one); fold 1 fits the highest command-head temperature ($T_{\text{cmd}}^{(1)} = 1.77$) because fold 1 is the outlier fold with the largest covariate-shift effect (Section~\ref{sec:lim-covariate}). Across the 5 folds, the temperature-scaled command head reaches ECE $0.020 \pm 0.011$ (mean $\pm$ std) on the evaluation half --- a $3.0$~pp ECE reduction relative to the un-scaled cross-fold baseline of $0.050 \pm 0.038$. Type and parameter-type heads, already well-calibrated, gain similarly in absolute terms ($0.033 \to 0.004$ and $0.025 \to 0.002$ on ECE; \texttt{audit/calibration\_cross\_fold.json}). Accuracy is unchanged because temperature scaling does not move the argmax. One caveat for the over-sharpened heads: on the already-well-calibrated parameter-type head, $T\!<\!1$ reduces mean ECE but \emph{raises} the worst-bin MCE on several folds (up to $0.43$; per-fold range $0.11$--$0.43$), trading average calibration for a heavier worst-bin tail. A verification rule that thresholds on a single high-confidence bin should therefore apply temperature scaling to the over-confident command head but may prefer the un-scaled parameter-type head (\texttt{audit/calibration\_cross\_fold.json}). Temperature scaling adds a single fit-time scalar per head and no inference-time cost; we recommend it as the default deployment-time calibration step for the command head. As a single-scalar post-hoc correction it is known to degrade under distribution shift~\cite{ovadia2019trust}, consistent with the fold-1 outlier behavior.

\subsection{Failure-Mode Analysis}
\label{sec:failure-modes}

Beyond aggregate accuracies, we classify the qualitative shape of the decoder's worst predictions to characterize \emph{how} the model fails when it fails. From each fold's test split we extract the 20 worst-edit-distance sequences (100 sequences across 5 folds) and bucket them by the structural difference between the predicted and target token streams: (a)~\emph{dropped command} --- target contains a G-command, prediction does not; (b)~\emph{wrong command} --- both contain G-commands but they differ; (c)~\emph{hallucinated command} --- target has no G-command (a continuation row), prediction introduces one; (d)~\emph{value-only error} --- structural tokens match but numeric values differ. Figure~\ref{fig:failure_examples} shows two representative examples per bucket drawn from the worst-edit-distance pool, with per-token differences highlighted in bold. The bucket counts (out of 100) are: dropped 17, wrong 1, hallucinated 8, value-only 37, and ``other'' 37, where ``other'' captures same-command structural mismatches dominated by address-letter swaps (Y$\leftrightarrow$X). Command-identity failures (a--c) together account for 26\% of the worst sequences; the remaining residual is split between value-only errors and axis-letter swaps. The worst failures are therefore predominantly structural rather than arithmetic. Section~\ref{sec:disc-failure-mode} interprets this pattern.

\subsubsection{Autoregressive Mode Collapse on Full-Window Targets}
\label{sec:disc-mode-collapse}

The qualitative-failure analysis above is computed on per-row predictions, where each sample is a single short G-code line and autoregressive decoding spans only $\sim$5--15 tokens. On full-window targets ($\sim$1{,}000--1{,}400 tokens per sample), the autoregressive decoder exhibits a qualitatively different and more severe failure mode: \emph{mode collapse to a corpus-modal token sequence}. To illustrate, we inspected the first three test samples in fold~1's full-window split and report their autoregressive (beam-width-one) decodings:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
\begin{tabular}{l p{0.78\textwidth}}
sample 1 TRUE & X NUM\_X\_1627 Y NUM\_Y\_1464 Z NUM\_Z\_0095 X NUM\_X\_1623 Y NUM\_Y\_1687 \ldots \\
sample 1 PRED & X NUM\_X\_1466 Y NUM\_Y\_1630 Z NUM\_Z\_0052 X NUM\_X\_1455 Y NUM\_Y\_1607 \ldots \\
\addlinespace
sample 2 TRUE & X NUM\_X\_1466 Y NUM\_Y\_1630 Z NUM\_Z\_0052 X NUM\_X\_1457 Y NUM\_Y\_1607 \ldots \\
sample 2 PRED & X NUM\_X\_1466 Y NUM\_Y\_1630 Z NUM\_Z\_0052 X NUM\_X\_1457 Y NUM\_Y\_1607 \ldots \\
\addlinespace
sample 3 TRUE & X NUM\_X\_1569 Y NUM\_Y\_1453 Z NUM\_Z\_0097 X NUM\_X\_1676 Y NUM\_Y\_1645 \ldots \\
sample 3 PRED & X NUM\_X\_1466 Y NUM\_Y\_1630 Z NUM\_Z\_0052 X NUM\_X\_1455 Y NUM\_Y\_1607 \ldots \\
\end{tabular}
\end{quote}
Samples 1 and 3 receive essentially the same predicted token stream as each other, despite having visibly different TRUE sequences. Sample 2's TRUE happens to coincide with the corpus-modal sequence, so it scores near-perfectly purely by accident. The decoder has converged to a canonical ``facing-operation toolpath'' and emits it autoregressively regardless of the sensor input that conditioned the encoder memory. Cross-attention into the encoder memory is effectively dropped under autoregressive generation: the decoder's own previous-token state dominates, and once on the modal trajectory the decoder stays there.

This mode collapse is the mechanism behind the autoregressive-token-accuracy drop reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}; the categorical heads are unaffected for the reason stated in Section~\ref{sec:metrics}. Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} quantifies the effect as a pairwise prediction-similarity matrix across test samples. The primary evidence for the mechanism is the output-diversity audit ($3.3\times$ over-representation of the modal pattern) and the per-operation-class breakdown; the target-length correlation (Figure~\ref{fig:lenacc}, Pearson $r \approx -0.15$) is weak and we treat it only as corroborating, not load-bearing. Section~\ref{sec:disc-when-to-use} recommends per-row mode at deployment time on this basis.

\paragraph{Output-string diversity (partial vs.\ total collapse).} A natural quantitative test of mode collapse is the diversity of distinct output strings produced across the test set. Across the 549 test samples in the baseline 5-fold split, the autoregressive decoder produces 407 distinct predicted token-strings (74\% of inputs map to a distinct output); the corresponding ground-truth set contains 459 distinct strings (84\%). The modal predicted string appears in $9.5\%$ of predictions on average, versus a modal ground-truth string appearing in $2.9\%$ of windows --- a $3.3\times$ over-representation of the modal pattern (\texttt{audit/ar\_output\_diversity.json}). The collapse is therefore \emph{partial}: the decoder does not produce a single canonical output, but it over-concentrates on the modal facing-pattern at roughly three times the natural rate. This is consistent with the per-class breakdown above and the heatmap in Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}: within an operation-class block the predictions are mostly the same, but across classes the model does emit visibly different (though equally wrong) token strings.

\subsubsection{Positional-Metadata Ablation}
\label{sec:abl-positional}
Re-training the decoder \emph{with} positional metadata exposed (window index, total-window count, source-file hash) produces the per-metric effect sizes in Table~\ref{tab:anova}. One-way ANOVA across the 5 folds of each configuration shows a clean factorization:

\begin{itemize}
\item Categorical heads --- unaffected by positional metadata (command $F=0.02$, $p=0.89$; type $F=0.04$, $p=0.85$; param-type $F=0.01$, $p=0.93$; sign $F=0.00$, $p=0.96$, all under AR evaluation; identical p-values under TF).
\item Autoregressive heads --- token $F=4.52$, $p=0.066$, $\Delta=+7.5$~pp; sequence $F=27.18$, $p=0.0008$, $\Delta=+7.6$~pp; numeric $F=18.96$, $p=0.0024$, $\Delta=+8.0$~pp. Sequence and numeric heads are improved by positional metadata under AR evaluation at $p < 0.01$; the token-head improvement is borderline ($p=0.066$, marginal at $\alpha=0.05$). Under TF evaluation the same comparison shows no improvement on any head (token $p=0.21$, numeric $p=0.13$, sequence the sole TF-significant comparison at $p=0.0006$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Categorical heads} (command, type, parameter-type, sign, per-axis presence and sign) are robustly recoverable from sensors alone, and exposing positional metadata does not improve them under any evaluation regime. The with-shortcuts 5-fold command-accuracy mean is $0.882 \pm 0.056$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}), statistically indistinguishable from the no-shortcuts $0.8875 \pm 0.0558$ baseline. These heads are independent per-position classifiers on the encoder memory; their accuracies are identical under teacher-forced and autoregressive decoding (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}).

\textbf{Autoregressive-token heads} (token, numeric, sequence) show a more nuanced picture that depends on the evaluation regime. Under teacher-forced eval the position metadata provides no lift and is mildly harmful ($-2.7$~pp on token, $-5.3$~pp on numeric in 5-fold means). Under autoregressive deployment-true evaluation, however, the picture reverses: position metadata produces approximately $+7.5$~pp on token and $+8.0$~pp on numeric accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}). The mechanism is mitigation of autoregressive mode collapse: in the no-shortcuts AR setting, the decoder converges to a corpus-modal token sequence regardless of sensor input; exposing the window index gives the decoder a per-window anchor that breaks the modal trajectory on a fraction of test samples. Position metadata is therefore not a ``shortcut'' that inflates sweep-time accuracy; it is a deployment-time disambiguation signal that partially compensates for exposure bias in long-sequence autoregressive decoding. Bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals for both configurations appear in Table~\ref{tab:bootstrap_ci}.

\paragraph{Decomposing the collapse mechanism.} The position-metadata
mitigation establishes that per-window anchoring partly breaks the modal
trajectory; the no-numeric retraining ablation (Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric})
takes the complementary view, asking what the collapse \emph{is made of}.
Retraining the decoder with every coordinate value collapsed to a single
placeholder token --- so that a numeric error can no longer desynchronize
the surrounding tokens --- improves autoregressive per-token structural
accuracy by $+8.8$~pp (paired across the five folds, $5/5$ positive,
paired $t=4.02$, $p\!<\!0.02$) but leaves window-level structural exact-match
essentially unchanged ($0.066$ vs.\ $0.058$). The collapse therefore
decomposes into a measurable numeric-token-desynchronization contribution
(eliminated by the ablation) and a dominant intrinsic mode-collapse of the
structural token stream itself (unaffected by the ablation): the latter,
not the numeric vocabulary, is the deployment-relevant bottleneck.

\paragraph{Multiple-comparisons correction on the drilled grid.} The per-class / per-axis / per-position ANOVA grid is run as the methodology section commits (Appendix~\ref{app:repl-stats}). The grid covers 8 ablation configurations (noise augmentation and the seven zeroed-modality ablations: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, environmental, color, audio-RMS, electrical) against the no-shortcuts baseline, drilled across the per-class precision/recall/F1 for type, command, parameter-type, sign, and the per-digit-value / per-position numeric heads, for a total of $630$ (ablation, drilled-metric) tests (\texttt{audit/anova\_per\_class\_results.json}). Under Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (BH-FDR) adjustment ($\alpha=0.05$), $320 / 630$ tests are flagged; under the more conservative Holm-Bonferroni adjustment, $72 / 630$. We stress that this grid is a \emph{robustness sweep}, not $320$ independently established effects: each test is an $n=5$ ANOVA, and FDR/Holm control the false-discovery/family-wise error of the \emph{decision rule} but do nothing to remedy the low power and assumption-fragility of a five-point test, so the long tail of small-support per-class cells is uninformative either way. The signal in the grid is carried entirely by a few \emph{large-effect} ablations that need no multiplicity machinery to see: the noise-augmentation ablation (per-class command F1 drops of $0.3$--$0.7$ for G0/G1/G2/G3) and the uniform command-head degradation shared by all seven sensor-modality ablations (consistent with the non-significant between-modality ANOVA in Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}). We therefore read the grid as ``these few large effects are robust to multiplicity correction,'' not as a count of confirmed discoveries; per-class cells with $n_{\text{support}}\le 50$ are underpowered regardless of adjustment (Section~\ref{sec:lim-power}). The macro-metric ANOVA results in Table~\ref{tab:anova} survive both Holm-Bonferroni and BH-FDR adjustment for the sequence-head and numeric-head AR-evaluation positives ($p < 0.01$); the token-head $p=0.066$ borderline does not survive either adjustment.

\subsubsection{No-Numeric Retraining: Decomposing the Autoregressive Collapse}
\label{sec:abl-nonumeric}

In the full-window tokenization roughly half of every G-code line's positions
are coordinate \emph{values} (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}), each a
hard selection from the $\sim$2{,}400-entry numeric vocabulary, and a single
mispredicted value desynchronizes the autoregressive decode of the remainder
of the line. This motivates a direct test of whether the autoregressive
collapse of Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} is \emph{driven} by the
numeric tokens: if the decoder were relieved of predicting coordinate values,
the structural skeleton --- command codes and axis letters --- might decode
correctly end-to-end.

A naive test --- re-scoring the headline decoder's autoregressive predictions
with the numeric positions excluded from the metric --- is confounded: the
decoder still \emph{emits} a coordinate value at every numeric position, so a
mispredicted value still desynchronizes the surrounding structural tokens
before the metric masks them, leaving structural-only autoregressive token
accuracy ($0.317$) only modestly above the unmasked figure ($0.215$) --- a
contaminated lower bound. The clean test removes the numeric tokens from the
decoder's prediction problem entirely: we retrain the decoder with every
coordinate value collapsed to a single placeholder token --- a 24-entry
structural vocabulary in place of the $\sim$2{,}400-entry one --- so the
decoder predicts only the structural skeleton and a numeric error can never
desynchronize the decode. The frozen encoder, the 5-fold file-level split,
and all decoder hyperparameters are unchanged from the headline configuration;
the digit head is disabled. Training uses teacher forcing throughout:
scheduled sampling, used by the headline model, destabilizes optimization
once the numeric vocabulary is collapsed --- the placeholder vocabulary
offers no per-position lexical variety for the decoder to re-anchor on ---
and is therefore disabled for this ablation, after which validation token
accuracy converges stably ($\sim$$0.96$ across folds; last-25-epoch standard
deviation $<\!0.01$ on every fold).

Table~\ref{tab:nonumeric} reports the comparison. \textbf{Under teacher
forcing} the no-numeric decoder reaches structural token accuracy
$0.927 \pm 0.024$ against the headline decoder's $0.960 \pm 0.020$, and
structural sequence exact-match $0.068 \pm 0.017$ against the headline's
$0.213 \pm 0.048$ --- a real but modest cost from collapsing the varied
numeric tokens to a single placeholder, consistent with the loss of
per-position lexical variety as positional scaffolding for the decoder's
attention. \textbf{Under autoregressive decoding, the two effects decompose.}
Structural per-token accuracy improves by $+8.8$~pp ($0.405 \pm 0.143$ vs.\
$0.317 \pm 0.116$); the improvement is consistent across all five folds
(paired mean $+0.088 \pm 0.049$ across folds, $5/5$ folds positive,
paired $t=4.02$, $p\!<\!0.02$) and is the cleanest direct measurement of the
contribution of numeric-token desynchronization to the autoregressive
collapse, since the placeholder cannot be mis-predicted in a way that shifts
subsequent token positions. \emph{Structural sequence exact-match, however,
is essentially unchanged} ($0.066 \pm 0.014$ vs.\ $0.058 \pm 0.039$; paired
mean $+0.008$, $2/5$ folds positive) --- the deployment-relevant window-level
recovery does not improve. The mode-collapse mechanism of
Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} therefore decomposes into two
contributors: (a) \textbf{numeric-token desynchronization}, which the ablation
eliminates and which contributes a measurable, paired-significant
$\sim$$9$-pp per-token autoregressive effect; and (b) \textbf{intrinsic
autoregressive mode-collapse of the structural token stream itself}, which the
ablation does \emph{not} affect and which dominates the window-level
recovery. Mechanism~(b) is consistent with the position-metadata mitigation
of Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} (per-window anchoring of the structural
decode) and the null beam-search result (Appendix~\ref{app:decoding}): the
structural collapse is intrinsic to autoregressive decoding of the long
full-window target, not driven by the numeric vocabulary. This ablation
complements the vocabulary-precision pilot (Section~\ref{sec:abl-vocab}):
neither coarsening the numeric vocabulary nor eliminating numeric prediction
entirely materially lifts window-level recovery, jointly localizing the
bottleneck off the numeric vocabulary and onto the autoregressive sequence
mechanism on the structural stream.

\subsubsection{Target-Mode Ablation (Per-Row vs.\ Full-Window)}
\label{sec:abl-target-mode}

The per-row target formulation (Section~\ref{sec:per-row}) leaves the decoder without a within-window row-identification signal: every row of a given window shares the same encoder memory, and the decoder must predict a specific row from that shared input. A natural alternative is full-window targeting, in which the supervisory signal is the entire multi-line G-code that fired within the window, generated autoregressively. Row identification then comes from the decoder's own output context: row $r$ is predicted conditional on rows $1\!\dots\!r-1$. The corresponding row in Table~\ref{tab:ablations} reports the resulting accuracies.

The target-mode contrast --- per-row (one G-code line per window) versus full-window (the full multi-line sequence within each window) --- isolates the contribution of autoregressive context to row-level recovery. Holding the architecture, hyperparameters, and training data fixed, the full-window formulation produces a substantial lift on the command head: command accuracy moves from per-row $0.499$ to full-window $0.888$, a +38.9~pp gain. Because the command head is an independent classifier on the encoder memory and not autoregressive in its output stream, this lift is robust across both teacher-forced and autoregressive evaluation regimes (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}); it reflects an actual change in what the decoder is able to recover, not a difference in eval condition. Section~\ref{sec:disc-failure-mode} interprets the lift mechanistically: in per-row mode all rows within a 64-second window share the same encoder memory, so the decoder cannot disambiguate which of $\sim$60 candidate rows it is asked to emit; in full-window mode the multi-line target gives every row a unique within-window position signal, restoring identifiability for the command head.

On the autoregressive-token heads (token, numeric, sequence), the per-row vs.\ full-window contrast is more nuanced. In teacher-forced evaluation the full-window formulation reports higher token and numeric accuracy than per-row, but in autoregressive evaluation the difference shrinks substantially because exposure-bias errors compound over the longer full-window sequences. We therefore report the full-window formulation as the deployment-recommended configuration only when categorical recovery is the objective; when end-to-end sequence reproduction is the objective, per-row mode is comparable in autoregressive performance and has the practical advantage of much shorter, parallelizable per-row inference.

\subsection{RQ3: Sensor-Modality Ablation}
\label{sec:abl-sensors}
For each of seven sensor modality groups (accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, environmental, color, audio-RMS, electrical) we zero the corresponding sensor channels at the encoder input, then evaluate the decoder. The encoder is not retrained; the zeroing happens only at decoder-inference time, which isolates the marginal contribution of each modality \emph{conditional on the encoder's existing weight allocation}. We emphasize that this is a \emph{lower bound} on the value of each modality: an encoder retrained without a given modality could re-allocate capacity onto the remaining channels and would be the appropriate test of modality-redundancy at the system level; the inference-time zeroing reported here tests only what the existing encoder happens to extract from that modality. Six of the seven modality groups are evaluated at the full 5-fold ($n=5$) protocol; the audio-RMS group has $n=4$ (one fold's checkpoint was unrecoverable), so its ANOVA, Welch, and Mann--Whitney statistics are computed at $n=4$ and are flagged accordingly in Table~\ref{tab:sensor_ablation} and Table~\ref{tab:anova}.

\paragraph{The finding: uniform collapse, not modality differentiation.} The ablation is complete at 5 folds for six modality groups and at 4 folds for audio-RMS. At the inference-time-zeroing test every modality is critical to the command head and the magnitude is statistically uniform: zeroing any single modality drops command accuracy from the full-modality baseline of $0.888 \pm 0.056$ to the $0.449$--$0.484$ band (accelerometer $0.484$, environmental $0.474$, magnetometer $0.465$, gyroscope $0.462$, rms $0.460$, color $0.457$, electrical $0.449$; all $\pm 0.05$--$0.11$ across folds), a uniform $-40$ to $-44$~pp collapse. A one-way ANOVA across the seven zeroed-modality groups is \emph{not} significant ($F=0.07$, $p=0.998$; \texttt{audit/sensor\_anova\_7modality.json}) --- the seven modalities are statistically indistinguishable from one another --- while every modality \emph{versus} the full-modality baseline \emph{is} significant under a Welch test ($p<0.001$ in all seven cases, $t$ from $-7.1$ to $-11.5$). \textbf{No single modality is more deletable than another at this test;} the test confirms joint necessity but cannot rank importance. Token, type, and parameter-type heads are far more robust: token drops $\sim 4$~pp, type $<1$~pp, and parameter-type $\sim 1.5$~pp across every ablation. Table~\ref{tab:sensor_ablation} reports the per-modality numbers (5-fold; audio-RMS 4-fold) and Figure~\ref{fig:sensor_ablation} shows the bar chart.

\input{../tables/sensor_ablation.tex}

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The encoder's learned modality-gating routes essential command-discrimination signal through every modality at once; when any one is zeroed at inference the gating mechanism degenerates to a representation that is informative enough for the coarse (type, param-type) heads but not for the finer command head. This means the inference-time zeroing test cannot rank the relative importance of modalities --- it can only confirm that they are all jointly important. The genuine modality-importance ranking, and the answer to ``which sensors can a production deployment afford to omit'', requires an encoder retrained from scratch without each modality. That experiment --- per-modality encoder retraining --- is performed in Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}: the retrained encoder fully compensates for any single modality removal (paired $\Delta$ command $\le 0.013$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F=0.91$, $p=0.499$), so the frozen-encoder ``joint necessity'' reading above is a gating artifact rather than a statement of modality information content.

\paragraph{Comparison with encoder-paper modality-importance.} The encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper} reported gyroscope, color, and electrical as the highest-utility modalities for operation classification under a per-modality SHAP-style attribution at the encoder input. The decoder-side ablation reported here does not reproduce that ranking because it operates on a frozen, fully-trained encoder rather than on the marginal information content of each modality; the two analyses answer different questions. The decoder ablation says ``every modality is jointly necessary at inference under the existing encoder''; the encoder paper says ``these modalities carried the most operation-class signal during encoder training.'' The non-significant $7$-group ANOVA ($p=0.998$) is the formal statement of this: across the full 5-fold sweep the encoder-paper's ``most-important'' modalities (gyroscope, color) are not separable from the rest at the decoder-ablation level. The reconciliation is the retrained-encoder ablation referenced above: if encoder-retraining-without-modality-X still gives the decoder reasonable command accuracy, then modality X is deployment-deletable; otherwise modality X is genuinely load-bearing. That experiment, not inference-time zeroing, is the correct test of the deployment cost-reduction question; it is reported in Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}, where the retrained encoder fully compensates for any single modality removal ($\Delta$ command $\le 0.013$; $0/7$ significant under Holm correction).

\subsubsection{Per-Modality Encoder Retraining (Leave-One-Modality-Out)}
\label{sec:abl-lomo}

The inference-time-zeroing ablation of Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors} cannot rank modalities because zeroing any one disrupts the frozen encoder's learned gating, producing a statistically uniform $40$--$44$~pp collapse ($F\!=\!0.07$, $p\!=\!0.998$). The deployment-relevant question is whether the encoder, when retrained without a modality, can reallocate capacity onto the remaining channels. This subsection reports that experiment.

\paragraph{Protocol.} For each of seven modality groups we retrain the
multi-modal denoising encoder from scratch on the $98$-feature sensor base
with that modality's channels removed, then train a fresh six-head decoder on
the resulting frozen encoder under the full-window target mode --- the same
two-stage pipeline (and exact decoder recipe) used for the headline model.
Results are reported at the full five-fold file-level cross-validation
protocol, with a full-stack baseline cell (no modality removed) retrained
under the identical protocol as the reference. The seven groups are
accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, color, temperature, audio-RMS, and
electrical. (\emph{Reconciliation with Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}}: the
inference-time-zeroing ablation grouped pressure, temperature, and proximity
into a single ``environmental'' modality; the LOMO study operates on the
$98$-feature base from which pressure and proximity are already excluded as
low-activity channels, so the environmental group reduces to temperature.)

\paragraph{Validity of the paired comparison.} A LOMO attribution is only
sound if every modality cell is scored on the \emph{same} held-out test set;
otherwise a modality effect is confounded with test-set difficulty. We found
and corrected a latent reproducibility defect that would have broken this: the
file-level cross-validation split's coverage-repair step iterated an unordered
set, making the validation/test partition dependent on process-level hash
ordering and therefore different on every preprocessing run. Imposing a
deterministic ordering makes the split byte-identical across all modality
exclusions. In addition, a per-cell channel-identity gate verifies, before any
GPU time is spent on a cell, that (i)~exactly the intended channels were
removed, (ii)~the retrained encoder and the decoder consume an identical
surviving-channel set in identical order, and (iii)~the train/validation/test
file partition and the G-code target multiset are invariant across modality
exclusions --- i.e.\ only the sensor tensors differ. Every cell reported here
passed all five checks of that gate.

\paragraph{The finding.} Under LOMO retraining the full-stack baseline reaches
command accuracy $0.904 \pm 0.039$; removing a single modality and retraining
yields accelerometer $0.903 \pm 0.037$, gyroscope $0.905 \pm 0.030$,
magnetometer $0.896 \pm 0.033$, color $0.904 \pm 0.044$, temperature
$0.904 \pm 0.038$, audio-RMS $0.905 \pm 0.039$, and electrical
$0.891 \pm 0.051$ (five-fold mean $\pm$ s.d.). The fold-paired effect ---
the mean over folds of command$(m,f)-$command$(\text{baseline},f)$, which
removes the substantial fold-to-fold variance ($\sigma \approx 0.04$, an order
of magnitude larger than any modality effect) --- is within $\pm 0.013$ for
every modality, with electrical the largest in magnitude at
$-0.013 \pm 0.015$. The fold-blocked one-way ANOVA across the seven LOMO
conditions is non-significant ($F=0.91$, $p=0.499$), and the Holm-corrected
per-modality paired $t$-test against zero rejects none of the seven
($0/7$ significant at $\alpha=0.05$; smallest adjusted $p$ is $0.41$ for
magnetometer, Hedges' $g_{z}\!\approx\!-0.94$, a medium-large effect that
fails statistical significance at $n\!=\!5$ but is informative). Token and numeric heads behave the same way: all paired
effects within $\pm 0.011$ for token and $\pm 0.022$ for numeric, all
non-significant. \textbf{Retraining the encoder without any single modality
produces no measurable change in any decoder head.}
Table~\ref{tab:lomo_modality} reports the per-modality numbers and
Figure~\ref{fig:lomo_modality} shows the per-modality paired $\Delta$ command;
full per-fold $\Delta$, Holm-corrected $p$, and effect sizes are in
\texttt{audit/lomo\_modality\_attribution.json}.

\subsubsection{Per-Sensor Encoder Retraining (Leave-One-Sensor-Out)}
\label{sec:abl-lomo-sensor}

The leave-one-modality-out study (Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}) ablates the
\emph{channel-type} axis: it removes a sensing modality --- e.g.\ all
gyroscope channels --- across every sensor board at once. The multi-modal
stack, however, has an orthogonal axis. The continuous channels are
physically distributed across \textbf{six sensor units} at distinct machine
locations, and each unit carries every per-board modality. A modality cannot
be removed from a deployed machine without removing the boards that carry
it; a real cost-reduced deployment removes \emph{sensor units}. We therefore
also run leave-one-\emph{sensor}-out (LOSO), the directly deployment-relevant
test of which physical sensor a production monitor could ship without.

\paragraph{Protocol.} The 98-channel base comprises six physical sensor
units --- \texttt{frame\_l2}, \texttt{frame\_l3}, \texttt{frame\_r2},
\texttt{spindle2}, \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}, \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} --- each
carrying an identical 15-channel set (3 accelerometer, 3 gyroscope, 3
magnetometer, 4 color, 1 temperature, 1 audio-RMS); the 8 electrical
channels are machine-controller signals, not a removable board, and are
retained throughout. For each of the six units we retrain the encoder from
scratch on the 83-channel set with that unit's channels removed, then train
a fresh six-head decoder on the resulting frozen encoder (the same

\subsection{RQ4: Cross-Class Generalization (Leave-One-Class-Out)}
\label{sec:loco}

For each of the nine operation classes (\texttt{adaptive}, \texttt{adaptive150025}, \texttt{face}, \texttt{face150025}, \texttt{pocket}, \texttt{pocket150025}, \texttt{damageadaptive}, \texttt{damageface}, \texttt{damagepocket}), we train the decoder without that class in the training partition and evaluate on its held-out test samples. This stress-tests the decoder's ability to generalize across machining operations rather than memorize per-class shortcuts.

Averaged across all nine LOCO holdouts: token accuracy $0.501 \pm 0.093$ (95\% CI $[0.435, 0.555]$), sequence accuracy $0.001 \pm 0.002$, command accuracy $0.213 \pm 0.191$ (95\% CI $[0.103, 0.353]$; median $0.21$), numeric accuracy $0.152 \pm 0.133$ (95\% CI $[0.068, 0.241]$) (one fold per holdout, fold-1 file split; bootstrap 10{,}000 percentile-method resamples on the nine per-holdout means; \texttt{audit/loco\_bootstrap\_ci.json}). Type and parameter-type accuracy are markedly more robust: type $0.883 \pm 0.061$ (95\% CI $[0.844, 0.922]$) and parameter-type $0.945 \pm 0.036$ (95\% CI $[0.921, 0.968]$) across the nine holdouts. Relative to the in-distribution 5-fold baseline ($0.781 / 0.026 / 0.888 / 0.585$), the LOCO setting incurs a $\sim$$28$~pp drop on token accuracy, a $\sim$$3$~pp drop on sequence accuracy, a $\sim$$67$~pp drop on command accuracy (mean), and a $\sim$$43$~pp drop on numeric accuracy. The degradation is statistically significant on every recoverable metric: a Welch two-sample test between the nine LOCO holdouts and the five in-distribution folds gives command $t=-9.24$, $p<10^{-5}$; token $t=-8.14$, $p<10^{-4}$; numeric $t=-8.68$, $p<10^{-4}$, with the nonparametric Mann--Whitney $U$ confirming each at $p \le 0.0034$ (\texttt{audit/loco\_significance.json}). The cross-class generalization gap is therefore not attributable to fold-level sampling noise.

\paragraph{Per-holdout variance and the \texttt{damagepocket} outlier.} The nine per-holdout command accuracies span $[0.000, 0.678]$ with $\sigma = 0.191$. Eight of the nine holdouts cluster in $[0.000, 0.273]$ ($\sim$ in line with the mode-collapse story below); one holdout, \texttt{damagepocket}, reaches command accuracy $0.678$, token $0.548$, and numeric $0.402$. The structural reason is that the \texttt{damagepocket} test class shares most of its motion vocabulary (predominantly G1 linear cuts with modest G3 finishing arcs) with the \texttt{pocket} and \texttt{pocket150025} variants retained in training; the model transfers its in-distribution pocket-pattern command discrimination to the held-out damaged-pocket samples with only modest degradation. By contrast, \texttt{damageadaptive} (command $0.000$) and \texttt{damageface} (command $0.094$) do not benefit similarly from their parent operations because the adaptive and face motion patterns differ more strongly between damaged and non-damaged variants than the pocket variants do. The lesson is that cross-class generalization is not uniformly absent: it succeeds when the held-out class shares motion vocabulary with retained training classes, and collapses otherwise. The median across the nine holdouts ($0.21$) better reflects the typical cross-class behavior than the mean.

\paragraph{Per-class LOCO command F1.} The collapse spans every command class (\texttt{audit/loco\_per\_class\_9holdouts.json}): per-class F1 is G0 $0.084 \pm 0.136$, G1 $0.243 \pm 0.277$ (modal class; variance from the \texttt{damagepocket} outlier), G2 $0.044 \pm 0.107$, G3 $0.083 \pm 0.134$, G53/M30 $0.000$ ($n\le5$). This is the mode-collapse mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}) under an unseen motion vocabulary: the model defaults to the modal G1. The heads degrade asymmetrically: type and parameter-type survive LOCO ($-9$/$-5$~pp), so the encoder preserves gross instruction structure even for a novel class, while command and numeric require the in-distribution operation pattern. The deployment-realistic claim for an instruction-level monitor is therefore bounded by these LOCO numbers, not the 5-fold headline (the open-vocabulary counterpart of the closed-vocabulary regime, Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}). Figure~\ref{fig:loco_per_holdout} shows the full per-holdout, per-head breakdown.

\subsection{Noise Augmentation}
\label{sec:noise-aug}

Gaussian sensor noise ($\sigma=0.02$) plus feature-dropout and token-masking during training is a clean \emph{negative} result (5-fold, per-row, teacher-forced): token $0.732 \pm 0.011$ and numeric $0.448 \pm 0.010$ are preserved but command drops $\sim$53~pp to $0.331 \pm 0.076$ (parameter-type $0.978$ holds). The regularization pushes the command head toward the modal class (G1) by making the marginal command signal less reliable to the decoder. Not recommended here; it may still help under a wider per-class corpus or a loss-weighting that decouples token- and command-level regularization (future work).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What Is Recoverable, and Why}
\label{sec:disc-recoverability}

Recoverability factorizes into three structural tiers (numbers in Section~\ref{sec:results-headline} and Table~\ref{tab:per_axis}). \emph{Categorical} structure (command and axis identity) is the strongest tier: the sensor pathway determines which class of motion is occurring and which axes are involved independently of file position; the metadata ablation (Section~\ref{sec:abl-positional}) confirms positional features add nothing.

\emph{Motion sign} is recovered with a clean axis asymmetry: Y near-perfect, X and Z lower. On this geometry the result suggests the $\pm X$ and $\pm Z$ sensor signatures are less separable than $\pm Y$, plausibly because of sensor-board layout relative to the workpiece origin or X-symmetric workpiece geometry; a workpiece-rotation experiment would isolate the cause.

\emph{Numeric precision} is the limiting tier, and the limit is information-theoretic. Loss concentrates at the middle (fine-value) digit positions (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}), and a 64-second window observes only relative motion, not absolute machine coordinates: recovering the latter requires positional context (deliberately withheld) or longer-range autoregressive context (which the per-row formulation does not provide). The structural-vs-arithmetic split is also visible in the failure modes: the worst-edit-distance failures are dominated by command-identity confusion (dropped/swapped/hallucinated G-commands), with pure value-only errors essentially absent (Section~\ref{sec:failure-modes}). This is the within-window row-identification ambiguity (Section~\ref{sec:per-row}); full-window targeting supplies the previous row's command as autoregressive context and removes it (Section~\ref{sec:abl-target-mode}). The deeper question --- whether the categorical tier is genuine sensor recovery or the encoder's class prior --- is the subject of the next subsection.\label{sec:disc-failure-mode}

\subsection{Is Categorical Recovery Sensor-Driven or Operation-Class Structure?}
\label{sec:disc-confound}

The frozen encoder was trained for nine-class operation classification on the \emph{same} file-level fold partitions used here (Appendix~\ref{app:encoder-spec}); its memory is strongly low-rank and linearly separates the operation classes (Section~\ref{sec:encoder-probe}). A direct concern (and one a careful reader should press) is therefore whether the headline command accuracy is genuine per-instruction sensor recovery or operation-class structure re-expressed at the command level. We quantify this with a class-prior control (\texttt{audit/confound\_class\_prior.json}; \texttt{scripts/analysis/confound\_class\_prior.py}) that, at every COMMAND-token position of the full-window predictions, compares the decoder against (i) a \emph{class-prior} predictor that emits the modal command of the window's operation class (modal computed on the fold's training split) and (ii) a within-window modal predictor.

The result is a partial vindication with an explicit caveat. The decoder's command accuracy at command positions is $0.795 \pm 0.121$ (5-fold; the spread is dominated by the fold-1 covariate-shift outlier, Section~\ref{sec:lim-covariate}), versus a class-prior baseline of $0.572 \pm 0.004$ and a within-window-modal baseline of $0.587 \pm 0.003$. (The $0.795$ here is command accuracy evaluated only at COMMAND-token positions of the full-window predictions, a different position subset and aggregation than the $0.8875$ head accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:headline}; the two are consistent measurements of the same predictions, not a discrepancy, exactly as for the per-file mean in Section~\ref{sec:lim-per-file}.) Two things follow. First, a majority of the headline command signal --- roughly $0.57$ of the $0.79$ --- \emph{is} attributable to operation-class structure the encoder already encodes; the absolute command numbers must be read in that light, and this is the representational counterpart of the leave-one-class-out collapse (Section~\ref{sec:loco}). Second, the decoder nevertheless adds $\sim\!22$~pp of genuine, sensor-conditioned, within-class command discrimination above the class prior, and it does so on the $97.4\% \pm 1.4\%$ of windows that contain more than one distinct command (its accuracy on those command-heterogeneous windows, $0.794 \pm 0.122$, is indistinguishable from its pooled accuracy --- it is not merely echoing a per-window stereotype). The honest statement is therefore neither ``the decoder recovers commands from sensors'' nor ``the decoder only re-reads the class label'': it recovers a real but bounded increment of per-instruction command information on top of a large operation-class prior, and that increment, not the headline number, is the magnitude that survives a change of operation class.

\subsection{Sensor-Modality Importance and Deployment Implications}
\label{sec:disc-modality}

\textbf{The deployment-relevant takeaway is that a cost-reduced production monitor is viable: drop \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}, retain \texttt{spindle2}.} The reasoning, summarized in three steps, follows.

First, the inference-time modality ablation establishes joint necessity, not a deletability ranking: zeroing any single modality drops command accuracy by a uniform $40$--$44$~pp with a non-significant between-modality ANOVA (Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}), while type and parameter-type stay robust ($\le\!3$~pp). The encoder's learned gating routes command-discrimination signal through all modalities jointly; under the frozen encoder no single modality is individually omittable, so on that test alone the defensible claim is that the \emph{full} multi-modal stack is required for the categorical-recovery story reported here~\cite{kimmell2025cnc,nist2023sp80082,nist2024fmnet}.

Second, the encoder-retraining complement (Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}) is the deployment-relevant test: which modalities can a deployed system actually omit? With the encoder retrained from scratch, no single sensing modality is individually load-bearing (paired $\Delta$ command $\le\!0.013$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F\!=\!0.91$, $p\!=\!0.499$). The $40$--$44$~pp frozen-encoder collapse is therefore a gating artifact rather than a statement of modality information content; the multi-modal stack carries highly redundant command-discrimination signal along the channel-type axis.

Third, the orthogonal axis --- which physical \emph{sensor unit} a deployment could ship without --- is addressed by the companion leave-one-sensor-out study (Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo-sensor}): removing any single sensor unit and retraining costs $\le\!2.6$~pp on command (largest: \texttt{spindle2} $\Delta\!=\!-0.026$; smallest: \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} $\Delta\!\approx\!0$), with no removal reaching Holm-corrected significance ($p_{\text{Holm}}\!\ge\!0.064$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F\!=\!1.04$, $p\!=\!0.415$). Across both axes, any single sensing modality can be dropped at $\le\!1.3$~pp expected cost and any single physical sensor board at $\le\!2.6$~pp. The strongest single recommendation is to drop \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}, which is fully redundant with its spatial neighbor \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3} at zero observed effect ($p_{\text{Holm}}\!=\!0.975$); \texttt{spindle2} is the unit a deployment should be most cautious about omitting (largest-magnitude effect, also the most workpiece-variable).

\subsection{Comparison with Side-Channel Recovery in Additive Manufacturing}
\label{sec:disc-comparison-am}

Additive-manufacturing side-channels~\cite{alfaruque2016acoustic,song2016my,chhetri2016kcad,jamarani2024screg,chattopadhyay2025video} reconstruct full print geometry; we match their categorical fidelity but not their geometric fidelity. The gap is expected: the AM literature samples raw acoustics at kHz--MHz, whereas the present 4-Hz stack is deliberately low-rate (Section~\ref{sec:related-sidechan}, Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}). The comparison bounds what the low-rate stack forgoes, not a deficiency of the method.

\subsection{When to Use This Decoder vs.\ Alternative Approaches}
\label{sec:disc-when-to-use}

The decoder is best suited to one of three deployment patterns:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Process verification.} Run the decoder in parallel with the controller and flag discrepancies between commanded G-code and the per-row prediction. Command-level disagreements (e.g.\ G0 commanded, G1 predicted; or X commanded, Y predicted) are high-precision alerts.
\item \textbf{Post-hoc forensic analysis.} On suspect runs, the decoded G-code provides an auditable record independent of the controller log. Numeric-value precision is too low for forensic reconstruction of coordinates, but command-level analysis is reliable.
\item \textbf{Anomaly localization.} Operations where command-level predictions deviate from the controller's commanded program point to specific G-code lines worth investigating, complementing session-level anomaly classifiers~\cite{coelho2024anomaly,kimmell2025cnc}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Deployment-time target mode: prefer per-row.} In autoregressive deployment the per-row target mode (each window predicts the single G-code line that fired within it) is preferable to the full-window mode used for training-time analysis. Per-row predictions are short (5--15 tokens), independent across rows, and consequently robust to the autoregressive mode-collapse that affects full-window long-sequence generation (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}). Categorical-head accuracy --- the primary signal for the three deployment patterns above --- is unaffected by target mode because the categorical heads are independent classifiers on the encoder memory rather than autoregressive token predictors. The full-window mode remains the natural training-time configuration for studying within-window row-disambiguation, but it should not be used at deployment time when the goal is reliable per-instruction recovery.

Conversely, the decoder is \emph{not} a stand-alone replacement for the controller log (no exact-line reproduction) and is not yet suitable for sub-millimeter toolpath verification (numeric precision is insufficient). The combination of the present decoder with the encoder paper's operation classifier provides a layered verification stack: the encoder flags session-level operation-class anomalies, and the decoder localizes them to specific G-code lines.

\paragraph{Differentiation from file-level program audit and controller attestation.} The cheap orthogonal defenses are a cryptographic hash / signature on the G-code file (e.g.,~\cite{wells2014cyber,nist2024fmnet}) and TCB attestation on the controller (e.g., secure boot / remote attestation for PLCs~\cite{zonouz2014plc}). Both are necessary first lines and dominate the sensor decoder on the attack classes they cover: program-level substitution before execution (file hash) and firmware-substitution-at-load (controller attestation). The sensor decoder is physics-grounded and observes what the machine \emph{did}, not what the controller claims; it catches threat classes orthogonal to those defenses --- (i)~a controller that loads the signed program but executes a substituted one (a runtime substitution that survives the file hash and beats attestation that does not extend to runtime memory); (ii)~selective skip, duplicate, or stall of executed lines while the controller log reports nominal execution; (iii)~operations on the wrong workpiece, fixturing, or tool where the program is unchanged but the physics is not what was commanded. The honest residual after a file-hash plus attestation deployment is therefore narrow: of the five attack classes formalized below, the sensor decoder adds material detection capability on (i)~runtime substitution, (ii)~skip/duplicate, and (iii)~wrong-workpiece-or-fixturing --- and within those, only on the in-distribution command/axis/sign substitutions characterized in Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}. A file-hash audit plus controller attestation plus the sensor decoder, run in parallel, is the defense-in-depth stack proposed here; the sensor line's contribution is scoped to that envelope.

\subsection{Threat Model and Cybersecurity Positioning}
\label{sec:disc-threat-model}

The trust boundary and the five attack classes are formalized in Section~\ref{sec:problem-threat-model}; this section provides the per-attack-class detectability characterization, the operating-point analysis, and the sensor-side adversary discussion. We follow the manufacturing-cybersecurity literature~\cite{sturm2014cyber,yampolskiy2018security,kimmell2025cnc,zonouz2014plc,goh2017dataset,nist2023sp80082} throughout.

\paragraph{Sensor-side adversary (out of scope, enumerated).} A sensor-resident or sensor-bus adversary can defeat the monitor by attacking the sensor path before the decoder. We list the four classes a deployment must close operationally and which the present detector explicitly does \emph{not} address: \emph{(i) wire-tap / line-fault injection} on shared sensor cabling (mitigation: physically secured wiring, dedicated acquisition lines, twisted-pair / differential signaling, shielded conduit); \emph{(ii) EMI / electromagnetic injection} of falsified sensor signal into accelerometer/magnetometer/audio inputs while cutting is active~\cite{nist2023sp80082} (mitigation: EMI shielding, sensor-cable routing away from controller power lines, cross-modality consistency checks); \emph{(iii) packet replay or substitution} on the telemetry path between sensor boards and the verification host (mitigation: authenticated sensor streams with per-packet nonces or HMAC, integrity-protected acquisition pipeline; cf.\ IEC~62443-3-3 SR~3.1 Communication Integrity~\cite{iec62443-3-3}); \emph{(iv) sensor calibration drift / spoofing-via-stimulus} where a co-located adversary couples a calibrated stimulus into the machine frame that mimics the signature of an honest cut (mitigation: periodic offline sensor calibration audit, multi-board cross-checks). None of these is benchmarked in this paper. A deployment that does not enforce these operational mitigations should not invoke the ``physics-of-record'' framing for the sensor decoder's outputs.

\paragraph{Scope boundary for the upstream adversary.} The decoder's value against the upstream CAM/post-processor adversary is fundamentally limited by the closed-vocabulary regime characterized in Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}. If the substituted toolpath stays within the in-vocabulary G-code-line distribution the model was trained on, the decoder's prediction will agree with the (already-malicious) controller stream and the tamper is invisible to it; the decoder catches an upstream substitution only when the malicious toolpath drives the machine into a physically distinguishable, in-distribution-recognizable regime. This is a material blind spot and we state it plainly: the present system is a controller-side-substitution and execution-integrity monitor, not an upstream-design-integrity monitor. Detecting upstream CAM compromise requires either a trusted design-to-file provenance chain (orthogonal to this work) or open-vocabulary recovery strong enough to flag an unfamiliar-but-valid toolpath --- which the LOCO results (Section~\ref{sec:loco}) show the present model does not have.

\paragraph{Detection capability by attack class.} Based on the per-field recoverability characterization reported in this paper:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Command-identity substitution} (G0 $\to$ G1, G2 $\to$ G3): \emph{detectable.} Command-accuracy at $0.8875 \pm 0.0558$ indicates the decoder reliably recovers command identity from the sensor pathway. A substituted command produces a controller-log vs.\ decoder disagreement at the row level.
\item \textbf{Axis-identity substitution} (X $\to$ Y): \emph{detectable.} Parameter-type accuracy at $0.9931 \pm 0.0036$ and decoder per-axis presence in the $0.96$--$1.00$ range (Table~\ref{tab:per_axis}) support detection of axis swaps.
\item \textbf{Motion-sign substitution} (X+ $\to$ X-): \emph{partially detectable, with a prior-vs-sensor asymmetry that matters.} Per-axis sign accuracies (Table~\ref{tab:per_axis}, 5-fold mean) are Y $0.997$, X $0.942$, Z $0.938$; the corresponding per-axis sign source entropies are $H_{Y}^{\text{sign}} = 0.08$~bits ($99\%$ positive --- prior-deterministic), $H_{X}^{\text{sign}} = 0.38$~bits ($93\%$ positive), $H_{Z}^{\text{sign}} = 1.04$~bits ($44\%$ positive / $56\%$ negative --- a genuine binary classification; Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}). Y-sign ``detection'' is therefore essentially prior mismatch rather than sensor evidence and would fail on a workpiece of different orientation or origin; only Z-sign detection carries non-trivial sensor signal.
\item \textbf{Numeric-value tampering} (X1.5 $\to$ X1.6): \emph{not detectable at fine precision by an information-theoretic bound, not a tuning failure.} Per-axis slot-5 ($10^{-4}$~inch) source entropy on adaptive HSM toolpaths is $\approx 3.3$~bits, indistinguishable from uniform random over $\{0,\ldots,9\}$ (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}, Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_accuracy_overlay}); no decoder consuming this sensor stack can recover the $10^{-4}$-inch digit substantially above the bucket prior. Coarse-resolution tampering on the integer or tenths place \emph{may} be detectable in-distribution if the perturbed value falls outside the per-operation-class value range observed in training (e.g., outside the four X-overrun coordinates that dominate face operations); fine-precision substitutions within the within-window value range will produce decoder predictions consistent with the substituted value.
\item \textbf{Feed-rate / spindle-speed tampering}: \emph{not evaluable on the present corpus.} Evaluating these fields would require a corpus that systematically varies feed rate and spindle speed, which the present data does not.
\item \textbf{Replay}: \emph{detectable indirectly (argued, not benchmarked).} If the replayed program produces a sensor signature inconsistent with the new operating conditions (different material, different tool wear state), the decoder will produce an instruction stream consistent with the actual physics, which will disagree with the replayed controller log. We state this as a mechanism, not a measured result.
\item \textbf{Skip/duplicate at per-row granularity}: \emph{not directly addressed by the current decoder.} Per-row prediction in this paper assumes one row per query; detecting omitted rows requires temporal alignment between sensor windows and controller log, which is operationally feasible but is not benchmarked here.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{First-pass tamper-injection operating points.} To quantify the tamper-detection guarantee implied by the per-field recoverability characterization, we ran a synthetic tamper-injection audit on the 5-fold full-window predictions under autoregressive deployment-true decoding with the FSM grammar layer enabled (Section~\ref{sec:grammar}). For each held-out sample we (i) form the honest pair $(g_{\text{true}}, g_{\text{pred}})$ and (ii) mutate $g_{\text{true}}$ in one of three ways --- command swap, motion-sign flip on the first signed axis, and feed-rate $\pm 10\times$ edit --- to form a tampered pair. The detector flags a tamper when the relevant token-class (command, sign, F-value) in $g_{\text{pred}}$ differs from the claimed value, with thresholds chosen to match the field's own confidence profile (exact match for command and sign, $>50\%$ relative deviation for F). The operating points below are computed per attack class on the 5-fold-pooled applicable test samples ($n\!=\!483$ command-swap, $549$ sign-flip, $111$ feed-edit), reported separately for the baseline (no positional metadata) and the with-positional-metadata configurations (\texttt{audit/threat\_model\_tamper\_AR\_5fold\_FSM.json} and \texttt{audit/threat\_model\_tamper\_AR\_5fold\_FSM\_shortcuts.json}):
\begin{itemize}
\item Command-swap (e.g., G0$\to$G1): \textbf{baseline} FPR $0.299$, TPR $0.921$; \textbf{with metadata} FPR $0.222$, TPR $0.948$.
\item Sign-flip (e.g., X$+ \to$ X$-$): \textbf{baseline} FPR $0.350$, TPR $0.703$; \textbf{with metadata} FPR $0.244$, TPR $0.798$.
\item Feed-rate $\pm 10\times$ edit: \textbf{baseline} FPR $0.0055$, TPR $0.216$; \textbf{with metadata} FPR $0.0146$, TPR $0.532$.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:tamper_auc} summarizes the ROC-AUC and the threshold-matched operating point per attack class, separately under the in-distribution closed-vocabulary regime and the open-vocabulary leave-one-class-out (LOCO) regime. \paragraph{False-alarm cost.} The baseline command-swap operating point is TPR $0.92$ at FPR $0.30$ (with-metadata: TPR $0.95$ at FPR $0.22$); the sign-flip operating point is TPR $0.70$/$0.80$ at FPR $0.35$/$0.24$. On a production CNC executing tens of thousands of G-code lines per shift, an FPR in the $0.2$--$0.35$ range translates to thousands of nuisance alerts per operator per day and is not viable for a row-level verification monitor without further work. Three candidate mitigations are evaluated below; none yields a row-level operating point that survives the measured-autocorrelation, prior-driven, and open-vocabulary qualifiers established in the next two paragraphs: \emph{(a) aggregation} over a sliding window of $K$ adjacent rows; \emph{(b) operating-point selection at a fixed low FPR (e.g., $0.01$)} on the ROC operating points (Figure~\ref{fig:tamperroc}), which buys a lower (but still not validated) false-alarm budget at a much lower TPR; \emph{(c) temperature-scaled calibration} (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) of the command head reduces the deployment-relevant cross-fold ECE from $0.050$ to $0.020$ (the fold-1-outlier $0.122 \to 0.029$ is not the deployment figure; Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) and tightens the high-confidence threshold band, which is the input the detector consumes. The operating points reported above are the threshold-matched first-pass numbers without these enhancements.

\paragraph{Aggregation (remedy a), in brief.} A Monte-Carlo $K$-row aggregation (any/all/majority-of-K, $K\!\in\!\{1,3,5,10,20,50\}$; \texttt{audit/tamper\_aggregation\_sweep.json}) shows the majority-of-$K$ rule produces an optimistic command-swap point that holds \emph{only} under the (false) row-independence assumption (with-metadata $K\!=\!10$: TPR $1.00$, FPR $\sim\!0.04$); sign-flip benefits weakly and feed-rate not at all. The next paragraph replaces this row-independent best-case figure with the empirically measured-$\rho$ number, which is the deployment-relevant operating point. The full $(K, \text{rule})$ curves and the ROC-space view are in Appendix~\ref{app:diag} (Figures~\ref{fig:tamperagg}, \ref{fig:tamperroc}).

\paragraph{Measured autocorrelation, a sensor-vs-prior control, and the open-vocabulary operating point.} The three results in this paragraph materially qualify the windowed-monitoring claim and we state them as the security-relevant numbers. \emph{(1)~Measured autocorrelation.} Rather than assume $\rho$, we measured the within-source-file lag-1 autocorrelation of the honest command-swap alert on the real 5-fold AR+FSM predictions (\texttt{scripts/analysis/tamper\_alert\_autocorrelation.py}; \texttt{audit/tamper\_alert\_autocorrelation.json}): $\rho_{\text{honest}} = 0.42 \pm 0.22$, i.e.\ between the two assumed levels and closer to the pessimistic one. Re-running the correlated aggregation at the measured $\rho$ (\texttt{audit/tamper\_aggregation\_correlated.json}, \texttt{rho\_empirical}) gives, for command-swap at $K=10$ majority, with-metadata FPR $0.098$ at TPR $0.97$ and baseline FPR $0.184$ at TPR $0.94$. The deployable-operating-point claim therefore holds only in the weak sense ``$\sim\!0.10$ FPR with metadata at the measured autocorrelation,'' not the $0.04$ best case; the honest headline figure is FPR $\approx 0.10$--$0.18$ at TPR $\approx 0.94$--$0.97$. \emph{(2)~The alert is largely prior-driven.} Substituting the corpus-modal predicted string (a sensor-\emph{independent} prediction) for the decoder output and re-scoring the command-swap detector yields TPR $0.84 \pm 0.02$ at FPR $0.39 \pm 0.02$, versus the real decoder's TPR $0.92 \pm 0.04$ at FPR $0.30 \pm 0.07$. The sensor-conditioned decoder adds only $\sim\!8$~pp of TPR and $\sim\!9$~pp of FPR-reduction over a predictor that ignores the sensors entirely: most of the row-level command-swap detectability is a property of the closed-vocabulary command \emph{prior}, not of per-instruction sensor recovery (the concern raised about a frozen class-trained encoder, Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}). \emph{(3)~Open-vocabulary collapse.} Re-running the full tamper-injection on the nine leave-one-class-out checkpoints under deployment-true AR (\texttt{scripts/analysis/threat\_model\_tamper\_injection.py --loco}; \texttt{audit/threat\_model\_tamper\_loco.json}) gives command-swap FPR $0.52$ at TPR $0.85$, sign-flip FPR $0.75$ at TPR $0.14$, and feed-edit TPR $0.00$. Against an adversary not confined to trained toolpaths the command-swap detector raises a false alarm on more than half of honest windows and the sign/feed detectors are worse than useless. The security-relevant operating point is therefore the open-vocabulary one (command-swap FPR $0.52$/TPR $0.85$), with the in-distribution $K$-aggregated figures as an optimistic in-envelope ceiling.

The feed-rate detector's low FPR ($0.005$--$0.015$) is an \emph{in-distribution} artifact and does not translate to a usable detector: its TPR is only $0.22$--$0.53$ in-distribution and $0.00$ under the open-vocabulary LOCO regime --- and the feed rate is also the canonical adaptive-adversary bypass field (below). Closing this would require recovering the feed-rate field, which the present 4-Hz sensor stack and corpus do not support; it is not a current capability.

\paragraph{Adaptive adversary (a structural bypass, not a caveat).} The detector's blind spots are not incidental --- they define a complete bypass for any adversary who knows the scheme. The recoverable fields are command identity, axis presence, and motion sign; the \emph{un}-recoverable fields are numeric coordinate value and feed rate (Section~\ref{sec:per-axis}; sign-flip and feed-edit detectors are at or below chance, AUC $\sim\!0.5$). An adversary who keeps the command/axis/sign envelope fixed and corrupts only coordinates or feed rate is therefore \emph{invisible by construction}, and no $K$-row aggregation helps because the per-row signal on those fields is already at chance. This is exactly the high-value attack class the Introduction motivates --- wrong depth of cut, wrong feed, a scrapped part or damaged tooling --- so the residual security value against a non-naive adversary is small: the monitor meaningfully constrains only command/axis/sign substitutions that also stay within the trained toolpath envelope. We state this as a structural limitation, not a tunable parameter.

\paragraph{Limitations of the verification.} The decoder cannot detect attacks whose physical effect is indistinguishable from a benign program at the 4-Hz sample rate; sub-second motion details (rapid clearance moves, small finishing passes) may be temporally aliased. Mitigations (higher sample rate, additional sensor modalities, encoder retraining with row-level supervision) are open directions (Section~\ref{sec:future}); none closes the adaptive-adversary bypass above, which structurally requires recovering the numeric/feed fields the present sensor stack does not carry. The float-epsilon target-encoding artifact of Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding} is closed by the patch on the development branch; an adversary aware of the historical encoding could in principle craft ``corrected'' G-code to trigger false alarms on a monitor trained on buggy targets, and the patch closes this narrow detection-mismatch path for future deployments.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\subsection{Dataset Limitations}
\label{sec:lim-dataset}

\paragraph{Continuous-parameter coverage.} The most consequential dataset limitation is the near-absence of variation in feed rate (F), spindle speed (S), and arc parameters (I/J). The data-coverage table (Table~\ref{tab:data_coverage}) reports each field's positive support: F appears in $<\!1\%$ of per-row samples; S is essentially absent from both modes; I and J never appear (arcs use R notation only). The accuracy numbers for these fields in Table~\ref{tab:per_axis} are therefore artifacts of their near-zero baseline support, not evidence of sensor-driven recovery. Addressing these gaps would require a designed factorial data-collection campaign that systematically varies feed rate, spindle speed, depth of cut, and material at a higher sampling rate (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\paragraph{Material variety.} All recorded runs use a single workpiece material on a single CNC platform (a Bantam Tools desktop mill). Material-class generalization cannot be tested on the present corpus; doing so would require a multi-material corpus (e.g., aluminum, steel, and Delrin).

\paragraph{Label set scale.} The corpus is dominated by a small set of frequently-recurring G-code lines (G1 strongly dominant) with a long low-frequency tail --- per fold, of order $10^3$ distinct lines but only a handful of distinct motion-command classes (Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset} reports the exact per-fold distinct-line counts and the train/test overlap). Per-class metrics are macro-averaged over that tail; results for G3, M30, and arc parameters would benefit from substantially more diverse data.

\paragraph{Verbatim train/test line overlap (closed-vocabulary regime).} The file-level 5-fold splits guarantee file-level disjointness between train and test, but the coverage-repair procedure (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage-repair}) was designed to ensure that every G-code line in the test partition has been seen during training of that fold. The resulting overlap, measured at the line level (\texttt{audit/train\_test\_gcode\_overlap.json}), is high in both modes: per\_row mode has $97.6\%$ of distinct test lines and $99.0\%$ of test line-instances appearing verbatim in train (averaged across the 5 folds); full\_window mode has $97.9\%$ and $99.1\%$ at the line level. This is by design --- the 5-fold experiments evaluate the model's ability to map a sensor signal to one of the in-vocabulary G-code lines (a closed-vocabulary regime), not its ability to invent novel lines unseen during training. The genuinely open-vocabulary generalization test is the leave-one-operation-class-out (LOCO) protocol (Section~\ref{sec:loco}); LOCO command accuracy degrades from $0.888$ on the in-distribution 5-fold to a 9-holdout mean of $0.213 \pm 0.191$ (median $0.21$), with eight of the nine holdouts in $[0.000, 0.273]$ and one outlier (\texttt{damagepocket}) at $0.678$ where the held-out class shares motion vocabulary with retained classes. LOCO numeric accuracy collapses from $0.585$ to a 9-holdout mean of $0.152$. The headline accuracy numbers should therefore be interpreted as a closed-vocabulary classification result; the LOCO numbers are the appropriate evidence for cross-operation-class generalization.

\subsection{Methodological Limitations}
\label{sec:lim-method}

\paragraph{Frozen-encoder representation.} The encoder was trained on the same fold splits as the decoder, and its training objective (nine-class operation classification) does not include row-level G-code supervision. Both factors may limit the encoder's preservation of fine-grained per-row sensor structure. Retraining the encoder with row-level supervision and re-running the headline 5-fold would quantify the residual encoder-side contribution; this is the principal open direction (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\paragraph{Sampling rate.} Sensor data is recorded at 4~Hz. This is not a marginal concern: measuring the actual G-code row execution durations across the 120-file aligned corpus (\texttt{scripts/analysis/gcode\_row\_durations.py}; \texttt{audit/gcode\_row\_durations.json}; $21{,}541$ row-execution instances) shows the \emph{median} row executes in a single $0.25$~s sample, and \textbf{$77.7\%$ of row-execution instances occupy $\le\!1$ sample period ($\le\!0.25$~s) --- unresolvable as a distinct transient at 4~Hz --- with $83.1\%$ $\le\!0.5$~s and $86.3\%$ $\le\!1$~s}. The majority of individual G-code instructions are therefore sub-sample events that the 4~Hz stack cannot resolve as distinct transients; what \emph{is} observable is the lower-frequency cutting-regime envelope, which is precisely why categorical/regime-level structure is recoverable while per-instruction numeric precision is not (the mechanism is physical, not only the ``exposure bias'' the sequence head adds on top). For context, conventional acoustic-emission and vibration tool-condition monitoring samples at $\geq\!10$~kHz~\cite{teti2010advanced}, so the present stack is deliberately at the low-rate, low-bandwidth end of the sensing-cost spectrum and the recoverability characterization should be read in that light. We stress that 4~Hz is \emph{not} the sole numeric-recovery bottleneck: the per-row target formulation (Section~\ref{sec:per-row}), which maps a 64-second window containing many G-code rows onto a single row's tokens, imposes a within-window row-identification ambiguity that is co-responsible for the numeric ceiling independently of sample rate (the per-digit and output-position analyses in Sections~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition} and~\ref{sec:output-position-failure} localize both effects). A higher-rate sensor stack (outside the scope of the present corpus) would address the aliasing component; the autoregressive sequence head addresses the per-row component.

\paragraph{Window length.} The 256-sample window length was inherited from the encoder paper's classification configuration~\cite{encoder_paper} to ensure consistent encoder inputs. A full window/stride sweep requires retraining the encoder for each $(\text{window}, \text{stride})$ pair and is therefore not separable on the present frozen encoder; it remains an open question tied to the encoder-retraining direction (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\subsection{Statistical Power Limitations}
\label{sec:lim-power}

Per-class metrics for the command head are computed on small per-class supports in the test split: G0 ($n\!=\!86$), G1 ($n\!=\!1{,}095$), G2 ($n\!=\!475$), G3 ($n\!=\!395$), none ($n\!=\!5{,}886$). A power analysis (\texttt{audit/extended\_rigor\_audits.json}, key \texttt{rec6\_power\_analysis}) computes the minimum detectable effect (MDE) at $\alpha\!=\!0.05$, $\beta\!=\!0.20$ for each class:

\begin{itemize}
\item G0 ($n\!=\!86$, baseline $\sim$0.40): MDE $\approx 18.8$~pp. Differences below this size are not statistically distinguishable from sampling noise.
\item G1 ($n\!=\!1{,}095$, baseline $\sim$0.94): MDE $\approx 2.3$~pp.
\item G2 ($n\!=\!475$, baseline $\sim$0.50): MDE $\approx 8.0$~pp.
\item G3 ($n\!=\!395$, baseline $\sim$0.30): MDE $\approx 8.4$~pp.
\item none ($n\!=\!5{,}886$, baseline $\sim$0.95): MDE $\approx 0.9$~pp.
\item param-types X, Y, Z, R: MDE $\le 1.5$~pp (large supports).
\end{itemize}

\noindent ``Not significant'' on the long-tail classes (G0, G3) often means \emph{underpowered}, not \emph{equivalent}. Resolving these per-class differences would require substantially larger per-class supports than the present corpus provides.

\paragraph{Covariate-shift audit.}\label{sec:lim-covariate} A per-field train-vs-test positive-support audit (\texttt{audit/extended\_rigor\_audits.json}, keys \texttt{rec7\_covariate\_shift\_*}) flags only modest shifts ($<\!6$~pp) on already-low-support fields: per-row fold-1 Z $-4.4$~pp; full-window fold-1 F $+3.2$~pp and fold-5 F $-5.3$~pp; no flagged fields on the remaining folds. This contributes to the cross-fold standard deviation in Table~\ref{tab:per_axis} but does not change the per-axis qualitative ranking.

\subsection{Per-File Performance Variance}
\label{sec:lim-per-file}

Slicing the 5-fold predictions by source file ($n=91$) shows command accuracy is concentrated, not heavy-tailed ($0.953 \pm 0.088$; 10th percentile still $0.846$), so the categorical-head success is not concentrated on a few memorizable files; AR token accuracy shows the expected wide per-file spread tracking the per-operation-class breakdown (Section~\ref{sec:per-op-class}). The full per-file distribution, and the reconciliation of the unweighted per-file mean ($0.953$) against the per-fold-mean headline ($0.8875 \pm 0.0558$, an aggregation difference, not a prediction discrepancy), are in Appendix~\ref{app:perfile}.

\subsection{Evaluation Limitations}
\label{sec:lim-eval}

\paragraph{Single-run per condition.} Each operation-class file represents a single execution. The metrics in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} therefore conflate cross-condition variance (different operation classes) with cross-trial variance (rerunning the same operation). A designed multi-replicate corpus (multiple executions per condition) would support variance decomposition; the present corpus does not.

\paragraph{Decoder-only inference cost.} The decoder generates token-by-token autoregressively under a grammar mask. For long sequences (full-window mode with multi-line targets) this is a substantial inference cost. A beam-search or non-autoregressive variant has not been evaluated; doing so would inform deployment-cost trade-offs (Section~\ref{sec:disc-modality}).

\paragraph{$K$-spectrum methodology confound: resolved.} The four-point vocabulary-cardinality sweep originally compared the headline $K\!=\!2{,}418$ row (SS$=$0.5, dw$=$1.0) against three smaller-$K$ variants under Design~B (SS$=$0, dw$=$0). The smaller-$K$-vs-headline comparison was a joint condition contrast (vocabulary cardinality $+$ training schedule). A methodology-matched $K\!=\!2{,}418$ control trained under SS$=$0/dw$=$0 (T3.1; Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric}) resolves the confound: under matched methodology, $K\!=\!2{,}418$ is the highest-performing variant on every metric (AR struct token $0.470 \pm 0.034$ vs.\ K=335's $0.429 \pm 0.123$ and the headline's $0.317 \pm 0.116$). The earlier $+11.1$~pp K=335 advantage over the headline was the SS/dw schedule change, not vocabulary cardinality; the K=335 ``sweet spot'' framing is retracted in this revision. The deployment-relevant configuration for AR structural recovery is the original $K\!=\!2{,}418$ vocabulary with SS$=$0 and dw$=$0; the smaller-$K$ family is now reported as a clean vocabulary-cardinality contrast within one methodology, with the deployment story carried by the matched-methodology control. Corpus generalization (single Bantam Tools desktop mill, 120 files, nine operation classes) is a separate open question and the K=2418/SS=0/dw=0 result requires cross-platform replication before it can carry forward to other platforms or material classes (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\paragraph{Pooled numeric accuracy averages memorization against an information-theoretic floor.} The pooled slot-5 ($10^{-4}$~inch) accuracy of $0.864$ in Table~\ref{tab:per_digit_position} is the deployment-relevant qualifier of the body's per-regime characterization (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}, Figure~\ref{fig:slot5_by_op_class}): on adaptive HSM rows the per-axis digit is information-theoretically near-unrecoverable, while on facing rows the high accuracy reflects the recall of four geometry-specific X-overrun values that account for $96.5\%$ of face X-coordinates and would not transfer to a different workpiece. Any deployment claim of $10^{-4}$-inch coordinate reconstruction should therefore be read against the per-axis source entropy in Table~\ref{tab:per_digit_position} and the quantization map in Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding}, not the pooled curve. Figure~\ref{fig:per_file_fingerprint} makes the memorization-vs-entropy split directly visible at the per-file level.

\subsection{Future Directions}
\label{sec:roadmap}

Four directions remain open: (a)~broader continuous-parameter coverage (feed rate, spindle speed, depth of cut, material) collected at a higher sampling rate, to populate the F/S/I/J fields the present corpus cannot evaluate; (b)~retraining the encoder with row-level supervision, which would quantify the residual encoder-side contribution and is a prerequisite for (c); (c)~a window/stride re-optimization, which requires the encoder retraining of (b) and is therefore not separable on the present frozen encoder; and (d)~cross-platform validation on a second CNC platform and material class. None of these is addressed by the present work; each is an open problem rather than a scheduled experiment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study characterizes which components of an executing G-code instruction are recoverable from multi-modal CNC sensor data and which are not, using \modelname{} --- a six-head Transformer decoder on a frozen multi-modal denoising encoder, per-row targets, grammar-constrained inference --- under five-fold file-level cross-validation (120 files, nine operation classes). Four findings, each detailed in the section cited:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=*, nosep]
\item \textbf{Categorical structure} (command, axis presence, motion direction) is recovered from sensors alone at $0.89$/$0.99$/$0.99$, TF/AR-invariant by construction (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}); a class-prior control attributes $\sim\!0.57$ of the $0.79$ command accuracy to the encoder's operation-class prior, leaving a genuine $\sim\!22$~pp within-class sensor-driven increment (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}) --- that increment, not the headline, is the robust core.
\item \textbf{End-to-end token/numeric} reproduction is bounded by three coupled mechanisms.

(2a)~\emph{Exposure-bias collapse.} The sequence head collapses to $\sim\!0.026$ sequence exact-match under autoregressive decoding (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}). A no-numeric retraining ablation decomposes the collapse into a $\sim\!9$-pp per-token numeric-desynchronization piece (paired $5/5$, $p\!<\!0.02$) and a dominant intrinsic structural mode-collapse, the latter being the deployment-relevant bottleneck (Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric}). The five-point $K$-spectrum, completed with a methodology-matched $K\!=\!2{,}418$ control (T3.1), shows the matched control is the highest-performing variant on every metric (AR struct token $0.470$; AR struct sequence $+7.1$~pp paired vs.\ headline, Holm-adjusted $p\!=\!0.006$, $g_{z}\!=\!2.76$); the apparent $K\!=\!335$ maximum of the earlier four-point sweep was the SS/dw schedule effect, not vocabulary cardinality.

(2b)~\emph{Physical co-cause.} A measured 4-Hz row-duration analysis shows $77.7\%$ of rows occupy $\le\!1$ sample period, making them temporally unresolvable as distinct transients (Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}).

(2c)~\emph{Information-theoretic floor on fine-precision recovery.} Per-(axis, slot) source-entropy analysis (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}, Pearson $r\!=\!-0.96$ between per-slot entropy and per-slot accuracy) decomposes the digit head's apparent ``bathtub'' into three operation-class regimes: adaptive HSM at the uniform-noise ceiling ($\sim\!3.3$ bits per axis at slot~5); traditional facing driven by geometric coordinate repetition; pocket-style between the two. $10^{-4}$-inch coordinate reconstruction is therefore unsupported on adaptive workloads and geometry-dependent on facing/pocket.
\item \textbf{Sensor-stack redundancy.} Inference-time modality removal collapses command accuracy uniformly under the frozen encoder ($p\!=\!0.998$ between modalities; Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}); encoder-retraining ablations (LOMO and LOSO; Section~\ref{sec:disc-modality}) show no single sensing modality or sensor unit is individually load-bearing ($\Delta\!\le\!2.6$~pp), enabling a cost-reduced production monitor.
\item \textbf{Tamper detection} is largely operation-class-prior-driven ($\approx\!8$~pp sensor-conditioned) and collapses open-vocabulary (leave-one-class-out command-swap FPR $0.52$); the in-distribution $K\!=\!10$ point at the measured $\rho\!=\!0.42$ (FPR $0.10$--$0.18$ at TPR $\approx\!0.94$--$0.97$) is an optimistic in-envelope ceiling, not a validated detector (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}).
\end{enumerate}

Closing the gap to full reconstruction therefore requires attention at the sequence head, not the encoder: the standard exposure-bias remedies~\cite{he2019exposure,ott2018uncertainty,ranzato2016sequence,holtzman2020curious,welleck2020neural} combined with richer continuous-parameter data, a higher sample rate, and row-level encoder pre-training, which remain the principal directions for future work (Section~\ref{sec:future}).

\section{Coverage-Repair Split Procedure}
\label{app:coverage-repair}

File-level stratified $k$-fold splits prevent within-file leakage but do not guarantee \emph{token coverage}: a held-out fold may contain G-code lines that never appear in the corresponding training partner, in which case the decoder is being evaluated on a vocabulary it has not had the opportunity to learn. The coverage-repair procedure described here \emph{constructs a closed-vocabulary evaluation regime} --- it deliberately removes out-of-vocabulary test lines so that the 5-fold experiment measures within-vocabulary classification rather than open-vocabulary generalization --- while preserving the file-level disjointness. This is a design choice, not a bias correction: the resulting headline numbers are within-vocabulary near-retrieval results, and the open-vocabulary capability is reported separately by the leave-one-class-out protocol (Section~\ref{sec:loco}). We make the regime explicit here and quantify the resulting train/test line overlap in Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}.

Given a candidate (train, test) partition for fold $i$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=*]
\item Tokenize the G-code text of every window in both splits and compute $U_{\text{test}}$ and $U_{\text{train}}$, the sets of distinct G-code lines occurring in each.
\item Let $M = U_{\text{test}} \setminus U_{\text{train}}$ be the set of test-only lines.
\item If $M = \emptyset$, accept the partition. Otherwise identify the file $f^\star \in \text{test}$ contributing the largest number of $M$-lines, and the file $g^\star \in \text{train}$ whose removal-and-replacement minimally perturbs the per-class fold proportions. Swap $f^\star$ and $g^\star$.
\item Recompute $M$ and repeat until $M = \emptyset$ or until a swap-budget of $10$ is exhausted (in which case the residual $M$-lines are recorded as a "novel-vocabulary at test" diagnostic but the partition is accepted).
\end{enumerate}

In practice the procedure converges in 0--3 swaps per fold on the present corpus. Recorded fold sizes after coverage repair differ from the stratified target by at most one file per class.

\subsection{Test-Time Sensor-Noise Sensitivity}
\label{app:noise}

To characterize how robust the categorical heads are to deployment-time sensor degradation, we re-evaluate fold 1 of the baseline 5-fold checkpoint while injecting additive Gaussian noise into the frozen-encoder memory at varying $\sigma$ relative to the memory's empirical standard deviation (\texttt{audit/test\_time\_noise\_sensitivity.json}). This is a noise-robustness experiment on the \emph{encoder-to-decoder interface}, not on the \emph{raw sensor inputs}: noise injected at the memory level probes how stable the decoder is to perturbations in its conditioning vector but does not characterize robustness to raw-sensor degradation (the encoder is non-linear and may not propagate input noise to memory noise in a simple way). A raw-sensor-input noise sweep is outside the scope of the present study. The categorical heads tested at the memory level are exceptionally robust: across $\sigma \in [0, 2]$ (i.e., up to twice the magnitude of the signal itself), command-head accuracy varies from $0.78$ to $0.82$, type accuracy stays at $0.97$, and parameter-type accuracy stays at $0.98$ (Figure~\ref{fig:noise}). The slight upward drift on the command head is consistent with random noise acting as a label-smoothing regularizer, pushing predictions toward the modal class (G1) on borderline cases. The sign head is omitted from Figure~\ref{fig:noise} for a methodological reason that does \emph{not} affect the headline. The headline sign accuracy ($0.989$, Table~\ref{tab:headline}) is computed by the robust path that parses the decoded and target G-code text and compares the extracted per-axis signs (\texttt{scripts/analysis/v8\_per\_field\_eval.py}); the noise-sensitivity audit instead re-derived the sign target from a shifted raw target tensor under a mismatched index alignment, which yields an artifactual $0.10$ at $\sigma=0$ rather than the true $\approx\!0.99$. We re-ran the robust path on fold~1 and confirmed it reproduces the per-axis sign accuracies of Table~\ref{tab:per_axis} ($0.88$/$0.98$/$0.91$ for X/Y/Z at fold~1, the documented covariate-shift fold); the defect is confined to the noise-audit script and the headline sign metric is unaffected. We therefore omit the sign curve from the noise figure rather than report a number derived from the defective audit path. The other three heads validate cleanly against their headline fold-1 numbers at $\sigma=0$. The qualitative conclusion is that the categorical-recovery story this paper reports does not degrade gracefully but holds with surprising robustness to encoder-memory degradation; whether this robustness extends to raw-sensor noise on the input side is not addressed here.

\subsection{Per-File Performance Variance}
\label{app:perfile}

A separate concern is whether the model memorizes a small set of files rather than learning the operation-level signal. We slice the 5-fold full-window test predictions by source file and report per-file accuracy (the per-file slice is derived from the released \texttt{full\_window\_5fold} prediction JSONs; $n=91$ files appear in at least one test split across the 5 folds). The cross-file distribution is concentrated, not heavy-tailed: command accuracy is $0.953 \pm 0.088$ across files with range $[0.571, 1.000]$ and 10th--90th percentile range $[0.846, 1.000]$; parameter-type accuracy is $0.991$ across files. This per-file mean ($0.953$) is an \emph{unweighted average over the $91$ files} and is not directly comparable to the $0.8875 \pm 0.0558$ headline command accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:headline}, which is the mean over the five file-level folds: the per-file slice weights every file equally regardless of which fold it fell in, whereas the headline weights every fold equally; the gap reflects this aggregation difference (and the fold-1 command outlier, Section~\ref{sec:lim-covariate}) rather than a discrepancy in the underlying predictions. Token accuracy under autoregressive decoding shows much wider per-file variance ($0.302 \pm 0.283$, range $[0.052, 0.869]$), which is the per-file signature of the same per-operation-class breakdown reported in Section~\ref{sec:per-op-class}: \texttt{face}-class files cluster at the high end and the other classes at the low end. The narrowness of the command-accuracy distribution and the absence of a heavy left tail support the interpretation that the categorical-head success is not concentrated on a small set of memorizable files: at the 10th percentile of files, command accuracy is still $0.846$.

\section{Numeric Target Encoding Artifacts}
\label{app:digit_encoding}

This appendix audits a float-epsilon target-encoding artifact (with committed patch) supporting the per-axis source-entropy claims of Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}; the per-(axis, slot) quantization map itself is Figure~\ref{fig:quantization_heatmap} in the body.

\paragraph{Float-epsilon target-encoding audit and patch.} The historical \texttt{encode\_values\_to\_digits} (\texttt{digit\_value\_head.py}) iteratively multiplied the decimal part by ten and truncated (\texttt{slot[s] = int(dec\_part * 10) \% 10}), which is exact in IEEE 754 only for exactly representable values; e.g., \texttt{1.5680} is stored as $1.567999\ldots$, so the chain yields \texttt{int(5679.99\ldots) \% 10 = 9} at slot~5 rather than the authored $0$. Auditing every $(\text{axis},\text{val})$ pair extracted from \texttt{gcode\_texts} across the 5-fold test corpus ($n\!=\!79{,}563$ slot-5 positions; Table~\ref{tab:float_epsilon_audit}) finds $23.6\%$ of CAM-authored terminal-zero values encoded as slot-5~$=9$ (X $32.8\%$; R $0.0\%$, R being uniformly 3-decimal and FP-clean); the same mechanism produces $d\!\to\!d\!+\!1$ shifts on $26.3\%$ of upstream-decimal positions overall. A round-to-scaled-integer rewrite ($\texttt{scaled} = \texttt{round}(\lvert v \rvert \cdot 10^{4})$ once, then floor-divide per slot) has been committed to \texttt{src/miracle/model/digit\_value\_head.py} on the development branch; the verification harness (\texttt{scripts/analysis/verify\_digit\_encoding\_fix.py}) confirms it produces zero corruptions on the same $79{,}563$ positions ($4.05\%\!\to\!0.000\%$ at slot~5). The patch is \emph{not} active in the released checkpoint that populates the paper's numbers (Tables~\ref{tab:headline}, \ref{tab:per_axis}, \ref{tab:per_digit_position}, and the threat-model operating points). The artifact lives at the model-target level, not at the source distribution, so the per-operation-class regimes, four-X-overrun finding, cutting-depth-step quantization on Z, and arc-radius-vocabulary structure on R from Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition} are unaffected.
