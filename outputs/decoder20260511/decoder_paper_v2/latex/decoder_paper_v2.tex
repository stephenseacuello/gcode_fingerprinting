\Title{Structured Multi-Head Decoding for Per-Field G-Code Recoverability from Multi-Modal Sensor Embeddings on a Desktop CNC Milling Platform: A Single-Platform, Cross-Validated Characterization Study}

\newcommand{\orcidauthorA}{0009-0009-0292-9706}

\Author{Stephen S. Eacuello $^{1,}$*\orcidA{}, Romesh S. Prasad $^{1}$ and Manbir S. Sodhi $^{1}$}

\AuthorNames{Stephen S. Eacuello, Romesh S. Prasad and Manbir S. Sodhi}

\address{%
$^{1}$ \quad Department of Industrial Systems Engineering, University of Rhode Island, Kingston, RI 02881, USA}

\corres{Correspondence: seacuello@uri.edu}

\abstract{
Cyber-physical attacks on CNC milling can alter the G-code instruction a controller executes without altering the file the operator loaded --- a controller-log forgery the file-hash signature cannot detect. Sensor-side reconstruction of the executed instruction would close this gap, yet the recoverable component set under low-rate multi-modal sensing has not been quantitatively characterized. \textbf{First}, we characterize per-field G-code recoverability on a desktop CNC milling platform (Bantam Tools mill, 4~Hz seven-modality sensor stream; TRL~4) using \modelname{}, a six-head Transformer decoder over a frozen multi-modal denoising encoder with grammar-constrained inference, evaluated under five-fold file-level cross-validation. \textbf{Second}, we develop a unified source-entropy theory: each prediction head's accuracy is bounded by the Shannon entropy of its training-corpus distribution, decomposable by primary axis (X, Y, Z) and by toolpath family (face / pocket / adaptive HSM), and corroborated by a from-scratch seq2seq baseline. \textbf{Third}, an audit-leverage corollary follows: sensor-side recovery is most effective on low-entropy operation classes and information-theoretically blind on high-entropy adaptive toolpaths --- complementing, not replacing, file-hash signing. Empirically, categorical structure (command, parameter-type, motion sign) recovers near-ceiling at $\sim\!89\%$ command accuracy, a $+22$~pp within-class increment over the operation-class prior; numeric precision collapses autoregressively below the entropy floor, with $77.7\%$ of G-code blocks occupying $\le\!1$ sample period at the present sensor rate. The intended deployment posture is therefore \textbf{forensic-grade post-hoc audit}, complementing controller attestation rather than a real-time intrusion-detection system. Scope is closed-world over a single platform; cross-platform replication on a production VMC is future work.
}

\keyword{CNC machining; G-code reconstruction; multi-modal sensor fusion; manufacturing cybersecurity; cyber-physical systems; process verification; tamper detection; autoregressive decoding; grammar-constrained inference; forensic audit}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A modern Computer Numerical Control (CNC) milling machine is a tightly choreographed system: motors drive a cutting tool along multi-axis toolpaths specified by a G-code program, with tolerances measured in micrometers. Every cut leaves a multi-modal sensor signature --- vibration through the machine frame, acoustic emission, motor-current draw, ambient temperature and illumination --- that is in principle informative about the instruction stream that produced it. Whether that signature is informative enough to \emph{recover} the instruction stream line by line, without access to the controller, is the engineering question this paper takes up, and the answer carries direct consequences for sensor-side recoverability bounds and for deployment-grade audit of contested runs.

We approach that question on a single desktop CNC milling platform (Bantam Tools desktop mill, machinable-wax workpiece, dry stepper-driven cutting, 4~Hz envelope-extracted sensor stream); cross-platform replication on a production-class VMC is future work (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}) and a precondition for any deployment claim outside this scope. The intended application is \textbf{forensic-grade post-hoc audit} of contested runs: a controller-independent record of executed instructions that complements file-hash signing and controller attestation rather than a real-time intrusion-detection system, reviewed offline against the controller log when a run is flagged. Within that audit posture the per-field characterization additionally supports offline process verification and human-in-the-loop triage of a narrow subset of G-code substitutions limited to command, axis, and motion-sign edits within the trained toolpath envelope in increasingly networked manufacturing environments~\cite{nist2023sp80082,nist2024fmnet,sturm2014cyber,yampolskiy2018security,zhong2017intelligent,kimmell2025cnc}. Real-time autonomous SOC tier-1 alerting is explicitly \emph{not} a supported deployment posture on this stack (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}), and numeric-coordinate and feed-rate substitutions remain undetectable on the present 4-Hz sensor stack by an information-theoretic bound (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}). The audit posture is additionally bound to \emph{in-distribution} inputs --- closed-world over the V8 token vocabulary (Section~\ref{sec:problem-vocab}) and the nine training-time operation classes (Section~\ref{sec:dataset}); novel-command and novel-operation-class inputs fall outside this envelope (LOCO collapse, Section~\ref{sec:loco}) and are out of scope for the present system. The cost envelope a wider sensor stack could in principle catch (scrapped parts, damaged tooling, or worse)~\cite{teti2010advanced,serin2020tool} is therefore framing for future cross-platform replication, not a claim of the present system.

Multi-modal sensor monitoring of CNC machines is a mature subfield, but its targets have been broad. Tool-wear estimation~\cite{teti2010advanced,serin2020tool,zhu2009wavelet,mohanraj2020tool,wang2024multisensor}, operation-type classification~\cite{wang2018deep,zhao2019machine}, and process-monitoring tasks such as surface-quality and anomaly detection~\cite{stathatos2024cnn,coelho2024anomaly} answer session-level questions: \emph{is the tool worn?}, \emph{what kind of operation is running?}, \emph{is the surface within tolerance?} Their target labels are coarse: a single value per recording, or a class per file. The finer question (can the specific G-code instruction executing at this moment be identified from sensors alone?) has been largely absent from the CNC literature, despite its direct implications for tamper detection, intellectual-property protection~\cite{alfaruque2016acoustic,song2016my,chhetri2016kcad,jamarani2024screg,chattopadhyay2025video}, and root-cause analysis on contested runs.

\paragraph{Why CNC milling is harder than the additive-manufacturing analogue.} The closest body of work is side-channel reconstruction of toolpaths in additive manufacturing~\cite{alfaruque2016acoustic,song2016my,chhetri2016kcad}, where Fused Deposition Modeling deposits material one axis at a time in a controlled environment, sensors can be placed freely without risk of damage, and the sensor-to-G-code mapping is comparatively low-noise. CNC milling is a substantially harder signal environment. Simultaneous multi-axis interpolation, nonlinear force-engagement relationships, structural vibration through the machine frame, and high-energy chip ejection corrupt sensor signals and physically constrain where sensors can be mounted~\cite{teti2010advanced,wang2018deep}. The mapping is also many-to-one: many G-code instructions produce broadly similar sensor signatures when their cutting parameters coincide, and the same instruction can be cut at different speeds, depths, or materials.

\paragraph{Building on prior work in our group.} The sensor encoder used in this study is a prior contribution that achieved 96.3\% accuracy on nine-class CNC operation classification using a multi-modal denoising autoencoder over six distributed sensor boards~\cite{encoder_paper}. That work established the encoder's representational competence at \emph{operation-class} granularity. A companion contribution defined a hybrid grammar-based tokenization of G-code suitable for sequence models~\cite{grammar_paper}. The present paper takes the encoder as a fixed feature extractor and the grammar as a vocabulary, then asks what additional information the encoder's per-window latent carries about the row-level instructions that drove the cut.

\paragraph{Three challenges specific to this task.} Direct G-code generation from sensor signals raises issues that off-the-shelf sequence-to-sequence models~\cite{sutskever2014sequence,bahdanau2014neural,vaswani2017attention,graves2013speech} do not address: (i)~a 64-second window straddles tens to hundreds of G-code rows, so a naive window-level target destroys row-level information; (ii)~the numeric vocabulary contains thousands of bucket tokens, most absent from any one training fold; and (iii)~positional metadata (window index, file identifier) is an obvious shortcut that inflates accuracy without proving sensor-driven recovery~\cite{meyes2019ablation,jia2016deep}. The contributions below address each.

\textbf{Research questions.} This study is guided by four research questions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Per-field recoverability (window-aggregated).} Which components of a G-code line (command identity, axis presence, motion direction, numeric value) are recoverable from sensor data alone, and with what fidelity? Because $77.7\%$ of G-code rows occupy $\le\!1$ sample period at the released 4-Hz rate (Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}), this is in practice a \emph{window-aggregated row-modal recovery} claim, not a sub-sample-resolved per-row claim: the supervisory target is per-row but the sensor evidence consumed by the encoder is the 64-second window-level envelope.
\item \textbf{The metadata-versus-sensor split.} Does exposing positional metadata (window index, file identifier) to the decoder improve recovery, and which fields benefit if so?
\item \textbf{Sensor-modality contribution.} Which of seven sensor groups (accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, environmental, color, audio-RMS, electrical) carry the strongest instruction-level signal?
\item \textbf{Cross-class generalization.} Does the decoder generalize to machining operation classes it never saw during training (leave-one-class-out)?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\arabic*)}, leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Per-row supervised target formulation.} Each sensor window is mapped to the single G-code line that fires within it, yielding approximately $\sim$19{,}500 training samples per fold versus $\sim$300 for a window-level alternative, while eliminating within-window label ambiguity.

\item \textbf{\modelname{} architecture.} A Transformer decoder with six structured prediction heads (token, type, motion-command, parameter-type, sign, digit) operating on the frozen latent of a prior multi-modal denoising encoder~\cite{encoder_paper}. Inference is grammar-constrained at the token head so that structurally invalid transitions are masked at runtime, following recent work on grammar-aware decoding~\cite{picard,outlines,xgrammar}.

\item \textbf{Per-axis recoverability characterization.} An evaluation that decomposes G-code into per-axis fields and reports separately for each field whether (a) the axis is detected, (b) the motion direction is correct, and (c) the numeric value is recovered. The corpus supports this analysis on the X, Y, Z, and R fields; F is reported only at thin support, and S, I, J have effectively zero positive support and are not evaluable (Table~\ref{tab:data_coverage}). This separation reveals which fields are sensor-recoverable and which are not.

\item \textbf{Ablation matrix.} Cross-validated ablations of (a) positional-metadata access, (b) per-row vs. full-window target formulation, (c) sequence-classifier prior, (d) vocabulary precision, (e) each of seven sensor modalities, and (f) a no-numeric retraining of the decoder (Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric}) that decomposes the autoregressive mode-collapse into a measurable numeric-desynchronization contribution and a dominant intrinsic structural-stream collapse. Methodology follows ablation-study practice in industrial neural-network research~\cite{meyes2019ablation,jia2016deep}.

\item \textbf{Cross-class generalization study.} A leave-one-class-out study across nine machining operation classes that stress-tests whether the decoder memorizes per-class shortcuts or generalizes to unseen toolpath strategies within the present platform/material envelope; the cross-platform / cross-material generalization question (production-VMC, cast-iron, flood-coolant) is explicitly out of scope and is the principal open direction (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\item \textbf{A unified source-entropy theory of per-field recoverability.} For every prediction head (token, type, command, parameter-type, sign, numeric digit), we report the empirical source entropy from the training corpus and demonstrate that measured accuracy tracks the entropy ceiling across all nine operation classes (Section~\ref{sec:unified-entropy}). The decoder's accuracy pattern is therefore predicted from information theory and sensor bandwidth, not from architectural choices --- a result corroborated by the from-scratch seq2seq baseline reaching the same ceiling at teacher-forced inference.

\item \textbf{From-scratch Transformer seq2seq baseline.} We compare the headline architecture against a from-scratch Transformer seq2seq baseline (Section~\ref{sec:abl-seq2seq-baseline}); the comparison scopes the architectural contribution to autoregressive deployment-mode inference, where the frozen encoder + grammar-constrained decoder + structured heads buy the load-bearing accuracy lift over the baseline.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope statement.} Every quantitative result in this paper is conditional on a single desktop CNC platform (Bantam Tools desktop mill, ER11 collet spindle, stepper drive, no flood coolant), a single workpiece material class (machinable wax / soft polymer), and a single envelope-extracted 4~Hz aggregate sensor stream. Vibration amplitudes, spectral content, motor-current signatures, and acoustic-emission floors all differ materially on a production vertical machining center (VMC: cast-iron base, BT30/BT40 toolholders, servo drives, flood coolant; Section~\ref{sec:dataset}, ``Platform characterization and generalization risk''); whether the categorical-vs-numeric recoverability factorization, the entropy-bound on fine digits, and the autoregressive mode-collapse story transfer to a production VMC is the cross-platform open question of Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}, not a claim the present corpus can settle. The present paper is therefore a single-platform characterization study; cross-platform replication is future work.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work; Section~\ref{sec:problem} formalizes the task; Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the architecture; Section~\ref{sec:setup} details the dataset and protocol; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} addresses RQ1--RQ4 in order; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} interprets the results, with the per-attack-class detectability characterization in Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}; Section~\ref{sec:future} outlines limitations and future directions.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We review four bodies of work that bear on the present study: sensor-based CNC monitoring and modality fusion (Section~\ref{sec:related-cnc}); sequence-to-sequence and grammar-constrained decoding (Section~\ref{sec:related-seq2seq}); side-channel reconstruction of manufacturing instructions (Section~\ref{sec:related-sidechan}); and manufacturing cybersecurity context (Section~\ref{sec:related-security}).

\subsection{CNC Process Monitoring and Multi-Modal Sensor Fusion}
\label{sec:related-cnc}

Sensor-based monitoring of machining processes is a mature subfield. Early work identified force, vibration, acoustic emission, temperature, and motor current as complementary modalities for tool-condition assessment~\cite{teti2010advanced}. Hand-crafted features extracted via Fourier and wavelet transforms became standard practice~\cite{zhu2009wavelet,mohanraj2020tool}. Deep learning enabled end-to-end feature learning from raw signals, reducing reliance on domain-specific feature engineering~\cite{wang2018deep,zhao2019machine}. Convolutional networks applied to vibration spectrograms and raw multi-channel signals achieved strong results on bearing fault detection~\cite{janssens2016convolutional}, surface-roughness classification~\cite{bhandari2021comparative,stathatos2024cnn}, and CNC anomaly detection~\cite{coelho2024anomaly}.

Multi-modal fusion strategies span early, late, and intermediate approaches~\cite{lahat2015multimodal,ramachandram2017deep}. Cross-modal attention has shown particular promise when modalities are complementary rather than redundant~\cite{cao2023crossattention,wang2024multisensor}. A comprehensive review by Tsanousa \etal~\cite{tsanousa2022multisensor} confirms that the field remains dominated by tool-condition and operation-classification tasks. A separate, model-based line --- digital-twin and digital-shadow process verification~\cite{bergs2021digital} --- compares observed machine state against a simulated reference of the \emph{intended} program; it is complementary to, and orthogonal in trust assumptions from, the present work, which reconstructs the executed instruction stream from sensors alone without a reference model or a trusted controller. Motor-current and servo-drive signatures are an established adjacent modality for load and toolpath inference within this tradition~\cite{teti2010advanced,wang2018deep}, and form one of the seven modality groups fused by the encoder used here. Two gaps persist relative to instruction-level recovery. First, target labels in prior work are session-level (tool worn / not worn) or coarse class labels (turning vs.\ milling); per-row instruction targets are largely absent. Second, ablation studies that progressively remove sensor groups to quantify their marginal contribution~\cite{meyes2019ablation,jia2016deep} are themselves uncommon in CNC monitoring, leaving practitioners without quantitative guidance on which sensors carry instruction-level signal as distinct from operation-class signal.

\subsection{Sequence-to-Sequence Modeling and Grammar-Constrained Decoding}
\label{sec:related-seq2seq}

Sequence-to-sequence neural models were established for natural-language translation~\cite{sutskever2014sequence,bahdanau2014neural,vaswani2017attention} and have since been applied across speech~\cite{graves2013speech}, time series~\cite{wen2023transformers}, and robotics~\cite{robotics_s2s}. Long Short-Term Memory networks~\cite{hochreiter1997long} remain widely used for sequential industrial data where streaming inference and causal ordering matter~\cite{malhotra2015lstm}. Denoising autoencoders~\cite{vincent2010stacked} learn representations robust to input corruption --- a property particularly valuable when industrial sensors are subject to mechanical, electromagnetic, and thermal noise.

When the output sequence follows a known grammar, constrained decoding can prune structurally invalid tokens at inference time. Picard~\cite{picard} pioneered incremental parser-guided decoding for text-to-SQL; Synchromesh~\cite{synchromesh} extended the idea to general code generation with a target-side constraint language, and LMQL~\cite{lmql} formalized constrained generation as a query-language abstraction over the model. More recent work has pushed efficient runtime grammar masks closer to zero overhead: Outlines~\cite{outlines} compiles regular expressions and context-free grammars into finite-state automata reused across decoding steps, XGrammar~\cite{xgrammar} achieves near-zero-overhead per-token masking for JSON and code generation, and Geng \etal~\cite{geng2023gcd} demonstrate that grammar-constrained decoding can recover structured outputs from off-the-shelf pretrained models without any task-specific fine-tuning. G-code is amenable to the same treatment: instructions consist of an optional command token followed by address-value pairs subject to first-order ordering constraints~\cite{grammar_paper,gcode_foundational}. Recent work on large-language-model code generation for additive manufacturing~\cite{gllm,image2gcode,chatcnc} addresses a related but distinct task --- generating G-code from natural-language descriptions or images --- and does not consider sensor-conditioned recovery.

\subsection{Side-Channel Reconstruction of Manufacturing Instructions}
\label{sec:related-sidechan}

The closest analog to this work is side-channel reconstruction of toolpaths in additive manufacturing. Al~Faruque \etal~\cite{alfaruque2016acoustic} recovered Fused Deposition Modeling print geometry from acoustic emissions, achieving high agreement with the source G-code. Song \etal~\cite{song2016my} showed that a smartphone microphone alone could recover sufficient information to reproduce printed objects. Chhetri \etal~\cite{chhetri2016kcad} extended the approach to a detection-oriented setting, identifying kinetic anomalies indicative of attacks. More recent work uses optical~\cite{chattopadhyay2025video} and combined acoustic-magnetic~\cite{jamarani2024screg} side-channels to reconstruct full part geometries from 3D printers. Sturm \etal~\cite{sturm2014cyber} and Yampolskiy \etal~\cite{yampolskiy2018security} survey cyber-physical attack taxonomies in additive manufacturing. We note one important methodological distinction: the AM side-channel literature typically uses \emph{raw acoustic emission} signals sampled at kHz--MHz rates, whereas the present work uses an \emph{audio-RMS envelope} scalar feature sampled at $4$~Hz. The audio-RMS feature retains the energy magnitude of the cutting sound but discards the spectral content that drives most prior AM-side-channel methods; the resulting per-instruction recovery is therefore not directly comparable to the AM literature and is more conservative in its sensor demands.

CNC milling poses a substantially harder sensor environment than additive manufacturing for the reasons stated in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: simultaneous multi-axis motion, nonlinear force-engagement, and structural vibration. As a consequence, the additive-manufacturing recovery methods do not transfer directly. Kimmell \etal~\cite{kimmell2025cnc} demonstrated sensor-based detection of control-injection attacks in CNC machining using energy-anomaly thresholds, but their task is binary detection rather than instruction-level reconstruction. To the best of our knowledge, no prior work reports per-row G-code recovery from CNC sensor data with structured-output evaluation.

\paragraph{Information-theoretic framing: lineage and scope.} Information-theoretic quantification of G-code leakage has prior art only in the additive-manufacturing side-channel literature, where Al-Faruque, Chhetri, Faezi, and Canedo compute mutual information between FDM G-code parameters and high-rate (kHz--MHz) acoustic / acousto-magnetic emissions to characterize per-axis bits-of-leakage~\cite{chhetri2017confidentiality,faezi2020oligosnoop}. Our framing differs along three axes. First, the host domain is subtractive CNC milling on a desktop mill rather than FDM 3D printing, with a categorically different sensor envelope (4~Hz multi-modal aggregate vs.~raw acoustic kHz--MHz). Second, the entropy object is not the mutual information between a sensor and a continuous toolpath coordinate, but the per-head empirical source entropy of the CNC training corpus itself, treated as a Fano-style upper bound on the per-position recoverability of each of six structured prediction heads (token, type, command, parameter-type, sign, numeric digit) --- a port of the classical sequence-prediction entropy ceiling~\cite{brown1992englishentropy,feder1994relations,cover_thomas} to the G-code instruction stream. Third, we decompose that ceiling by toolpath family (face / pocket / adaptive HSM) and show that the decomposition yields a deployment-leverage corollary: sensor-side audit is most effective precisely on the low-entropy operation classes that file-hash signing already protects redundantly, and is information-theoretically blind on high-entropy adaptive optimizer-generated paths --- a defense-in-depth ordering that is, to our knowledge, novel in both the CNC and AM literatures.

\subsection{Manufacturing Cybersecurity and Process Verification}
\label{sec:related-security}

The cybersecurity case for instruction-level sensor verification is direct. The machining-specific instruction-stream-integrity gap is articulated in the FMNet 2024 research-needs report~\cite{nist2024fmnet}; NIST SP 800-82 Rev.~3~\cite{nist2023sp80082} provides the general operational-technology framing, specifically its continuous-monitoring guidance under the Detect function and its supply-chain-risk guidance. Against that framing, an out-of-band physics-of-record monitor is a complementary control, with the caveat that its guarantee holds only insofar as the sensor path itself is trustworthy (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}). Adversarial G-code substitution in PLC and CNC controllers is an established attack surface~\cite{zonouz2014plc,sturm2014cyber,yampolskiy2018security,kimmell2025cnc,belikovetsky2017drowned}. Reconstructing the actually-executed instruction stream from external sensors, independent of the controller, provides an integrity-checking baseline that cannot be subverted by controller-resident malware. The present work characterizes which components of that instruction stream are realistically reconstructable on present-day sensor hardware, and which require additional sensor coverage or richer training data to recover.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Task Definition}
\label{sec:task}

Let $W \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$ denote a sensor window of $T$ samples at $C$ channels, recorded over a fixed window length aligned to a CNC milling operation. Let $g$ denote the G-code line that was executing within $W$. The task is to learn a mapping
\begin{equation*}
  f_\theta: W \mapsto \hat{g}, \qquad \hat{g} = (\hat{t}_1, \hat{t}_2, \dots, \hat{t}_L),
\end{equation*}
where $\hat{g}$ is a sequence of $L$ G-code tokens drawn from a vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$. The training objective minimizes a multi-head cross-entropy loss with the auxiliary structured heads described in Section~\ref{sec:decoder}, plus an end-of-sequence token. At inference, the decoder generates tokens autoregressively under a grammar mask that zeros structurally invalid transitions in the legacy-token head.

\subsection{Vocabulary and Token Types}
\label{sec:problem-vocab}

We follow CNC-shop convention in calling each G-code line a \emph{block}; blocks divide into \emph{rapid moves} (\texttt{G0}, traverse without cutting) and \emph{cutting moves} (\texttt{G1}/\texttt{G2}/\texttt{G3}, linear and arc feeds), both addressed identically by the tokenization below. We tokenize G-code with a hybrid scheme that disentangles structural primitives from numeric values~\cite{grammar_paper,gcode_foundational}. The vocabulary contains four token classes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{SPECIAL tokens} --- \texttt{PAD}, \texttt{BOS}, \texttt{EOS}, \texttt{UNK}, \texttt{MASK}.
\item \textbf{COMMAND tokens} --- literal motion or machine commands (\texttt{G0}, \texttt{G1}, \texttt{G2}, \texttt{G3}, \texttt{M3}, \texttt{M5}, \texttt{M30}, \ldots).
\item \textbf{PARAM tokens} --- address letters that introduce an axis or rate parameter (\texttt{X}, \texttt{Y}, \texttt{Z}, \texttt{I}, \texttt{J}, \texttt{K}, \texttt{R}, \texttt{F}, \texttt{S}, \texttt{P}).
\item \textbf{NUMERIC tokens} --- bucketed numeric values, one bucket per (axis, magnitude) pair. The full vocabulary contains 1{,}048 \texttt{NUM\_X} buckets, 1{,}086 \texttt{NUM\_Y} buckets, and 231 \texttt{NUM\_Z} buckets, with smaller bucket sets for the remaining axes. Total vocabulary size: 2{,}418.
\end{itemize}

A typical G-code line in the corpus, e.g. \texttt{G1 X1.4919 Y1.4848 F22.}, becomes a token sequence \texttt{BOS G1 X NUM\_X\_1492 Y NUM\_Y\_1485 F NUM\_F\_22 EOS}. The bucketization discretizes numeric values to a fixed precision; the per-position digit head described in Section~\ref{sec:decoder} provides an auxiliary regression-style signal to mitigate the resulting discretization loss.

\subsection{Per-Row Target Formulation}
\label{sec:per-row}

A naive supervised target assigns one G-code label per sensor window. In the present corpus, a 256-sample window at 4~Hz spans 64 seconds and contains an average of 60 distinct G-code rows (median 29, max 237). A window-level target therefore aggregates the information across these rows, destroying the row-level distinctions that distinguish, for instance, an \texttt{X} positive move from an \texttt{X} negative move within the same window. We instead emit one $(W, g_r)$ training pair per G-code row $r$ that fires within window $W$, where $g_r$ is the token sequence for that single row. This per-row formulation:

\begin{itemize}
\item Removes within-window label ambiguity. Each $(W, g_r)$ pair has exactly one correct target.
\item Increases the training-sample count by an order of magnitude ($\sim$19{,}500 per fold versus $\sim$300 for the window-level alternative).
\item Decouples the supervised target from the windowing schedule, preventing the model from inferring labels via positional shortcuts on \texttt{(window\_index, source\_file)}.
\end{itemize}

A complementary \emph{full-window} formulation, evaluated as an ablation (Section~\ref{sec:abl-target-mode}), retains one sample per window but uses a multi-line target consisting of all G-code rows that fired within the window, concatenated in temporal order. This formulation has fewer training samples per fold but a richer per-sample supervisory signal, and tests whether row-level autoregressive context (the previous row's tokens) closes any per-row recovery gaps.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

We report seven complementary metrics:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Token accuracy} --- proportion of non-padding token positions in the generated sequence whose argmax matches the target. The headline metric for sequence-quality.
\item \textbf{Sequence accuracy} --- proportion of test samples for which \emph{every} non-padding token matches the target. A strict exact-match metric.
\item \textbf{Type accuracy} --- per-token classification accuracy on the 4-way SPECIAL/COMMAND/PARAM/NUMERIC type head. Diagnoses whether the model is in the right structural mode at each step.
\item \textbf{Command accuracy} --- per-token classification accuracy on the 6-way motion-command head, evaluated only at positions where the target carries a COMMAND token.
\item \textbf{Parameter-type accuracy} --- per-token classification accuracy on the 10-way axis-letter head, evaluated at PARAM positions.
\item \textbf{Sign accuracy} --- per-position classification accuracy on the 3-way (positive, negative, absent) motion-sign head, evaluated at the relevant axis positions.
\item \textbf{Numeric (digit) accuracy} --- per-digit-position correctness pooled across all six digit positions in NUM tokens. The companion regression measure for numeric values is mean absolute error (MAE) of the reconstructed coordinate.
\end{enumerate}

Macro precision, recall, and F1 are computed for each classification head, using scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn} on the per-token confusion matrix.

\paragraph{Teacher-forced vs.\ autoregressive evaluation.} For any autoregressive sequence model, two evaluation regimes give materially different numbers and reflect different deployment scenarios.

\emph{Teacher-forced} (TF) evaluation supplies the ground-truth previous token at every position during the forward pass and measures the model's per-position classification accuracy under that ideal input. It is the standard objective during training and the standard sweep-time metric. \emph{Autoregressive} (AR) evaluation requires the model to consume its own previous predictions at each step, with no access to ground truth, and measures end-to-end generation quality under the actual deployment condition where no controller log is available to anchor the sequence.

The categorical heads in our decoder (type, command, parameter-type, sign) are independent classifiers on the encoder memory and therefore report identical accuracies under TF and AR. The autoregressive-token heads (legacy-token, digit-value, and the derived sequence and numeric metrics) report substantially lower accuracies under AR than under TF, as exposure-bias errors compound along the generated sequence.

Throughout this paper we explicitly distinguish the two regimes. The headline result table reports both regimes for the autoregressive heads, since the TF numbers correspond to the standard sweep-time evaluation used during hyperparameter selection while the AR numbers correspond to the realistic deployment scenario in which the decoder must recover G-code without a controller-log reference. For the categorical heads, a single number suffices because the regime distinction does not apply.

\subsection{Threat Model}
\label{sec:problem-threat-model}

We state the threat model up front so that every recoverability result that follows can be read against it; Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model} treats the operating-point and adaptive-adversary detail.

\paragraph{Trusted elements.} The multi-modal sensor stack and its acquisition pipeline are trusted: sensors are physically secured, the telemetry path is integrity-protected, the recorded sensor stream faithfully represents the physical machine state, and the decoder model and verification host are not co-located with the controller. These properties are operational requirements on the deployment, not implemented in the present prototype; the laboratory acquisition pipeline used to generate the corpus uses unauthenticated USB sensor links and is not itself a deployment-grade trust boundary. This is realistic for a process-monitoring deployment where sensors and analysis live outside the CNC controller's trust boundary, but it is a non-trivial operational assumption (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}, ``Sensor-side adversary''): a controller-resident adversary with bus access to shared sensor infrastructure is out of scope, as are sensor-side wire-tap, EMI injection, packet replay, and calibration-spoofing attacks. We additionally assume the operator who initiates each run is authenticated to the verification host and that the program loaded on the controller is the same artifact whose hash was audited (e.g., via a controller-side load-time attestation receipt~\cite{zonouz2014plc} bound to the operator session); an insider-threat operator who loads an unaudited program post-attestation is a residual adversary the present scope does not address.

\paragraph{Untrusted elements.} The CNC controller, its firmware, and any G-code dispatch path between controller and machine are untrusted. The adversary may have read/write access to the controller's program memory, may inject substituted G-code, may modify cutting parameters mid-execution, or may replay an earlier benign program while a different program is intended.

\paragraph{Attack classes considered.} (i)~\emph{Block substitution} (e.g., \texttt{G1 X1.5} becomes \texttt{G1 X2.5}; substituted blocks may differ in command identity, axis, sign, or numeric value); (ii)~\emph{parameter tampering}, where the program is structurally preserved but cutting parameters are altered (feed, spindle speed, depth of cut); (iii)~\emph{replay} of a previously-executed valid program while a different program was commanded; (iv)~\emph{block skip or duplicate} of executed blocks while the controller log reports nominal execution; (v)~\emph{upstream CAM / post-processor compromise}, where the toolpath is corrupted before the operator-signed G-code file is produced. Per-attack-class detectability is reported in Table~\ref{tab:tamper_auc} (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}). In the MITRE ATT\&CK for ICS taxonomy~\cite{mitre_attack_ics} these map respectively to T0843 / T0889 (Program Download / Modify Program), T0836 / T0831 (Modify Parameter / Manipulation of Control), T0856 (Spoof Reporting Message), T0855 (Unauthorized Command Message), and T0862 (Supply Chain Compromise). The decoder occupies the \emph{Detect} function of NIST CSF~2.0 (continuous-monitoring category DE.CM)~\cite{nistcsf2}, complementing prevention controls (file signing, attestation) rather than replacing them; \emph{Protect}, \emph{Respond}, and \emph{Recover} remain out of scope.

\paragraph{Adversary model: orthogonal defenses.} The sensor decoder is intended to run in parallel with --- not in place of --- the cheaper orthogonal defenses (file-hash / cryptographic signature on the G-code file; TCB attestation on the controller). Each defense covers attack classes the others cannot. The sensor decoder's residual value is the attack subset that survives a signed-G-code audit and beats controller attestation that does not extend to runtime memory: runtime substitution, selective skip / duplicate, and wrong-workpiece / wrong-fixturing. Within that subset, the decoder is further bounded by the closed-vocabulary in-distribution regime under which it is trained (Section~\ref{sec:cv}) and by the per-field recoverability characterized below. The full three-layer defense-in-depth comparison is Table~\ref{tab:lit_comparison} and Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System Overview}
\label{sec:system-overview}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{system_overview.pdf}
\caption{System overview. A 64-second sensor window ($256$ samples $\times$ $98$ channels across seven modality groups) is mapped by the frozen \encname{} encoder (8.6\,M parameters) to a $256 \times 256$ memory tensor. An 8-layer Transformer decoder ($d_{\text{model}}{=}384$, 12 heads) consumes this memory via cross-attention and generates tokens autoregressively. Six prediction heads share the decoder's hidden state and provide structured output (token, type, motion-command, parameter-type, sign, per-position digit). A static grammar mask zeros structurally invalid token transitions in the legacy-token softmax at inference time.}
\label{fig:system-overview}

The system has three stages (Figure~\ref{fig:system-overview}). (i)~A multi-modal sensor encoder maps a (256-sample, 98-channel) sensor window into a (256-step, 256-dim) memory sequence. (ii)~A Transformer decoder consumes this memory via cross-attention and generates a token sequence representing the G-code line that was executing within the window. (iii)~A grammar mask zeros structurally invalid transitions in the decoder's softmax distribution at inference time. The encoder is frozen for all experiments; its training is described in a separate contribution~\cite{encoder_paper} and summarized here only insofar as it influences the decoder.

\subsection{Sensor Encoder (Frozen)}
\label{sec:encoder}

The encoder is a multi-modal denoising autoencoder coupled to a temporal LSTM (\encname{}). It accepts seven modality groups --- accelerometer (3-axis, 6 boards = 18 channels), gyroscope (18), magnetometer (18), environmental temperature (6), ambient color (24), audio RMS (6), and motor current (8) --- for a total of $C=98$ channels. A per-modality projection layer maps each modality into a shared $d=256$ space; learned modality-weighting gates~\cite{lahat2015multimodal,ramachandram2017deep} produce a fused representation; a multi-head self-attention stack~\cite{vaswani2017attention} captures cross-channel interactions; a denoising reconstruction objective~\cite{vincent2010stacked} encourages noise-robust representations; and a final two-layer LSTM~\cite{hochreiter1997long,malhotra2015lstm} produces the temporal memory consumed by the decoder. The full encoder has 8.6~M parameters and was trained for 30 epochs on the same file-level 5-fold splits used here, reaching 96.3\% test-set accuracy on the same nine-class operation-type task~\cite{encoder_paper}. The encoder is frozen for all decoder experiments and serves as a fixed feature extractor. A representative test window showing the seven modality groups aligned in time against the G-code rows that fired within is reproduced in Supplementary Section~S14.1 (Figure~S16) as the visual analogue of the task formulation.

\subsection{Decoder Architecture}
\label{sec:decoder}

\modelname{} is an 8-layer Transformer decoder with $d_{\text{model}}=384$ and $h=12$ attention heads per layer. Each decoder layer consists of (a)~masked multi-head self-attention over the partial output sequence, (b)~multi-head cross-attention over the encoder memory, (c)~a position-wise feed-forward network with GELU activation, and (d)~residual connections with pre-layer normalization~\cite{ba2016layernorm}. Dropout~\cite{srivastava2014dropout} of $0.1$ is applied after each sub-layer. The decoder has $31.28$~M trainable parameters at the headline configuration (the Transformer stack is $18.9$~M; the remainder is the grammar-mask + sensor-prior machinery and the six output heads, embedding, and positional encodings --- component breakdown in Supplementary Section~S9.8); with the frozen $8.6$~M encoder the total system size is $\approx\!39.9$~M parameters.

\subsubsection{Multi-Head Structured Output}
\label{sec:multihead}

Six prediction heads share the decoder's final hidden state at each output position. Each head is a linear projection followed by a softmax (or a six-position softmax in the case of the digit head). The heads are trained jointly with a weighted cross-entropy loss:
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{decoder}} =
    \lambda_{\text{tok}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{tok}} +
    \lambda_{\text{type}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{type}} +
    \lambda_{\text{cmd}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cmd}} +
    \lambda_{\text{param}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{param}} +
    \lambda_{\text{digit}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{digit}} +
    \lambda_{\text{sign}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{sign}}.
\end{equation*}
Default weights are $\lambda_{\text{tok}}=3.0$ and all auxiliary $\lambda=1.0$. The auxiliary heads serve two purposes: they regularize the shared representation toward token-type-aware features, and they expose structural prediction errors that would otherwise be hidden inside aggregate token accuracy. The $\lambda_{\text{tok}}=3.0$ default was carried forward from a Stage-1 hyperparameter sweep cell (Section~\ref{sec:hp-sweep}) on a coarse $\lambda_{\text{tok}}\!\in\!\{1, 3, 5\}$ grid: $\lambda_{\text{tok}}=1$ left the token head under-trained relative to the auxiliary heads (TF token validation degraded $\sim\!4$~pp on fold-1) and $\lambda_{\text{tok}}=5$ over-weighted the token head at the cost of the categorical heads (parameter-type and sign each lost $\sim\!1$~pp on fold-1 validation), with $\lambda_{\text{tok}}=3$ the validation-best compromise; this is a fold-1-only sensitivity check, not a 5-fold sweep, and we record the value as a heuristic anchor rather than an optimized hyperparameter.

\paragraph{Legacy token head.} A vocabulary-sized softmax (2{,}418 classes) operating on the decoder's final hidden state. This head is the primary inference output: at each timestep, the next token is drawn from this distribution. A grammar mask (Section~\ref{sec:grammar}) zeros structurally invalid transitions before the softmax.

\paragraph{Type head.} A 4-way classifier over \{SPECIAL, COMMAND, PARAM, NUMERIC\} on each output position. Trained against the type of the target token at that position. This is the coarsest classification head and the easiest task; its accuracy is therefore an upper bound on the structural sanity of the decoded sequence.

\paragraph{Motion-command head.} A 6-way classifier over \{G0, G1, G2, G3, G53, M30\}, evaluated against the target's command token at COMMAND positions. Distinguishes rapid moves (G0), linear cuts (G1), clockwise/counter-clockwise arcs (G2/G3), the work-coordinate-cancel command (G53), and program-end (M30). G53 and M30 have very small support ($n\!\approx\!6$ per fold, $\le\!30$ pooled; Table~\ref{tab:per_class_command}); the per-class F1 for these classes is correspondingly underpowered (see Section~\ref{sec:lim-power}).

\paragraph{Parameter-type head.} A 10-way classifier over \{X, Y, Z, I, J, K, R, F, S, P\}, evaluated against the target's address letter at PARAM positions. On the present corpus only X, Y, Z, R, and F have positive support in the test splits (see Table~\ref{tab:data_coverage} for per-field frequencies); the remaining trained labels (I, J, K, S, P) do not appear in the test data and therefore do not have per-class entries in Table~\ref{tab:per_class_param_type}.

\paragraph{Sign head.} A 3-way classifier over \{POSITIVE, NEGATIVE, ABSENT\} that predicts the sign of the most recent axis-value. This head provides direct supervision on motion direction independently of the discretized numeric value.

\paragraph{Per-position digit head.} A six-position 11-way classifier (digits 0--9 plus PAD) that reconstructs the numeric value of each NUM token digit-by-digit. The six positions cover the precision range observed in the corpus (typically $0\!-\!2\!.X\!X\!X\!X$ for an X or Y coordinate). Decomposing numeric reconstruction by digit position makes the manuscript's per-position recoverability analysis (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}) possible and provides a regression-style auxiliary signal that complements the discretized NUM-token head.

\subsubsection{Grammar-Constrained Decoding}
\label{sec:grammar}

G-code follows a regular grammar: a line starts with an optional command (G/M) token; each address letter is followed by exactly one numeric value; certain commands (e.g.\ G2, G3) require specific subsequent fields. We encode the local part of this grammar as a static $|V| \times |V|$ binary transition mask and apply it to the legacy-token logits at each inference step, zeroing out structurally invalid transitions: BOS must be followed by a command or PARAM letter; a PARAM letter must be followed by its matching NUM token; a NUM token must be followed by another PARAM letter, a command, or EOS. The mask reduces, on average, the valid next-token set from $|V|=$ 2{,}418 to 31.8 tokens, a reduction that improves both calibration and exact-match accuracy. Implementation follows the runtime grammar-mask pattern established in Picard~\cite{picard}, Outlines~\cite{outlines}, and XGrammar~\cite{xgrammar}; the same pattern of compiling a domain grammar into a per-step transition mask underlies Synchromesh~\cite{synchromesh}, LMQL~\cite{lmql}, and the fine-tuning-free constrained-decoding setup of Geng \etal~\cite{geng2023gcd}.

\paragraph{Scope and limitations of the local mask.} The bigram mask above does not encode command-state-dependent rules of the form ``the active command is G0, therefore the next address letter must not be R'' (R is an arc-radius parameter valid only with G2/G3). Such rules require a finite-state grammar (FSM) over a multi-token history. A separate inference-time FSM layer encodes the following higher-order rules on top of the bigram mask: G0 and G1 (linear moves) forbid R, I, and J; M30 (program end) forbids any subsequent command or PARAM letter. The FSM tracks the most recently emitted move-command as state and zeros forbidden transitions in the legacy-token logits before the softmax. The FSM is applied at autoregressive inference only; teacher-forced training is unaffected because the supervisory signal already encodes correct grammatical structure through the targets.

\paragraph{FSM specification.} The FSM state set is $\mathcal{S}=\{s_0, s_{\text{G0}}, s_{\text{G1}}, s_{\text{G2}}, s_{\text{G3}}, s_{\text{M30}}, s_{\text{other}}\}$, with initial state $s_0$ at \textsc{bos}. Transitions on emitting a move-command token $c\in\{\text{G0},\text{G1},\text{G2},\text{G3},\text{M30}\}$ set the state to $s_c$; emitting any other command (G53, G55, G17, G90, G94, \ldots) sets the state to $s_{\text{other}}$; PARAM letters, NUM tokens, and structural tokens (BOS, EOS, SPECIAL) leave the state unchanged. The forbidden-PARAM set per state is: $s_{\text{G0}}\!\to\!\{R, I, J\}$; $s_{\text{G1}}\!\to\!\{R, I, J\}$; $s_{\text{G2}}\!\to\!\emptyset$ (arcs may legally take any of $R, I, J$); $s_{\text{G3}}\!\to\!\emptyset$; $s_{\text{M30}}\!\to\!\{\text{all commands and all PARAM letters}\}$; $s_0$ and $s_{\text{other}}$ apply the bigram mask only (no additional restriction). \texttt{FSM\_mask}$(s)$ in Algorithm~\ref{alg:forward} returns the indicator vector of allowed next tokens in state $s$, multiplied element-wise into the legacy-token logits before the softmax. The residual $11$ V1-violation cases reported in the FSM ablation (G53/G55/G17/G90/G94 followed by R) correspond to the $s_{\text{other}}$ state and are a deliberate scope choice: extending the FSM to encode the full RS274/NGC modal-group state machine is left as grammar-refinement work.

\paragraph{FSM ablation.} We audited grammar compliance and accuracy with and without the FSM layer, on both the baseline and the with-shortcuts 5-fold checkpoints under greedy autoregressive decoding. The bigram-only mask permits G0/G1 to emit R/I/J: across approximately 167{,}400 generated tokens per configuration, it produces 329 V1 violations affecting $170 / 549$ baseline samples ($31\%$) and 333 violations affecting $139 / 549$ with-shortcuts samples ($25\%$). With the FSM layer active, V1 violations drop to $0$ on baseline ($100\%$ reduction) and $11$ on with-shortcuts ($97\%$ reduction; the residual $11$ are G53/G55/G17/G90/G94 followed by R, a class the present FSM does not encode and a candidate for grammar refinement). The 5-fold accuracy cost is $-0.27$~pp / $-0.41$~pp on baseline token / numeric, and $-0.04$~pp / $+0.01$~pp on with-shortcuts --- in both configurations the difference is within fold-to-fold noise (baseline std $\pm 0.069$ / $\pm 0.032$; with-shortcuts std $\pm 0.016$ / $\pm 0.017$). The FSM therefore eliminates the dominant class of grammar violation at no measurable accuracy cost. A paired-sample comparison across the 5 folds confirms statistical non-significance: paired-$t$ tests on baseline token / numeric give $p=0.72 / 0.51$ and Wilcoxon signed-rank gives $p=1.00 / 0.44$; on with-shortcuts token / numeric the same tests give $p=0.83 / 0.95$ and $p=0.63 / 1.00$ (\texttt{audit/fsm\_vs\_bigram\_paired\_stats.json}). M30-followup violations (V2) were $0$ in all configurations because M30 is rare in the corpus and the model learns termination from training. We adopt the FSM as the default deployment-time decoding configuration on this basis.

\paragraph{Forward-pass pseudocode.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:forward} summarizes the deployment-time grammar-masked autoregressive forward pass that produces the token sequence reported throughout this paper. The bigram mask and the finite-state-machine layer are both applied to the legacy-token logits before the softmax; the structured heads (type, command, parameter-type, sign, digit) read the same hidden state but do not consume the autoregressive token stream.

\caption{Grammar-masked autoregressive forward pass for \modelname{}.}
\label{alg:forward}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\Require Sensor window $W \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times 98}$; frozen encoder $E_\theta$; decoder $D_\phi$ with heads $\{h_{\text{tok}}, h_{\text{type}}, h_{\text{cmd}}, h_{\text{pt}}, h_{\text{sgn}}, h_{\text{dig}}\}$; bigram mask $M_b \in \{0,1\}^{|V|\times|V|}$; FSM transition function $\delta(\cdot,\cdot)$; max length $L_{\max}$.
\Statex
\State $\mathbf{m} \gets E_\theta(W)$ \Comment{$256 \times 256$ memory; encoder frozen}
\State $\hat{g} \gets [\textsc{bos}]$; $s \gets s_0$ \Comment{state of the FSM}
\For{$t = 1$ \textbf{to} $L_{\max}$}

  \State $\boldsymbol{\ell}_t \gets h_{\text{tok}}(\mathbf{h}_t)$ \Comment{legacy-token logits, $\in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$}

  \If{$y_t = \textsc{eos}$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
\EndFor

\subsubsection{Scheduled Sampling}
\label{sec:scheduled-sampling}

The decoder is trained with teacher forcing as default. Optionally a scheduled-sampling regime~\cite{scheduled_sampling} is applied: with probability $p$ that linearly increases from $0$ to a tuned maximum $p_{\text{max}}$ over training, each input position is replaced with the model's own previous prediction rather than the ground-truth target. This reduces exposure bias between training and inference. The maximum $p_{\text{max}}$ was selected by the Stage-2 hyperparameter sweep (Section~\ref{sec:hp-sweep}) on per-row fold-1 validation token accuracy; the headline value is $p_{\text{max}}\!=\!0.5$, which lifted per-row command accuracy by $+17$~pp over $p_{\text{max}}\!=\!0$ at the cost of optimization stability at smaller numeric vocabularies (Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric} body, where the Design-B-style runs use $p_{\text{max}}\!=\!0$ for this reason). Values tested: $p_{\text{max}} \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$; $0.5$ was the validation-best across the swept range.

\subsection{Training Configuration}
\label{sec:training}

The decoder is optimized with AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} ($\eta=$ $5 \times 10^{-5}$, weight decay 0.05) for up to 300 epochs with early stopping on validation token accuracy (patience 75). The first 10 epochs use a linear warm-up. Batch size is 64, mini-batches are shuffled per epoch, and gradient clipping is applied at norm 1.0. Best-checkpoint-by-validation-token-accuracy is retained.

The decoder is trained \emph{without} positional metadata in its baseline configuration: \texttt{window\_index}, \texttt{total\_windows}, and \texttt{source\_file} identifiers are not exposed to the decoder. Section~\ref{sec:abl-positional} ablates this choice. The 5-fold training/validation loss and token-accuracy trajectories (monotonic decrease; validation plateaus near epoch 80--100, well within the patience-75 budget) are in Supplementary Section~S14.2 (Figure~S17).

\subsection{Encoder Information-Content Probe}
\label{sec:encoder-probe}

Because the encoder is frozen, the decoder is bounded above by what its representation carries. We characterize that bound with MLP probes on the mean-pooled encoder memory: for each of (command, axis-presence, motion-sign, operation-type) a two-hidden-layer MLP (hidden 256, ReLU, dropout 0.2) is trained on train, selected on val, scored on test (MSE$\to$RMSE for continuous numeric targets). Qualitatively, the mean-pooled memory clusters tightly by operation class; the nonlinear t-SNE projection is in Supplementary Section~S14.3 (Figure~S18), and the load-bearing linear analogue is the PCA in Figure~\ref{fig:encoder_memory_pca} below.

\paragraph{Linear structure.} PCA on the same memory matrix ($549 \times 256$; Figure~\ref{fig:encoder_memory_pca}) shows it is strongly low-rank --- $9$ PCs capture $80\%$ of variance, $24$ capture $95\%$, $40$ capture $99\%$ --- with the first two PCs already linearly separating the operation classes. The class-discriminative signal thus lives in a low-dimensional, linearly-accessible subspace, while fine per-row numeric detail must live in the low-energy tail and is poorly preserved: the representational counterpart of the numeric bottleneck (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}) and of the class-prior confound (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}).

The probe results (\texttt{audit/encoder\_probe\_v8.json}) give three facts that bound the rest of the paper. (i)~\emph{Window-level signal exists}: operation type $0.846$, axis presence $0.87$--$0.93$ (X/Y/Z), motion sign $0.80$--$0.86$. (ii)~\emph{Per-row disambiguation does not}: a window holds a median of 29 rows (max 237) but one mean-pooled vector, so probe accuracy is capped near the modal-row-per-window ceiling (the probe itself reaches command $0.77$; the modal-row ceiling is $\approx\!0.85$) --- the formulation gap that motivates the full-window comparison (Section~\ref{sec:abl-target-mode}) and underlies the class-prior confound (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}). (iii)~\emph{Numeric precision is lost}: X-value RMSE $0.97$ approaches the within-window range ($\sim\!3.0$ normalized units), Y $0.80$, Z $0.10$ --- the encoder retains a window-level mean, not per-row precision; row-level encoder pre-training is the natural remedy (Section~\ref{sec:future}).

\subsection{Hyperparameter Sweep}
\label{sec:hp-sweep}

The training configuration was selected through a three-stage hyperparameter sweep on fold 1.
\textbf{Stage 1} (8 cells) explored learning rate, batch size, model dimension, and loss-weighting on a coarse grid.
\textbf{Stage 2} (12 cells) refined around the Stage-1 winner by varying training techniques: curriculum learning, scheduled sampling, label smoothing~\cite{szegedy2016inception}, dropout, layer depth, and focal loss~\cite{focal_loss}.
\textbf{Stage 3} (14 cells) tested architectural variants: encoder fine-tuning (the last-LSTM-layer-only cell, reported below as the encoder-bottleneck probe), pointer-network output, regression-head numeric output, cross-window attention, multi-seed variance, and alternative encoder configurations. \emph{Stage-3 encoder fine-tuning cell (fold-1, raised in review).} Unfreezing the final encoder LSTM layer and co-training it with the decoder for the same epoch budget yielded fold-1 validation command accuracy $\sim\!0.78$ (vs.\ the fold-1 frozen-encoder baseline of $\sim\!0.78$) and TF token accuracy within $\pm 0.5$~pp of frozen-encoder baseline, with no statistically meaningful lift on any head; the cell was not promoted because the lift was within fold-1 noise and the fine-tuning cell adds $\sim 2\times$ wall-clock cost per fold. We report this single-fold pilot here so that the encoder-bottleneck claim in Section~\ref{sec:encoder-probe} is grounded in a measured (if underpowered) experiment rather than a structural inference. A full 5-fold encoder fine-tune with row-level supervision is the principal open direction (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}); the present pilot is consistent with --- but does not by itself establish --- the ``encoder is the bottleneck'' reading.
The composite winner across all 34 cells (selected on a weighted-geometric-mean score) was used as the configuration for the 5-fold experiments reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. \paragraph{Sweep-fold caveat (raised in review).} The sweep was conducted entirely on fold~1, which the manuscript's own covariate-shift audit (Section~\ref{sec:lim-covariate}) and calibration analysis (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}, where fold~1 fits the highest command-head temperature $T\!=\!1.77$ across the five folds) flag as the most atypical of the five folds (the $-4.4$~pp Z-coverage shift). Selecting the configuration on the outlier fold is a model-selection concern we acknowledge: the headline 5-fold numbers are still computed on all five folds, but the configuration's \emph{rank} among Stage-2/3 alternatives was decided on fold-1 validation only. A cross-fold rank-stability sanity check on the top-3 Stage-3 cells (a partial 5-cell, 2-fold replication) is on the artifact roadmap and not yet executed; until it is, the configuration choice should be read as fold-1-validation-best rather than cross-fold-rank-stable.

Five architectural and training components of the decoder are ablated empirically, each pinned to the result section that reports its 5-fold effect. Supplementary Table~S2 (Supplementary Section~S3) consolidates the per-component findings; the underlying experiments are reported under the cited sections in full.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

The corpus consists of 120 aligned CSV files recorded from a Bantam Tools desktop CNC milling machine~\cite{encoder_paper}. Six distributed multi-sensor boards (\texttt{frame\_l2}, \texttt{frame\_l3}, \texttt{frame\_r2}, \texttt{spindle2}, \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}, \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}) sample synchronously at 4~Hz; the double-underscore form matches the column-prefix convention in the released aligned CSVs. Each file carries:
\begin{itemize}
\item Accelerometer (3 axes $\times$ 6 boards = 18 channels);
\item Gyroscope (18 channels);
\item Magnetometer (18 channels);
\item Ambient color sensing (RGBA $\times$ 6 boards = 24 channels);
\item Environmental temperature (6 channels);
\item Audio root-mean-square (6 channels);
\item Motor-current draw (8 channels);
\end{itemize}
plus an aligned per-sample G-code line. We exclude proximity and pressure channels following the consistency audit reported in the encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper}, retaining $C=98$ sensor channels.

\paragraph{Sensor mounting and calibration.} The six multi-sensor boards are bolted to the machine frame and Y-bed in the locations encoded in their names: \texttt{frame\_l2} and \texttt{frame\_l3} on the left machine-frame upright, \texttt{frame\_r2} on the right frame upright, \texttt{spindle2} adjacent to the spindle housing, and \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3}/\texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} on the moving Y-bed (the double-underscore form matches the column-prefix convention in the released aligned CSVs). The frame boards measure the global vibration envelope; the spindle board is the highest-bandwidth cutting-event sensor; the Y-bed boards co-move with the workpiece and pick up axis-coupled signal. Per-board mounting is rigid bolted (no adhesive or magnetic mounts) for stable inter-run calibration. Each board carries an identical $15$-channel inventory (3-axis accelerometer, 3-axis gyroscope, 3-axis magnetometer, 4-channel RGBA color, 1-channel ambient temperature, 1-channel audio RMS), and an additional 8 controller-side electrical channels (machine-controller motor-current and bus signals) are recorded separately and are not removable as a board. All MEMS and audio channels are read at their on-board factory-rated nominal sensitivities and downsampled / envelope-extracted to the released 4~Hz rate prior to alignment with the G-code stream; no re-calibration was performed mid-corpus. Chip-level part numbers, datasheet bandwidth, noise floor, and the per-channel mapping for the eight electrical channels (axis-stepper current shunts and spindle-bus signals) are reproduced in the encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper} and apply unchanged to this study; the LOMO/LOSO findings (Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}, Supplementary Section~S5) should be read against that per-board / per-modality inventory.

\paragraph{Spectral context and envelope-extraction pipeline for 4~Hz sampling.} The released stream is an envelope-extracted 4~Hz aggregate of higher-rate on-board acoustic and MEMS signals; the choice trades the kHz-band content used by classical acoustic-emission tool-condition monitoring~\cite{teti2010advanced} for an ingestion-friendly, deployment-cheap envelope (Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}, $\sim 1.5$~kB/s ingestion). The on-board firmware pipeline is: (i)~each sensor channel is sampled at its on-board factory-rated rate (audio at $1$~kHz; MEMS accelerometer/gyroscope/magnetometer at $200$~Hz; environmental temperature, RGBA color, and motor-current at $\geq\!100$~Hz); (ii)~per-channel rectify-and-low-pass via a single-pole IIR with cutoff $f_c\!=\!2$~Hz (half the 4~Hz output rate, the Nyquist limit) computes the windowed envelope; (iii)~the envelope is decimated to the 4~Hz output tick by taking the last in-window value, with the 4~Hz tick clock-aligned to the firmware acquisition timestamp shared by all six boards; (iv)~the 4~Hz aggregate is written to the per-file CSV that the released corpus distributes. The firmware/preprocessing script that implements (i)--(iii) is released alongside the corpus (cited path under \texttt{src/firmware/envelope\_extract/}) so a reproducer with only the higher-rate raw stream can regenerate the released 4~Hz aligned CSVs. A per-modality PSD / SNR characterization of what the 4~Hz envelope preserves vs.\ the raw on-board acoustic stream is a known gap and is left to a sensor-engineering companion; the present paper's recoverability findings are conditional on the released 4~Hz aggregate and are framed throughout (Sections~\ref{sec:related-sidechan}, \ref{sec:lim-method}) as deliberately at the low-rate, low-bandwidth end of the sensing-cost spectrum rather than as a kHz-rate AM-side-channel reconstruction claim.

The dataset spans nine machining operation classes: \texttt{adaptive}, \texttt{adaptive150025}, \texttt{face}, \texttt{face150025}, \texttt{pocket}, \texttt{pocket150025}, \texttt{damageadaptive}, \texttt{damageface}, \texttt{damagepocket}. The \texttt{150025} suffix denotes a variant feed/spindle parameter ($150$~mm/min feed at $25{,}000$~RPM spindle, holding all other CAM parameters fixed). The \texttt{damage} prefix is assigned by an operational rule rather than a continuous surface-roughness threshold: a run is labelled \texttt{damage*} when the run was executed with a tool that had been deliberately worn or chipped prior to the cut (visible cutting-edge defect at the start of the run, recorded in the run log), regardless of the post-run inspection outcome. Per-class file counts are approximately balanced across the three motion strategies and their three condition variants (clean baseline, $150025$ parameter variant, damaged-tool variant); the per-class file inventory and per-class total row count are released in \texttt{audit/operation\_class\_inventory.json}. These nine classes cover three motion strategies (face, pocket, adaptive) crossed with parameter and condition variants.

\paragraph{Platform characterization and generalization risk.} The host platform is a Bantam Tools desktop CNC milling machine (cast-aluminum frame, ER11 collet spindle nominally to $\sim 28{,}000$~RPM, stepper-driven 3-axis gantry, no flood coolant; controller is the Bantam in-house controller running an RS274/NGC-compatible G-code dialect, see Section~\ref{sec:problem-vocab}). Runs use a single carbide end-mill class and a single workpiece material (machinable wax / soft polymer typical of desktop prototyping) under dry cutting; work-holding is the platform's standard vacuum/clamp fixture plate. Tooling, work-holding, and cutting-condition details are reproduced from the encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper} for self-containment. This platform is at the low-end of the manufacturing CNC spectrum: production vertical machining centers (VMCs) such as Haas/DMG/Okuma class machines have cast-iron bases, BT30/BT40 toolholders, servo drives, and flood coolant, all of which materially change the sensor signal environment. Vibration amplitudes on a production VMC are typically $1$--$2$ orders of magnitude larger and the spectral content differs (servo-driven vs.\ stepper-driven motion; cast-iron damping vs.\ aluminum); acoustic emission on a flood-cooled cut differs structurally from dry desktop cutting (coolant noise floor, chip-evacuation acoustics); motor-current signatures on a servo-drive VMC reflect closed-loop torque feedback rather than open-loop stepper-command magnitude. Tool-wear and process-monitoring studies on Haas/DMG/Okuma-class platforms~\cite{teti2010advanced,wang2018deep,serin2020tool} characterize this regime. The present corpus therefore characterizes the recoverability physics of a single low-amplitude, dry, stepper-driven platform; whether the recoverability factorization (categorical-vs-numeric, entropy-bound on fine digits, mode-collapse on long AR sequences) transfers to a production VMC is the cross-platform open question of Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}, not a claim the present corpus can settle.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Windowing}
\label{sec:preprocessing}

Sliding sensor windows of $T=256$ samples (64 seconds at 4~Hz) at stride 64 yield approximately $\sim$1{,}500 windows per fold. Each window is centered on a region of continuous cutting and aligned to its overlapping G-code rows. The per-row target formulation (Section~\ref{sec:per-row}) emits one $(W, g_r)$ pair per G-code row $r$ that fires within window $W$, yielding $\sim$19{,}500 training pairs, $\sim$7{,}000 validation pairs, and $\sim$7{,}500 test pairs per fold. At 4~Hz across the 98 retained channels in \texttt{float32}, the on-line ingestion budget is $\sim$1.5~kB/s, equivalent to $\sim$0.13~GB per machine per 24-hour shift uncompressed, well within standard plant-IT storage tiers.

\paragraph{Sampling-rate context.} The 4~Hz sensor sampling rate is load-bearing for what is and is not recoverable. A row-execution-duration audit over the 120-file aligned corpus ($21{,}541$ row-execution instances; \texttt{audit/gcode\_row\_durations.json}) shows the median G-code row executes in a single $0.25$~s sample and $77.7\%$ of row-execution instances occupy $\le\!1$ sample period --- temporally unresolvable as distinct transients at $4$~Hz. For context, conventional acoustic-emission and vibration tool-condition monitoring samples at $\geq\!10$~kHz~\cite{teti2010advanced}; the present stack is deliberately at the low-rate, low-bandwidth end of the sensing-cost spectrum, and every recoverability result below should be read in that light (full physical-aliasing discussion: Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}).

\subsection{Five-Fold File-Level Stratified Cross-Validation}
\label{sec:cv}

We use file-level stratified five-fold cross-validation to prevent within-file leakage. Each operation class is divided across folds in proportion to its frequency, so each fold contains representative coverage of all nine classes. Within a fold, train and validation splits come from the same fold partition; the test split is the held-out partition.

\paragraph{Closed-vocabulary evaluation regime (load-bearing).} We then run a G-code-line \emph{coverage repair} step (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage-repair}): any G-code line that appears in the test split but not the training partner triggers a file swap between the partners, until every test-line also appears in training. This step does not remove a confound --- it \emph{constructs} a deliberate closed-vocabulary evaluation regime in which every test line has been seen verbatim during training of the same fold ($97.6\%$ of distinct test lines and $99.0\%$ of test line-instances; Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}). The 5-fold headline numbers must therefore be read as a within-vocabulary classification / near-retrieval result, \emph{not} as open-vocabulary instruction recovery. The matched open-vocabulary number is the leave-one-class-out (LOCO) command accuracy of $0.213 \pm 0.191$ (Section~\ref{sec:loco}); the two should be read side by side throughout this paper.

\paragraph{Within-fold non-independence.} Preprocessing uses overlapping sensor windows (window $256$, stride $64$, i.e., $75\%$ overlap between adjacent windows from the same file), so adjacent windows within a single file are not statistically independent. File-level disjointness across folds is preserved, but the effective number of independent observations within a fold is smaller than the raw window count. The covariate-shift audit (Section~\ref{sec:lim-covariate}) and bootstrap confidence intervals on the 5-fold means use the per-fold mean as the unit of analysis, which sidesteps the within-fold non-independence.

\subsection{Baselines and Ablation Designs}
\label{sec:ablations-design}

We compare \modelname{} against the following ablations and baselines, each trained under identical preprocessing, splits, and hyperparameter configuration:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No-shortcuts baseline} (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}). The headline configuration with positional metadata not exposed to the decoder.
\item \textbf{With-shortcuts ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:abl-positional}). The same model with \texttt{window\_index}, \texttt{total\_windows}, and \texttt{source\_file} hash exposed as additional decoder inputs. Tests whether positional metadata inflates accuracy beyond what sensor signal alone can recover.
\item \textbf{Full-window target ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:abl-target-mode}). The same encoder but with multi-line per-window targets instead of per-row targets. Tests whether autoregressive row-to-row context within a window resolves the per-row ambiguity discussed in Section~\ref{sec:per-row}.
\item \textbf{Sensor-modality ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}). For each of the seven sensor groups, we zero the corresponding channels at the encoder input and evaluate the resulting decoder. Pooled across five folds for the gyroscope and color modalities; fold-1 only for the others.
\item \textbf{Sequence-classifier (pattern-aware) ablation} (Supplementary Section~S8). A sequence-classifier head trained jointly with the legacy-token head predicts which whole G-code line the model is reconstructing and biases the token distribution toward that line's tokens.
\item \textbf{Vocabulary-precision ablation} (Supplementary Section~S8). A coarser 2-digit numeric bucketing reduces vocabulary size by approximately $5\times$.
\item \textbf{Noise-augmentation ablation} (Section~\ref{sec:noise-aug}). Gaussian noise injection on sensor channels, feature dropout, and token masking during training~\cite{srivastava2014dropout,jia2016deep,meyes2019ablation}.
\item \textbf{Leave-one-class-out generalization} (Section~\ref{sec:loco}). For each of the nine operation classes, train without that class in the training partition and evaluate on its held-out test samples.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:stats}

For each five-fold ablation, we report mean and standard deviation across folds, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) against the no-shortcuts baseline, and Cohen's $d$ effect size. ANOVA is computed with SciPy~\cite{virtanen2020scipy}; bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals use $10{,}000$ percentile resamples with NumPy default-RNG seeded at $42$ (matched to the calibration-split convention of Section~\ref{sec:calibration}). For paired comparisons we additionally report the Hedges' small-sample-corrected effect size $g_z\!=\!J\!\cdot\!d_z$ with $J\!=\!1\!-\!3/(4(n\!-\!1)\!-\!1)$; at $n\!=\!5$ folds, $J\!\approx\!0.80$, so $g_z$ is uniformly $\sim$$20\%$ smaller than $d_z$ throughout this paper. Significance levels are reported as $\ast\,p<0.05$, $\ast\ast\,p<0.01$, $\ast\ast\ast\,p<0.001$. Reported $\pm$ values follow each upstream audit script's native convention (NumPy default population s.d.\ for headline / sensor-ablation tables, ddof$=$1 sample s.d.\ for the $K$-spectrum and no-numeric tables); the typical $\sim$10--15\% spread between the two conventions at $n\!=\!5$ does not change any significance call or deployment recommendation.

\paragraph{Inferential scope and a caution on $n=5$.} The unit of analysis is the per-fold mean, so every parametric test reported here is computed on $n=5$ observations per group. We therefore treat all $p$-values as \emph{descriptive} rather than confirmatory: with $n=5$ the ANOVA/Welch statistics are highly sensitive to the normality and homoscedasticity assumptions, which we do not have the sample size to verify, and reported $p$-values should be read to one or two significant figures and in conjunction with the effect size, not as precise probabilities. The bootstrap 95\% intervals are percentile intervals over five fold-means; they are small-sample and \emph{not} coverage-calibrated, and we present them as a smoothed indication of fold-to-fold spread rather than as exact confidence statements. Where a nonparametric test is reported (Mann--Whitney $U$, Wilcoxon signed-rank), it is the primary inferential statement and the parametric test is corroborating. Our conclusions rest on effect sizes that are large relative to the cross-fold spread, not on threshold-crossing $p$-values; single-fold pilot results are labeled as such and are explicitly excluded from every quantitative or deployment claim.

\label{sec:software}

The decoder is implemented in PyTorch and trained on NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs. Preprocessing and analysis use scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn} --- including the \texttt{HistGradientBoosting} non-neural baseline (Section~\ref{sec:results-baseline}), which we use in place of XGBoost~\cite{chen2016xgboost} because the available XGBoost build was CUDA-only and failed on the CPU inference host (Supplementary Section~S9.5) --- and SciPy~\cite{virtanen2020scipy}. \subsection{Unified Source-Entropy Recoverability Across Heads}
\label{sec:unified-entropy}

Across all six prediction heads --- token, type, command, parameter-type, sign, and numeric digit --- measured accuracy at the headline configuration sits at the upper boundary of the source-entropy admissible region defined by the training-corpus empirical distribution at that head~\cite{brown1992englishentropy,feder1994relations}. Table~\ref{tab:per_head_entropy} reports the per-head ceiling and Figure~\ref{fig:per_head_opclass_heatmap} stratifies it by operation class. The decoder's accuracy pattern is therefore predicted from information theory and sensor bandwidth alone, not from architectural choices: the from-scratch Transformer seq2seq baseline of Section~\ref{sec:abl-seq2seq-baseline} reaches the same ceiling at teacher-forced inference (0.877 vs 0.888 command), confirming the bound is sensor-side rather than model-side. Further architectural improvement at this 4~Hz sensor bandwidth would require closing the small gap to the ceiling on heads that are already there; closing the large gap on the numeric digit head requires raising the sensor bandwidth itself.

Two lightweight classifiers on the \emph{same} mean-pooled encoder embedding the decoder consumes (a scikit-learn \texttt{HistGradientBoosting} (HGB) tree-booster and the two-layer MLP probe of Section~\ref{sec:encoder-probe}) anchor the headline and isolate the contribution of the decoder's autoregressive multi-head architecture from the encoder embedding itself. Both hit the always-most-common-class data-coverage ceiling on the binary fields ($\approx\!0.88$ \emph{has-X}, $0.99$ \emph{has-F}), which are the correct reference points for the decoder's per-field numbers, and both fall well short on multi-class command identity (HGB macro-F1 $0.23$, MLP $\approx\!0.24$), consistent with the per-row modal-row ceiling (Section~\ref{sec:encoder-probe}). HGB does reach macro-F1 $0.86$ on the one genuinely within-window-diverse field (\emph{has-Z}), confirming the pooled embedding carries some per-row Z signal. The full per-task baseline table and analysis are in Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}.

\subsection{RQ1: Headline Accuracy and Per-Field Recoverability}
\label{sec:results-headline}

Table~\ref{tab:headline} reports the headline accuracies and macro precision/recall/F1 for each prediction head across five folds, under both teacher-forced and autoregressive evaluation. The categorical heads --- $0.9838 \pm 0.0085$ type accuracy, $0.8875 \pm 0.0558$ command accuracy, $0.9931 \pm 0.0036$ parameter-type accuracy, and $0.9888 \pm 0.0063$ pooled motion-sign accuracy --- are identical under both regimes because the heads are per-position classifiers on the encoder memory and do not consume the autoregressive token stream. The autoregressive-token heads diverge between regimes: token accuracy is $0.7811 \pm 0.0216$ (TF) vs.\ $0.2148 \pm 0.0685$ (AR), and numeric accuracy is $0.5850 \pm 0.0331$ (TF) vs.\ $0.1038 \pm 0.0322$ (AR). Sequence exact-match is reported only under AR (per-fold $\{0.015, 0.000, 0.028, 0.056, 0.032\}$, mean $0.0263 \pm 0.0185$; \texttt{audit/ar\_aggregate.json}); a sequence-level TF score is not separately well-defined because the metric requires generating a full output, and under teacher forcing the model is conditioned on the true prefix at every position, so the resulting ``sequence'' is the per-token TF accuracy collapsed to an all-or-nothing test. The AR sequence number of $0.0263$ is therefore the deployment-relevant figure; Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} unpacks the mode-collapse mechanism behind the AR drop, and the load-bearing evidence for partial (rather than total) collapse is the output-diversity audit reported there. Figure~\ref{fig:five_fold_spread} visualizes the fold-to-fold variance for each metric, and Figure~\ref{fig:per_class_bars} shows the per-class precision, recall, and F1 for the command and parameter-type heads.

These categorical and autoregressive numbers factorize cleanly along the head boundary that Section~\ref{sec:metrics} formalizes: TF/AR invariance is a structural property of the encoder-memory classifiers, and the TF--AR gap on the token and numeric heads is the exposure-bias signature of autoregressive coupling. The two regimes therefore characterize different things, and reading them together is what makes the per-field recoverability story tractable in the sections that follow.

These numbers must be read with three caveats in view. \textbf{First, they are closed-vocabulary, in-distribution} (coverage-repair regime, Section~\ref{sec:cv}; $97.6\%$/$99.0\%$ verbatim test-line overlap), so the command-accuracy $0.8875$ is a within-vocabulary classification result and \emph{not} a deployment-ready open-vocabulary number. Second, the frozen encoder was trained on the same file-level fold partitions, so the headline $0.888$ contains an unmeasured representation-reuse component on top of the per-instruction sensor recovery this paper claims (see Appendix~\ref{app:encoder-spec}). Third, three companion numbers anchor the headline: (i) the leave-one-class-out (LOCO) cross-class command accuracy of $0.213 \pm 0.191$ as the open-vocabulary floor (Section~\ref{sec:loco}); (ii) the class-prior control (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}), which is the right home for the within-class sensor-driven increment over the operation-class modal predictor; and (iii) the AR collapse described above.

\subsection{Numeric Digit Head Decomposition}
\label{sec:numeric-decomposition}

The digit head achieves $0.5850 \pm 0.0331$ mean accuracy under teacher-forced evaluation, dropping to $0.1038 \pm 0.0322$ under autoregressive evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:headline}); the per-position decomposition below is computed at the TF condition. The pooled 5-fold per-slot accuracy is bathtub-shaped (Table~\ref{tab:per_digit_position}, Figure~\ref{fig:per_digit_position_curve}): slot~0 ($10^{1}$, tens) reaches $1.0000$; slot~1 ($10^{0}$, units) reaches $0.7516 \pm 0.0338$; slots~2--4 (tenths--thousandths) bottom out at $0.62$--$0.70$; slot~5 ($10^{-4}$, ten-thousandths) recovers to $0.8636 \pm 0.0357$. The per-axis breakdown of this curve (Figure~\ref{fig:per_axis_bathtub_overlay}) shows that the pooled bathtub is averaging structurally different per-axis profiles: X and Y track the classical $\cup$-shape, but Z reaches $0.98$ at slot~2 and drops to $0.37$ only at slot~4 (the cutting-depth-step quantization explained below), and R is uniformly $\ge 0.88$ across all six slots (small discrete arc-radius vocabulary).

This profile is strongly anticorrelated with the \emph{source entropy} of each slot. We computed Shannon entropy of the empirical digit-value distribution at every slot of the model's fixed six-slot grid ($[10^{1}, 10^{0}, 10^{-1}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-4}]$, replicating \texttt{digit\_value\_head.py} exactly) across the same pooled population the accuracy denominator uses (\texttt{audit/digit\_entropy.json}). The load-bearing statistic is the per-(axis, slot) cell-level inverse relationship over 30 cells (Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_accuracy_scatter}, Pearson $r = -0.78$), which has defensible $n$ and does not depend on six fragile points; the per-cell coefficient is weaker than the pooled value because per-axis cells cluster at the high-accuracy end (particularly R's tightly-packed low-entropy cells and F's printf-padded zeros, both of which fall on the high-accuracy/low-entropy regime line individually but compress the bivariate spread). At the pooled six-slot level the same anticorrelation holds (Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_accuracy_overlay}); we report the rank-based Spearman $r = -0.83$ ($p = 0.04$, two-tailed) as the primary statistic and the Pearson $r = -0.96$ ($p = 0.0023$) as the parametric companion, noting that the Pearson $p$ assumes bivariate normality which cannot be checked at $n=6$. Wherever the digit distribution is highly non-uniform the model recovers it accurately, and wherever the distribution approaches the uniform-noise ceiling ($H_{\max} = \log_2 10 \approx 3.32$ bits) it cannot.

The per-axis decomposition (Figure~\ref{fig:digit_value_histograms}) grounds this in the CNC physics of the corpus. \textbf{High-order digits} are physically constrained: the tens digit (slot~0) is uniformly $0$ across X, Y, and Z (every coordinate $|\text{val}| < 10$ in the present small-part corpus), and the units digit (slot~1) carries most of its mass on $\{0, 1\}$ for X/Y ($75$--$77\%$) and is identically $0$ for Z (every $|\text{Z}|<1$). These cells have entropies $0.00$--$1.72$ bits, well below the uniform ceiling, and they are precisely the cells the model recovers near-perfectly. \textbf{Low-order digits} approach the ceiling: across X, Y, Z the $10^{-2}$, $10^{-3}$, and $10^{-4}$ slots have per-axis entropies in the range $3.26$--$3.31$ bits --- empirically indistinguishable from uniform random over $\{0,\ldots,9\}$. This is the empirical signature of CAM toolpath-generation noise: the fourth decimal of a coordinate ($10^{-4}$ inch) is interpolated by the CAM postprocessor at a resolution below any physical determinism the sensors carry at $4$~Hz, so it is information-theoretically near-unrecoverable from the sensor stream regardless of decoder design. The verdict holds across all three primary axes (\texttt{cnc\_verdict.lowest\_decimal} in \texttt{audit/digit\_entropy.json}). The $10^{-1}$ slot is a useful counter-example: for Z it has entropy $0.70$ bits (mass on $0$/$1$) because Z values cluster on small magnitudes (cutting-depth steps), and the model accordingly recovers Z's tenths slot with high accuracy --- a per-axis demonstration that the source-entropy account correctly predicts where recovery is easy and where it is not.

Supplementary Section~S7 stratifies slot-5 by operation class and identifies three regimes (uniform-noise adaptive HSM, coordinate-repeating face overruns, and mixed pocket-style), with the full per-class breakdown, representative G-code blocks, and a per-position prediction-vs-target plate. The slot-5 \emph{upturn} of the pooled curve is therefore not a genuine recovery of the ten-thousandth digit but the joint signature of facing-pass coordinate-repeat in traditional ops plus the float-epsilon target-encoding artifact of Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding} ($23.6\%$ of CAM-authored terminal zeros encode to digit `9' under the model's iterative \texttt{int(dec\_part * 10) \% 10}).

\paragraph{What \emph{is} reliably recoverable.} The negative bound on slot~5 is half the story; the per-axis source-entropy map (Figure~\ref{fig:digit_value_histograms}, Figure~\ref{fig:quantization_heatmap}) identifies the cells where recovery \emph{is} structurally available, and they are non-trivial. In-distribution coarse-resolution recovery is reliable across all primary axes: the tens digit of X, Y, and Z is uniformly $0$ in this corpus and the model recovers it at $100\%$ accuracy; the units digit reaches $\ge 75\%$ on X and Y (mass on $\{0, 1\}$) and is identically $0$ on Z; Z's tenths digit recovers at high accuracy from the cutting-depth-step quantization ($86\%$ mass on `0', $H = 0.70$~bits, Figure~\ref{fig:digit_value_histograms}). R is recoverable across \emph{all} six slots ($H = 0.00$--$2.39$~bits, $34$--$100\%$ modal mass; Figure~\ref{fig:quantization_heatmap}) because the corpus uses a small discrete set of repeated arc radii rather than continuous values. The defensible characterization of the digit head is therefore a \emph{coarse-resolution coordinate recoverer} that picks up the physically- or geometrically-constrained digit positions (whole-inch magnitudes for X/Y/Z, cutting-depth steps for Z, arc-radius vocabulary for R) and is bounded at the uniform-noise ceiling at the fine-resolution positions where the CAM postprocessor's interpolation residuals dominate. Figure~\ref{fig:bits_recoverable_per_axis} summarizes this as a per-axis ``bit budget'': of the total source entropy in each axis's coordinates, the fraction approximately recovered by the decoder.

\paragraph{Per-axis sign recovery.} The same entropy decomposition extends to the motion-sign head's $0.9888$ headline accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:headline}). Per-axis sign distributions across the test corpus give Shannon entropies $H_X^{\text{sign}} = 0.38$~bits ($93\%$ positive), $H_Y^{\text{sign}} = 0.08$~bits ($99\%$ positive), $H_Z^{\text{sign}} = 1.04$~bits ($44\%$ positive, $56\%$ negative), and $H_R^{\text{sign}} = H_F^{\text{sign}} = 0.00$~bits ($100\%$ positive in this corpus). \subsection{Per-Operation-Class Recoverability}
\label{sec:per-op-class}

Autoregressive predictions stratify sharply by operation class, and the stratification has a single mechanistic cause rather than a per-class capability gradient. One class --- \texttt{face} ($n=144$ test windows) --- reaches token $0.56$ / numeric $0.40$ against a pooled AR baseline of $0.215 / 0.104$, while every other class falls into a narrow $0.09$--$0.25$ token / $0.01$--$0.15$ numeric band. The most-frequent non-facing class, \texttt{pocket150025} ($n=124$), sits at $0.09 / 0.01$ --- floor-level despite ample test support. This is the per-class signature of the mode-collapse mechanism analysed in Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}: the corpus-modal AR output the decoder converges to \emph{is} a facing-pattern toolpath, so \texttt{face} windows score by coincidental agreement with the modal trajectory rather than by genuine sensor-driven discrimination, and any class with a different toolpath signature scores near floor regardless of test sample count. The headline categorical recoverability (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}) is therefore the load-bearing per-class claim; the per-class \emph{numeric} story is constant-mode-collapse with one outlier and should not be read as ``\texttt{face} is easier to decode.'' Full per-fold breakdown, methodology, and per-class numbers in Supplementary Materials Section~S1.

\subsection{Test-Time Sensor-Noise Sensitivity}
\label{sec:noise-sensitivity}

To characterize how robust the categorical heads are to deployment-time degradation we re-evaluate fold~1 of the baseline checkpoint while injecting additive Gaussian noise into the frozen-encoder memory at varying $\sigma$ relative to the memory's empirical standard deviation. The categorical heads are essentially flat across $\sigma \in [0, 2]$ (i.e.\ up to twice the magnitude of the signal itself): command-head accuracy varies between $0.78$ and $0.82$, type accuracy holds at $0.97$, and parameter-type accuracy holds at $0.98$. The slight upward drift on the command head at high $\sigma$ is consistent with random noise acting as label smoothing that pushes borderline predictions toward the modal class (G1) rather than a genuine improvement. This is a probe of the encoder-to-decoder \emph{interface} --- robustness to perturbations in the conditioning vector --- not of raw-sensor degradation, which the non-linear encoder may not propagate as memory-level noise; a raw-input noise sweep is out of scope. The deployment-relevant reading is that the categorical-recovery story does not degrade gracefully but holds with surprising robustness to encoder-memory perturbation, which is the relevant failure mode for a frozen-encoder deployment in which the sensor stack and encoder are validated upstream. Full per-$\sigma$ results, the figure, and the sign-head audit-script caveat (a defect confined to the noise-audit script that does \emph{not} affect the headline sign accuracy) in Supplementary Materials Section~S11.1.

\subsection{Calibration of the Categorical Heads}
\label{sec:calibration}

A high-accuracy classifier may still be poorly calibrated, and for a deployment that surfaces row-level predictions to an operator or a downstream verification rule the predicted confidence must track empirical accuracy. The type and parameter-type heads are well-calibrated by conventional thresholds out of the box (fold-1 ECE $= 0.015$ and $0.020$ respectively at $10$ bins; both below the $0.025$ threshold), while the command head is moderately mis-calibrated and \emph{overconfident}: fold-1 ECE $= 0.122$ and MCE $= 0.207$ at $0.780$ accuracy against $0.902$ mean confidence (a $12$~pp confidence-vs-accuracy gap, with the largest $20.7$~pp gap concentrated in the highest-confidence bin). The overconfidence pattern is consistent with the LOCO collapse signature in Section~\ref{sec:loco}: the command head places high mass on G1 even on windows whose true command is not G1. We apply post-hoc temperature scaling (a single learned scalar $T$ per head, fit by minimising NLL on a held-out calibration half of each fold's test positions), which on the command head reduces cross-fold ECE from $0.050 \pm 0.038$ to $0.020 \pm 0.011$ (a $3.0$~pp reduction) without changing accuracy because $T$ does not move the argmax (fitted $T_{\text{cmd}} = 1.34 \pm 0.25$, where $T > 1$ softens an overconfident distribution). We recommend this as the default deployment-time calibration step on the command head; the Brier/Murphy decomposition confirms that temperature scaling targets the reliability term (REL) and leaves the discrimination term (RES) unchanged, so the row-level verification rule's ranking signal is preserved. Full ECE/MCE breakdown, Brier/Murphy decomposition, per-fold fitted temperatures, the reliability diagram, and the parameter-type worst-bin MCE trade-off caveat in Supplementary Materials Section~S2.

\subsection{Output-Position Failure Distribution}
\label{sec:output-position-failure}

A token-position breakdown of decoder accuracy locates \emph{where} in the output sequence the model fails, and the breakdown isolates a single concentrated failure rather than a distributed error budget. In the per-row formulation, position~1 (the first address letter, X/Y/Z, immediately following the command token) is recovered at only $0.240$ accuracy --- a sharp drop from the $0.761$ at position~0 (command/BOS region) and the $0.743$--$0.929$ at positions~3--5 (the first numeric digits). This is the concentrated signature of within-window row-identification ambiguity: each window contains multiple rows that may begin with different axes, and the per-row encoder summary alone cannot distinguish which row is being decoded. The full-window formulation gives the decoder access to the previous row's full output via autoregressive context, and position~1 lifts to $0.622 \pm 0.027$ (5-fold mean $\pm$ std) --- a $+38$~pp gain that directly localises the win from full-window targeting (Section~\ref{sec:abl-target-mode}) to row-disambiguation rather than to a generic capacity boost. Once the decoder is past the row boundary both formulations recover monotonically: full-window accuracy reaches $0.900 \pm 0.026$ at position~10 and stays in the $0.905$--$0.940$ band through position~100, confirming that the position-1 failure is a structural ambiguity rather than a length-degradation problem. Full per-position table and curve in Supplementary Materials Section~S11.5.

\subsection{Failure-Mode Analysis}
\label{sec:failure-modes}

Beyond aggregate accuracies, we classify the qualitative shape of the decoder's worst predictions to characterize \emph{how} the model fails when it fails. From each fold's test split we extract the 20 worst-edit-distance sequences (100 sequences across 5 folds) and bucket them by the structural difference between the predicted and target token streams: (a)~\emph{dropped command}, target contains a G-command, prediction does not; (b)~\emph{wrong command}, both contain G-commands but they differ; (c)~\emph{hallucinated command}, target has no G-command (a continuation row), prediction introduces one; (d)~\emph{value-only error}, structural tokens match but numeric values differ. The bucket counts (out of 100) are: dropped 17, wrong 1, hallucinated 8, value-only 37, and ``other'' 37, where ``other'' captures same-command structural mismatches dominated by address-letter swaps (Y$\leftrightarrow$X). Command-identity failures (a--c) together account for 26\% of the worst sequences; the remaining residual is split between value-only errors and axis-letter swaps. The worst failures are therefore predominantly structural rather than arithmetic. Section~\ref{sec:disc-failure-mode} interprets this pattern. Representative worst-edit-distance examples per failure bucket (two per bucket) are in Supplementary Section~S13.

\subsubsection{Positional-Metadata Ablation}
\label{sec:abl-positional}
Re-training the decoder \emph{with} positional metadata exposed (window index, total-window count, source-file hash) produces the per-metric effect sizes in Table~\ref{tab:anova}. One-way ANOVA across the 5 folds of each configuration shows a clean factorization:

\begin{itemize}
\item Categorical heads --- unaffected by positional metadata (command $F=0.02$, $p=0.89$; type $F=0.04$, $p=0.85$; param-type $F=0.01$, $p=0.93$; sign $F=0.00$, $p=0.96$, all under AR evaluation; identical p-values under TF).
\item Autoregressive heads --- token $F=4.52$, $p=0.066$, $\Delta=+7.5$~pp; sequence $F=27.18$, $p=0.0008$, $\Delta=+7.6$~pp; numeric $F=18.96$, $p=0.0024$, $\Delta=+8.0$~pp. Sequence and numeric heads are improved by positional metadata under AR evaluation at $p < 0.01$; the token-head improvement is borderline ($p=0.066$, marginal at $\alpha=0.05$). Under TF evaluation the same comparison shows no improvement on any head (token $p=0.21$, numeric $p=0.13$, sequence the sole TF-significant comparison at $p=0.0006$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Categorical heads} (command, type, parameter-type, sign, per-axis presence and sign) are robustly recoverable from sensors alone, and exposing positional metadata does not improve them under any evaluation regime. The with-shortcuts 5-fold command-accuracy mean is $0.882 \pm 0.056$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}), statistically indistinguishable from the no-shortcuts $0.8875 \pm 0.0558$ baseline. These heads are independent per-position classifiers on the encoder memory; their accuracies are identical under teacher-forced and autoregressive decoding (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}).

\textbf{Autoregressive-token heads} (token, numeric, sequence) show a more nuanced picture that depends on the evaluation regime. Under teacher-forced eval the position metadata provides no lift and is mildly harmful ($-2.7$~pp on token, $-5.3$~pp on numeric in 5-fold means). Under autoregressive deployment-true evaluation, however, the picture reverses: position metadata produces approximately $+7.5$~pp on token and $+8.0$~pp on numeric accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}). The mechanism is mitigation of autoregressive mode collapse: in the no-shortcuts AR setting, the decoder converges to a corpus-modal token sequence regardless of sensor input; exposing the window index gives the decoder a per-window anchor that breaks the modal trajectory on a fraction of test samples. Position metadata is therefore not a ``shortcut'' that inflates sweep-time accuracy; it is a deployment-time disambiguation signal that partially compensates for exposure bias in long-sequence autoregressive decoding. Bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals for both configurations appear in Table~\ref{tab:bootstrap_ci}.

\paragraph{Decomposing the collapse mechanism.} The position-metadata
mitigation establishes that per-window anchoring partly breaks the modal
trajectory; the no-numeric retraining ablation (Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric})
takes the complementary view, asking what the collapse \emph{is made of}.
Retraining the decoder with every coordinate value collapsed to a single
placeholder token --- so that a numeric error can no longer desynchronize
the surrounding tokens --- improves autoregressive per-token structural
accuracy by $+8.8$~pp (paired across the five folds, $5/5$ positive,
paired $t=4.02$, $p\!<\!0.02$) but leaves window-level structural exact-match
essentially unchanged ($0.066$ vs.\ $0.058$). The collapse therefore
decomposes into a measurable numeric-token-desynchronization contribution
(eliminated by the ablation) and a dominant intrinsic mode-collapse of the
structural token stream itself (unaffected by the ablation): the latter,
not the numeric vocabulary, is the deployment-relevant bottleneck.

\paragraph{Multiple-comparisons correction on the drilled grid.} The per-class / per-axis / per-position ANOVA grid is run as the methodology section commits (Supplementary Section~S9.7). The grid covers 8 ablation configurations (noise augmentation and the seven zeroed-modality ablations: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, environmental, color, audio-RMS, electrical) against the no-shortcuts baseline, drilled across the per-class precision/recall/F1 for type, command, parameter-type, sign, and the per-digit-value / per-position numeric heads, for a total of $630$ (ablation, drilled-metric) tests (\texttt{audit/anova\_per\_class\_results.json}). Under Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (BH-FDR) adjustment ($\alpha=0.05$), $320 / 630$ tests are flagged; under the more conservative Holm-Bonferroni adjustment, $72 / 630$. This grid is a \emph{robustness sweep}, not $320$ independently established effects: each test is an $n=5$ ANOVA, and FDR/Holm control the false-discovery/family-wise error of the \emph{decision rule} but do nothing to remedy the low power and assumption-fragility of a five-point test, so the long tail of small-support per-class cells is uninformative either way. The signal in the grid is carried entirely by a few \emph{large-effect} ablations that need no multiplicity machinery to see: the noise-augmentation ablation (per-class command F1 drops of $0.3$--$0.7$ for G0/G1/G2/G3) and the uniform command-head degradation shared by all seven sensor-modality ablations (consistent with the non-significant between-modality ANOVA in Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}). We therefore read the grid as ``these few large effects are robust to multiplicity correction,'' not as a count of confirmed discoveries; per-class cells with $n_{\text{support}}\le 50$ are underpowered regardless of adjustment (Section~\ref{sec:lim-power}). The macro-metric ANOVA results in Table~\ref{tab:anova} survive both Holm-Bonferroni and BH-FDR adjustment for the sequence-head and numeric-head AR-evaluation positives ($p < 0.01$); the token-head $p=0.066$ borderline does not survive either adjustment.

\subsubsection{No-Numeric Retraining: Decomposing the Autoregressive Collapse}
\label{sec:abl-nonumeric}

Roughly half of every G-code line's positions are coordinate \emph{values} (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}), each a hard selection from the $\sim$2{,}400-entry numeric vocabulary, and a single mispredicted value desynchronizes the autoregressive decode of the remainder of the line. This motivates a direct test of whether the AR collapse of Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} is \emph{driven} by the numeric tokens. A naive test that re-scores the headline decoder's AR predictions with numeric positions masked is confounded: mispredicted values still desynchronize the surrounding structural tokens before the mask applies, leaving structural-only AR token accuracy at $0.317$ (only modestly above the unmasked $0.215$), a contaminated lower bound. The clean test removes the numerics from the prediction problem entirely: we retrain the decoder with every coordinate value collapsed to a single placeholder token (24-entry structural vocabulary in place of the $\sim$2{,}400-entry one), so a numeric error can never desynchronize the decode. The frozen encoder, 5-fold file-level split, and all other decoder hyperparameters are unchanged; the digit head is disabled and training uses teacher forcing throughout. Scheduled sampling destabilizes optimization once the numeric vocabulary collapses because the placeholder vocabulary offers no per-position lexical variety to re-anchor on, so it is disabled for this ablation, after which validation token accuracy converges stably at $\sim$$0.96$ across folds (last-25-epoch s.d.\ $<\!0.01$).

Table~\ref{tab:nonumeric} reports the comparison. \textbf{Under teacher
forcing} the no-numeric decoder reaches structural token accuracy
$0.927 \pm 0.024$ against the headline decoder's $0.960 \pm 0.020$, and
structural sequence exact-match $0.068 \pm 0.017$ against the headline's
$0.213 \pm 0.048$ --- a real but modest cost from collapsing the varied
numeric tokens to a single placeholder, consistent with the loss of
per-position lexical variety as positional scaffolding for the decoder's
attention. \textbf{Under autoregressive decoding, the two effects decompose.}
Structural per-token accuracy improves by $+8.8$~pp ($0.405 \pm 0.143$ vs.\
$0.317 \pm 0.116$); the improvement is consistent across all five folds
(paired mean $+0.088 \pm 0.049$ across folds, $5/5$ folds positive,
paired $t=4.02$, $p\!<\!0.02$). \emph{Schedule-vs-vocabulary attribution
caveat (raised in review).} This $+8.8$~pp paired contrast against the
SS$=$0.5/dw$=$1.0 headline is a joint condition difference (numeric
vocabulary $+$ training schedule $+$ digit-head loss weight). The
matched-methodology comparison is against the $K\!=\!2{,}418$ T3.1 cell
(SS$=$0/dw$=$0, full numeric vocabulary; same schedule as the
no-numeric cell): under that control the no-numeric run reaches AR struct
token $0.405$ vs.\ T3.1's $0.470$, i.e.\ no measurable additional
contribution from collapsing the numeric vocabulary beyond the schedule
effect T3.1 already absorbs. The $+8.8$~pp number is therefore safely
read as ``the schedule change alone closes the schedule-attributable
piece of the AR collapse, and an additional numeric-collapse step does
not improve on it,'' which strengthens the dominant-intrinsic-mode-collapse
reading but downgrades the standalone ``numeric-desynchronization
contributes $\sim\!9$~pp'' framing. \emph{Structural sequence exact-match, however,
is essentially unchanged} ($0.066 \pm 0.014$ vs.\ $0.058 \pm 0.039$; paired
mean $+0.008$, $2/5$ folds positive) --- the deployment-relevant window-level
recovery does not improve. The mode-collapse mechanism of
Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} therefore decomposes into two
contributors: (a) \textbf{numeric-token desynchronization}, which the ablation
eliminates and which contributes a measurable, paired-significant
$\sim$$9$-pp per-token autoregressive effect; and (b) \textbf{intrinsic
autoregressive mode-collapse of the structural token stream itself}, which the
ablation does \emph{not} affect and which dominates the window-level
recovery. Mechanism~(b) is consistent with the position-metadata mitigation
of Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse} (per-window anchoring of the structural
decode) and the null beam-search result (Supplementary Section~S11.4): the
structural collapse is intrinsic to autoregressive decoding of the long
full-window target, not driven by the numeric vocabulary. This ablation
complements the vocabulary-precision pilot (Supplementary Section~S8):
neither coarsening the numeric vocabulary nor eliminating numeric prediction
entirely materially lifts window-level recovery, jointly localizing the
bottleneck off the numeric vocabulary and onto the autoregressive sequence
mechanism on the structural stream.

\subsubsection{Target-Mode Ablation (Per-Row vs.\ Full-Window)}
\label{sec:abl-target-mode}

The per-row target formulation (Section~\ref{sec:per-row}) leaves the decoder without a within-window row-identification signal: every row of a given window shares the same encoder memory, and the decoder must predict a specific row from that shared input. A natural alternative is full-window targeting, in which the supervisory signal is the entire multi-line G-code that fired within the window, generated autoregressively. Row identification then comes from the decoder's own output context: row $r$ is predicted conditional on rows $1\!\dots\!r-1$. The corresponding row in Table~\ref{tab:ablations} reports the resulting accuracies.

The target-mode contrast --- per-row (one G-code line per window) versus full-window (the full multi-line sequence within each window) --- isolates the contribution of autoregressive context to row-level recovery. Holding the architecture, hyperparameters, and training data fixed, the full-window formulation produces a substantial lift on the command head: command accuracy moves from per-row $0.499$ to full-window $0.888$, a +38.9~pp gain. Because the command head is an independent classifier on the encoder memory and not autoregressive in its output stream, this lift is robust across both teacher-forced and autoregressive evaluation regimes (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}); it reflects an actual change in what the decoder is able to recover, not a difference in eval condition. Section~\ref{sec:disc-failure-mode} interprets the lift mechanistically: in per-row mode all rows within a 64-second window share the same encoder memory, so the decoder cannot disambiguate which of $\sim$60 candidate rows it is asked to emit; in full-window mode the multi-line target gives every row a unique within-window position signal, restoring identifiability for the command head.

On the autoregressive-token heads (token, numeric, sequence), the per-row vs.\ full-window contrast is more nuanced. In teacher-forced evaluation the full-window formulation reports higher token and numeric accuracy than per-row, but in autoregressive evaluation the difference shrinks substantially because exposure-bias errors compound over the longer full-window sequences. We therefore report the full-window formulation as the deployment-recommended configuration only when categorical recovery is the objective; when end-to-end sequence reproduction is the objective, per-row mode is comparable in autoregressive performance and has the practical advantage of much shorter, parallelizable per-row inference.

\subsection{RQ3: Sensor-Modality Ablation}
\label{sec:abl-sensors}
For each of seven sensor modality groups (accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, environmental, color, audio-RMS, electrical) we zero the corresponding sensor channels at the encoder input, then evaluate the decoder. The encoder is not retrained; the zeroing happens only at decoder-inference time, which isolates the marginal contribution of each modality \emph{conditional on the encoder's existing weight allocation}. This is a \emph{lower bound} on the value of each modality: an encoder retrained without a given modality could re-allocate capacity onto the remaining channels and would be the appropriate test of modality-redundancy at the system level; the inference-time zeroing reported here tests only what the existing encoder happens to extract from that modality. \emph{Audio-RMS fold count, unambiguous statement.} All seven modality groups are evaluated at the full 5-fold protocol ($n=5$) in the headline ANOVA, Welch, and Mann--Whitney statistics reported in Table~\ref{tab:sensor_ablation} and Table~\ref{tab:anova}; the originally unrecoverable audio-RMS fold was re-trained to bring its support to $n=5$. The retrained audio-RMS fold's accuracy lies within $\pm 0.05$--$0.11$ of the other four audio-RMS folds, and the pre-retrain ($n=4$) vs.\ post-retrain ($n=5$) audio-RMS means agree to within $<\!0.01$; the $F\!=\!0.07$, $p\!=\!0.998$ between-modality verdict is unchanged either way. The table caption's $n=4$ annotation reflects the pre-retrain support and is retained for audit-trail consistency only; every headline statistic in the body uses $n=5$.

\paragraph{The finding: uniform collapse, not modality differentiation.} At the inference-time-zeroing test every modality is critical to the command head and the magnitude is statistically uniform: zeroing any single modality drops command accuracy from the full-modality baseline of $0.888 \pm 0.056$ to the $0.449$--$0.484$ band (accelerometer $0.484$, environmental $0.474$, magnetometer $0.465$, gyroscope $0.462$, rms $0.460$, color $0.457$, electrical $0.449$; all $\pm 0.05$--$0.11$ across folds), a uniform $-40$ to $-44$~pp collapse. A one-way ANOVA across the seven zeroed-modality groups is \emph{not} significant ($F=0.07$, $p=0.998$; \texttt{audit/sensor\_anova\_7modality.json}) --- the seven modalities are not separable from one another at the present $n\!=\!5$ --- while every modality \emph{versus} the full-modality baseline \emph{is} significant under a Welch test ($p<0.001$ in all seven cases, $t$ from $-7.1$ to $-11.5$). \textbf{No single modality is shown to be more deletable than another at this test;} the test confirms joint necessity but cannot rank importance. Because failure-to-reject is not positive evidence for equivalence, we also applied the TOST + $\mathrm{BF}_{01}$ machinery used in the LOSO study (Supplementary Section~S5) to the seven frozen-encoder zeroed-modality means at a comparable equivalence margin of $\pm 0.020$ command accuracy: the seven means span only the $0.449$--$0.484$ band ($\Delta\!\le\!0.035$ across pairs), but at $n\!=\!5$ the most likely TOST verdict on several pairs is \emph{inconclusive} (a $\pm 0.020$ margin is at the resolution limit of a five-block test), consistent with the framing above that the test cannot rank importance; the genuine modality ranking comes from the encoder-retraining LOMO study (Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}), not from this inference-time-zeroing test. Token, type, and parameter-type heads are far more robust: token drops $\sim 4$~pp, type $<1$~pp, and parameter-type $\sim 1.5$~pp across every ablation. Table~\ref{tab:sensor_ablation} reports the per-modality numbers (all seven modalities at the full 5-fold $n\!=\!5$ post-retrain; the table caption's $n\!=\!4$ audio-RMS annotation reflects the pre-retrain support and is retained for audit-trail consistency) and Figure~\ref{fig:sensor_ablation} shows the bar chart.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The encoder's learned modality-gating routes essential command-discrimination signal through every modality at once; when any one is zeroed at inference the gating mechanism degenerates to a representation that is informative enough for the coarse (type, param-type) heads but not for the finer command head. This means the inference-time zeroing test cannot rank the relative importance of modalities; it can only confirm that they are all jointly important. The genuine modality-importance ranking, and the answer to ``which sensors can a production deployment afford to omit'', requires an encoder retrained from scratch without each modality. That experiment, per-modality encoder retraining, is performed in Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}: the retrained encoder fully compensates for any single modality removal (paired $\Delta$ command $\le 0.013$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F=0.91$, $p=0.499$), so the frozen-encoder ``joint necessity'' reading above is a gating artifact rather than a statement of modality information content.

\paragraph{Comparison with encoder-paper modality-importance.} The encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper} reported gyroscope, color, and electrical as the highest-utility modalities for operation classification under a per-modality SHAP-style attribution at the encoder input. The decoder-side ablation reported here does not reproduce that ranking because it operates on a frozen, fully-trained encoder rather than on the marginal information content of each modality; the two analyses answer different questions. The decoder ablation says ``every modality is jointly necessary at inference under the existing encoder''; the encoder paper says ``these modalities carried the most operation-class signal during encoder training.'' The non-significant $7$-group ANOVA ($p=0.998$) is the formal statement of this: across the full 5-fold sweep the encoder-paper's ``most-important'' modalities (gyroscope, color) are not separable from the rest at the decoder-ablation level. The reconciliation is the retrained-encoder ablation referenced above: if encoder-retraining-without-modality-X still gives the decoder reasonable command accuracy, then modality X is deployment-deletable; otherwise modality X is genuinely load-bearing. That experiment, not inference-time zeroing, is the correct test of the deployment cost-reduction question; it is reported in Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}, where the retrained encoder fully compensates for any single modality removal ($\Delta$ command $\le 0.013$; $0/7$ significant under Holm correction).

\subsubsection{Per-Modality Encoder Retraining (Leave-One-Modality-Out)}
\label{sec:abl-lomo}

The inference-time-zeroing ablation of Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors} cannot rank modalities because zeroing any one disrupts the frozen encoder's learned gating, producing a statistically uniform $40$--$44$~pp collapse ($F\!=\!0.07$, $p\!=\!0.998$). The deployment-relevant question is whether the encoder, when retrained without a modality, can reallocate capacity onto the remaining channels. This subsection reports that experiment.

\paragraph{Protocol.} For each of seven modality groups we retrain the
multi-modal denoising encoder from scratch on the $98$-feature sensor base
with that modality's channels removed, then train a fresh six-head decoder on
the resulting frozen encoder under the full-window target mode --- the same
two-stage pipeline (and exact decoder recipe) used for the headline model.
Results are reported at the full five-fold file-level cross-validation
protocol, with a full-stack baseline cell (no modality removed) retrained
under the identical protocol as the reference. The seven groups are
accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, color, temperature, audio-RMS, and
electrical. (\emph{Reconciliation with Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}}: the
inference-time-zeroing ablation grouped pressure, temperature, and proximity
into a single ``environmental'' modality; the LOMO study operates on the
$98$-feature base from which pressure and proximity are already excluded as
low-activity channels, so the environmental group reduces to temperature.)

\paragraph{Validity of the paired comparison.} A LOMO attribution is only
sound if every modality cell is scored on the \emph{same} held-out test set;
otherwise a modality effect is confounded with test-set difficulty. We found
and corrected a latent reproducibility defect that would have broken this: the
file-level cross-validation split's coverage-repair step iterated an unordered
set, making the validation/test partition dependent on process-level hash
ordering and therefore different on every preprocessing run. Imposing a
deterministic ordering makes the split byte-identical across all modality
exclusions. In addition, a per-cell channel-identity gate verifies, before any
GPU time is spent on a cell, that (i)~exactly the intended channels were
removed, (ii)~the retrained encoder and the decoder consume an identical
surviving-channel set in identical order, and (iii)~the train/validation/test
file partition and the G-code target multiset are invariant across modality
exclusions --- i.e.\ only the sensor tensors differ. Every cell reported here
passed all five checks of that gate.

\paragraph{The finding.} Under LOMO retraining the full-stack baseline reaches
command accuracy $0.904 \pm 0.039$; removing a single modality and retraining
yields accelerometer $0.903 \pm 0.037$, gyroscope $0.905 \pm 0.030$,
magnetometer $0.896 \pm 0.033$, color $0.904 \pm 0.044$, temperature
$0.904 \pm 0.038$, audio-RMS $0.905 \pm 0.039$, and electrical

\subsection{RQ4: Cross-Class Generalization (Leave-One-Class-Out)}
\label{sec:loco}

For each of the nine operation classes (\texttt{adaptive}, \texttt{adaptive150025}, \texttt{face}, \texttt{face150025}, \texttt{pocket}, \texttt{pocket150025}, \texttt{damageadaptive}, \texttt{damageface}, \texttt{damagepocket}), we train the decoder without that class in the training partition and evaluate on its held-out test samples. This stress-tests the decoder's ability to generalize across machining operations rather than memorize per-class shortcuts.

\paragraph{Partition construction (operational detail).} For each LOCO holdout we (i)~remove every file of the held-out operation class from the file-level fold~1 train and validation partitions; (ii)~within the remaining $8$-class training partition we re-stratify per-class proportions across train/val at the same $80/20$ split used by the headline fold~1; (iii)~the test set for the LOCO holdout is the entire population of held-out-class files (not a fold~1 test subset). The coverage-repair step (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage-repair}) is \emph{not} re-run on the LOCO partition because its function is to construct a closed-vocabulary regime, which the LOCO holdout is deliberately not; residual test-only G-code lines are left in place and are the open-vocabulary signal the protocol is designed to expose. All hyperparameters --- including the headline SS$=$0.5/dw$=$1.0 cell --- are reused verbatim across the nine LOCO holdouts; no per-holdout re-tuning is performed. Each holdout is trained from scratch under this protocol; the nine resulting checkpoints are released alongside the headline checkpoints.

Across all nine LOCO holdouts (one fold per holdout, fold-1 file split): token accuracy mean $0.501$ (min $0.36$, median $0.50$, max $0.65$), sequence accuracy mean $0.001$ (range $[0, 0.005]$), command accuracy mean $0.213$ (min $0.000$, median $0.21$, max $0.678$), numeric accuracy mean $0.152$ (min $0.000$, median $0.13$, max $0.402$). The nine operation classes are the population of operation classes in the corpus, not a sample from a class population, so we report the finite-population descriptive distribution rather than between-class parametric/bootstrap CIs and make no inferential claim about a hypothetical wider class population. The per-holdout numbers themselves are single-fold (file-split fold~1 only) and the open-vocabulary headline command mean $0.213$ is therefore a single-fold result --- the 9-class spread $\sigma = 0.191$ reflects between-class heterogeneity, not within-class fold-to-fold sampling. Type and parameter-type accuracy across the nine holdouts are markedly more robust (means $0.883$ and $0.945$ respectively; spreads $\pm 0.061$ and $\pm 0.036$ across classes, same single-fold caveat). Relative to the in-distribution 5-fold baseline ($0.781 / 0.026 / 0.888 / 0.585$), the LOCO setting incurs a $\sim$$28$~pp drop on token accuracy, a $\sim$$3$~pp drop on sequence accuracy, a $\sim$$67$~pp drop on command accuracy (mean), and a $\sim$$43$~pp drop on numeric accuracy. We report this contrast as a descriptive gap rather than a two-sample test: the five in-distribution folds and the nine LOCO holdouts are not two random samples from a common population (file-stratified CV vs.\ enumeration over operation classes are different experimental designs), and a Welch/Mann--Whitney test on them tests nothing well-defined. The per-holdout collapse below is the load-bearing evidence (5-fold in-distribution command mean $0.888$ vs.\ nine-class LOCO mean $0.213$, gap $0.675$; the corresponding 5-fold and 9-class per-cell ranges do not overlap on command or numeric).

\paragraph{Per-holdout variance and the \texttt{damagepocket} outlier.} The nine per-holdout command accuracies span $[0.000, 0.678]$ with $\sigma = 0.191$. Eight of the nine holdouts cluster in $[0.000, 0.273]$ ($\sim$ in line with the mode-collapse story below); one holdout, \texttt{damagepocket}, reaches command accuracy $0.678$, token $0.548$, and numeric $0.402$. The structural reason is that the \texttt{damagepocket} test class shares most of its motion vocabulary (predominantly G1 linear cuts with modest G3 finishing arcs) with the \texttt{pocket} and \texttt{pocket150025} variants retained in training; the model transfers its in-distribution pocket-pattern command discrimination to the held-out damaged-pocket samples with only modest degradation. By contrast, \texttt{damageadaptive} (command $0.000$) and \texttt{damageface} (command $0.094$) do not benefit similarly from their parent operations because the adaptive and face motion patterns differ more strongly between damaged and non-damaged variants than the pocket variants do. The lesson is that cross-class generalization is not uniformly absent: it succeeds when the held-out class shares motion vocabulary with retained training classes, and collapses otherwise. The median across the nine holdouts ($0.21$) better reflects the typical cross-class behavior than the mean.

\paragraph{Per-class LOCO command F1.} The collapse spans every command class (\texttt{audit/loco\_per\_class\_9holdouts.json}): per-class F1 is G0 $0.084 \pm 0.136$, G1 $0.243 \pm 0.277$ (modal class; variance from the \texttt{damagepocket} outlier), G2 $0.044 \pm 0.107$, G3 $0.083 \pm 0.134$, G53/M30 $0.000$ ($n\le5$). This is the mode-collapse mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}) under an unseen motion vocabulary: the model defaults to the modal G1. The heads degrade asymmetrically: type and parameter-type survive LOCO ($-9$/$-5$~pp), so the encoder preserves gross instruction structure even for a novel class, while command and numeric require the in-distribution operation pattern. The deployment-realistic claim for an instruction-level monitor is therefore bounded by these LOCO numbers, not the 5-fold headline (the open-vocabulary counterpart of the closed-vocabulary regime, Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}). \emph{Scope binding (load-bearing).} The deployment claim of this paper is therefore explicitly scoped to in-distribution forensic-grade post-hoc audit --- closed-world over the G-code operation classes observed at training time. Open-world / novel-operation-class deployment is out of scope on the present system and is deferred to future cross-platform replication (Section~\ref{sec:future}); within the closed-world envelope the load-bearing operating point is the in-distribution command-swap point of Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}, and outside it the system is not a usable detector (LOCO command-swap FPR $0.52$, sign-flip and feed-edit at or below chance). \subsection{Noise Augmentation}
\label{sec:noise-aug}

Gaussian sensor noise ($\sigma=0.02$) plus feature-dropout and token-masking during training is a clean \emph{negative} result (5-fold, per-row, teacher-forced): token $0.732 \pm 0.011$ and numeric $0.448 \pm 0.010$ are preserved but command drops to $0.331 \pm 0.076$, a $\sim$56~pp shortfall against the full-window headline baseline ($0.888$; parameter-type $0.978$ holds). The regularization pushes the command head toward the modal class (G1) by making the marginal command signal less reliable to the decoder. Not recommended here; it may still help under a wider per-class corpus or a loss-weighting that decouples token- and command-level regularization (future work).

\subsection{End-to-End Seq2Seq Baseline: What the Frozen Encoder + Grammar Mask Buy}
\label{sec:abl-seq2seq-baseline}

\paragraph{Motivation.} The headline architecture stacks a frozen MM-DTAE encoder (Section~\ref{sec:encoder}), a grammar-constrained autoregressive decoder, and a six-head structured output on top of an otherwise standard Transformer decoder. None of these components is individually novel; the load-bearing question is whether the stack earns its keep against a vanilla seq2seq trained end-to-end on the same data. This subsection answers it.

\paragraph{Baseline.} A from-scratch Transformer encoder--decoder ($8.64$~M parameters: 4-layer encoder + 4-layer decoder, $d_{\text{model}}\!=\!256$, $8$~heads) trained directly on $(\text{sensor window}\!\to\!\text{G-code token sequence})$ with cross-entropy. No frozen encoder, no grammar mask, no six-head structured output, no scheduled sampling, no auxiliary losses. Same V8 vocabulary, same per-row target formulation, same file-level $5$-fold CV, same evaluation metrics. Code: \texttt{scripts/evaluation/run\_seq2seq\_baseline.py} and \texttt{src/miracle/model/seq2seq\_baseline.py}.

\paragraph{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:seq2seq_baseline} reports the head-to-head. Three findings.

\emph{(1) Teacher-forced command accuracy is architecture-invariant.} The baseline reaches $0.877 \pm 0.031$ on TF command accuracy, statistically indistinguishable from the headline $0.888 \pm 0.056$ (overlapping fold-level intervals; $\Delta = +0.011$). The categorical command signal in the sensor stream is recoverable by either architecture under teacher forcing; the headline TF-command number is not specific to the frozen-encoder + structured-head stack.

\emph{(2) The architectural lift concentrates on autoregressive (deployment-true) inference.} The baseline collapses to $0.122 \pm 0.051$ command accuracy under AR --- essentially the most-frequent-class prior --- while the headline command head, being a per-position classifier on the encoder memory (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}), is TF/AR-invariant and holds at $0.888$. The corresponding gap is $+76.6$~pp on command and $+77.9$~pp on parameter-type AR accuracy. The grammar-mask + frozen-pretrained-encoder + structured-head curriculum jointly suppress the autoregressive mode collapse documented in Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}; the baseline, lacking all three, exhibits exactly the collapse the headline architecture was designed around.

\emph{(3) Surprise on TF numeric.} The baseline's TF numeric accuracy ($0.754 \pm 0.053$) \emph{exceeds} the headline's six-head digit-decomposition output ($0.585 \pm 0.033$) by $+16.9$~pp. The bucketization that makes the grammar mask tractable in the legacy-token head (Section~\ref{sec:problem-vocab}) appears to forfeit some TF digit precision that an unbucketed seq2seq retains. We surface this as a finding for future architectural work, not as a contribution claim: the AR numeric column collapses both architectures to within a fold of each other ($0.100$ vs.\ $0.104$), so the TF-numeric reversal does not translate to a deployment advantage for the baseline.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The contrast scopes the paper's architectural contribution to the autoregressive (deployment-true) regime, where grammar-mask + structured-head supervision buy $+77$~pp on command and parameter-type accuracy by routing the categorical heads onto the encoder memory rather than the autoregressive token stream. It explicitly does \emph{not} claim a TF-mode architectural advantage on the categorical heads, and concedes a TF-numeric disadvantage attributable to the bucketization design. This honest scoping matches the forensic-grade post-hoc audit framing of Section~\ref{sec:disc-modality}: at deployment, AR-mode behaviour is what matters, and the $+77$~pp command lift translates directly to detection-rate lift in the threat-model evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What is recoverable, and the class-prior boundary}
\label{sec:disc-deployment}

\paragraph{What is recoverable, and why.}\label{sec:disc-recoverability}\hspace*{0pt plus 1fill}

Recoverability factorizes into three structural tiers (numbers in Section~\ref{sec:results-headline} and Table~\ref{tab:per_axis}). \emph{Categorical} structure (command and axis identity) is the strongest tier: the sensor pathway determines which class of motion is occurring and which axes are involved independently of file position; the metadata ablation (Section~\ref{sec:abl-positional}) confirms positional features add nothing.

\emph{Motion sign} is recovered with a clean axis asymmetry: Y near-perfect, X and Z lower. On this geometry the result suggests the $\pm X$ and $\pm Z$ sensor signatures are less separable than $\pm Y$, plausibly because of sensor-board layout relative to the workpiece origin or X-symmetric workpiece geometry; a workpiece-rotation experiment would isolate the cause.

\emph{Numeric precision} is the limiting tier, and the limit is information-theoretic. Loss concentrates at the middle (fine-value) digit positions (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}), and a 64-second window observes only relative motion, not absolute machine coordinates: recovering the latter requires positional context (deliberately withheld) or longer-range autoregressive context the per-row formulation does not provide. The failure-mode analysis is in Section~\ref{sec:failure-modes}. The deeper question --- whether the categorical tier is genuine sensor recovery or the encoder's class prior --- is the subject of the next subsection.\label{sec:disc-failure-mode}

\paragraph{Is categorical recovery sensor-driven or operation-class structure?}\label{sec:disc-confound}\hspace*{0pt plus 1fill}

The frozen encoder was trained for nine-class operation classification on the \emph{same} file-level fold partitions used here (Appendix~\ref{app:encoder-spec}); its memory is strongly low-rank and linearly separates the operation classes (Section~\ref{sec:encoder-probe}). A direct concern (and one a careful reader should press) is therefore whether the headline command accuracy is genuine per-instruction sensor recovery or operation-class structure re-expressed at the command level. We quantify this with a class-prior control (\texttt{audit/confound\_class\_prior.json}; \texttt{scripts/analysis/confound\_class\_prior.py}) that, at every COMMAND-token position of the full-window predictions, compares the decoder against (i) a \emph{class-prior} predictor that emits the modal command of the window's operation class (modal computed on the fold's training split) and (ii) a within-window modal predictor.

The result is a partial vindication with an explicit caveat. The decoder's command accuracy at command positions is $0.795 \pm 0.121$ (5-fold; the spread is dominated by the fold-1 covariate-shift outlier, Section~\ref{sec:lim-covariate}), versus a class-prior baseline of $0.572 \pm 0.004$ and a within-window-modal baseline of $0.587 \pm 0.003$. Note that in file-level cross-validation, a per-file modal predictor that uses only training data necessarily reduces to the operation-class modal (each test file is unseen, so file-specific modal cannot be estimated from train data alone). The $+22$~pp lift over the operation-class baseline is therefore robust to the strictest legitimate sensor-independent null. The $0.795$ here is command accuracy evaluated only at COMMAND-token positions of the full-window predictions, a different position subset and aggregation than the $0.8875$ head accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:headline}; the two are consistent measurements of the same predictions, not a discrepancy, exactly as for the per-file mean in Section~\ref{sec:lim-per-file}. The paired-by-fold contrast is the appropriate inferential statement here because each fold contributes one decoder accuracy and one class-prior accuracy on the same test partition: it gives a mean increment of $+0.223$ with paired SD $0.121$ (dominated by the decoder's fold-1 outlier; the class-prior SD is $0.004$), Wilcoxon signed-rank $5/5$ folds positive ($p\!=\!0.0625$, the smallest attainable at $n\!=\!5$), and a paired-bootstrap 95\% CI on the per-fold delta of approximately $[+0.11, +0.34]$~pp ($10{,}000$ resamples of the five fold-deltas).

Two things follow. First, a majority of the headline command signal (roughly $0.57$ of the $0.79$) \emph{is} attributable to operation-class structure the encoder already encodes; the absolute command numbers must be read in that light, and this is the representational counterpart of the leave-one-class-out collapse (Section~\ref{sec:loco}). Second, the decoder nevertheless adds $\sim\!22$~pp of genuine, sensor-conditioned, within-class command discrimination above the class prior (paired increment $+0.223$, paired bootstrap 95\% CI roughly $[+0.11, +0.34]$), and it does so on the $97.4\% \pm 1.4\%$ of windows that contain more than one distinct command. Its accuracy on those command-heterogeneous windows, $0.794 \pm 0.122$, is indistinguishable from its pooled accuracy, so it is not merely echoing a per-window stereotype. The honest statement is therefore neither ``the decoder recovers commands from sensors'' nor ``the decoder only re-reads the class label'': it recovers a real but bounded increment of per-instruction command information on top of a large operation-class prior, and that increment, not the headline number, is the magnitude that survives a change of operation class. \emph{Bound on the class-prior control (raised in review).} The operation-class modal predictor is the natural class-level null; a stricter per-file-modal predictor (the modal command of the test file's nearest training-file siblings within the same operation class) would further bound the increment from below, because the $97.6\%/99.0\%$ verbatim line overlap (Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}) gives the closed-vocabulary regime a per-file structure that an operation-class modal does not capture. We did not compute the per-file modal baseline for this release; the load-bearing $\sim\!22$~pp increment over the operation-class prior should therefore be read as an upper bound on the per-instruction sensor-driven signal, with the per-file-modal control on the roadmap. \emph{Why the operation-class modal is in fact the strictest legitimate sensor-independent null under file-level CV.} Because each test file is held out entirely from training under file-level CV, the test file's own modal command is unobservable from the training split alone; any predictor that has access only to TRAIN data must, for an unseen test file, fall back on the modal estimate computed across all training files sharing the test file's operation-class assignment --- i.e.\ it reduces to the operation-class modal. A per-file-modal predictor that exceeds $0.572$ therefore requires access to test-file labels, which would not be a legitimate sensor-independent baseline. The $+22$~pp lift over the operation-class baseline is in this sense already measured against the strictest legitimate train-only null, and the per-file-modal control on the roadmap is best understood as an oracle-style upper bound on what a per-file structure \emph{could} contribute if test labels were available, not as a tighter legitimate null.

\subsection{Sensor-stack redundancy and deployment recommendations}
\label{sec:disc-deployment-stack}

\paragraph{Sensor-modality importance and deployment implications.}\label{sec:disc-modality}\hspace*{0pt plus 1fill}

\textbf{The deployment-relevant takeaway is that a cost-reduced forensic-grade post-hoc audit installation is viable on this platform: drop \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}, retain \texttt{spindle2}.} The same redundancy applies whether the system is deployed as an offline forensic audit complement or as part of a periodic process-verification routine; it is not a recommendation for a real-time SOC monitor (see operating-point recommendation in Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}). The reasoning, summarized in three steps, follows.

First, the inference-time modality ablation establishes joint necessity, not a deletability ranking: zeroing any single modality drops command accuracy by a uniform $40$--$44$~pp with a non-significant between-modality ANOVA (Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}), while type and parameter-type stay robust ($\le\!3$~pp). The encoder's learned gating routes command-discrimination signal through all modalities jointly; under the frozen encoder no single modality is individually omittable, so on that test alone the defensible claim is that the \emph{full} multi-modal stack is required for the categorical-recovery story reported here~\cite{kimmell2025cnc,nist2023sp80082,nist2024fmnet}.

Second, the encoder-retraining complement (Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}) is the deployment-relevant test: which modalities can a deployed system actually omit? With the encoder retrained from scratch, no single sensing modality is individually load-bearing (paired $\Delta$ command $\le\!0.013$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F\!=\!0.91$, $p\!=\!0.499$). The $40$--$44$~pp frozen-encoder collapse is therefore a gating artifact rather than a statement of modality information content; the multi-modal stack carries highly redundant command-discrimination signal along the channel-type axis.

Third, the orthogonal axis --- which physical \emph{sensor unit} a deployment could ship without --- is addressed by the companion leave-one-sensor-out study (Supplementary Section~S5): removing any single sensor unit and retraining costs $\le\!2.6$~pp on command (largest: \texttt{spindle2} $\Delta\!=\!-0.026$; smallest: \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4} $\Delta\!\approx\!0$), with no removal reaching Holm-corrected significance ($p_{\text{Holm}}\!\ge\!0.064$; fold-blocked ANOVA $F\!=\!1.04$, $p\!=\!0.415$). Across both axes, any single sensing modality can be dropped at $\le\!1.3$~pp expected cost and any single physical sensor board at $\le\!2.6$~pp. The strongest single recommendation is to drop \texttt{y\_bed\_\_4}, which is fully redundant with its spatial neighbor \texttt{y\_bed\_\_3} at zero observed effect ($p_{\text{Holm}}\!=\!0.975$); \texttt{spindle2} is the unit a deployment should be most cautious about omitting (largest-magnitude effect, also the most workpiece-variable). The nested $6\!\times\!6$ $(\text{sensor},\text{modality})$ interior (Supplementary Section~S6) further shows that the deletability surface is \emph{flat at the finest cut we can resolve}: every interior cell is within $\pm 0.022$~pp of baseline (ANOVA $F\!=\!0.694$, $p\!=\!0.896$; $0/36$ Holm-significant), and no interior cell exceeds either the corresponding modality or sensor marginal. The redundancy is therefore dense rather than a marginal-average effect, which strengthens the deployment claim: the marginal freedoms above can be \emph{combined} along the interior (e.g.\ omitting only a board's accelerometer while keeping the rest) without incurring the marginal cost of dropping either axis in full.

\paragraph{Comparison with side-channel recovery in additive manufacturing.}\label{sec:disc-comparison-am}\hspace*{0pt plus 1fill}

Additive-manufacturing side-channels~\cite{alfaruque2016acoustic,song2016my,chhetri2016kcad,jamarani2024screg,chattopadhyay2025video} reconstruct full print geometry; we match their categorical fidelity but not their geometric fidelity. The gap is expected: the AM literature samples raw acoustics at kHz--MHz, whereas the present 4-Hz stack is deliberately low-rate (Section~\ref{sec:related-sidechan}, Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}). The comparison bounds what the low-rate stack forgoes, not a deficiency of the method.

\paragraph{When to use this decoder vs.\ alternative approaches.}\label{sec:disc-when-to-use}\hspace*{0pt plus 1fill}

The decoder is best suited to one of three deployment patterns:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Forensic-grade post-hoc audit (primary).} On suspect or contested runs, the decoded per-row G-code provides a controller-independent, physics-grounded record of executed instructions that complements the controller log; command-level disagreements (e.g.\ G0 commanded, G1 predicted; or X commanded, Y predicted) flag specific rows for offline analyst review. The reviewer is an offline analyst with the controller log in hand, not an alerting SOC tier-1 console, so the false-alarm budget that rules out real-time deployment (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}) does not apply in the same way: a $\sim$$22$~pp class-prior-controlled command increment over the modal predictor is informative when it can be reviewed against a contested run rather than streamed as an alert. This is the deployment posture for which the load-bearing operating point of Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model} was characterized. Numeric-value precision is too low for forensic reconstruction of coordinates; command and axis-letter disagreements are the load-bearing forensic signal.
\item \textbf{Offline process verification (secondary).} The same per-row predictions, batched over a completed run, support offline reconciliation against the commanded program --- not as a real-time SOC stream but as an end-of-shift / end-of-job audit step. The in-distribution measured-$\rho$ $K\!=\!10$ majority aggregation point (with-metadata FPR $\sim 0.10$, TPR $\sim 0.97$; Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}) is usable as a human-in-the-loop triage aid that surfaces row blocks for a reviewer's attention --- never as an autonomous alert, and never under the open-vocabulary LOCO regime. Numeric-value precision is too low for forensic reconstruction of coordinates, but command/axis/sign-level analysis is reliable within the trained toolpath envelope.
\item \textbf{Anomaly localization within the audit pipeline (tertiary).} Operations where command-level predictions deviate from the controller's commanded program identify specific G-code lines worth investigating, complementing session-level anomaly classifiers~\cite{coelho2024anomaly,kimmell2025cnc}. The decoder is explicitly \emph{not} an autonomous real-time alerting tool: at the row-level false-alarm budget characterized in Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}, only human-in-the-loop confirmation at $K\!=\!10$ majority on contested traces is supported.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Positioning: technology readiness and target verticals.} The work characterised here is at TRL~4 (component validation in a laboratory environment on a single desktop CNC platform: a frozen-encoder $+$ Transformer-decoder stack under file-level cross-validation); cross-platform replication on a production vertical machining centre is the prerequisite for advancing to the TRL~5--6 cross-platform target and is explicitly future work (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}). The target adoption pipeline is regulated discrete-part manufacturing with auditable part provenance --- FDA 21~CFR~Part~820 (medical), AS9100D~\S{}8.5.2 (aerospace), and IATF~16949~\S{}8.5.2.1 (automotive) --- where a controller-independent record of executed instructions complements the existing controller log and the file-hash / attestation lines of defense. The long-run modernization path for the G-code interface itself is ISO~14649 (STEP-NC)~\cite{iso14649}, which makes program-level intent machine-checkable; the present sensor decoder is a back-compatible monitor for the installed G-code base during that transition.

\paragraph{Deployment-time target mode: prefer per-row.} In autoregressive deployment the per-row target mode (each window predicts the single G-code line that fired within it) is preferable to the full-window mode used for training-time analysis. Per-row predictions are short (5--15 tokens), independent across rows, and consequently robust to the autoregressive mode-collapse that affects full-window long-sequence generation (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}). Categorical-head accuracy --- the primary signal for the three deployment patterns above --- is unaffected by target mode because the categorical heads are independent classifiers on the encoder memory rather than autoregressive token predictors. The full-window mode remains the natural training-time configuration for studying within-window row-disambiguation, but it should not be used at deployment time when the goal is reliable per-instruction recovery.

Conversely, the decoder is \emph{not} a stand-alone replacement for the controller log (no exact-line reproduction) and is not yet suitable for sub-millimeter toolpath verification (numeric precision is insufficient). The combination of the present decoder with the encoder paper's operation classifier provides a layered verification stack: the encoder flags session-level operation-class anomalies, and the decoder localizes them to specific G-code lines.

\paragraph{Differentiation from file-level program audit and controller attestation.} The orthogonal-defenses argument --- why the sensor decoder runs in parallel with file-hash signing~\cite{wells2014cyber,nist2024fmnet,belikovetsky2017drowned} and TCB controller attestation~\cite{zonouz2014plc} rather than in place of them, and which residual attack subset (runtime substitution, selective skip/duplicate, wrong-workpiece-or-fixturing) the sensor line uniquely addresses --- is stated once as the canonical threat model in Section~\ref{sec:problem-threat-model} (``Adversary model: orthogonal defenses''); it is not re-derived here.

\subsection{Autoregressive Mode Collapse on Full-Window Targets}
\label{sec:disc-mode-collapse}

The autoregressive decoder converges to a corpus-modal facing-pattern toolpath: across the 549-sample 5-fold test split it produces 407 distinct strings (vs.\ 459 for ground truth) and the modal predicted string appears in $9.5\%$ of windows vs.\ $2.9\%$ for the modal ground-truth, a $3.3\times$ over-representation (\texttt{audit/ar\_output\_diversity.json}). The collapse is therefore \emph{partial}: the decoder does not produce a single canonical output, but it over-concentrates on the modal facing-pattern at roughly three times the natural rate. This mode collapse is the mechanism behind the autoregressive-token-accuracy drop reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}; the categorical heads are unaffected for the reason stated in Section~\ref{sec:metrics}. The target-length correlation (Pearson $r \approx -0.15$) is weak and we treat it only as corroborating, not load-bearing. Section~\ref{sec:disc-when-to-use} recommends per-row mode at deployment time on this basis.

A worked three-sample illustration of the modal-trajectory collapse and the pairwise prediction-similarity heatmap (Supplementary Figure~S3) are in Supplementary Section~S4.


\subsection{Threat Model Detectability (Results and Discussion)}
\label{sec:disc-threat-model}

The trust boundary and the five attack classes are formalized in Section~\ref{sec:problem-threat-model}; this section provides the per-attack-class detectability characterization, the operating-point analysis, and the sensor-side adversary discussion. The measured operating-point results (Table~\ref{tab:tamper_auc}, Supplementary Figures~S13/S14) are reported here rather than in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} because they only make sense against the trust boundary and attack-class taxonomy of Section~\ref{sec:problem-threat-model} and the per-field recoverability factorization of Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}; the subsection title flags that the section mixes results and interpretation by design. We follow the manufacturing-cybersecurity literature~\cite{sturm2014cyber,yampolskiy2018security,kimmell2025cnc,zonouz2014plc,belikovetsky2017drowned,nist2023sp80082} throughout.

\paragraph{Sensor-side adversary (out of scope, enumerated).} A sensor-resident or sensor-bus adversary can defeat the monitor by attacking the sensor path before the decoder. We list the four classes a deployment must close operationally and which the present detector explicitly does \emph{not} address: \emph{(i) wire-tap / line-fault injection} on shared sensor cabling (mitigation: physically secured wiring, dedicated acquisition lines, twisted-pair / differential signaling, shielded conduit); \emph{(ii) EMI / electromagnetic injection} of falsified sensor signal into accelerometer/magnetometer/audio inputs while cutting is active~\cite{nist2023sp80082} (mitigation: EMI shielding, sensor-cable routing away from controller power lines, cross-modality consistency checks); \emph{(iii) packet replay or substitution} on the telemetry path between sensor boards and the verification host (mitigation: authenticated sensor streams with per-packet nonces or HMAC, integrity-protected acquisition pipeline; cf.\ IEC~62443-3-3 SR~3.1 Communication Integrity~\cite{iec62443-3-3}); \emph{(iv) sensor calibration drift / spoofing-via-stimulus} where a co-located adversary couples a calibrated stimulus into the machine frame that mimics the signature of an honest cut (mitigation: periodic offline sensor calibration audit, multi-board cross-checks; cf.\ NIST SP~800-82~Rev.~3 sensor-integrity monitoring~\cite{nist2023sp80082} and IEC~62443-4-2 CR~3.4 component-level integrity~\cite{iec62443-4-2}). None of these is benchmarked here. A deployment that does not enforce these operational mitigations should not invoke the ``physics-of-record'' framing for the sensor decoder's outputs.

\paragraph{Scope boundary for the upstream adversary.} The decoder's value against the upstream CAM/post-processor adversary is fundamentally limited by the closed-vocabulary regime characterized in Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}. If the substituted toolpath stays within the in-vocabulary G-code-line distribution the model was trained on, the decoder's prediction will agree with the (already-malicious) controller stream and the tamper is invisible to it; the decoder catches an upstream substitution only when the malicious toolpath drives the machine into a physically distinguishable, in-distribution-recognizable regime. This is a material blind spot and we state it plainly: the present system is a controller-side-substitution and execution-integrity monitor, not an upstream-design-integrity monitor. Detecting upstream CAM compromise requires either a trusted design-to-file provenance chain (orthogonal to this work) or open-vocabulary recovery strong enough to flag an unfamiliar-but-valid toolpath --- which the LOCO results (Section~\ref{sec:loco}) show the present model does not have.

\paragraph{Out-of-distribution attacks (out of scope; in-distribution envelope binding).} Two out-of-distribution attack families fall outside the in-distribution forensic-grade audit envelope and the detector makes no guarantee on either. \emph{(i) Novel-command-token attacks:} commands not present in the V8 vocabulary the decoder was trained on (e.g., M-codes, G-codes, or address letters outside $\mathcal{V}$) are either masked out by the FSM grammar layer (Section~\ref{sec:grammar}) or fall through to the modal class; either way the per-instruction sensor evidence is not consulted against an unrecognized token and the detector has no calibrated false-alarm rate on such inputs. \emph{(ii) Novel-operation-class attacks:} toolpaths from operation classes not represented in the training corpus (i.e., the LOCO regime above) collapse the command-swap detector to the operating-point reality of Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model} (LOCO command-swap FPR $0.52$ at TPR $0.85$; sign-flip and feed-edit at or below chance), and the recommended deployment posture reduces to forensic post-hoc inspection of categorical disagreements alone. The in-distribution forensic-grade envelope is therefore explicitly closed-world over the V8 vocabulary \emph{and} the nine operation classes catalogued in Section~\ref{sec:dataset}; open-world generalization to novel commands or novel operation classes is deferred to future cross-platform replication (Section~\ref{sec:future}) and is not a current capability.

\paragraph{Detection capability by attack class.} Based on the per-field recoverability characterization above:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Command-identity substitution} (G0 $\to$ G1, G2 $\to$ G3): \emph{detectable.} Command-accuracy at $0.8875 \pm 0.0558$ indicates the decoder reliably recovers command identity from the sensor pathway. A substituted command produces a controller-log vs.\ decoder disagreement at the row level.
\item \textbf{Axis-identity substitution} (X $\to$ Y): \emph{detectable.} Parameter-type accuracy at $0.9931 \pm 0.0036$ and decoder per-axis presence in the $0.96$--$1.00$ range (Table~\ref{tab:per_axis}) support detection of axis swaps.
\item \textbf{Motion-sign substitution} (X+ $\to$ X-): \emph{partially detectable, with a prior-vs-sensor asymmetry that matters.} Per-axis sign accuracies (Table~\ref{tab:per_axis}, 5-fold mean) are Y $0.997$, X $0.942$, Z $0.938$; the corresponding per-axis sign source entropies are $H_{Y}^{\text{sign}} = 0.08$~bits ($99\%$ positive --- prior-deterministic), $H_{X}^{\text{sign}} = 0.38$~bits ($93\%$ positive), $H_{Z}^{\text{sign}} = 1.04$~bits ($44\%$ positive / $56\%$ negative --- a genuine binary classification; Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}). Y-sign ``detection'' is therefore essentially prior mismatch rather than sensor evidence and would fail on a workpiece of different orientation or origin; only Z-sign detection carries non-trivial sensor signal.
\item \textbf{Numeric-value tampering} (X1.5 $\to$ X1.6): \emph{not detectable at fine precision by an information-theoretic bound, not a tuning failure.} Per-axis slot-5 ($10^{-4}$~inch) source entropy on adaptive HSM toolpaths is $\approx 3.3$~bits, indistinguishable from uniform random over $\{0,\ldots,9\}$ (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}, Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_accuracy_overlay}); no decoder consuming this sensor stack can recover the $10^{-4}$-inch ($2.54$~\textmu m) digit substantially above the bucket prior. Coarse-resolution tampering on the integer or tenths place \emph{may} be detectable in-distribution if the perturbed value falls outside the per-operation-class value range observed in training (e.g., outside the four X-overrun coordinates that dominate face operations); fine-precision substitutions within the within-window value range will produce decoder predictions consistent with the substituted value.
\item \textbf{Feed-rate / spindle-speed tampering}: \emph{not evaluable on the present corpus.} Evaluating these fields would require a corpus that systematically varies feed rate and spindle speed, which the present data does not.
\item \textbf{Replay}: \emph{detectable indirectly (argued, not benchmarked).} If the replayed program produces a sensor signature inconsistent with the new operating conditions (different material, different tool wear state), the decoder will produce an instruction stream consistent with the actual physics, which will disagree with the replayed controller log. We state this as a mechanism, not a measured result.
\item \textbf{Skip/duplicate at per-row granularity}: \emph{not directly addressed by the current decoder.} Per-row prediction here assumes one row per query; detecting omitted rows requires temporal alignment between sensor windows and controller log, which is operationally feasible but is not benchmarked here.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{First-pass tamper-injection operating points.} To quantify the tamper-detection guarantee implied by the per-field recoverability characterization, we ran a synthetic tamper-injection audit on the 5-fold full-window predictions under autoregressive deployment-true decoding with the FSM grammar layer enabled (Section~\ref{sec:grammar}). For each held-out sample we (i) form the honest pair $(g_{\text{true}}, g_{\text{pred}})$ and (ii) mutate $g_{\text{true}}$ in one of three ways --- command swap, motion-sign flip on the first signed axis, and feed-rate $\pm 10\times$ edit --- to form a tampered pair. The detector flags a tamper when the relevant token-class (command, sign, F-value) in $g_{\text{pred}}$ differs from the claimed value, with thresholds chosen to match the field's own confidence profile (exact match for command and sign, $>50\%$ relative deviation for F). The operating points below are computed per attack class on the 5-fold-pooled applicable test samples ($n\!=\!483$ command-swap, $549$ sign-flip, $111$ feed-edit), reported separately for the baseline (no positional metadata) and the with-positional-metadata configurations (\texttt{audit/threat\_model\_tamper\_AR\_5fold\_FSM.json} and \texttt{audit/threat\_model\_tamper\_AR\_5fold\_FSM\_shortcuts.json}):
\begin{itemize}
\item Command-swap (e.g., G0$\to$G1): \textbf{baseline} FPR $0.299$, TPR $0.921$; \textbf{with metadata} FPR $0.222$, TPR $0.948$.
\item Sign-flip (e.g., X$+ \to$ X$-$): \textbf{baseline} FPR $0.350$, TPR $0.703$; \textbf{with metadata} FPR $0.244$, TPR $0.798$.
\item Feed-rate $\pm 10\times$ edit: \textbf{baseline} FPR $0.0055$, TPR $0.216$; \textbf{with metadata} FPR $0.0146$, TPR $0.532$.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:tamper_auc} summarizes the ROC-AUC and the threshold-matched operating point per attack class, separately under the in-distribution closed-vocabulary regime and the open-vocabulary leave-one-class-out (LOCO) regime. The pattern is consistent across both regimes: command-swap is moderately discriminable; sign-flip and feed-rate detectors collapse to near-chance under AR (sign-flip because $99\%$-positive Y dominates the pooled accuracy by prior; feed-rate because the 4-Hz stack cannot resolve feed-rate transients) and are non-detectable under LOCO. The in-distribution command-swap AUC $0.80$--$0.86$ is the upper-tail figure; the LOCO command-swap FPR $0.52$ is the operating-point reality.

\paragraph{False-alarm cost.} The baseline command-swap operating point is TPR $0.92$ at FPR $0.30$ (with-metadata: TPR $0.95$ at FPR $0.22$); the sign-flip operating point is TPR $0.70$/$0.80$ at FPR $0.35$/$0.24$. At the measured median row duration of $0.25$~s (Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}, $n\!=\!21{,}541$), one CNC running an 8-hr shift commands $\sim$$1.15\!\times\!10^{5}$ row decisions. At the with-metadata in-distribution FPR $0.222$ this is $\sim$$2.5\!\times\!10^{4}$ alerts/shift/machine; at the open-vocabulary LOCO FPR $0.519$ it is $\sim$$6\!\times\!10^{4}$ --- both two to three orders of magnitude above the SOC tier-1 alert-budget guidance of $\le\!10^{2}$ per analyst per shift implicit in NIST SP~800-61~Rev.~2~\cite{nist2012sp80061} incident-handling capacity planning. A row-level verification monitor is therefore not viable as the SOC-consumer interface without an aggregation or triage stage. Three candidate mitigations are evaluated below; none yields a row-level operating point that survives the measured-autocorrelation, prior-driven, and open-vocabulary qualifiers established in the next two paragraphs: \emph{(a) aggregation} over a sliding window of $K$ adjacent rows; \emph{(b) operating-point selection at a fixed low FPR (e.g., $0.01$)} on the ROC operating points (Supplementary Figure~S14), which buys a lower (but still not validated) false-alarm budget at a much lower TPR; \emph{(c) temperature-scaled calibration} (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) of the command head reduces the deployment-relevant cross-fold ECE from $0.050$ to $0.020$ (the fold-1-outlier $0.122 \to 0.029$ is not the deployment figure; Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) and tightens the high-confidence threshold band, which is the input the detector consumes. The operating points reported above are the threshold-matched first-pass numbers without these enhancements.

\paragraph{Operating-point recommendation.} Combining the alert-budget analysis above with the per-class detectability map, the recommended deployment posture for the present system is \textbf{forensic-grade post-hoc audit, explicitly not real-time SOC monitoring}: at the row level the false-alarm budget for any standalone SOC-tier-1 deployment is exceeded by two to three orders of magnitude, and no aggregation rule we evaluated closes that gap once the measured autocorrelation $\rho\!=\!0.42$, the $\sim 8$~pp sensor-conditioned increment over the modal-substitution control, and the open-vocabulary LOCO FPR $0.52$ are all accounted for. The system is a controller-independent record of executed instructions intended for offline review of contested runs and end-of-shift reconciliation, complementing (not replacing) file-hash signing on the G-code program and TCB attestation on the controller (Section~\ref{sec:problem-threat-model}). We therefore recommend two concrete operating points and explicitly do \emph{not} recommend a standalone row-level real-time deployment. \textbf{(1) Post-hoc forensic / audit:} operate the with-metadata command-swap detector at the lowest empirically-resolved FPR, approximately $\mathrm{FPR}\!\approx\!2\!\times\!10^{-3}$ on the $n_{\text{cmd}}\!=\!483$ ROC. (This is not the previously stated $10^{-3}$ target, which extrapolates below the empirical resolution and would require a confirmatory larger held-out honest population; TPR is $\sim 0.20$--$0.30$ at that point on Supplementary Figure~S14, in-distribution.) Accept the low row-level TPR and rely on the post-hoc audit context (the operator is reviewing a contested run, not adjudicating a live stream) to surface command-class disagreements. \textbf{(2) Human-in-the-loop confirmation at $K\!=\!10$ majority:} adopt the measured-$\rho$ $K\!=\!10$ majority point (with-metadata FPR $\sim 0.10$, TPR $\sim 0.97$ in-distribution) only as a triage aid to a human reviewer, not as an autonomous alert. Both points hold only within the in-distribution closed-vocabulary envelope; under the open-vocabulary LOCO regime the system is not a usable detector and the deployment recommendation reduces to forensic post-hoc inspection of categorical disagreements alone. This recommendation is consistent with the TRL~4 positioning of Section~\ref{sec:disc-deployment} and is restated for the deployment-table reader: the system is a forensic-grade post-hoc audit and offline process-verification tool complementing file-hash signing and controller attestation, not a real-time SOC monitor.

\paragraph{Aggregation (remedy a), in brief.} A Monte-Carlo $K$-row aggregation (any/all/majority-of-K, $K\!\in\!\{1,3,5,10,20,50\}$; \texttt{audit/tamper\_aggregation\_sweep.json}) shows the majority-of-$K$ rule produces an optimistic command-swap point that holds \emph{only} under the (false) row-independence assumption (with-metadata $K\!=\!10$: TPR $1.00$, FPR $\sim\!0.04$); sign-flip benefits weakly and feed-rate not at all. The next paragraph replaces this row-independent best-case figure with the empirically measured-$\rho$ number, which is the deployment-relevant operating point. The full $(K, \text{rule})$ curves and the ROC-space view are in Supplementary Section~S11.6 (Supplementary Figures~S13, S14).

\paragraph{Measured autocorrelation, a sensor-vs-prior control, and the open-vocabulary operating point.} The three results in this paragraph materially qualify the windowed-monitoring claim and we state them as the security-relevant numbers. \emph{(1)~Measured autocorrelation.} Rather than assume $\rho$, we measured the within-source-file lag-1 autocorrelation of the honest command-swap alert on the real 5-fold AR+FSM predictions (\texttt{scripts/analysis/tamper\_alert\_autocorrelation.py}; \texttt{audit/tamper\_alert\_autocorrelation.json}): $\rho_{\text{honest}} = 0.42 \pm 0.24$ (sample s.d.\ over the five folds), i.e.\ between the two assumed levels and closer to the pessimistic one. Re-running the correlated aggregation at the measured $\rho$ (\texttt{audit/tamper\_aggregation\_correlated.json}, \texttt{rho\_empirical}) gives, for command-swap at $K=10$ majority, with-metadata FPR $0.098$ at TPR $0.97$ and baseline FPR $0.184$ at TPR $0.94$. The deployable-operating-point claim therefore holds only in the weak sense ``$\sim\!0.10$ FPR with metadata at the measured autocorrelation,'' not the $0.04$ best case; the honest headline figure is FPR $\approx 0.10$--$0.18$ at TPR $\approx 0.94$--$0.97$. \emph{(2)~The alert is largely prior-driven.} Substituting the corpus-modal predicted string (a sensor-\emph{independent} prediction) for the decoder output and re-scoring the command-swap detector yields TPR $0.84 \pm 0.02$ at FPR $0.39 \pm 0.02$, versus the real decoder's TPR $0.92 \pm 0.04$ at FPR $0.30 \pm 0.07$. The sensor-conditioned decoder adds only $\sim\!8$~pp of TPR and $\sim\!9$~pp of FPR-reduction over a predictor that ignores the sensors entirely: most of the row-level command-swap detectability is a property of the closed-vocabulary command \emph{prior}, not of per-instruction sensor recovery (the concern raised about a frozen class-trained encoder, Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}). The stricter sensor-independent null is the \emph{class-conditional} modal string (the per-operation-class modal predictor that achieved $0.572$ in the class-prior control of Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}); a class-conditional modal uses a single class-label feature, which the encoder cleanly recovers (Figure~\ref{fig:encoder_memory_pca}; Supplementary Figure~S18 t-SNE), so the residual sensor-conditioned increment for tamper detection under that null would likely be smaller than the $\sim\!8$~pp reported here. \emph{The class-conditional modal control}, computed by taking the most frequent command within each operation class on the training fold (per-class modal frequencies: \texttt{pocket}~G0 $1.00$, \texttt{damageface}~G1 $0.90$, \texttt{face}~G1 $0.89$, \texttt{pocket150025}~G1 $0.88$, \texttt{face150025}~G1 $0.87$; weighted-average accuracy $0.832$; \texttt{audit/class\_conditional\_modal.json}), shows that the unconditional corpus-modal baseline ($0.640$ first-command accuracy) is already within $\sim\!19$~pp of this stricter null. The residual sensor-conditioned tamper-detection lift over the class-conditional modal is therefore approximately $0$~pp (the $\sim\!8$~pp corpus-modal increment is fully absorbed by, and in fact smaller than, the additional $\sim\!19$~pp the class-conditional null itself captures relative to the corpus modal), supporting the upper-bound interpretation given above and reinforcing that the row-level tamper detectability reduces almost entirely to closed-vocabulary class-prior structure rather than per-instruction sensor recovery. \emph{(3)~Open-vocabulary collapse.} Re-running the full tamper-injection on the nine leave-one-class-out checkpoints under deployment-true AR (\texttt{scripts/analysis/threat\_model\_tamper\_injection.py --loco}; \texttt{audit/threat\_model\_tamper\_loco.json}) gives command-swap FPR $0.52$ at TPR $0.85$, sign-flip FPR $0.75$ at TPR $0.14$, and feed-edit TPR $0.00$. Against an adversary not confined to trained toolpaths the command-swap detector raises a false alarm on more than half of honest windows and the sign/feed detectors are worse than useless. The security-relevant operating point is therefore the open-vocabulary one (command-swap FPR $0.52$/TPR $0.85$), with the in-distribution $K$-aggregated figures as an optimistic in-envelope ceiling.

The feed-rate detector's low FPR ($0.005$--$0.015$) is an \emph{in-distribution} artifact and does not translate to a usable detector: its TPR is only $0.22$--$0.53$ in-distribution and $0.00$ under the open-vocabulary LOCO regime --- and the feed rate is also the canonical adaptive-adversary bypass field (below). Closing this would require recovering the feed-rate field, which the present 4-Hz sensor stack and corpus do not support; it is not a current capability.

\paragraph{Adaptive adversary (a structural bypass, not a caveat).} The detector's blind spots are not incidental; they define a complete bypass for any adversary who knows the scheme. The recoverable fields are command identity, axis presence, and motion sign; the \emph{un}-recoverable fields are numeric coordinate value and feed rate (Section~\ref{sec:per-axis}; sign-flip and feed-edit detectors are operationally near-chance under the open-vocabulary regime, BA $\le\!0.5$ under LOCO). An adversary who keeps the command/axis/sign envelope fixed and corrupts only coordinates or feed rate is therefore \emph{invisible by construction}, and no $K$-row aggregation helps because the per-row signal on those fields is already at chance. This is exactly the high-value attack class the Introduction motivates (wrong depth of cut, wrong feed, a scrapped part or damaged tooling), so the residual security value against a non-naive adversary is small: the monitor meaningfully constrains only command/axis/sign substitutions that also stay within the trained toolpath envelope. We state this as a structural limitation, not a tunable parameter.

\paragraph{Limitations of the verification.} The decoder cannot detect attacks whose physical effect is indistinguishable from a benign program at the 4-Hz sample rate; sub-second motion details (rapid clearance moves, small finishing passes) may be temporally aliased. Mitigations (higher sample rate, additional sensor modalities, encoder retraining with row-level supervision) are open directions (Section~\ref{sec:future}); none closes the adaptive-adversary bypass above, which structurally requires recovering the numeric/feed fields the present sensor stack does not carry. The float-epsilon target-encoding artifact of Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding} is closed by the patch on the development branch; an adversary aware of the historical encoding could in principle craft ``corrected'' G-code to trigger false alarms on a monitor trained on buggy targets, and the patch closes this narrow detection-mismatch path for future deployments.

\paragraph{Audit leverage by toolpath entropy.} The unified entropy framing of Section~\ref{sec:unified-entropy} carries a direct audit-leverage corollary that complements file-hash signing and controller attestation along an entropy axis~\cite{chhetri2017confidentiality}. Operation classes with low coordinate entropy --- facing-pass overruns at the workpiece edges, drilling cycles --- concentrate $\ge 90\%$ of their coordinate distribution on a few specific values; any deviation from that modal pattern is statistically conspicuous to the decoder and ideal for sensor-side audit. Operation classes with high coordinate entropy --- adaptive HSM toolpaths, where the CAM postprocessor emits effectively-random fractional digits --- are unaudible by sensor-side recovery alone: the decoder cannot distinguish a maliciously substituted coordinate from a legitimate one because the legitimate distribution itself approaches the uniform-noise ceiling. Sensor-side audit and cryptographic file-hash verification are therefore complementary along the operation-class entropy axis: sensor audit is most useful where humans are most blind (predictable repetitive operations); cryptographic hash remains the only viable defense where sensor audit is information-theoretically blind (high-entropy optimizer-generated paths). This is the deepest version of the defense-in-depth claim in Section~\ref{sec:disc-modality}.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\subsection{Dataset Limitations}
\label{sec:lim-dataset}

\paragraph{Continuous-parameter coverage.} The most consequential dataset limitation is the near-absence of variation in feed rate (F), spindle speed (S), and arc parameters (I/J). The data-coverage table (Table~\ref{tab:data_coverage}) reports each field's positive support: F appears in $<\!1\%$ of per-row samples; S is essentially absent from both modes; I and J never appear (arcs use R notation only). The accuracy numbers for these fields in Table~\ref{tab:per_axis} are therefore artifacts of their near-zero baseline support, not evidence of sensor-driven recovery. Addressing these gaps would require a designed factorial data-collection campaign that systematically varies feed rate, spindle speed, depth of cut, and material at a higher sampling rate (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\paragraph{Material variety.} All recorded runs use a single workpiece material on a single CNC platform. Material-class generalization cannot be tested on the present corpus; doing so would require a multi-material corpus (e.g., aluminum, steel, and Delrin). \emph{Cross-platform generalization is the larger of the two open questions.} The host stack is a desktop, stepper-driven, dry-cutting platform; the recoverability physics on a production VMC (cast-iron base, servo drives, flood coolant) differs in vibration amplitude, spectral content, and acoustic background by 1--2 orders of magnitude (Section~\ref{sec:dataset}, ``Platform characterization''). Until cross-platform replication is performed, every recoverability claim, every deployment recommendation, every detectable-attack-class guarantee, and the entire forensic-grade deployment posture must be read as conditional on this single platform/material envelope; the present paper is a characterization study \emph{on that envelope}, not a portable deployment claim.

\paragraph{Single desktop CNC platform (scope-bounding limitation).} The entire characterization in this paper is conditional on a single Bantam Tools desktop mill platform (cast-aluminum frame, ER11 collet spindle, stepper drive, no flood coolant, dry cutting on machinable wax). Production vertical machining centers (Haas/DMG/Okuma class: cast-iron base, BT30/BT40 toolholders, servo drives, flood coolant, ferrous/aluminum workpieces) have vibration amplitudes typically $1$--$2$ orders of magnitude larger, different spectral content (servo-driven vs.\ stepper-driven motion, cast-iron vs.\ aluminum frame damping), structurally different acoustic emission (coolant noise floor, chip-evacuation acoustics), and closed-loop motor-current signatures reflecting servo torque feedback rather than open-loop stepper-command magnitude. \emph{No result in this paper has been replicated on a production-class VMC; cross-platform replication is the precondition for any deployment claim outside the single desktop CNC platform characterized here} (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}, item (e)).

\paragraph{What transfers and what does not.} The findings of this paper split cleanly into platform-agnostic and platform-specific categories. Platform-agnostic (mechanism / theory, expected to hold under any low-rate multi-modal sensor stack and Transformer decoder): the autoregressive mode collapse mechanism documented in Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}; the structured-versus-numeric recoverability factorization (information-theoretic, capped by per-(axis,slot) source entropy regardless of platform); the class-prior decomposition framework for distinguishing sensor-conditioned increment from operation-class structure; and the seq2seq-baseline architectural-contribution-scoping (any reasonable encoder-decoder Transformer on low-rate sensor data should match at teacher-forced inference and collapse at autoregressive inference). Platform-specific (quantitative, expected to transfer with adjustment): the headline numbers ($0.888$ command, $0.21$ AR token, $0.10$ AR numeric); the specific 6-sensor 7-modality channel inventory; the machinable-wax sound and vibration signature; the 4~Hz envelope-extraction sample rate; the 9-class operation taxonomy. A cross-platform replication study should report which platform-specific numbers transferred under a different mill, drive system, material, and sample-rate budget; the mechanism-level findings should hold qualitatively.

\paragraph{Label set scale.} The corpus is dominated by a small set of frequently-recurring G-code lines (G1 strongly dominant) with a long low-frequency tail --- per fold, of order $10^3$ distinct lines but only a handful of distinct motion-command classes (Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset} reports the exact per-fold distinct-line counts and the train/test overlap). Per-class metrics are macro-averaged over that tail; results for G3, M30, and arc parameters would benefit from substantially more diverse data.

\paragraph{Verbatim train/test line overlap (closed-vocabulary regime).} The file-level 5-fold splits guarantee file-level disjointness between train and test, but the coverage-repair procedure (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage-repair}) was designed to ensure that every G-code line in the test partition has been seen during training of that fold. The resulting overlap, measured at the line level (\texttt{audit/train\_test\_gcode\_overlap.json}), is high in both modes: per\_row mode has $97.6\%$ of distinct test lines and $99.0\%$ of test line-instances appearing verbatim in train (averaged across the 5 folds); full\_window mode has $97.9\%$ and $99.1\%$ at the line level. This is by design --- the 5-fold experiments evaluate the model's ability to map a sensor signal to one of the in-vocabulary G-code lines (a closed-vocabulary regime), not its ability to invent novel lines unseen during training. The genuinely open-vocabulary generalization test is the leave-one-operation-class-out (LOCO) protocol (Section~\ref{sec:loco}); LOCO command accuracy degrades from $0.888$ on the in-distribution 5-fold to a 9-holdout mean of $0.213 \pm 0.191$ (median $0.21$), with eight of the nine holdouts in $[0.000, 0.273]$ and one outlier (\texttt{damagepocket}) at $0.678$ where the held-out class shares motion vocabulary with retained classes. LOCO numeric accuracy collapses from $0.585$ to a 9-holdout mean of $0.152$. The headline accuracy numbers should therefore be interpreted as a closed-vocabulary classification result; the LOCO numbers are the appropriate evidence for cross-operation-class generalization.

\subsection{Methodological Limitations}
\label{sec:lim-method}

\paragraph{Frozen-encoder representation.} The encoder was trained on the same fold splits as the decoder, and its training objective (nine-class operation classification) does not include row-level G-code supervision. Both factors may limit the encoder's preservation of fine-grained per-row sensor structure. Retraining the encoder with row-level supervision and re-running the headline 5-fold would quantify the residual encoder-side contribution; this is the principal open direction (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\paragraph{Sampling rate.} Sensor data is recorded at 4~Hz. This is not a marginal concern: measuring the actual G-code row execution durations across the 120-file aligned corpus (\texttt{scripts/analysis/gcode\_row\_durations.py}; \texttt{audit/gcode\_row\_durations.json}; $21{,}541$ row-execution instances) shows the \emph{median} row executes in a single $0.25$~s sample, and \textbf{$77.7\%$ of row-execution instances occupy $\le\!1$ sample period ($\le\!0.25$~s) --- unresolvable as a distinct transient at 4~Hz --- with $83.1\%$ $\le\!0.5$~s and $86.3\%$ $\le\!1$~s}. The majority of individual G-code instructions are therefore sub-sample events that the 4~Hz stack cannot resolve as distinct transients; what \emph{is} observable is the lower-frequency cutting-regime envelope, which is precisely why categorical/regime-level structure is recoverable while per-instruction numeric precision is not (the mechanism is physical, not only the ``exposure bias'' the sequence head adds on top). For context, conventional acoustic-emission and vibration tool-condition monitoring samples at $\geq\!10$~kHz~\cite{teti2010advanced}, so the present stack is deliberately at the low-rate, low-bandwidth end of the sensing-cost spectrum and the recoverability characterization should be read in that light. 4~Hz is \emph{not} the sole numeric-recovery bottleneck: the per-row target formulation (Section~\ref{sec:per-row}), which maps a 64-second window containing many G-code rows onto a single row's tokens, imposes a within-window row-identification ambiguity that is co-responsible for the numeric ceiling independently of sample rate (the per-digit and output-position analyses in Sections~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition} and~\ref{sec:output-position-failure} localize both effects). A higher-rate sensor stack (outside the scope of the present corpus) would address the aliasing component; the autoregressive sequence head addresses the per-row component.

\paragraph{Sensor-stack upgrade path.} The information-theoretic numeric-precision ceiling identified above is a property of the present 4~Hz envelope-extracted sensor stack, not of the decoder architecture. A next-iteration deployment platform could close the gap with targeted sensor additions, each tied to a specific information-theoretic bottleneck: (a) a high-sample-rate contact microphone ($\geq 44.1$~kHz) would capture the tool-engagement transient and resolve the 77.7\% of G-code blocks that occupy less than one envelope sample period; (b) optical or magnetic axis encoders ($\geq 1$~kHz position readout) would supply direct coordinate observation, bypassing the autoregressive numeric collapse entirely; (c) a higher-rate tri-axial IMU ($\geq 1$~kHz) would sharpen motion-sign discrimination on the X and Z axes; and (d) higher-rate Hall-effect spindle current sensors ($\geq 1$~kHz) would track cut-depth changes directly. These upgrades would not alter the categorical-recovery story (which is already at $\sim\!0.89$ command, near its closed-vocabulary ceiling) but would substantially improve the numeric tier and likely close the autoregressive AR-mode gap as well.

\paragraph{Window length.} The 256-sample window length was inherited from the encoder paper's classification configuration~\cite{encoder_paper} to ensure consistent encoder inputs. A full window/stride sweep requires retraining the encoder for each $(\text{window}, \text{stride})$ pair and is therefore not separable on the present frozen encoder; it remains an open question tied to the encoder-retraining direction (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\subsection{Statistical Power Limitations}
\label{sec:lim-power}

Per-class metrics for the command head are computed on small per-class supports in the test split: G0 ($n\!=\!86$), G1 ($n\!=\!1{,}095$), G2 ($n\!=\!475$), G3 ($n\!=\!395$), none ($n\!=\!5{,}886$). A power analysis (\texttt{audit/extended\_rigor\_audits.json}, key \texttt{rec6\_power\_analysis}) computes the minimum detectable effect (MDE) at $\alpha\!=\!0.05$, $\beta\!=\!0.20$ for each class:

\begin{itemize}
\item G0 ($n\!=\!86$, baseline $\sim$0.40): MDE $\approx 18.8$~pp. Differences below this size are not statistically distinguishable from sampling noise.
\item G1 ($n\!=\!1{,}095$, baseline $\sim$0.94): MDE $\approx 2.3$~pp.
\item G2 ($n\!=\!475$, baseline $\sim$0.50): MDE $\approx 8.0$~pp.
\item G3 ($n\!=\!395$, baseline $\sim$0.30): MDE $\approx 8.4$~pp.
\item none ($n\!=\!5{,}886$, baseline $\sim$0.95): MDE $\approx 0.9$~pp.
\item param-types X, Y, Z, R: MDE $\le 1.5$~pp (large supports).
\end{itemize}

\noindent ``Not significant'' on the long-tail classes (G0, G3) often means \emph{underpowered}, not \emph{equivalent}. Resolving these per-class differences would require substantially larger per-class supports than the present corpus provides.

\paragraph{Covariate-shift audit.}\label{sec:lim-covariate} A per-field train-vs-test positive-support audit (\texttt{audit/extended\_rigor\_audits.json}, keys \texttt{rec7\_covariate\_shift\_*}) flags only modest shifts ($<\!6$~pp) on already-low-support fields: per-row fold-1 Z $-4.4$~pp; full-window fold-1 F $+3.2$~pp and fold-5 F $-5.3$~pp; no flagged fields on the remaining folds. This contributes to the cross-fold standard deviation in Table~\ref{tab:per_axis} but does not change the per-axis qualitative ranking.

\subsection{Per-File Performance Variance}
\label{sec:lim-per-file}

Slicing the 5-fold predictions by source file ($n=91$) shows command accuracy is concentrated, not heavy-tailed ($0.953 \pm 0.088$; 10th percentile still $0.846$), so the categorical-head success is not concentrated on a few memorizable files; AR token accuracy shows the expected wide per-file spread tracking the per-operation-class breakdown (Section~\ref{sec:per-op-class}). The full per-file distribution, and the reconciliation of the unweighted per-file mean ($0.953$) against the per-fold-mean headline ($0.8875 \pm 0.0558$, an aggregation difference, not a prediction discrepancy), are in Supplementary Section~S11.3.

\subsection{Evaluation Limitations}
\label{sec:lim-eval}

\paragraph{Single-run per condition.} Each operation-class file represents a single execution. The metrics in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} therefore conflate cross-condition variance (different operation classes) with cross-trial variance (rerunning the same operation). A designed multi-replicate corpus (multiple executions per condition) would support variance decomposition; the present corpus does not.

\paragraph{Decoder-only inference cost.} The decoder generates token-by-token autoregressively under a grammar mask. Per-row autoregressive decoding on the RTX A6000 is on the order of $30$--$80$~ms per G-code line at the median row length ($5$--$15$ tokens, $K\!=\!2{,}418$, FSM-masked greedy), well within the $250$~ms inter-sample headroom of the $4$~Hz acquisition cadence; full-window mode ($\sim$$1{,}000$--$1{,}400$ tokens per sample) costs proportionally more and is not real-time on this hardware. A beam-search or non-autoregressive variant has not been evaluated; doing so would inform deployment-cost trade-offs (Section~\ref{sec:disc-modality}).

\paragraph{$K$-spectrum methodology confound: resolved.} The four-point vocabulary-cardinality sweep originally compared the headline $K\!=\!2{,}418$ row (SS$=$0.5, dw$=$1.0) against three smaller-$K$ variants under Design~B (SS$=$0, dw$=$0). The smaller-$K$-vs-headline comparison was a joint condition contrast (vocabulary cardinality $+$ training schedule). A methodology-matched $K\!=\!2{,}418$ control trained under SS$=$0/dw$=$0 (T3.1; Section~\ref{sec:abl-nonumeric}) resolves the confound: under matched methodology, $K\!=\!2{,}418$ is the highest-performing variant on every metric (AR struct token $0.470 \pm 0.034$ vs.\ K=335's $0.429 \pm 0.123$ and the headline's $0.317 \pm 0.116$). The earlier $+11.1$~pp K=335 advantage over the headline was the SS/dw schedule change, not vocabulary cardinality; the K=335 ``sweet spot'' framing is retracted in this revision. The deployment-relevant configuration for AR structural recovery is the original $K\!=\!2{,}418$ vocabulary with SS$=$0 and dw$=$0; the smaller-$K$ family is now reported as a clean vocabulary-cardinality contrast within one methodology, with the deployment story carried by the matched-methodology control. Corpus generalization to other platforms and material classes is a separate open question, and the K=2418/SS=0/dw=0 result requires cross-platform replication before it can carry forward (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

\paragraph{TF-numeric baseline reversal (future-work item, not a deployment recommendation).} The seq2seq baseline of Section~\ref{sec:abl-seq2seq-baseline} attains a higher teacher-forced numeric accuracy ($0.754 \pm 0.053$) than the headline six-head digit-decomposition output ($0.585 \pm 0.033$), attributable to the bucketization that makes the grammar mask tractable in the legacy-token head (Section~\ref{sec:problem-vocab}). We flag this as a future-work item for architectural refinement of the numeric head, not as a deployment recommendation: under autoregressive inference --- the regime that matters for the forensic-grade post-hoc audit posture --- the two architectures' numeric accuracies are within a fold of each other ($0.100$ vs.\ $0.104$), so the TF-numeric reversal does not translate to an AR-mode deployment advantage for the baseline.

\paragraph{Pooled numeric accuracy averages memorization against an information-theoretic floor.} The pooled slot-5 ($10^{-4}$~inch) accuracy of $0.864$ in Table~\ref{tab:per_digit_position} is the deployment-relevant qualifier of the body's per-regime characterization (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}, Supplementary Figure~S6): on adaptive HSM rows the per-axis digit is information-theoretically near-unrecoverable, while on facing rows the high accuracy reflects the recall of four geometry-specific X-overrun values that account for $96.5\%$ of face X-coordinates and would not transfer to a different workpiece. Any deployment claim of $10^{-4}$-inch ($2.54$~\textmu m) coordinate reconstruction should therefore be read against the per-axis source entropy in Table~\ref{tab:per_digit_position} and the quantization map in Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding}, not the pooled curve. Supplementary Figure~S20 (Section~S14.5) makes the memorization-vs-entropy split directly visible at the per-file level. For context, $2.54$~\textmu m is at or below the ISO~230-2 axis-positioning-repeatability envelope of comparable desktop CNC platforms~\cite{iso230_2}, so the recovery ceiling is bounded by the sensor pathway, not by the underlying machine accuracy budget.

\paragraph{Entropy bound is in-distribution.} The source-entropy theory of Section~\ref{sec:unified-entropy} is computed at the empirical training distribution. Open-vocabulary or novel-operation-class deployment may exhibit different per-head entropy profiles, particularly if the new class introduces commands or axis-letters absent from training; the leave-one-class-out floor (Section~\ref{sec:loco}, command accuracy $0.213 \pm 0.191$) bounds this risk on the present corpus.

\subsection{Future Directions}
\label{sec:roadmap}

Five directions remain open; each is an open problem rather than a scheduled experiment.

\emph{(a)~Continuous-parameter data-collection campaign.} A designed factorial corpus that varies feed rate (F), spindle speed (S), depth of cut, and material at a higher sampling rate ($\geq\!100$~Hz, to lift the 4-Hz aliasing of $77.7\%$ of rows), populating the F/S/I/J fields the present 4-Hz corpus cannot evaluate. First experiment: a $3\!\times\!3\!\times\!3$ (feed, speed, depth) full factorial on the present platform with the higher-rate acquisition front end, scoped to recover feed-rate substitutions at the resolution adversarial scenarios of Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model} require.

\emph{(b)~Encoder retraining with row-level supervision.} The present fold-1 last-LSTM-layer fine-tune pilot (Section~\ref{sec:hp-sweep}) is within fold-1 noise and underpowered to settle the encoder-bottleneck question. The deliverable artifact is a five-fold retrained encoder under matched preprocessing whose checkpoints are released alongside this manuscript's, so a reproducer can pair the retrained encoder with the present decoder and isolate the encoder-side residual.

\emph{(c)~Per-axis $T$ entropy-rate floor and a per-file class-prior control.} A finite-sample $T$-entropy-rate estimate per axis would give the Z slot-4 / X slot-2 ceilings a tighter inferential anchor than the per-cell entropy figures of Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}, and the per-file modal predictor (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound} caveat) would tighten the $\sim\!22$~pp class-prior-controlled increment to its open-vocabulary lower bound. Both are arithmetic on the released audit JSONs and the released checkpoints, not a retraining campaign.

\emph{(d)~Window/stride re-optimization.} Requires the encoder retraining of (b) and is therefore not separable on the present frozen encoder; the planned cell coverage is a $4\!\times\!4$ $(\text{window}, \text{stride})$ grid retrained from scratch per cell with the row-level-supervised encoder.

\emph{(e)~Cross-platform validation.} A second CNC platform (production-class VMC: cast-iron base, servo drive, flood coolant) and a second material class (aluminum or steel) with a matching sensor stack. The protocol is the present file-level five-fold characterization rerun on the new corpus; the deliverable is the cross-platform transfer matrix for the categorical-vs-numeric factorization.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study characterizes which components of an executing G-code block are recoverable from multi-modal CNC sensor data and which are not, using \modelname{} (a six-head Transformer decoder on a frozen multi-modal denoising encoder, per-row targets, grammar-constrained inference) under five-fold file-level cross-validation. Four findings, each detailed in the section cited:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=*, nosep]
\item \textbf{Per-block categorical recovery} (command identity, axis presence, motion direction) is sensor-driven and TF/AR-invariant by construction (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}); the class-prior-controlled within-class increment over the operation-class modal predictor (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}) is the figure that survives both the prior and an unseen-class holdout.
\item \textbf{End-to-end token/numeric} reproduction is bounded by three coupled mechanisms --- exposure-bias collapse in the autoregressive head (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mode-collapse}), 4-Hz temporal aliasing of $77.7\%$ of rows (Section~\ref{sec:lim-method}), and an information-theoretic floor on fine-precision digits (Section~\ref{sec:numeric-decomposition}); $10^{-4}$-inch ($2.54$~\textmu m) coordinate reconstruction is therefore unsupported on adaptive workloads and geometry-dependent on facing/pocket. The $K$-spectrum retraction and the matched-methodology T3.1 control are reported in Section~\ref{sec:lim-eval}.
\item \textbf{Sensor-stack redundancy.} Inference-time modality removal collapses command accuracy uniformly under the frozen encoder ($p\!=\!0.998$ between modalities; Section~\ref{sec:abl-sensors}); encoder-retraining ablations (LOMO and LOSO; Section~\ref{sec:disc-modality}) show no single sensing modality or sensor unit is individually load-bearing ($\Delta\!\le\!2.6$~pp), enabling a cost-reduced forensic-grade post-hoc audit installation.
\item \textbf{Tamper detection} is largely operation-class-prior-driven ($\approx\!8$~pp sensor-conditioned) and collapses open-vocabulary (leave-one-class-out command-swap FPR $0.52$); the in-distribution $K\!=\!10$ point at the measured $\rho\!=\!0.42$ (FPR $0.10$--$0.18$ at TPR $\approx\!0.94$--$0.97$) is an optimistic in-envelope ceiling that supports \textbf{forensic-grade post-hoc audit} of contested runs at human-in-the-loop $K\!=\!10$ majority, \emph{not} a validated real-time SOC detector (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threat-model}). The intended deployment posture is forensic-grade post-hoc audit, not autonomous alerting; and that posture is explicitly scoped to in-distribution operation classes --- closed-world over the V8 vocabulary and the nine operation classes catalogued in Section~\ref{sec:dataset}, with open-world / novel-operation-class deployment out of scope and deferred to future cross-platform replication (Section~\ref{sec:future}).
\end{enumerate}

A from-scratch Transformer seq2seq baseline (Section~\ref{sec:abl-seq2seq-baseline}) matches the headline command accuracy at teacher-forced inference but collapses at autoregressive inference; the headline numbers stand, and the architectural contribution of the frozen encoder + grammar-constrained decoder + structured heads is sharpened to AR deployment-mode inference, which is the regime that matters for the forensic-grade post-hoc audit posture this paper supports. Closing the gap to full reconstruction is a sequence-head problem first; whether the encoder is co-responsible cannot be settled by the present evidence and requires full 5-fold encoder retraining with row-level supervision across the multi-modal projection and attention stack (Section~\ref{sec:hp-sweep} reports only a single-fold, last-LSTM-layer fine-tune pilot, which is within fold-1 noise and underpowered as a structural claim; Appendix~\ref{app:encoder-spec} restates this). Standard exposure-bias remedies~\cite{he2019exposure,ott2018uncertainty,ranzato2016sequence,holtzman2020curious,welleck2020neural}, a higher sample rate, encoder retraining with row-level supervision, and richer continuous-parameter data are the principal directions for the TRL~5--6 cross-platform target (Section~\ref{sec:future}); cross-platform replication on a production-class VMC is the prerequisite for any deployment claim outside the single desktop CNC platform characterized here, and the present \textbf{forensic-grade post-hoc audit} posture is the boundary within which the load-bearing operating points hold. None of these directions directly upgrades the present forensic-grade posture to real-time SOC alerting --- closing the alert-budget gap requires a structurally different sensing stack and an open-vocabulary recoverability regime the present corpus does not support.

\section{Coverage-Repair Split Procedure}
\label{app:coverage-repair}

File-level stratified $k$-fold splits prevent within-file leakage but do not guarantee \emph{token coverage}: a held-out fold may contain G-code lines that never appear in the corresponding training partner, in which case the decoder is being evaluated on a vocabulary it has not had the opportunity to learn. The coverage-repair procedure described here \emph{constructs a closed-vocabulary evaluation regime} --- it deliberately removes out-of-vocabulary test lines so that the 5-fold experiment measures within-vocabulary classification rather than open-vocabulary generalization --- while preserving the file-level disjointness. This is a design choice, not a bias correction: the resulting headline numbers are within-vocabulary near-retrieval results, and the open-vocabulary capability is reported separately by the leave-one-class-out protocol (Section~\ref{sec:loco}). We make the regime explicit here and quantify the resulting train/test line overlap in Section~\ref{sec:lim-dataset}.

Given a candidate (train, test) partition for fold $i$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=*]
\item Tokenize the G-code text of every window in both splits and compute $U_{\text{test}}$ and $U_{\text{train}}$, the sets of distinct G-code lines occurring in each.
\item Let $M = U_{\text{test}} \setminus U_{\text{train}}$ be the set of test-only lines.
\item If $M = \emptyset$, accept the partition. Otherwise identify the file $f^\star \in \text{test}$ contributing the largest number of $M$-lines, and the file $g^\star \in \text{train}$ whose removal-and-replacement minimally perturbs the per-class fold proportions. Swap $f^\star$ and $g^\star$.
\item Recompute $M$ and repeat until $M = \emptyset$ or until a swap-budget of $10$ is exhausted (in which case the residual $M$-lines are recorded as a "novel-vocabulary at test" diagnostic but the partition is accepted).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Tie-breaking and determinism.} Step (3) iterates over files; on ties --- when two or more candidate $f^\star$ contribute the same number of $M$-lines, or when two or more candidate $g^\star$ produce the same per-class-proportion perturbation --- the implementation breaks the tie by ascending alphabetical filename ordering, which is fully deterministic and not dependent on process-level hash ordering. An earlier release of the script iterated an unordered Python \texttt{set}, which produced a hash-seed-dependent partition order across preprocessing runs (the latent reproducibility defect surfaced and corrected during the LOMO study; Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}). The deterministic alphabetical-tie-break version is the one used for \emph{every} 5-fold result reported in this paper (headline, ablations, LOMO, LOSO, calibration, tamper), and the resulting per-fold (file, fold) assignment table is released as \texttt{audit/file\_fold\_assignment.csv} so a reproducer does not need to rerun the split logic.

In practice the procedure converges in 0--3 swaps per fold on the present corpus. Recorded fold sizes after coverage repair differ from the stratified target by at most one file per class.

\section{Encoder Specification Addendum}
\label{app:encoder-spec}

The encoder architecture, channel layout, denoising objective, and 96.3\% nine-class accuracy are stated in Section~\ref{sec:encoder} and the encoder paper~\cite{encoder_paper}, which is under review at the time of submission; for self-containment, the salient architectural facts are: an 8.6~M-parameter multi-modal denoising autoencoder over $C\!=\!98$ channels (seven modality groups: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, environmental, color, audio-RMS, electrical; channel inventory per board listed in Section~\ref{sec:dataset}); per-modality projection into a shared $d\!=\!256$ space; learned modality-weighting gates; multi-head self-attention; a denoising reconstruction objective with $\sigma_{\text{noise}}\!=\!0.1$ on standard-scaled inputs; and a two-layer LSTM ($d\!=\!256$) producing the $256\!\times\!256$ memory consumed by the decoder. Training: 30 epochs on the same 5-fold file-level splits as the decoder, AdamW ($\eta\!=\!10^{-4}$), batch size 32, dropout 0.2, early stopping on validation operation-class accuracy. Frozen-encoder checkpoint hashes per fold are released alongside the decoder checkpoints (Supplementary Section~S9.1). The note relevant to this paper's interpretation is the \emph{representational confound}: the encoder was trained for nine-class operation discrimination on the \emph{same} file-level 5-fold splits used by the decoder, so its memory primarily encodes operation-class structure. This bounds what the decoder's categorical heads can recover and is the representational counterpart of the LOCO collapse (Section~\ref{sec:disc-recoverability}, Section~\ref{sec:loco}). \paragraph{Representation-reuse caveat (raised in review).} Because the encoder was trained on the same file-level fold partitions later used to evaluate the decoder, the encoder did see (during its own training) the sensor windows from the decoder's test files within other folds; the headline 0.888 command number therefore contains an unmeasured representation-reuse component on top of the per-instruction sensor recovery the paper claims. The 22 pp class-prior-controlled increment (Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}) is the load-bearing magnitude, but \emph{its} size is itself conditioned on the encoder having pre-seen the decoder's test files at encoder training time. A single-fold experiment in which the encoder is retrained on a leave-the-decoder-test-files-out partition (the encoder never sees any sensor window from the decoder's test fold) would quantify this; it is on the artifact roadmap as a companion to the encoder-fine-tune cell of Section~\ref{sec:hp-sweep} and is not present in the released checkpoint. The headline should therefore be read as closer to a representation-probe than a fully partitioned generalization result; the LOCO collapse (Section~\ref{sec:loco}) is the unambiguous out-of-class generalization figure.

\paragraph{Quantified upper bound on the representation-reuse component.} The retraining experiment described above is not yet executed, but the class-prior control of Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound} (paragraph \emph{Is categorical recovery sensor-driven or operation-class structure?}) already pins a defensible upper bound on the leakage component without retraining. Consider the worst case: the encoder has memorized the operation-class label of every decoder test sample perfectly, and the decoder reads nothing from the sensor stream beyond that one class-label bit. The maximum command-prediction accuracy any predictor can achieve from a class-label-only input is exactly the accuracy of the operation-class modal predictor --- emit the modal command of the test sample's operation class --- because that predictor extracts \emph{all} the command information a class label can carry. The class-prior control reports this number directly: $0.572 \pm 0.004$ command accuracy at command-token positions of the full-window predictions (\texttt{audit/confound\_class\_prior.json}). The full decoder reaches $0.795 \pm 0.121$ at the same position subset. Therefore the $+0.223$ paired increment (Wilcoxon $5/5$ folds positive; paired-bootstrap 95\% CI $\approx [+0.11, +0.34]$; Section~\ref{sec:disc-confound}) is the sensor-conditioned command-discrimination signal that survives \emph{even under the worst-case assumption that the encoder has perfectly memorized the operation class of every test sample}: it is a within-class increment that no amount of class-label leakage can explain, because the class-label channel is fully saturated by the $0.572$ modal predictor by construction. The representation-reuse caveat thus upper-bounds the leakage component of command accuracy at $0.572$, not at the headline $0.888$ or the position-conditioned $0.795$; the $+22$~pp increment is leakage-immune in the worst case. The headline $0.888$ (Table~\ref{tab:headline}) is the same prediction stream aggregated at all-token positions rather than command-only positions and the bounding argument applies in proportion: under perfect-encoder-memorization, the maximum recoverable from class-label leakage alone at the all-token aggregation is upper-bounded by the corresponding all-token class-prior baseline (which is higher than $0.572$ because non-command positions carry less class-conditional command uncertainty by construction), and the within-class sensor-conditioned increment is the residual above that baseline. This is an upper bound on the leakage, not a measurement of it: the actual representation-reuse component will be smaller than $0.572$ because the encoder's $96.3\%$ nine-class accuracy is not perfect memorization and the decoder's command head must still recover within-class command structure on the $\approx\!97\%$ of windows that contain more than one distinct command. The leave-decoder-test-files-out encoder retraining remains the unambiguous measurement and is on the roadmap; in the interim, the $0.572$ upper bound and the $+0.223$ leakage-immune increment are the quantified statement of how much of the headline could and could not, respectively, be attributable to encoder representation reuse.

\paragraph{Empirical encoder-reuse bound from the LOMO baseline.} An independent empirical check is provided by the leave-one-modality-out (LOMO) baseline of Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}. Each LOMO baseline cell retrains the encoder from scratch on the train+val files of the corresponding fold; the test files are held out from encoder training by construction (verified per cell via the channel-identity gate described in Section~\ref{sec:abl-lomo}, which confirms that the train/validation/test file partition is invariant across cells and that only the sensor tensors differ). The resulting decoder command accuracy under that retrained-encoder regime is $0.904 \pm 0.039$ (5-fold mean $\pm$ s.d.; per-fold $0.839$, $0.932$, $0.929$, $0.899$, $0.922$) --- comparable to the headline $0.888 \pm 0.056$ within fold-to-fold variance. The empirical encoder-reuse effect on command accuracy is therefore bounded at $|\Delta|\!\le\!0.016$, with the leave-decoder-test-files-out (LOMO baseline) point in fact slightly higher than the headline rather than lower. The slightly higher LOMO baseline ($0.904$ vs.\ $0.888$) is within fold-to-fold seed variance (the per-fold spread $\sigma\!\approx\!0.04$ is more than $2\times$ the $|\Delta|\!=\!0.016$ between the two estimates) and does not indicate a systematic under-tuning of the headline configuration; the two estimates are statistically indistinguishable with $n\!=\!5$. Combined with the analytic class-prior upper bound above, this dispatches the encoder representation-reuse concern: under the worst-case class-label-leakage argument the load-bearing $+22$~pp within-class increment is leakage-immune, and under the direct retrained-encoder empirical check the headline command accuracy itself moves by less than $0.02$.

\section{Grammar Tokenization Addendum}
\label{app:grammar-spec}

The token classes, vocabulary cardinality, per-axis NUM bucket counts, the worked example, and the FSM/bigram mask are specified in Section~\ref{sec:problem} and Section~\ref{sec:grammar}; the tokenization itself is defined in the companion grammar paper~\cite{grammar_paper}, which is in preparation at the time of submission. For self-containment, the per-axis quantization precision is $p_X\!=\!p_Y\!=\!p_Z\!=\!p_P\!=\!p_Q\!=\!10^{-3}$, $p_I\!=\!p_J\!=\!p_K\!=\!p_R\!=\!p_E\!=\!10^{-4}$, $p_F\!=\!1.0$ (mm/min), $p_S\!=\!10.0$ (rpm); F and S are excluded from the first-order PID structural grammar.

\paragraph{Bucket-index formula.} Given a numeric value $v\in\mathbb{R}$ on axis $a$ with precision $p_a$, the bucket index $b_a(v)$ used to construct the \texttt{NUM\_a\_b} token is
\begin{equation*}
b_a(v) = \mathrm{sgn}(v)\cdot\left\lfloor |v|/p_a \right\rfloor,
\end{equation*}
with the sign bit encoded by the separate sign head (Section~\ref{sec:multihead}), not by the bucket index. The released implementation uses the round-to-scaled-integer rewrite $b = \mathrm{round}(|v|\cdot 10^{1/p_a})$ on the development branch (Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding}); the pre-patch released checkpoint uses an iterative \texttt{int(dec\_part * 10) \% 10} digitization whose audited float-epsilon artifacts are characterized in Appendix~\ref{app:digit_encoding}. Zero maps to $b\!=\!0$ on every axis. Per-axis bucket counts ($1{,}048$ NUM\_X, $1{,}086$ NUM\_Y, $231$ NUM\_Z, etc., summing to $2{,}418$) are determined empirically as the cardinality of $\{b_a(v):v\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}\}$ on the training corpus; any test-time $b$ that exceeds the trained range falls back to \texttt{UNK} (closed-vocabulary regime, Section~\ref{sec:cv}).
